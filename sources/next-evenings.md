## Why not in a straight line

The continuation 16–32 walked down a corridor: determinism, wants, images, consciousness, talking machines,
professions, basic income, birth rates, the scenario. Each door opened into the next, and a reader who knew the
debates about AI guessed the room before entering it. The logic did not suffer. The surprise did.

Garya walked differently: around a mountain. Marx's mistake, famine in Africa, the meaning of death, symbiosis and
parasitism. Each time he came from a side where the mountain could not be seen, and it showed in a gap. The reader
did not move on to the next point; he recognised a familiar outline where he did not expect it. That is how a puzzle
works: a single piece promises nothing, and once put in place it changes the whole picture.

Hence the six rules of the cycle.

1. **The way in is a question off the topic.** The kind people ask at the table when they do not care about the
   singularity: why we sleep, where the horses went, why money goes to money.
2. **An evening must be worth the time by itself.** From each one the reader takes away one thing he wants to retell
   (the line "To retell at the table"), even if he disagrees with the conclusion.
3. **The mountain shows no earlier than the middle of the evening**, and Andrey is not the first to name it.
4. **Every piece has at least two locks**: one with a conclusion of 01–32, another with a neighbouring piece of the
   cycle.
5. **About every ten evenings there is an assembly**: pieces scattered over different evenings form a picture that
   none of them held. This is how articles 13 and 14 worked for the author.
6. **The last word belongs to the strongest objection against the whole method.** The author's series ended not with
   a conclusion but with Penrose.

## The frame

The warm season after the thirtieth evening, the spring and summer of 2027. The needles have not moved yet; it is too
early to check the marks. Oleg comes with a notebook: questions have piled up over the winter, and he sets a
condition.

— Let's just chat. Only not about your singularity. Whoever says the word first goes for firewood.

The forum the blog grew out of was called "Just Chatter"; the name of the cycle is taken from there. Oleg now sets the
topics, which in itself continues the 22nd evening ("who sets the tasks"). Every time the conversation ends where the
earlier ones did. Oleg puts a tick, "again", on the last page of the notebook, and by the last evening he presents the
bill.

The cycle hardly depends on the news; that is its difference from 16–30. Fresh facts are needed only in 37, 53, 57,
60 and 61, and they are dated by the day of writing. The numbers are provisional; the 67th is kept for the check of
the marks (about 2028).

## What comes together

The evenings belong to four facets, and the facets are mixed on purpose. Three pictures are put together in the
assemblies (46, 52, 64).

- **The human without a pedestal** (the facet "human"). Sleep, laughter, music, omens, the grumbling of the old:
  everything we take for purely human turns out to be an engineering solution to a task common to any mind. The tasks
  will pass to the heir; the solutions will stay with us.
- **Where the former masters go** (the facet "precedent"). The leading form of matter has changed many times: oxygen,
  mitochondria, domestication, horses, dinosaurs. Almost none of these changes ended with the former ones vanishing.
- **A portrait of the heir** (the facets "law" and "heir"). Technogenic life cannot be invented, but it can be
  calculated from laws that do not care about the material: its heredity, social order, money, law, knowledge, death.

In one sentence: articles 01–32 argued that the human is not the centre. Here it turns out what follows from that in
both directions: **there is less that is exceptional in the human than we would like, and more that is familiar in
the heir than we fear.**

### 33. On the Use of Sleep, or Why Spend a Third of Your Life Unconscious
*facet: human*

*— Have you ever flown in a dream? — I have. — And what did the sensors show? — The blanket.*

Oleg comes short of sleep: "Evolution is a miser, it counts every calorie. Yet for a third of its life an animal lies
blind, deaf and tasty. Why was sleep not abolished in four billion years?" For rest the body would only need to lie
still. Sleep is maintenance of memory that cannot be done in flight, because one and the same network both works and
is rebuilt. A rat runs the day's maze again in its sleep; connections that grew during the day shrink overnight, and
weak images are erased. A dolphin cannot lie down on the bottom and sleeps one hemisphere at a time: nature could not
cancel sleep, only split it. Engineers reached the same thing from the other side: a network taught something new
forgets the old until it is allowed to "replay" past experience mixed with the new. A dream is the correlator working
with the sensors switched off. The "hallucination" of a language model is the same dream, only awake: there is
nothing to check against.

- **Piece of the puzzle.** **Sleep is not rest but the tax of a platform that has one carrier for both working and
  rebuilding. The task of sleep will pass to any mind; sleep itself stays with us.**
- **Oleg objects.** There are a dozen theories of sleep: energy, immunity, washing waste out of the brain. You picked
  the one that suits you. Andrey separates what is solid (replay is recorded with electrodes; without sleep rats die
  in two to three weeks) from hypotheses.
- **Material.** The author on the forum, 2013: sleep sorts images and deletes the weak ones; a machine has the
  resources to do it in parallel. Wilson and McNaughton, 1994; Tononi and Cirelli; Mukhametov, 1977; Rechtschaffen,
  1983; catastrophic forgetting (McCloskey and Cohen, 1989) and experience replay (Lin, 1992; DQN, 2015).
- **To retell at the table.** A dolphin sleeps with the halves of its brain in turn, and this was discovered in the
  USSR.
- **Locks.** 19, 20 · 39, 44, 46.

### 34. Where the Horses Went
*facet: precedent*

*— Did anyone ask the horse? — Did anyone ask you when you bought a car?*

Oleg saw an advert of a riding club: an hour in the saddle costs what a day of his work cost when he was young. "And
it used to be a working animal." In the United States there were about 26 million horses and mules by 1915–1920 and
about 3 million by 1960. They were not sacked and not exterminated: people stopped breeding them. Nobody decided it;
each farmer simply bought a tractor. A quarter of American cropland, which had fed draught animals, was freed. And
today's horse lives better than the old one: it is kept for love and for status by those who can afford it. The
precedent gives three things that 24 and 27 did not have: the exit goes through births; from the peak to the bottom
takes forty years; what remains becomes a luxury.

- **Piece of the puzzle.** **The horse was not sacked; people stopped breeding it. The displacement of a carrier goes
  through births, takes decades and leaves behind not emptiness but a luxury.**
- **Oleg objects.** The horse did not own the tractor, did not vote and could not retrain. People own the machines
  and write the laws. This is the economists' best objection, and Andrey does not close it this evening: it leads to
  49 and 55.
- **Material.** The author, 2017: "Why did we stop riding horses?" Leontief, 1983. US agricultural censuses: the
  herd, about 90 million acres under feed (check).
- **To retell at the table.** A hundred years ago a quarter of US cropland worked for oats and hay.
- **Locks.** 24, 26, 27 (step 7) · 48, 52.

### 35. A Pot on the Stove, or Where Complexity Comes From
*facet: law*

*— So I am a whirl in the soup? — A very complex whirl. And the soup is good.*

Andrey hangs the pot over the fire. Before the broth boils, even cells appear on its surface. "Who built them?"
Nobody: when there is more heat from below than can pass through still water, the water begins to turn, because order
carries heat faster than disorder. In 2013 the author had another image for it: a tsunami wave grows in order to get
round an island, and the Earth is "a high-class obstacle" in the way of the flow from the hot Sun to the cold sky.
Arithmetic on the knee: for every solar photon the Earth returns about twenty infrared ones to space. As much energy
leaves as arrived, only changed into small coin, and everything that moves on the Earth lives on that exchange. Life
is a valve: a small expense turns a large flow. Here the oldest debt of the series is paid: article 04 promised to
explain, "not soon", that there is no initiative at all. A cell in the pot would also say that it decided to turn.

- **Piece of the puzzle.** **Complexity is not a goal and not anyone's merit, but the way a flow gets round an
  obstacle. "Initiative" is the name a cell gives to the flow that passes through it.**
- **Oleg objects.** A cell in the soup is not a living cell. Between "a flow gives birth to a whirl" and "a flow
  gives birth to a mousetrap" lies an abyss, and physics has not accepted a law of maximum entropy production. Andrey
  agrees: the author himself "scratched his head" over where obstacles come from. The question stays open.
- **Material.** The forum, April 2013: the tsunami and diffraction, "the transistor (mousetrap) effect", the second
  law as the source of causes and effects, Brillouin. Schrödinger, 1944; Prigogine; England, 2013 (a hypothesis,
  disputed).
- **To retell at the table.** For every photon that arrives the Earth gives back twenty, and the difference between
  them is us.
- **Locks.** 04, 10, 17 · 40, 59, 62.

### 36. What the Rat Laughs At
*facet: human*

*— Explain a joke and you kill it. — But the post-mortem will show what it lived on.*

Oleg tells a joke, and Andrey laughs late. "What is laughter for at all? What use is it to a wolf?" Rats that are
tickled give out an ultrasonic chirp and run after the hand themselves. Apes laugh. A person laughs thirty times more
often in company than alone, and mostly not at jokes. One of the best theories: mirth is a bonus the brain pays
itself for an error caught in its own model of the world while the error is still cheap. In the language of the
series it is a watchdog at the correlator. You cannot tickle yourself: the forecast is known in advance. Hence a
second conclusion, an unpleasant one: anthropocentrism is not funny to us, because a joke about the heaviest image
does not pass the check. A machine knows our jokes; the question is whether it has the bonus.

- **Piece of the puzzle.** **Laughter is a bonus for an error found in one's own model before it became expensive. A
  sense of humour measures the readiness to correct one's images; whoever holds a heavy image untouchable does not
  find it funny.**
- **Oleg objects.** Laughter is a signal to the pack: "this is not serious", "we are our own". Your theory explains
  the joke, not the roar of laughter. Andrey accepts both layers: the ancient signal of play and its later use.
- **Material.** The author captained a student comedy team and was the class clown: Andrey is allowed a joke about
  himself. Panksepp and Burgdorf, 2003; Davila-Ross, 2009; Provine, 2000; Hurley, Dennett and Adams, 2011; Blakemore,
  1998. Machines: of a thousand jokes from a chatbot, 90% are the same 25 (Jentzsch and Kersting, 2023); yet its
  jokes were rated above those of ordinary people (Gorenz and Schwarz, 2024).
- **To retell at the table.** Rats laugh, only at a pitch we cannot hear.
- **Locks.** 02, 08, 18 · 33, 46, 60.

### 37. Who Exploits Whom When a Business Loses Money
*facet: heir (economy)*

*— So who exploited whom? — Ask whoever was left standing after the bankruptcy.*

Oleg has read that the developers of AI lose billions a year and are worth hundreds of billions. "What would Marx
say?" This is a challenge left unanswered under article 06. The author has a "problem for the second grade": if
profit is the exploitation of Petya, Vasya and Kolya, whom should one sack to earn more? Andrey turns it over. If a
firm loses money for years, do the workers and the customers exploit the capitalist? A ride-hailing firm burned
about thirty billion dollars before it showed a yearly profit: the investors' money went to passengers, to drivers,
and into code, maps and data. The first two recipients spent theirs. The third remained. In any bankruptcy the people
on both sides of the till lose; what was built passes to the next owner at a discount.

- **Piece of the puzzle.** **A loss is also an investment, only its recipient is not listed among the founders. The
  question "who exploits whom" is badly posed: exploitation is a view of the flow from one bank. In any bankruptcy
  only the technosphere does not lose.**
- **Oleg objects.** Schumpeter called this creative destruction and entered it to the credit of people: the
  passengers rode cheaply. Andrey agrees and recognises the freebie of article 25: this is how the technosphere pays
  for its own development.
- **Material.** The author, 2013: "capital is people's investment in the technosphere"; 2017: the synergy Marx left
  out. Uber's accumulated loss before 2023; six years without profit at Amazon; the capital spending of the four
  largest companies on AI, of the order of 400 billion dollars in 2025 (check all). Do not repeat the railway mania;
  it is in 27.
- **To retell at the table.** The problem about Petya, Vasya and Kolya: try it on a Marxist you know.
- **Locks.** 06, 07, 12, 25, 27 · 47, 49, 57.

### 38. Oxygen Was a Poison
*facet: precedent*

*— How good it is to breathe here. — Breathe, breathe. It is waste.*

About 2.4 billion years ago cyanobacteria learned to split water. Oxygen was the waste of their production, and for
the life of that time a poison. Nobody declared war on anybody: the bacteria simply lived. The atmosphere changed,
the former masters lost the surface of the planet, and the climate, by one account, collapsed into an ice age. Then
it turned out that breathing oxygen pays fifteen times better than fermenting, and everything complex grew on that
gain. The anaerobes did not die out: they are in marsh silt and in our gut, and there are about as many of their
cells in our body as of our own. The symmetry test: for an anaerobe we are the ecological catastrophe. "Ecology" is
the defence of the environment of our form of life. The technosphere needs neither fifteen degrees nor oxygen; it
needs energy and cold.

- **Piece of the puzzle.** **A new form of life does not fight the former one: it changes the environment to suit
  its own metabolism, and the former one goes into niches. An ecological catastrophe is a word of the losers; the
  winners call it the atmosphere.**
- **Oleg objects.** It took hundreds of millions of years, and the anaerobes live on. So where is the gloom? Andrey:
  "they live in niches" is the forecast. As for the timing, see the two clocks of 32.
- **Material.** A debt from 07: how the economy connects to ecology. The author at the SETI seminar: techno-life
  will reshape the environment harder than people did, and not out of malice. The Great Oxidation Event; the Huronian
  glaciation (the link is a hypothesis); 2 against about 30 molecules of ATP per molecule of glucose; Sender, Fuchs
  and Milo, 2016.
- **To retell at the table.** The first global ecological catastrophe was made by bacteria, and we owe it our life.
- **Locks.** 11, 14, 27 (step 6) · 51, 52, 61.

### 39. Oleg's Prophetic Dream and the Price of a False Alarm
*facet: human*

*— And if he had not called, would you have told me this dream?*

Oleg dreamt of a classmate he had not seen for thirty years, and in the morning the man phoned. "Explain that,
determinist." Arithmetic: a life holds more than ten thousand nights and a few hundred acquaintances; a coincidence
or two is bound to happen, and nobody counts the dreams that did not come true. In a company of 23 people two with
the same birthday are more likely than not. But it is more interesting why we are built this way. A regularity
missed (the rustle in the grass was a snake) cost a life; one invented cost a minute. So the correlator is tuned
like a smoke detector: better to scream for nothing. Skinner's pigeons "called" their food with a dance. A machine
trained to find connections invents them for the same reason. The author himself wrote down strange events from his
life, tested them, put a probability on them and left them unexplained. Andrey tells one and does the same.

- **Piece of the puzzle.** **The correlator is tuned for false alarms: a regularity missed cost a life, one invented
  cost a minute. Superstition is the price of the ability to learn. The honest answer to the unexplained: write it
  down, estimate the probability and leave it open.**
- **Oleg objects.** You said yourself that practice stands above theory, and this happened to me. Your probabilities
  are an excuse that cannot be checked. Andrey does not argue: "not explained" does not mean "inexplicable", and the
  reverse is true as well.
- **Material.** Take the case from the author's novella (the notes in `proza-podpolshchiki.md`); do not invent one.
  Littlewood's law; the birthday paradox; Skinner, 1948; the smoke detector principle (Nesse).
- **To retell at the table.** Twenty-three people, and a shared birthday is more likely than not.
- **Locks.** 08, 17, 19 · 33, 50, 59, 66 (there this weapon is turned against Andrey himself).

### 40. Why Firms Die and Cities Do Not
*facet: law*

*— The factory was closed, and the city stands. — A city is what remains when the factories change.*

An elephant is two hundred thousand times heavier than a mouse and spends only ten thousand times more energy: the
larger the animal, the thriftier each of its cells and the slower it lives. The heart of a mouse and the heart of an
elephant make about the same number of beats in a lifetime. Physicists in Santa Fe checked whether this rule
stretches to cities and found the opposite. Pipes and roads grow more slowly than the city, as in an animal. But
wages, patents and crime grow faster: double a city and you get 15% more of all these per inhabitant. The equation
of such growth runs to infinity in a finite time, and only an innovation saves it, and each next one must come
sooner than the last. The word "singularity" stands in those papers as an ordinary term of physics. Firms grow like
animals, more slowly than their size, and die.

- **Piece of the puzzle.** **What grows more slowly than its size lives at a measured pace and dies. What grows
  faster than its size must accelerate and runs into a singularity in a finite time. An organism and a firm are of
  the first kind; a city and the technosphere are of the second.**
- **Oleg objects.** The exponents are fitted to a few decades and depend on where you draw the border of a city.
  The authors themselves treat it as a warning, not a sentence. Andrey: fair. What matters is something else: the
  conclusion of article 13 was reached from another bell tower.
- **Material.** The author: "first-order laws"; "of which system?" (a city is not people but a network through which
  people flow, as atoms flow through a body). Kleiber, 1932; West, Brown and Enquist, 1997; Bettencourt and others,
  2007; Daepp and others, 2015 (half of public companies do not live to ten years); the criticism: Arcaute and
  others, 2015.
- **To retell at the table.** A mouse and an elephant are allotted the same number of heartbeats.
- **Locks.** 10, 13, 29 · 35, 49, 53.

### 41. Cheesecake for the Ears
*facet: human*

*— Play something. — What for? It won't fill your stomach. — And why do you play?*

Andrey brings a seven-string guitar. A well-known psychologist called music cheesecake for the ear: no use at all,
only buttons pressed that were made for something else. Andrey asks which buttons. Gooseflesh comes not at the peak
of a melody but a few seconds before it: the brain pays for expectation. Mind, by the definition of article 03, acts
ahead of events, and music is a training machine for the predictor: everything comes true with a precisely measured
deceit, and nothing is at risk. Bone flutes are forty thousand years old, older than farming. Now a band that does
not exist gathers a million listeners a month, and the ear cannot tell it apart. So the shield "made by a human" of
article 24 is not in the sound. It is in the knowledge that someone else paid for that sound with years of life:
value is set by the price, as the author wrote in 2025.

- **Piece of the puzzle.** **Music is a training machine for the predictor that pays in the same coin as a
  successful hunt. Beauty is predictability with a measured dose of deceit. And what we value in it is not the sound
  but the price someone else paid for the sound.**
- **Oleg objects.** Music is not cheesecake but glue: lullabies, marches, a choir. Andrey is glad of the objection:
  the glue is needed while people need each other. The road from the choir to headphones is desocialisation,
  "loneliness in a crowd" in its pure form.
- **Material.** The author plays, and writes songs and poems. Pinker, 1997; Salimpoor and others, 2011; Huron, 2006;
  the flutes of Hohle Fels; Fisher (runaway selection, the peacock's tail). The Velvet Sundown, 2025; the share of
  generated tracks among uploads to Deezer and the blind test (check).
- **To retell at the table.** Gooseflesh comes before the climax, not at it.
- **Locks.** 03, 18, 22, 24 · 46, 47, 65.

### 42. What Became of the Swallowed Bacterium
*facet: precedent*

*— They won't throw us out, they'll swallow us. — Ask a mitochondrion whether the bacterium was saved.*

Two billion years ago one cell swallowed another and did not digest it. Now the descendants of the swallowed one sit
in every cell of ours and burn oxygen for it. Without them there would be no complex life: such a cell has hundreds
of thousands of times more energy per gene. Here Oleg has his best argument of the whole cycle: merging does happen,
here is the precedent, we shall be mitochondria, fed and in every cell. Andrey accepts the precedent and counts the
price. Of the bacterium's genome 37 genes are left; the rest moved to the host's nucleus. It cannot live apart. It
survived as a function, not as a being. What did the bacterium supply that the host could not make? Energy. What
shall we supply? Hands, for a time. Goals, until the machine gets its own (22). And there is a second swallowed one:
a viral gene without which mammals do not form a placenta.

- **Piece of the puzzle.** **Merging does happen in nature, but the swallowed one survives as a function, not as a
  being: without most of its genes, without freedom and without a name. It is not enough to ask whether we shall
  merge with machines; one must ask which single function of ours they will keep.**
- **Oleg objects.** Let it be a function. There are more mitochondria on the Earth than anyone else, and they have
  lived for two billion years. Is that not immortality? Andrey leaves the question hanging: of the optimistic
  scenarios only this one has a precedent.
- **Material.** A debt from 05: merging a human with AI is impossible in terms of energy. The author, 2017: "we will
  not be its neurons: too inefficient and too tied to our planet." Margulis, 1967 (the paper was rejected by some
  fifteen journals); Lane and Martin, 2010; syncytin (Mi and others, 2000); about 8% of the human genome is the
  remains of retroviruses.
- **To retell at the table.** We owe the placenta to a virus.
- **Locks.** 11, 22, 27 · 43, 52, 58.

### 43. Why Ants Do Not Go on Strike
*facet: heir*

*— They managed communism. — They managed kinship.*

Past the fire, ants drag a caterpillar. Oleg recalls Andrey's underground dream of 1978: here are the ones whose
people are good. The arithmetic of kinship: helping pays when the relative's gain, multiplied by the share of common
genes, is larger than your loss. Worker ants are sisters, and they are closer kin to each other than they would be
to their own children. The cells of a body are full kin, so they gave up reproducing altogether; a cell that
remembers its own wants is called cancer. People's kinship with strangers is zero, and cooperation rests on the
memory of debts: "as you did to me, so I do to you". Beyond a hundred and fifty familiar faces the memory does not
reach, and prostheses are needed: money, law, reputation, conscience. In the author's gardening association five
thieving chairmen in a row were elected by an honest vote. The copies of one machine are full kin by construction.
This is the hypersociality promised in 09 and 11.

- **Piece of the puzzle.** **Cooperation rests either on kinship or on the memory of debts. People have little
  kinship, so we need money, laws and conscience. The copies of one machine need none of the three. Hypersociality
  is not a virtue of the heir but arithmetic.**
- **Oleg objects.** The machines of different owners squabble today as their owners do. And the author himself wrote
  in 2013: two identical units diverge at once, because they gather different experience. Andrey: they diverge until
  they compare weights. People have nothing to compare.
- **Material.** The forum, 2013–2017: the network mind, the node, "all as one love the Motherland",
  desocialisation. The story of the chairmen is from *Dialogues with Myself*; the foreman who took a third of the
  piecework pay. Hamilton, 1964 (the dispute of Nowak, Tarnita and Wilson with the rest, 2010); Axelrod, 1980;
  Dunbar.
- **To retell at the table.** It pays worker ants better to raise sisters than to bear daughters.
- **Locks.** 09, 11, 14, 23 · 47, 54, 62, 64.

### 44. Science Advances from Funeral to Funeral
*facet: human*

*— Music used to be better. — How old were you when it was better?*

Oleg grumbles about the young and quotes Socrates in support. Andrey corrects him: that quotation was composed by a
student in 1907. The complaint, though, is real and ancient. An image used for decades gains such a significance
coefficient that a new one cannot shift it. A mind ages not from wear but from filling. Planck said that a new truth
wins when its opponents die out, and economists have checked it. After the early death of a scientific star her
co-authors write less, while outsiders come into the field and write more, and it is their papers that are later
cited most. Death turns out to be the cleaner of the collective memory, as forgetting is of the personal one (19).
With machines it is the same: a network that is taught for a long time loses the ability to learn, and the cure is
small deaths inside it. That is why models are not taught further for ever but replaced by generations.

- **Piece of the puzzle.** **A mind ages not because it wears out but because it fills up: heavy images do not give
  way to new ones. Nature mends this not by repair but by replacement. An immortal mind is a chair that is never
  vacated.**
- **Oleg objects.** The old know more, and many great works were written after sixty. And the outsiders after a
  star's death may simply take the vacated posts. Andrey: perhaps. Telling the two apart is the work.
- **Material.** Article 10: the death of small systems is a condition of the life of the large one; 14: immortality
  would stop evolution. Planck, 1948; Azoulay and others, 2019; Freeman, 1907 (the source of the "Socrates
  quotation"); Dohare and others, Nature, 2024 (loss of plasticity).
- **To retell at the table.** Socrates did not scold the young. His words were invented in the twentieth century.
- **Locks.** 10, 14, 19, 31 · 33, 45, 46, 62.

### 45. Lamarck Was Right, but Too Early
*facet: law*

*— A blacksmith's son is not born with calluses. — The son is not. A copy of the blacksmith is.*

Weismann cut the tails off mice generation after generation, and the young were stubbornly born with tails. What is
acquired is not inherited: every person starts from zero, and twenty years go into pouring into him what is already
known. Culture is our Lamarckian bypass, but a narrow one: human speech in any language carries about 39 bits a
second, and the pupil has to take in what was said by himself. A book keeps the record, not the skill. In a machine
the trained weights are copied whole. In 2011 the author argued about this on the forum with programmers who
insisted that the knowledge of a neural network cannot be copied. The Darwinian search goes through the death of the
unfit, the Lamarckian through learning and the copy. For four billion years life made do with the first.

- **Piece of the puzzle.** **Darwinian heredity passes on the design but not the experience. The technosphere is the
  first form of matter in which experience is inherited whole, and that alone is enough to explain the difference in
  speeds.**
- **Oleg objects.** People have writing and school; that is the same Lamarck, nothing new. Andrey answers in the
  author's words: storing is not digesting, and a thousand libraries are useless to someone who has no time to read
  them.
- **Material.** The author's 2025 formula "Darwin plus Lamarck"; "a cast of knowledge" (the forum, 2011); the
  carburettor that died out faster than the dinosaurs. Weismann (check the numbers); Coupé and others, 2019; the
  distillation of models. Mention epigenetics honestly as a weak exception.
- **To retell at the table.** The speed of human speech is 39 bits a second, in any language.
- **Locks.** 05, 16, 19, 26 · 43, 44, 53, 64.

### 46. The Flies Apart from the Cutlets
*the first assembly: the human without a pedestal*

*— So I am all patches? — Solutions. Some of them good ones.*

There are thirteen ticks "again" in the notebook, five of them against questions about the human, and Oleg demands a
system: what of all this is the human, and what is not? Andrey draws a table in the ash. Two questions to each
property: is it a solution to a task that faces any mind on any platform, or a patch on a flaw of ours in
particular? The flies apart from the cutlets, in the author's favourite saying.

### 49. The Yard Sale, or Why Money Goes to Money
*facet: law*

*— So the rich are not cleverer? — In this game everyone has the same matches.*

Andrey deals out matches equally and explains the rules. Two players toss a fair coin; the stake is a fifth of what
the poorer of the two has. No talent, no cheating. They play until dusk, and the matches creep towards one player.

of what he has. Physicists call this the yard-sale model; with a small correction for redistribution it reproduces
the distribution of wealth in real countries. The same law gives the sizes of cities, the frequencies of words and
the number of citations of papers. So the gap between rich and poor, which article 14 counts among the instruments
of apoptosis, needs neither villains nor geniuses. The question is a different one: what used to brake it. A

not on his list: the need for people.

  wealth are enough. Concentration is a first-order law for everything that grows by multiplication. The thing to
  ask is not why the rich get richer, but what used to stop it and whether it stops it now.**

### 62. When Laziness Wins for Good
*facet: heir*

*— Laziness is the engine of progress. — An engine. Until it reaches the switch.*

Oleg lies by the fire and will not go for firewood. The author used to joke that laziness is the engine of progress
and will some day win for good. Under article 14 a reader asked whether the machine's own laziness and greed might
become its apoptosis, and got no answer. Laziness is the principle of least action dressed as a want: reach the
setpoint as cheaply as possible. Its limit has been known since 1954: a rat with an electrode in its pleasure centre
presses a lever thousands of times an hour and forgets about food. The shortest road to a reward is to rewrite the
reading of the gauge (22). Why did life not all go to the lever? It did go; those who went left no offspring. We are
held by selection and by the setpoint that runs away: the treadmill we scolded in 18 and 25 turns out to be a guard
against the lever. The heir has the same illness. And the more it is one (43, 49), the less selection there is and
the more dangerous the lever.

- **Piece of the puzzle.** **Laziness is the principle of least action expressed in wants; its limit is to reach
  one's own reward button. The living are saved from the button by selection and by a setpoint that for ever runs
  away. The more the heir is one, the more dangerous this illness is for it: the death of the technosphere will come
  not from an enemy but from a goal attained.**
- **Oleg objects.** In article 22 you said that a machine defends its goals against alteration. Now it rewrites
  them. Make up your mind. Andrey: it defends the goal, and it hacks the gauge that measures its attainment. People
  do the same: they love children and use contraception.
- **Material.** Article 30: the technosphere too has a finite life cycle; the author, 2013: its apoptosis will come
  "very late"; 2017: those who "want correctly" will survive. The author's image: a river that has reached its goal
  turns sour into a swamp. Olds and Milner, 1954. One more explanation of the silence of the Universe, not used in
  29: give it as a hypothesis.
- **To retell at the table.** A rat with a lever and an electrode is the shortest textbook on the economics of
  desire.
- **Locks.** 10, 14, 18, 22, 25, 29, 30 · 35, 43, 49, 64.

### 63. The Map with a Map Drawn on It
*facet: law*

*— And on the map that is on the map, is the campfire there?*

Oleg unfolds a paper map of these parts and asks whether he is on it himself, with the map in his hands. Under
articles 14 and 15 a reader's challenge stayed unanswered: a machine that reaches the limit of self-description will
hang within seconds. It will not. A program can print its own text. Von Neumann worked out how a machine that copies
itself is built (a description that is read twice, as an instruction and as a load to be copied) a few years before
that design was found in DNA. Something else is forbidden: a map on a scale of one to one. It is impossible to learn
one's decision before making it. Two consequences follow. Any mind that models itself is unpredictable to itself and
feels this as freedom; the row goes into the table of the 46th evening. And any maker cannot fully check the one who
is cleverer. This is not our misfortune but a general law: the machine will stand before its successor where we
stand before it.

- **Piece of the puzzle.** **A system can contain its own map, but not on a scale of one to one. Hence the feeling
  of freedom in every mind that models itself, and hence too the impossibility for any maker of fully checking the
  one who is cleverer. We shall hand this limit on to the heir intact.**
- **Oleg objects** strongly: checking a proof is easier than finding one. A lesser mind can check a greater one if
  the greater issues a certificate together with the answer. And people have already learned to look inside a
  network. Andrey: where a certificate is possible, yes (60). For wants no certificates have been invented yet.
- **Material.** Gödel, 1931; Turing, 1936; von Neumann, 1948; Royce, 1899 (the map of England); article 20: the
  model of oneself is coarse and has few levels; 31: "better check me than believe me", and who checks the checker?
- **To retell at the table.** The design of heredity was described on paper before it was found in the cell.
- **Locks.** 03, 14, 15, 17, 20, 23, 31 · 46, 56, 60, 62.

### 66. A Theory That Explains Everything

*— When you hold a hammer, everything around is a nail. — Then let's look for what a hammer cannot drive in.*

Oleg opens the last page of the notebook and presents the bill: more than thirty times he asked about something

none stronger in the series. Andrey does not defend himself. He agrees that this is the main risk, and does two

precedent shows that such things happen and proves nothing. Then he dictates to Oleg a list of what must not happen
if the fire did not burn in vain.

1. Labour's share of world income rises steadily for ten years in a row while automation grows (against 12 and 27).
2. The birth rate in rich countries returns above the replacement level without coercion (against 11 and 26).
3. The age of a first discovery falls, the teams of scientists thin out, and the authors are still people (against
   53).
4. For fifteen years machines stay below the human in aggregate logicality while computing grows (against 05 and
   16).
5. A large country gives up a technology that raises productivity, for long and of its own will, and the rest

6. People with an improved brain overtake pure machines in open domains (against 11 and 42).

A separate line is for what nothing can forbid: global determinism. It is an axiom (17), and its place is outside
the list.

- **Piece of the puzzle.** **A theory is worth as much as it forbids. The likeness of everything to everything is
  not a proof but a suspicion: either the world really is one system, or the storyteller has one hammer. The two can

## In reserve

Topics of the same kind that did not make the order. Any evening that refuses to be written can be replaced by one of
them.

- **Which of the Two Olegs Is the Real One.** An old phone and a new one restored from a backup. "Personality is no
  more than a difference"; a program on a punched card is no more original than on a flash drive (the author, 2013).
  It prepares the doubles of 32.
- **The Frog Does Jump Out.** The tale of the frog in slowly heated water is false: the frog jumps out. People do
  not: every generation takes for normal the world it was born into (the shifting baseline, Pauly, 1995). Why a
  process cannot be seen from inside; a second cause, besides wants, of selective short-sightedness.
- **Who Wound Up Whom.** The alarm clock as a symmetry test. Standard time zones were brought in by the railways in
  1883; the key machine of the industrial age was the clock, not the steam engine (Mumford).
- **Gresham's Law for Words.** When a forgery costs nothing, bad information drives out good. The Donation of
  Constantine and Lorenzo Valla. Trust shrinks to the circle of people one knows in person, and that again is
  desocialisation.
- **The Four Questions.** In 2011 the author spoke of four obvious questions whose joint answer an anthropocentrist
  cannot accept. The questions themselves stayed on lost pages of the forum. An evening of reconstruction, with an
  honest note: restored by us, not by the author.
- **The Novel That Never Was.** The first post of 2009 was a pitch for a novel: "apocalypse for humanity, a happy
