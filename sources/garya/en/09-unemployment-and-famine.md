# 09. The history of unemployment, the causes of famine in Africa, and the selective short-sightedness of reason — notes

## Summary
Oleg comes back with an objection to the previous evening's crisis theory: if progress constantly destroys jobs, why did unemployment in developed countries stay flat for decades? Andrey answers by splitting history into phases. In antiquity the technosphere grew so slowly that human (and slave, and animal) muscle produced most goods, and human tools were barely different from what animals already do. From the 17th century money became a driver of the technosphere: gold flowing in from the colonies lost value, savers started lending at interest, and borrowers invested in technology to repay — so usury became respectable and progress accelerated. Phase one of hidden unemployment followed: the technosphere displaced slaves (and draft animals, which proves the cause was not morality), a process of three to four centuries ending with the Great Depression, when progress "bounced off" the limits of sales markets. Phase two, after WWII (which stimulated demand by forcing governments to spend), exported unemployment to the "third world": the parable of the "very smart African Einstein" who buys cheaper imported bread and, two moves later, ruins the baker, the miller, the farmer and finally himself. Famine in Africa is thus nobody's conspiracy but universal selective blindness — of Africans, of capitalists, of aid workers, of governments, of anyone who buys bananas and coffee. Credit cards extended markets "in time", but that too ran out, and the 2008 rebound (foreseen by Mikhail Khazin) closed phase two. Phase three is unemployment flooding the whole world, while every government answers with austerity, i.e. puts out the fire with gasoline. The deeper lesson: the "want" called anthropocentrism blocks human reason from seeing its real rival, the technosphere.

## Key theses
1. Human tools and practices (housing, weapons, farming) were not qualitatively new compared with animals; the technosphere's share of produced goods stayed tiny until about the 17th century.
2. From the 17th century, interest-bearing money turned into the engine of technosphere investment; this is what made usury and "real economy" business prestigious.
3. The abolition of slavery was driven mainly by the technosphere making slave labour unprofitable, not by moral progress (draft animal use fell in parallel, though no ethics protects mules).
4. History of unemployment has three phases, each shorter as evolution accelerates: (1) 17th c. to 1929 — displacement of slaves (3–4 centuries); (2) WWII to 2008 — unemployment "accumulated" in the third world (about half a century); (3) after 2008 — mass unemployment spreading over the whole planet.
5. Crises are oscillations of a feedback system with "regulation error": progress outruns market growth, rebounds, drops into a latent phase, and recovers only through demand stimulation (war spending, financial bubbles, credit).
6. Financial bubbles stole nothing: they redirected demand "into the air" and so kept the technosphere growing; without them the world would still look like the 1940s.
7. Third-world countries acted as "accumulators of unemployment" and as humanity's front line absorbing the technosphere's blow; famine there is a consequence of cheap and free imports from technological economies.
8. Nobody planned it: everyone — poor buyers, investors, aid missions, governments — acts on one- or two-move logic out of good intentions.
9. Human reason is selectively blind: trivial two-move logic is unavailable where "wants" (greed, laziness, anthropocentrism) block it (smoking vs the absurd "dirty-socks inhaler").
10. Austerity as the universal anti-crisis cure is exactly the wrong move; only spending slows the crisis.
11. Humanity does not control the process; it is in a field interaction with the technosphere, and anthropocentrism is a want that hides itself.
12. **Summary of the evening: phase one ended slavery, phase two parked unemployment in the third world, phase three is global unemployment; and people constantly act against their own interest while convinced they gain.**
