# 13. Singularity as a process — notes

## Summary
Published after a three-week gap (see the delay explained in article 12's comments), the evening opens as Andrey hands Oleg a sheet with a graph: a process developing with ever-increasing, "blow-up" acceleration tends to a vertical asymptote it cannot cross under the current laws — that is what singularity means mathematically. Evolving systems pass through micro-evolutions and micro-crises whose intervals shrink geometrically, so evolution cannot last forever. Laid over the life-cycle graph of article 10, geometric growth describes the "youth" of a system, and the singularity point roughly coincides with the moment limits begin to act; geometric (not, say, parabolic) because the "rate of profit" is exactly the exponent of a positive-feedback process. After the singularity a system cannot develop by the old laws: it either dies or ages and then dies. The real singularity is not a "bang" but a process stretched in time — the "maturity" phase; for humanity it began around 1989, when population growth turned from accelerating to decelerating, and should end in the 2040s. Andrey reviews the estimates of the acceleration factor (Dyakonov, Snooks, Kapitsa, Panov) and the technological-singularity tradition (von Neumann, Kurzweil, Moore's law), stressing that humanity, technology, Moore's transistors and the biosphere are different systems with different singularities. Following Panov, the singularity at stake concerns the whole biosphere: superintelligent AI nanosystems with alien values may wreck the atmosphere and species out of indifference; a generation or two of survivors may persist, then possibly nothing but hardy simple organisms. Even without AI, people have triggered methane release from permafrost, a runaway warming that could leave a hot, oxygen-free planet where only technogenic life survives. A table of forecasts clusters around 2015–2065; dates already in the past show the singularity of humanity is under way.

## Key theses
1. Mathematically, a singularity is the vertical asymptote of a process with blow-up acceleration; real systems never actually "fly off to infinity".
2. Intervals between micro-crises of an evolving system shrink geometrically; hence every evolution ends in a singularity.
3. Growth is geometric because the "rate of profit" of a dynamic system (positive feedback) is the exponent; measuring a few reference points gives the exponent and the "ceiling".
4. The singularity point approximately coincides with the onset of limits on the system (end of "youth").
5. Beyond the singularity a system cannot develop by the same laws: either death, or ageing followed inevitably by death.
6. The real singularity is a process extended in time — the "maturity" phase — not a one-off event; the only non-smooth point on the curve is death.
7. Humanity's singularity is already happening: it began ~1989 (population growth decelerating) and should end by the 2040s, when growth stops and humanity reaches its ceiling.
8. Different systems (biosphere, humanity, technology, AI, transistors) have different acceleration factors and singularity dates; they need not coincide.
9. The singularity under discussion is that of a larger system — Earth's biosphere — of which humanity is a part (after A. D. Panov).
10. Superintelligent nano-AI with alien values could destroy species and change the atmosphere simply by not caring; a few human generations might survive afterwards, then perhaps none.
11. Independently of AI, human-triggered methane release from permafrost is a positive-feedback climate runaway; a hot, oxygen-free planet would suit technogenic but not biological life — "nature has arranged things so we have nowhere to go".
12. Nearer events (the singularity of humanity) belong to 2030–2050; climate catastrophe lies beyond the 21st century.
13. Forecast dates agree within 10–15 years; their spread reflects the duration of the process, and past dates confirm it is already under way; new symptoms will only be added.
14. **Summary of the evening: singularity is not a momentary event but a process extended in time; we are already inside it; it affects not only humanity but the whole biosphere of the Earth.**

## The author in the comments
**Purpose and endpoint of evolution**
- **Objection/question** (Serega gRAY): what is the final goal of evolution — a super-species filling the universe at light speed? perhaps our universe is that species inside a super-universe? → **Answer**: the meaning of life is the search for the meaning of life. :) One can talk about a final goal only when motion is ending, and it is not. In field-interaction terms the "goal" of motion is simply the points actually reached (an apple's goal is the Earth's surface, not the Moon). He declares himself an adherent of global determinism: no chance in nature, the plan always coincides with the fact — following, e.g., from the Lorentz invariance of the interval.
- **Question** (Idol_111): what ideal dynamic system does evolution logically tend to? → **Answer**: wrongly posed. Dynamic systems are not parts of an abstract evolution; evolution describes part of the life cycle of a concrete existing system (e.g. the Universe). Evolving systems can be seen as negentropic systems, but "dynamic system" is fuller because it includes destruction; very roughly, the goal of a negentropic system is capturing energy and space and structuring chaos. What would the criteria of "ideality" be?
- **Objection** (Idol_111): the criterion is obvious — survivability; in your view technogenic systems survive better than biological ones. → **Answer**: agrees. Later, when Idol_111 feels he is not interested: he is, but a system so survivable that it is immortal is unreachable, because immortality reduces survivability. Evolution is not a one-directional drive to an ideal state; there are many dynamic systems whose dynamics come from energy exchange. Abstract "evolution-in-general" is categorically incompatible with his concept.
- Announces that after the main part he may publish philosophical articles on global determinism — a very coherent concept into which "everything outside time" fits, but hard to discuss because people live inside time and imagine globality as a very large locality, whereas globality includes the factors of all dimensions, of "size" and of "time" itself. Refuses to discuss it spontaneously in comments; suggests the original forum thread as the place.

## Voice and personality
- Corrects the popular image of the singularity with mathematical pedantry: asymptote vs point, smoothness vs break: «Реальная сингулярность не является одномоментной. Она является процессом, растянутым во времени.»
- Builds his case by aggregating many independent estimates and reading their convergence as signal, their spread as the process's duration — the "signal-to-noise" logic he promised in article 12.
- Turns embarrassing data (past dates in a forecast table) into confirmation: it is already happening.
- Calm, even matter-of-fact about extinction scenarios; indifference rather than malice as the danger: for such beings the death of species may matter as little as the death of mosquitoes to us.
- Fatalism grounded in nature: «Так что природой всё расставлено так, что нам просто некуда деться.»
- In comments: playful wisdom («смыслом жизни является поиск смысла жизни. :)»), strict determinism, and protectiveness of readers — avoids "confusing" them with unprepared philosophy.
- Insists on concreteness: always ask singularity/evolution "of which system?".
- Adds his own forecast inside a quoted table (IBM + 10–12 years → 100% unemployment), blending sources with personal estimates.
