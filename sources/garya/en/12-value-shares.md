# 12. The shares of value created by people and by the technosphere — notes

## Summary
The evening is a back-of-the-envelope calculation, promised in the comments to article 09: how much of the value of a loaf of bread is created by a person and how much by the technosphere. On an imagined six-sotka plot, Oleg estimates with Andrey the hours needed to grow, thresh, grind, knead and bake bread with only primitive tools (spade, sickle, flails, millstones, axe, saw, oven). Using Russian average wheat yield (2.15 t/ha) the plot gives about 129 kg of grain, 124 kg of flour, 186 kg of dough and 465 loaves for roughly 170 hours of work — about 22 minutes per loaf. A store loaf costs 12 roubles; corrected for a ~32% agricultural subsidy (PSE) it is about 17.65 roubles, which at the average Russian wage of 21,353.9 roubles and 165.5 working hours a month equals about 8.2 minutes of work. So the "man with a spade" share is about 37% against 63% for the "pure technosphere" — a 2.7-fold shift since primitive tools; counting the spade itself, the purely human share would be 1–5%. Oleg objects that the spade does not dig by itself; Andrey answers that nobody in modern production does anything "by himself", that the spade exists, reproduces and evolves, and that interaction is mutual (as in the "source of initiative" evening). Across the economy, he estimates, the human share is at most 5% and falling; it cannot go below zero, and reaching zero — when human-level AI makes people redundant because machines work for free — will bring a qualitative break in roughly 20–30 years. Optimistic futurologists of the "society of abundance" are dismissed as anthropocentric.

## Key theses
1. The value of a product can be split, roughly, into a human share and a technosphere share; bread is the test case.
2. Bread made by hand with primitive tools costs about 22 minutes of labour per loaf; a store loaf costs the average Russian about 8.2 minutes of wage time — 2.7 times less.
3. The difference comes from the technosphere: combines, tractors, pumps, gas/electric ovens, automated bakeries — even after adding storage, transport and packaging.
4. The 37% / 63% split is the share of the "man with a spade" vs the technosphere; the truly human share (bare hands) is of order 1–5%.
5. Who spends the energy is irrelevant: the spade exists, reproduces and evolves; the tool and the person interact mutually and both reach their own goals — symbiosis.
6. The spade's goal is simply to live, exist and develop; anthropocentrism makes us count only human goals.
7. Across all sectors the human share of labour is at most about 5% and steadily shrinking; it cannot fall below 0%.
8. At 0% the technosphere will no longer need humanity for its goals — a qualitative change in society, economy and the human–technosphere relation.
9. Zero will be reached when AI systems equivalent to human intelligence exist; economic expediency will force their use everywhere — they work for free.
10. The millennia-long trend is a law of nature; if it led to mechanisation and automation, it should lead to human-equivalent AI.
11. "Society of abundance" optimism stems from anthropocentrism: the belief that everything happens under the control of a god-man.
12. Rough forecast: the human share reaches zero in about 20–30 years.
13. **Summary of the evening: the share of value created by people keeps falling toward zero, the technosphere's share rises toward 100%, and the disproportion is already so large that the boundary state can be expected in the near future.**

## The author in the comments
**Is technogenic labour real?**
- **Objection** (Shark): robots are made by hands, all money goes to hands; the "technogenic Cthulhu" takes no money; the human share is 100%. → **Answer**: true, it takes no money. But robots are made with industrial robots — has anyone seen chips made by hand?
- **Objection** (vizit73): every chain of artefacts starts with HANDS. → **Answer**: no, it starts with the Big Bang. :)
- **Objection** (UK0IAI): the technosphere's demand for human labour grows faster than people can supply it; that is why it "has to" synthesise robots. (No direct reply; later UK0IAI insists contractors, educators and the "Ancients" must be counted.) → **Answer**: why count ancestors and contractors only among humans and not primates, vertebrates, prokaryotes, pre-biological evolution? Shares are not exactly definable; this is about nature-in-general. The choice of how many levels of "creators of creators" to count is subjective, and anthropocentrism picks whatever level contains humans. He takes only the last level, where production actually happens — otherwise a dead man in his grave would still be "producing".

**Energy and the meaning of the 2.7 factor**
- **Objection** (Mihail R., a mathematician): the extra energy of diesel fuel is unaccounted, and its link to the technosphere is not obvious. → **Answer**: first consider the spade: digging with bare palms vs a spade differs in energy and time. "Man with spade" is a MACROsystem whose synergetic super-profit is split: part goes to restoring, reproducing and developing spades, part to the person as time saved, energy saved and economic effect.
- **Objection** (Brassiere): 2.7 is far from any singularity; housing is harder to get than in Nekrasov's time; energy efficiency fell; the real singularity is the decline of consumer society, the technosphere being just a big, dulling spade. → **Answer**: compare palms vs spade, then multiply by 2.7 — then you'll see whether it's far. He uses many sources, not one; more sources means better signal-to-noise; promises a table of all sources and their dates in a later article (delivered in article 13).
- **Objection** (sobolev, writing as "Oleg"): then compute the loaf cost with the technosphere alone. → **Answer**: thanks — he had long expected such a question. Concedes that "value share" is ambiguous: a synergetic system is not an arithmetic sum, so the share is defined only where work is purely human, purely technogenic, or clearly divided; in synergy it is undefinable. Still, it can be estimated "on the fingertips" when one side approaches non-participation — as now. If the technosphere did not need humans it would not dig fields for bread at all; the activity itself would lose meaning. The numbers are largely abstract; what matters is that humans take ever less part in producing goods for themselves.
- **Objection** (XDiaBLo, 2015): "anthropogenic labour is several times *less*" — surely a typo, should be "more"? → **Answer** (2015): no typo; there is an order of magnitude less purely human labour in bread than automated/mechanised.

**Marxism and value**
- **Objection** (битый): fighting anthropocentrism leads you to deify the technosphere; you are a revisionist Marxist who believes value hides in goods; value appears only in exchange. → **Answer**: he does not deify it — the technosphere is not the highest form forever, its turn to yield to something better will come. Marxists would hardly agree he is one. He said in article 06 that price is set by supply and demand for goods and labour; added value is product price minus material price, also market-defined. Agrees with the Soviet-lamp anecdote. Technosphere as parents' inheritance — "yes, and so what?" Man is himself the inheritance of chordates; why do we cut off the evolution before and after humanity? The technosphere is part of nature, created by NATURE according to the LAWS OF NATURE.
