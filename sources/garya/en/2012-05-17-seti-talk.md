# 2012-05-17. The blog author's talk at the SETI Centre of SAI MSU — notes

## Summary
This is a short announcement, not a "Dialogue by the campfire" evening. On 11 May 2012 the author gave a talk at the SETI scientific coordination centre of the Sternberg Astronomical Institute (SAI MSU). The title was roughly "A counter-anthropocentric interpretation of the singularity of planetary evolution". The abstract sums up the whole blog up to that point. It traces how the technosphere and humanity have interacted through history and in the singularity phase of the biosphere. It treats crises in different areas of human activity as linked to each other. It claims to explain famine in Africa, the global economic crisis and the demographic transition. It ties together three singularities: technological, human and biospheric. It criticises transhumanism. It ends with a forecast of a post-singular era of intelligent life on a non-biological substrate. The post links a PDF of the slides and an audio recording: A. D. Panov's introduction, the talk, and a discussion with Panov, L. M. Gindilis, A. P. Nazaretyan, M. I. Weller and others. Weller gave the author a signed copy of his book "Energoevolutionism". A representative of the "Russia 2045" movement (makers of the robot "Dima") offered cooperation, and the author says he is open to it. A second seminar was planned for 8 June 2012, with several speakers, to examine Roger Penrose's position. Almost all the substance is in the comment thread, which ran from 2012 to 2015.

## Key theses
These come from the abstract and from the author's own glosses on slides in the comments.
1. Mathematically, the singularity is an artefact of fitting a curve to evolution before it enters a crisis. In reality nothing goes to infinity. Limits make the curve bend from a vertical asymptote to a horizontal one (slide 36).
2. The "singularity process" is the same thing as the "crisis phase". The author reports that everyone at the talk accepted this.
3. We are already inside a tangle of crises that run at the same time: economic, demographic and technological (slide 38). They share natural causes.
4. The demographic transition is a crisis in the life-cycle sense. The exhausted resource is the physiological limit of human learning, since education now costs about half of a lifetime.
5. Humanity and the technosphere are in symbiosis now, like a mother carrying a fetus. As the technosphere comes to reproduce itself, humanity turns into a brake on evolution.
6. All dynamic systems obey the same life-cycle laws: business, organisms, technical devices, dissipative structures. These are "first-order" laws. Social laws are special cases of them.
7. Everything that exists is intelligent to some degree. The split into living and non-living, subject and object, natural and artificial is anthropocentric.
8. Transhumanism is optimistic but idealistic. Upgrading humans is less efficient than building a new carrier of intelligence.
9. **The post-singular phase will be an era of a new, non-biological (nanotechnological, technospheric) form of intelligent life, which will take over humanity's place at the leading edge of evolution.**

## The author in the comments
The thread has five parts: a long, increasingly hostile debate with "...починяю примус" (late June to July 2012); an economics debate with Combinator (July 2012); a long, polite debate about the meaning of life and the limits of mind with "Сергей Михайлович-2", who addresses the author by name and patronymic as "Andrey Aleksandrovich" (Sept–Nov 2012); short exchanges with Brassiere; and in 2015 a point-by-point commentary on Panov's AI-2014 paper, prompted by Dorfe. The material is grouped by topic below.

### Singularity as process; crisis
- **Objection** (Idol_111): the whole theory rests on one leg, intelligence; nature does not hang on that "ramrod", and predictions never come true. → **Answer**: not every singularity theorist ties it to intelligence (Panov does not and still finds it inevitable). The author does, but in his sense: whatever exists is intelligent, because the ability to exist in unstable equilibrium is the ability to survive, which is mind.
- **Objection** (...починяю примус): the singularity is a made-up problem, and the economy will collapse without any singularity. → **Answer**: slide 36 shows that the singularity is a mathematical approximation and the reality is an extended crisis. Everyone at the talk agreed. The global economic crisis is already here and confirms his view.
- **Objection**: the construction hangs in the air; you only like the word. → **Answer**: he lays out the formula on slide 8 and asks which part the opponent rejects: that the denominator reaches zero, or that the fraction then goes to infinity, or mathematics itself. He suspects the objection is to the letters of the word "singularity", since the opponent accepts "crisis".
- **Objection** (Sergey M.): the Panov–Snooks curve shows only that our current criteria of development are exhausted. → **Answer**: he quotes Panov's conclusion: this is the end of an attractor that began with the origin of life, so it is a crisis of biological life as such, not of human culture. Techno-humanitarian balance belongs only to the tail of the curve (from the Quaternary onward). Adding points cannot change the shape of the curve. He asks three pointed questions: will the curve reach the axis, what does it describe, and where does it go afterwards?
- On what is special about his view: he calls his reading "non-classical". Classical singularitarians say everything beyond the point is unpredictable. He says new trends already show faintly before the point, and the transition takes visible time, so it can be watched.

### Universal life-cycle laws and method
- **Objection**: correlation is not causation; you transfer one curve onto a civilisation made of many independent systems. → **Answer**: these are not statistical correlations. The same feedback loops produce the same mathematics regardless of material: a toilet cistern, an op-amp, a rectangular-pulse generator, a growing business, an organism, a dissipative structure. Reinvesting profit is positive feedback. A resource limit turns the exponential into a damped oscillation. "Gas is also a society of molecules", yet Boyle–Mariotte predicts it. Social laws cannot contradict non-equilibrium thermodynamics, because people discover laws and do not create them.
- **Objection**: is humanity even one system? → **Answer**: any system that lowers its entropy in some phase obeys the laws that link entropy, mass and energy. The demographic curve shows the mass of all of humanity.
- His backstory (told to explain himself, with self-irony: "I'm surprised at myself"). He noticed the same life-cycle curve in business systems, living things and the reliability theory he studied at university, then in Altshuller and in dissipative structures. He realised that the demographic transition is a crisis and went looking for the cause, not for excuses. His own estimates put the destruction of the world economy at 2030–2035, with AI and nanotech devaluing not just human labour but the human as such, followed by population decline from about 2045 (a figure he says he borrowed). He says he "invented the singularity from scratch" before he knew Kurzweil, Vinge, Bill Joy or Panov–Snooks. For a long time he did not believe his own results. Only the match with world-class scientists' dates convinced him, and then he was horrified: "Иногда лучше быть в неведении."
- Several times he challenges the opponent to name an alternative exhausted resource: "Malthusianism doesn't work here, so what does?"

### Demography, education and the limit of human intellect
- **Claim**: the main brake on fertility is the cost of education, in money, time and ultimately energy. Raising ten highly educated children is unaffordable. Competitive higher education takes more than 20 years per child. Training time now reaches the edge of productive life. Nobody can learn the Dirac equation at age 5 (or 3). If the energy gained from education does not cover the energy spent on it, education is energetically inefficient. This is a natural law, not a social one.
- **Argument**: human knowledge does not add up arithmetically. A million Mendeleevs give maybe two or three Mendeleevs' worth. Even perfect addition gives a finite product of population and one person's capacity. He lists 6,000 languages, every instrument and every profession: why can no one master them all?
- **Objection** (...починяю примус, Sergey M.): information is stored outside individuals; nobody needs to know everything; ants divide labour; people's hard drives show underused brains. → **Answer**: storage is not digestion. A thousand libraries per person are useless if no one can absorb them, like terabytes of flash drives dumped on Mars. Flies and dogs have physiological ceilings, so Homo sapiens has one too, higher but real. The "underused brain" view assumes we know what the brain ought to be doing.
- **Claim**: modern office stress is a sign that the "race of intellectuality" has pushed the organism beyond what it can give.
- **Objection** (Sergey M.): the brain stopped growing tens of thousands of years ago because farming was less stressful than hunting. → **Answer**: then pythons, scorpions and tigers should have outstripped us. Harnessing a horse takes more foresight than killing and eating it. What matters is that further cephalisation is energetically impossible. He backs this with Laughlin's quote and points out the original source behind the journalist's article.
- **Objection** (Sergey M.): seven alternative causes of the demographic transition (lower child mortality, contraception, cost of children, new pleasures, stress, decline of the family, a cult of personal needs). → **Answer**: he takes them apart one by one with the "petrol" quip (the car burns more because it burns less). Lower mortality should raise numbers, and ancient peoples took drugs, lived under stress and still multiplied fast. He sends the opponent to Kapitsa and to Wikipedia's notes on education and "childfree".
- **Objection** (Panov, via Combinator's debate): human iterativity of concepts is unlimited, and science shows no end. → **Answer**: iterativity is large but finite. Concepts must also be revised and destroyed, not only stacked (the "caloric" example). They fight for existence in a virtual space of images and must die. When a platform hits its limits, nature finds a new platform, and he found no candidate other than the technosphere. He also notes that Panov himself speaks of a crisis of science.

### Technosphere, symbiosis and the fate of humanity
- **Objection**: technosphere and human are not separable, so evolutionary terms do not apply. → **Answer**: he uses analogies: the cuckoo and the sparrows, parasitoid wasps, predators and prey. Species boundaries are themselves conventional (lions and tigers interbreed). If someone has to draw the line, "consider that I did; am I worse than Lamarck? :)" The key image is a pregnant female seen by someone who has never watched a birth. He admits he is forecasting an event that is happening for the first time, so no repeated precedent can be shown.
- **Claim**: total entropy of technosphere plus humanity is falling, but more and more of that is due to the technosphere. The share of the technosphere reproducing itself is growing, driven by humans' own wish for productivity. If the trend holds, the technosphere becomes self-sufficient and humans become a brake. "What brakes evolution is removed by evolution."
- **Objection**: humans are valuable to themselves; who is the evaluator who would "devalue" humanity? → **Answer**: who evaluated gymnosperms into angiosperms, or wiped out the dinosaurs? "Это антропоцентризм-с... :)" Dinosaurs were valuable to themselves too. The image he uses is a cow being fattened for slaughter, which cares only that the grass tastes good.
- **Claim**: techno-life will quickly destroy biological life, not out of malice but because it will reshape the environment for itself even harder than humans did. It needs no oxygen, water, plants or animals, so they will be treated as junk.
- **Claim**: the successor is "intelligent cosmic dust", free of food, water, air, temperature and radiation limits, and dominant in the Solar System by the 23rd century. If a human tried to turn into such dust, would he still be human? Our "descendants" in the genetic sense have no future, but the new nanotech life might be called our heir, much as we are the "super-children" of single-celled organisms that we now kill with antibiotics.
- **Consolation** (to Sergey M.): just as people see themselves continued in their children, humanity can see itself continued in its offspring, the intelligent technosphere.
- **On Nazaretyan**: he says Nazaretyan, who was at the talk, agreed that humanity as we have known it has no future beyond the singularity, and that the mechanisms for sublimating aggression stop working there.

### Economics, unemployment and social tension
- **Objection** (Latr): after the singularity, who will sew clothes and bake bread, and who gets the seaside? → **Answer**: there will be no point forcing anyone, because the technosphere does everything better and asks for nothing. The real question will be how a huge mass of unneeded unemployed people survives.
- **Objection**: technology is a by-product; the economy suffers from people, not technology. → **Answer**: the economy is the set of production relations, and technology and the economy are "yin and yang". He asks for an example of an economy without technological production, or the reverse. He agrees that laziness drives progress, "and someday it will win completely :)".
- **Claim**: displaced workers used to retrain upwards. Now even high-skill work is being taken, so there is nowhere left to run.
- **Claim** (citing Bill Joy): the glue of society is mutual need. When everyone is self-sufficient through AI, society dissolves into a crowd of hermits. Idle humanity under machine abundance would degrade into drink, drugs and entertainment ("and how do you picture… communism? :)").
- **Objection** (Combinator): unemployment will grow, but average living standards will keep rising through taxes and benefits; office "plankton" previews the future. → **Answer**: the average will fall. The rich consume a shrinking share of their gains, the unemployed get less than their wages were, and inequality will raise social tension. He admits this is more a feeling than a proof. Useless office staff get cut, except in state structures and monopolies. His thought experiment: 999 jobless out of 1,000; what tax rate lets one person feed them all?
- **Key economic argument**: only *solvent* demand yields profit. If 99% pay with money the state took from the producer, the producer earns on only 1% and will simply scale down. The 1,000-rouble illustration: the state takes 990 and pays it back, and you gained nothing. He uses this to explain why the Sahara is not irrigated: nobody needs non-solvent demand.
- **On money printing**: it is a hidden tax, it works only while trust lasts, and through positive feedback it ends in hyperinflation and the end of all economic regulation. He still finds Combinator's scenario plausible (the ECB, Obama's fight over the debt ceiling) as a way the world economy could be destroyed.
- **On tax competition**: capital flees high taxes. In his example, a country that halves its rate from 90% to 45% attracts capital from ten others and collects five times more, which raises tension in the other nine. He names the USA as the likely player (S&P downgrade, falling taxes). He concedes that redistribution could work only in a closed economy that capital cannot leave.
- **On protests**: he agrees they are psychological. Inequality feels unjust even when nobody gets poorer ("cave people did not stage two revolutions a year").
- **Bottom line**: the elites see no way out, and neither does he. Economic troubles are just one face of the evolutionary crisis.

### Mind, information, subject and object (the anthropocentrism dispute)
- **Objection** (...починяю примус): humans uniquely separate information from its subject and object. → **Answer**: so do atoms, which "learn" of each other at a distance (a rubbed comb lifting a paper scrap), bees passing on a direction, and genes carrying eye colour in cells that have no eyes. The carrier does not matter, only the function: the Venus flytrap moves without muscles and digests without a stomach.
- **Objection**: living matter is matter that resists entropy by reproducing itself. → **Answer**: dissipative structures (lasers, stars, galaxies) also lower entropy locally. By that definition a computer virus is alive. Ageing organisms raise entropy. The living/non-living split is anthropocentric (as article 2 argues).
- **Objection**: objects behave predictably, subjects vary. → **Answer**: Heisenberg makes electrons only probabilistic, and subjects predictably pursue what they want (hit your finger with a hammer). The difference is only in degrees of freedom. "Change the gravitational constant for one minute, if you are a subject."
- **Claim**: humans obey field interactions too; on top of force fields act the "fields" of wants and emotions, higher-order drivers. The difference is like that between electric and magnetic causes.
- **Claim**: the real sticking point is initiative. Whether initiative lives inside a thing or outside it is a matter of worldview, and no fact can settle it, so the dispute becomes "whose religion is right". That is why he ended the debate.
- **Claim**: "natural" and "artificial" do not differ. A house is no more artificial than an anthill, a beaver dam or a stickleback's nest. Nature did not let humans fly; it "forced" them to fly by planting the want. His thought experiment is an observer who first takes rest as natural, then constant velocity, then constant acceleration, and so on, always calling the highest-order change he controls "artificial" and "purposeful".
- **Claim**: evolution has a direction (a vector), but whether it has a goal is unknown. A goal would mean an end point where motion stops, while evolution is endless. Asking whether the apple "aims" at the Earth is an ill-posed, anthropocentric question.
- **On recognising other minds**: he asks for signs of intelligence that would let a non-human observer classify Lem's Solaris as intelligent and the Earth as not. If intelligence is defined as morality, the definition becomes circular: "only humans count". On this measure ants out-moral people. Spiders' webs and fishing nets differ only in degree, and a web is built in "abstract" anticipation of a fly. The tumbleweed and the dung beetle use the principle of the wheel, and the E. coli flagellum is a rotary motor in molecular bearings. Butterflies "talked" over kilometres long before Popov's radio. Monkeys that learned our language get called primitive by people who never learned theirs.
- **SETI angle**: more advanced minds very likely exist already (he cites Panov's book). We may be looking straight at them and see "just a supernova flash", because their dynamics would look explosive to us, the way life does compared with a boulder. To them we may matter as little as a volvox colony that thinks itself the centre of the universe.
- **Claim**: "wants" control the mind, not the other way round. The mind is the tool of initiative, not its source. Nature built a handier tool and will replace it when it wears out: "Если "первая ступень ракеты" выработала своё топливо, она должна быть сброшена, потому что превратилась из двигателя эволюции в ее тормоз."
- **On the genome**: if you control your genome, become someone of another race by changing your genes, not with make-up. Human selection differs from natural selection only in that a human took part. Cosmic radiation also changes genotypes. "Recognising" special human purposefulness is like a Brezhnev pinning medals on himself.

### AI (the 2012 claims and the 2015 commentary on Panov)
- **Claim**: AI already exists. The Starfish robot built models of its own body and then of its movement: only two levels of images, so what will happen at 22 or 222 levels? Its potential is simply still below human.
- **Dates**: Modha's chart is two-thirds actual data, and the trend points to human-brain parameters by 2018–2019 ("2019 hasn't come yet :)"). He quotes Modha's November 2012 figures on the Sequoia simulation. Brassiere jokes that the project must be reading his blog, and he jokes back.
- **Objection** (Sergey M.): you cannot build what you have not understood; a real milestone would be machine translation of philosophical texts as good as a professional's. → **Answer**: who studied mind before human mind was created? Why can't the same "nobody" do it again? He asks the sceptic to tell NASA, the Pentagon, IBM and MIT that their SyNAPSE money is wasted. On Moore's law he shows the 2011 chart, and the opponent concedes the point.
- **2015 model of any intelligence**: sensorics, motorics, multilevel pattern recognition, a "correlator" that synthesises new images, management of the images' life cycle (a genetic algorithm, images competing to prove useful), and a preference system of wants and emotions. "Nothing more is needed." The only limit is hardware.
- **Personal story**: in 1989, on a Soviet DVK-4 computer, he built a self-learning system for diagnosing faults in electronic equipment. It applied test signals (motorics), gathered responses (sensorics), chose optimal test pairs statistically (a correlator using Lyapunov-style sensitivity functions) and synthesised fault images itself. It ended up finding faults far faster than he could, and he was a good radio engineer. That was when he first felt a chill down his spine, and when he understood that his own "operating principle" was the same, only with more resources.
- **Against Turing-machine and Penrose/Gödel arguments**: an ideal Turing machine has no contact with reality. Intelligence grows through the loop of poking reality "with a blind man's stick", getting a response and finding correlations, so information comes in from outside. A blind, deaf super-Einstein would never rise above a newborn. Mind is the recursive mapping of parts of reality into one another, and even two charges "sense, recognise and respond". More levels of mapping mean more intelligence (detailed rebuttal in article 15).
- **On microtubules and the 10^26 ops/s estimate**: he calls it perhaps the most serious counter-argument and says he would be glad if it were right, because then his fears would be unfounded. He still doubts it. A neuron has one axon and so gives one output "bit-image". Internal speed is not external speed: air molecules move at hundreds of m/s while wind blows at 80 at most. Beyond a certain point the quality of recognition stops mattering, as with screen resolution past what the eye can see.
- **Other 2015 replies**: Maxima on its own is a car parked in a garage. He points to speech recognition, self-driving cars, "Echelon" and Google's work on meaning. On "superabilities would isolate a robot", he says superabilities make adapting easier, even playing dumb, and recommends the film "Limitless". On storage substrates, it does not matter whether a flash drive or DNA holds the image. On subcellular consciousness, he "liked it very much" and thinks it surely exists, but notes a KT315 transistor works as designed whatever "family dramas" its electrons have.
- **Transhumanism** (to Dorfe): he likes its optimism and dislikes its idealism. Upgrading people is like upgrading a steam locomotive into a fighter jet, when it is far easier to just build the jet. Current flows along the path of least resistance. A few rich people may upgrade themselves, but not as far as cosmic nano-dust networks.

### Meaning of life, morality and why he writes
- **Objection** (Sergey M.): the meaning of life is nature knowing itself through humans in order to perfect itself and resist entropy. → **Answer**: nature already "has" all the information. Only an interpreter of reality, such as a human, can be wrong or curious. Humans do not resist entropy; they are products of the Sun's entropic flow. The answer to the moralised view is that nature is not grateful: do you spare the cow out of gratitude for steak?
- **His own meaning-of-life formula**: move purposefully in the direction set by the laws of nature. Earlier he had said the meaning of life is the search for the meaning of life. He also cites a TRIZ-specialist acquaintance who says everything that exists struggles to go on existing, and that this is the meaning.
- **On morality**: it has helped so far (he agrees with Nazaretyan), but it is not a guarantee, and it stops working at the singularity. A moral person cannot survive in an immoral environment. He knows this from experience, has had to be ruthless, and gives trusted readers access to a closed forum thread with the details. You can be decent and highly moral without believing in your own divinity.
- **On why he offers no remedy**: anyone who truly accepts that nature rules humans sees that "we must do something" is self-contradictory, since only someone who feels like a god thinks he can change the objective order. "Я избавился от антропоцентризма настолько, что вижу это противоречие. Поэтому я ничего не предлагаю, а только констатирую." A marketing teacher taught him that threats sell best. Whoever scares people with an apocalypse and then sells salvation is probably a con man, and he sells nothing.
- **On the blog's purpose**: it is for those who want to understand global processes. They cannot be stopped, but one can protect oneself and one's family for a while. When furious crowds stripped of their benefits by the technosphere start tearing each other apart over "who is to blame", the thing to do is to refuse to return aggression, withdraw to the backwoods, and prepare in advance for a hermit's life without gas or electricity, for as long as the water and air last. At the start of the thread he says people face harder times than ever before, and he wants to help those willing to see clearly.
- **On optimism**: a normal person who learns he is mortal stays cheerful, and the same holds for learning that humanity is mortal. "Вполне можно оставаться маленьким природным винтиком, и при этом счастливым винтиком, без претензий на господство над Вселенной. :)" He tells Sergey M. that he dislikes his own conclusions (as stated in the blog's introduction) but that his feelings do not change physics. He uses the analogy of cells that discover the whole organism is mortal, and the sarcastic picture of inventing a religion of immortality so as to "aim past the cemetery". The blog is not for people seeking comfort, since knowledge multiplies sorrow.
- **Majority and minority**: most people do look for a meaning and find an anthropocentric one (wealth, children, fatherland, self-improvement, helping others). A small minority understands that the search is pointless, explains this scientifically, and goes on loving children and helping the suffering because it brings satisfaction, which is nature rewarding them with good "wants". He fully shares the view that a good book beats a gourmet dinner, but says that is not what the blog is about.
- **On science and himself**: he dropped out of graduate school and regrets it. He wants scientists' attention and so must keep to scientific method: "in that sense I do claim it". He calls himself less cautious in his statements than Panov.

## Voice and personality
- He argues by universal analogy across domains (electronics, business, biology, thermodynamics). He holds that one piece of mathematics governs them all, and treats this as a "first-order law".
- He uses chains of either/or counter-questions that corner the opponent ("Either you haven't heard of the acceleration of evolution, or you reject it, or you reject evolution as such"). He likes to demand that the opponent name his own alternative, especially "name the exhausted resource".
- He turns the burden of proof around: whoever claims humans are exceptional must prove it, and if they cannot, doubting it is enough.
- He labels opposing intuitions "anthropocentrism", "deification of man" or "religion of self-deception", often with a dry ":)".
- Homely, vivid images do the work: the cow on the meadow, the pregnant female, the rocket's first stage, the steering wheel that thinks it drives, cells in a body, a molecule in a punctured balloon, the observer of derivatives, the petrol paradox, the happy little screw, the locomotive-into-fighter-jet upgrade, electrons' family dramas inside a KT315 transistor.
- He is an engineer at heart: feedback loops, asymptotes, derivatives, epsilon-neighbourhoods, LC circuits, Lyapunov sensitivity functions, Soviet hardware (DVK-4, KT315). He respects only what can be expressed "mathematically, without emotional evaluation".
- His stance is calm fatalism. He is personally saddened ("sometimes it's better not to know") but refuses both despair and prescriptions, and offers only survival advice for individuals and families.
- He leans on personal history and authority: reliability theory, business-crisis analysis, a marketing course, the 1989 diagnostic system, the abandoned graduate school, the closed forum thread about being ruthless.
- He claims to be independent ("I reinvented the singularity from scratch") and takes the convergence with famous scientists as confirmation.
- He is honest in small things. He concedes local points, admits when an argument is "more feeling than justification", and apologises for delays and for an emotional edit.
- He is courteous to sincere opponents and sharp with trolls. He is willing to end a discussion and to moderate firmly.
- He writes with CAPS and _underscores_ for emphasis, frequent ":)", "ПМСМ", "хотелки", ironic "гхм", "Вникните в эту мысль", and colloquialisms ("трындец", "загреметь в ящик").
- Science is both his authority and his target: he trusts thermodynamics, Panov–Snooks and Laughlin, but argues that scientists' conclusions are distorted by anthropocentrism without their noticing.
