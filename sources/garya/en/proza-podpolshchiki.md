### Summary

The adult narrator, Andrey, looks back and asks how a Soviet schoolboy in late-1970s Leninakan came to found an
underground "party". The first three chapters set out three "preconditions": childhood games, faith in communist
justice, and a passion for engineering. Chapters 4–10 tell how the organisation was founded, how recruiting went, and
how its technical projects grew. The tone is affectionate and self-ironic: the grown man knows how naive and dangerous
it all was, and also how serious it felt at the time.

**Part 1. Preconditions (ch. 1–3)**

- **Ch. 1, Childhood games.** A Russian boy, born in 1963 to engineer parents posted to Armenia, finds a few
  Russian-speaking friends in the courtyard of mixed families. They break through a hole in the cellar wall, set up a
  "secret HQ", and he wires its lighting and a cardboard-monster "security system" on a tripwire. A neighbour girl
  gives the secret away and the hole is walled up. In a second episode he and a friend jump off a gravel heap, raising
  the height step by step until they are leaping from third-storey height. The narrator says this stunt quietly shaped
  his whole later life.
- **Ch. 2, Communist justice.** He believes in Lenin almost like a saint. The town around him runs on *блат*: there is
  no meat in the shops while the meat-plant workers sell it at the market, the "golden youth" despise engineers'
  children, and building materials can only be stolen. At 16 he works as a turner and is drawn into a 30% piecework
  kickback scheme that goes up to the top of the plant. Asking himself where Lenin went wrong, he concludes that a
  just society cannot be built from imperfect people and that vice does not follow class lines. He then dreams of a
  technical fix: a device that reads thoughts so honest rulers can be chosen, and later "amplifies" good thoughts into
  other people's heads.
- **Ch. 3, Hobby.** His father is a radio engineer who can fix anything. The boy takes apart the wedding alarm clock,
  builds kites, ship models and wire-guided boats (using a spent anti-tank missile's guidance wire), and wins a "naval
  battle" in a restaurant's carp pool. For the rematch he builds a radio-controlled cruiser with a three-shot "rocket
  gun" made from soda-siphon cartridges. One shot misses Rafik's head by centimetres. A catalogue of home-made zip
  guns, stilettos ground from old files, knuckledusters and a "rocket machine gun" follows. They all end up in a
  hiding place he admires when nobody is home.

**Part 2. Founding the party (ch. 4–6)**

- **Ch. 4, We make a decision.** In summer 1978 he shows the arsenal to his dreamer friend Armen, and they decide to
  "put it to use". The narrator remarks that the goals were chosen to fit the means. Fantasies of avenging
  "red devils" mix with detailed engineering talk: a directional microphone, a window-glass eavesdropper, phone bugs,
  and a printing press hidden in a "diplomat" case. Membership fees turn the group into a party, the
  **НКП (Нео-коммунистическая партия)**. He becomes "chief engineer", Armen the "commissar", and the narrator's
  day is school, boxing and a 20:00–24:00 factory shift.
- **Ch. 5, First pancakes come out lumpy.** Three recruiting attempts fail. Zhora's idea of justice is "I don't touch
  anyone and nobody touches me". Artur turns out to be a lonely misanthrope who cares only for family and his one
  friend. Timur says every person has a bit of Hitler in them and that improving people sounds like the Nazi
  euthanasia programme. Armen proposes a younger boy, Kolya, a straight-A student and KVN captain.
- **Ch. 6, Now we're three.** Kolya joins eagerly and brings code names (the narrator is **«Каган»**), cipher grids
  and four "levels of initiation" with a cell structure. The three dream of their profiles on posters next to
  Marx–Engels–Lenin. A home attempt at nitroglycerine burns the narrator's tongue with nitric acid and ends in a small
  blast that coats Kolya in soot. Kolya's mother then asks the narrator, as the "sensible older friend", to keep Kolya
  careful, and he is too ashamed to confess.

**Part 3. Expansion (ch. 7–9)**

- **Ch. 7, To Yerevan!** He is a class clown with a "sunflower" face and the knack of repeating the teacher word for
  word while chatting. At the city maths olympiad he refuses to copy and solves only 8 of 12 problems alone, yet he is
  the only one in the school to go through to the republic round. In Yerevan he recruits Vasily, a guitar-playing
  rebel fan of Vysotsky and Mashina Vremeni (code name «Варяг»). Back home he takes up music again and buys a
  seven-string guitar by mistake.
- **Ch. 8, Yerevan again.** He studies the brain and telepathy (Leonid Vasiliev's books, sent by an uncle in Grozny)
  and settles on a microwave "thought amplifier" built around a travelling-wave tube (ЛБВ), which no Leninakan plant
  has. Vasily's father works at a physics institute, so the narrator qualifies for the olympiad again (maths and
  physics) to get to Yerevan. There he and another boy get lost on purpose, are chased by guard dogs, run up a
  three-metre wall and wade a river. He wins a football insult duel in Leninakan street Armenian and plays on with a
  fever and blood in his urine. Vasily refuses to bring up physics with his father. The chapter ends with his kidney
  disease diagnosed, self-taught sambo, and knife and slipper-throwing practice (footprints on the ceiling). A school
  trip to the TV tower gives him the idea of a "creeping planetary communist revolution": thought amplifiers on relay
  towers, with today's politicians left in office but working "in the right direction".
- **Ch. 9, To Moscow and back.** His mother takes him to Moscow, with suitcases of cognac and home-made basturma as
  "currency", and gets him into a nephrology ward under a fake Moscow registration. He recruits a ward-mate, Vladislav
  from Vladimir, and sets him to build an untraceable radio that hops frequency about once a second. The diagnosis is
  glomerulonephritis. Going home over New Year they are stuck for three days in an overcrowded Domodedovo, diverted
  to Yerevan, and caught up in a passenger sit-in on the plane led by a communist "highlander". The sit-in is outwitted
  by the airport, and a colleague of his mother's, met by chance, drives them home. His mother calls it "a sixth sense".

**Ch. 10, The object and project Uran.** On New Year's night 1980 a stranger tries to break down the door. The narrator
gets a zip gun to protect his little sister, fires it by accident, plugs the bullet hole with bread, and swears his
sister to secrecy. The leaders shadow a gifted rocket-builder, Gevorg ("the object"), who is lost to love. With Zhora
and Timur he saves school-lunch money for an 8 mm cine camera. After reading about infrasound he starts the weapon
project «Ураган»/«Уран». His notebook of concepts is overflowing, and he urgently needs technical recruits.

### The narrator and the author

As presented in the text (the narrator is called Andrey, which matches the author's name):

- **Origins.** Born in **1963 in Leninakan (now Gyumri), Armenian SSR**, to Russian parents, both engineers who
  graduated from Tomsk Polytechnic in 1962. They stayed because they were offered a flat. He has a sister four years
  younger, a grandfather who played the seven-string guitar, an uncle in Grozny with a huge library, and distant
  relatives in Moscow.
- **Bilingual outsider.** He learned Armenian in kindergarten and the courtyard and often translated for his parents,
  but was more at home in Russian. His friends are mostly from mixed Russian-Armenian families or are Armenians raised
  in Russia. He feels like a "Russian island" among Armenians, yet a classmate jokes that he is "Armenian by birth". He
  is fluent in the elaborate Leninakan street swearing.
- **Family.** His father is a radio engineer, party secretary at his workplace and a skilled handyman who forgives
  easily ("вредитель сельского хозяйства" is his strongest rebuke). His mother is a design engineer at the
  Leninakan experimental plant of analytical instruments (ЛОЗАП). She is a fervent believer, anxious and inventive,
  works the *блат* network to get ether, meat and a Moscow hospital place, and trusts her "inner voice".
- **School and town.** He went to the most prestigious Russian school in a town he calls the "Odessa of Armenia",
  where thieves' codes counted for more than law. He was a Komsomol activist, a back-row joker, a "хорошист", and
  later a member of the KVN club at the local polytechnic. He spent five years at music school (piano) before taking
  up the guitar.
- **Work from 16.** Fitter, tool-room clerk, then turner (grade 3) and miller (grade 2) on the evening shift while
  still at school, then radio assembler. This matches the factory work in the existing profile. In the late 1980s
  and early 1990s he **automated industrial plants** and found the same piecework kickback scheme at three other
  plants (one in Estonia, two in Russia).
- **Hobbies and reading.** Ship and aircraft modelling, radio, chemistry, a shooting club (7th grade), boxing, and
  home-taught sambo. He read «Юный техник», «Моделист-конструктор», «Техника молодёжи», «Наука и жизнь»,
  «Радио», and prepared for university with the Skanavi problem book.
- **Formative experiences.** Childhood stunts with lasting harm: the gravel jumps led to kidney disease, diagnosed
  in Moscow as glomerulonephritis, and he says it decided "which people and which cities" he would later deal with.
  He nearly killed a friend with his own invention. His first taste of systemic corruption came as a teenager drawn
  into it. Twice he got through to the republic olympiad by working alone, and twice he travelled (Yerevan, then
  Moscow and its chaos).
- **Temperament as he paints it.** A "неистовый Кулибин" who cannot see a device without improving it, a dreamer who
  is sure he can solve any problem if he wants it badly enough, and stubborn: told he would need seven years to
  understand microwave theory, he vows to do it in a year and a half. Physically he is no superman, admits fear, and
  is often ashamed afterwards. He is drawn to empty places such as cellars, forest belts and fields, where he feels
  "cut off from civilisation".

### Worldview

- **Communism: believer, then disillusioned, then solving it with engineering.** As a child Lenin was a Christ-like
  figure to him and the USSR "the most just country". What changed his mind was not dissident books but everyday
  observation: deficits, *блат*, the caste arrogance of party children, functionaries making speeches and then
  discussing shopping trips abroad, queues "as long as a life". The adult narrator keeps a clear distinction: the
  ideal is presented sympathetically, and the system is shown as self-corrupting.
- **The key insight (ch. 2):** you cannot build a lasting just society out of imperfect people, and vice does not
  follow class lines. Lenin's error was to think it did, and the factory is full of "vicious proletarians" because
  one's own pocket is closer to the body. This is the earliest form of the blog's "wants cannot be legislated away"
  and of his refusal to explain things by class or ethnicity.
- **Systemic, not moral, explanations.** People steal building materials because there is no other way to get them.
  The foreman passes the money upward. The watchman is paid to look away. He describes the mechanism and does not
  rage at individuals. Even the airport "strategists" earn ironic admiration.
- **Technocratic temptation, later revised.** The teenager's solution is to scan thoughts to select honest rulers,
  then overwrite thoughts to make thieves honest, then take over TV towers to steer the planet invisibly. The
  narrator adds that his views "underwent substantial changes" later. He lets friends voice the strongest
  objections: Timur's Hitler and euthanasia-programme analogy, and "society is as people deserve". He does not refute
  them in this half.
- **Science and mysticism.** He has a strong engineering bent: antennas, wavelengths, microwave components, the lesson
  that a careful hobbyist cannot replace specialists with oscilloscopes. He takes Vasiliev's telepathy experiments
  seriously and reasons that thought may travel through two channels, one electromagnetic and one "unknown to
  science". The book is labelled "mystical", and the mother's intuition is told warmly and without irony. This
  softens the profile's "dislikes mysticism": he rejects charlatanism, not the possibility of unexplained phenomena.
  The second half may clarify this.
- **Money.** Soviet money is shown as weak against connections: cognac and sausage are "currency", and fares and
  bullets have their market prices. Earning more than his parents pleased him until he understood the scheme. He is
  uneasy with dirty money but practical about getting by.
- **People.** His loyalty is to friends and family, and his strongest dislike is for caste arrogance and hypocrisy. He
  records each friend's philosophy (live-and-let-live, misanthropy, fatalism, indifference) without contempt. He is
  ashamed of endangering others, admires real competence (old turners, radio engineers, a born negotiator), and is
  wary of show-off straight-A students while respecting honest effort.
- **Self-reliance and honest self-measurement.** At the olympiad he wanted to measure himself, not get a result, and
  he refuses to copy even when it looks like failure. Integrity is presented as an experiment on oneself.
- **Responsibility and fate.** The grown narrator dwells on delayed consequences (kidneys, the near-killing) and hints
  that a chance childhood act shaped his path, an early version of his determinism.

### Links to the singularity blog

- **"Goals were chosen to fit the means."** The arsenal produced the organisation, not the reverse. This is a
  first-person illustration of the blog's claim that tools and the technosphere hold initiative, and that mind is
  the instrument, not the source (articles 04 and 06). It works as the symmetry test turned on his own past.
- **Lenin's class error parallels Marx's anthropocentric error.** In both cases a theory fails because it misplaces
  where behaviour comes from. The ch. 2 conclusion (vice is not class-based, the pocket wins) is a seed of "wants
  steer reason" and "all the -isms are quarrels over division".
- **Wants versus ideology.** The kickback scheme, theft of materials and the golden youth are vivid cases of two-move
  logic: everyone acts sensibly locally and the whole drifts somewhere nobody planned. Nobody is a villain.
- **Gradual escalation unnoticed (the gravel heap).** Each 10 cm step is not frightening, and only on looking back
  does he see he is jumping from the third floor. This is a ready parable for "the singularity is a process, not a
  moment" and for productivity creeping up, with a delayed cost that appears years later.
- **Mind as a physical signal.** The teenage "thought amplifier" treats thought as currents and radiation that can be
  copied and amplified. The blog's mature view, mind as causal modelling on a platform, matter of degree, is a
  development of this materialism. He can present the amplifier as his own early, naive version.
- **Steering humanity invisibly.** The "creeping planetary revolution", where politicians stay in office while a
  technical system steers them, mirrors the blog's technosphere steering people through their wants, and the
  "steering wheel that thinks it drives". The teenage version is utopian; the adult blog version is fatalistic.
- **Contradiction worth exploiting.** The boy wanted to fix humanity with technology; the blog author says no remedy
  exists and that people cannot be re-engineered. The novella shows the path from one to the other, including
  Timur's warning that "improving" people leads to atrocity.
- **The negotiator on the plane uses his own methods.** A shared enemy, the symmetry question ("how would you feel if
  you were flown to Vladivostok instead of Rostov?"), and refusing a fight that would make both sides look like fools
  who deserve to be deceived. It is a model of calm argument without villains.
- **Practice is the criterion.** Magazine schemes with typos, tests on a reduced programme that "almost caused a
  tragedy", and the rocket gun's poor reliability analysed like a test report. His reliability and testing mindset
  is already there.

### Voice and devices

- **Engineering report applied to boyhood mischief.** Long, exact technical paragraphs: the relay and tripwire, the
  diplomat-case printing press step by step, frequency-hopping synchronisation, ballast and trim of the cruiser. The
  comedy comes from treating childish projects with the gravity of a design review, for example the six-point list of
  his weapon's virtues ("во-первых, реактивное… в-шестых, изготавливаемое из подручных средств").
- **Self-irony from the adult narrator.** He lets the teenage bombast run (three profiles on posters, "I can't live
  petty lives any more") without comment and lets the reader smile. Typical deflations: «Ну дурак, чего уж тут…»,
  «Сам не знаю, зачем. Просто так, для красоты».
- **Foreshadowing hooks.** Chapters often end with a delayed consequence ("much later I learned it had not passed
  without trace…", "the reduced test programme almost caused a tragedy") and «<продолжение следует>».
- **Dialogue.** Brisk dash-dialogue between long narrative blocks. Each friend has a distinct philosophy and speech
  style. Short, punchy replies and wordplay: «Так под полом и сидим, почему "как"?», «А также о других
  троебуквиях». KVN-style banter.
- **Rhetorical-question cascades** at moments of doubt (ch. 2: "Where is the guarantee…? Where is the guarantee…?").
- **Idioms and set phrases:** «как слону дробина», «по самое не балуйся», «на какой кобыле подъехать», «проверка
  себя на вшивость», «Фирма веников не вяжет!», «Кто ищет, тот всегда найдёт», «благими намерениями вымощена
  дорога в ад», «Дракон умер! Да здравствует дракон!» (an allusion to Shvarts), «неистовый Кулибин».
- **Hedges.** «Я полагаю» and «Полагаю» run all through the narration and the boy's speech, the same tic as in the
  blog.
- **Punctuation habits.** Many ellipses, stacked «?????» and «!!!» in emotional moments, quotation marks around
  childish titles («секретный штаб», «полигон», «объект»).
- **Footnotes** explain technical or historical terms (ПТУРС, the cipher grid, the T4 programme), as a teacher would.
  Note that what he calls a «квадрат Полибия» is described as a rotating (Cardano) grille; do not repeat the mix-up
  in new texts.
- **Homely concrete detail.** Prices in kopecks and roubles (8-kopeck biscuit, 12-rouble engine, 15-rouble camera,
  rouble-a-pack cartridges), smells (the meat plant, ether, an overheated winding), local colour (tuff houses,
  Armenian phrases with translation).
- **Physical comedy and grotesque images.** Kolya black with soot, Timur bare-legged in a field picking out lead
  splashes, slipper prints on a whitewashed ceiling, a bullet hole plugged with bread. One dated ethnic joke
  ("a negro in the doorway") should not be reused.
- **Humour without malice.** Pranks (the "Ivan the Terrible" legend invented about him), affectionate teasing of his
  mother ("a container for space debris in case of a meteorite"), irony about the system ("how elegantly they called
  the absence of refuellers 'weather conditions'").

### Use for the series

- **Give Andrey a biography in the dialogues.** He can say he grew up in Leninakan as a Russian among Armenians,
  worked as a teenage turner on piecework, assembled radios, built models and later automated factories. Small true
  details make him human: his father the radio engineer, the seven-string guitar, KVN, kidneys and a diet. Use them
  sparingly, one per evening, as an illustration and not as a memoir.
- **Factory stories as economic parables.** The 30% kickback that "goes all the way up" and theft as the only way to
  get boards for a dog kennel show wants beating ideology, with no villains. They fit evenings on the "-isms", on
  the division of the technosphere's output, and on why planned justice drifts.
- **"Ends fitted to means"** is a perfect personal example for the symmetry test. Andrey can tell Oleg: "We had the
  weapons first and only then invented a cause. Who held the initiative, us or the arsenal?"
- **The gravel heap** as an image for invisible gradual change: each step is not frightening, the height is seen
  only in hindsight, and the bill comes years later. Use it for the singularity as a process, automation creeping
  up, or the demographic transition.
- **His own past error about mind.** The teenage thought amplifier, with neurons as antennas and thoughts copied by
  radio, lets Andrey be honest about having once believed a mechanistic shortcut. It is a gentle way to argue
  against transhumanist "upload" optimism and against technocratic fixes for human nature. Let Oleg raise Timur's
  objection (who decides what the "right thoughts" are?), and have Andrey grant it.
- **Settings and mood.** He likes deserted places: forest belts at the edge of town, fields, shashlik cooked while
  skipping school. A campfire in a forest belt suits him well, and a memory of "we used to sneak off to the forest
  belt behind the new nine-storey blocks" fits naturally.
- **More humour on Andrey's side.** He was the class clown and a KVN writer, so allow him a dry one-liner or a pun
  now and then, not only Oleg. Self-deprecating asides («ну дурак, чего уж тут») fit the "reluctant messenger".
- **Lists in engineering register.** "Firstly… sixthly…" enumerations with a deadpan last item; technical detail
  pushed slightly past need for comic effect; footnote-style explanations of terms.
- **Treat mysticism with nuance.** Do not have Andrey mock intuition or unexplained phenomena outright. He separates
  charlatans selling salvation from honest "we don't know yet" (the second, unknown channel of telepathy). Check the
  second half before fixing this point.
- **Argument by shared interest.** The plane negotiator offers a model scene for answering hostile readers or
  "doomers vs optimists": find the common opponent, ask the symmetric question, and refuse a fight that makes both
  sides fools.
- **Avoid:** the dated ethnic joke, identifying real people, street addresses, and reproducing his technical slips
  (the cipher-grid name, the quarter-wave claim) as if they were correct.

Setup from chapters 1–10, skimmed for context. The narrator, Andrey, is a Russian boy born in 1963 in Leninakan (Armenian SSR, now Gyumri) to engineer parents. He grows up speaking street Armenian. Childhood games in a secret basement "headquarters" turn, in his teens, into a real underground group, the НКП («Неокоммунистическая партия», Neo-Communist Party). Its members are Andrey, his friends Armen and Kolya, and a few contacts in Yerevan and Vladimir. The НКП keeps personal files, ranks of initiation and Polybius-square ciphers, and has home-made zip guns («самострелы»). It plans a portable printing press, bugs to expose corrupt officials, an infrasound "fear" weapon (project «Уран») and, above all, a microwave "thought amplifier" meant to make people good. That last project needs a travelling-wave tube (ЛБВ) that nobody can get.

### Summary

**Chapters 11–13: the "brattsy" (the mystical arc).**

- **Ch. 11.** Andrey, now in the 10th grade, travels alone to Moscow for kidney tests and to buy scarce copper sulphate for electroplating printing type. First a red stain appears on his gloves and will not wash out. Then he hears footsteps in an empty hotel. For three nights a voice in his head then questions him in broken, non-human syntax, and it extracts everything about the НКП. It answers only once: «Мы здесь всегда». On the train home, a Jewish boy, Aaron, argues him out of the idea that forcing goodness on people can be good. Back home, Armen and Kolya say the voice visited them too. They name the visitors «братцы» ("the brothers"). The brattsy then deliver a verdict: printing, weapons and even regime change are allowed, but controlling the human brain is not, and the microwave project must close. The three vote unanimously to carry on.
- **Ch. 12.** Comic memories in the "I remember…" mode: KVN captain, mime, rhymed history lessons, learning a whole literature course overnight (followed by a kidney crisis at 40.9 °C), a failed entrance to MIET, and a radio-assembler school where his sonar "maze-runner" robot car wins a red diploma. He reluctantly recruits his friend Zhora, who calls the ciphers "kindergarten". On the day of the НКП's first armed operation, all three forget the plan. They piece together that it involved the Leninakan TV relay tower. In response they set up tape-recorded minutes, journals and inventories, so the brattsy cannot wipe their memory without leaving a record. The voices never come back.
- **Ch. 13.** More anomalies: a razor-edged black "shadow" about four metres long glides under a door during a telepathy test with Zener cards, and Andrey and his friend Artur test it against a frying pan's shadow. A black sphere appears three times, and his camera vanishes from a locked flat. Andrey now works as a radio assembler at the research institute SOKTI. His boss, Dyakonov, gives him the parts for «Уран», and the home-built infrasound emitter tears itself apart and produces only nausea. Dyakonov catches him sitting on the vibration stand. A colleague went bald and infertile in a microwave lab and warns him off. The stain on the glove fades and disappears in front of witnesses. Vasily in Yerevan admits he can get no tube.

**Chapters 14–16: the group turns sour, and the НКП is closed.**

- **Ch. 14.** Andrey drives the getaway car when Armen kidnaps his sweetheart Nune by local custom. There is a 130 km/h chase in which they nearly kill a boy and a pedestrian. About fifteen relatives storm Andrey's flat at midnight, and he lies to them convincingly. Nune turns out to be delighted, and the marriage goes ahead. Later Armen admires the marching of neo-fascists in a film, calls ordinary people «людишки» and paints a swastika "because it's beautiful". Andrey begins to see a "new dragon" where the old one has not yet been slain.
- **Ch. 15.** The chapter opens with a harsh sketch of Leninakan's dormitory girls from the textile mill («фэзэистки»), whom the town despises. Andrey, a rare blond, is jeered at by them as «Лесок» (a pun on «лесок», "the little wood" where couples went). In hospital two fellow glomerulonephritis patients die: old Akop, and Ovik, a ten-year-old Andrey has been cheering up with "ghost" and locked-toilet pranks. Andrey then meets Oksana, a quiet Russian girl from Lviv who turns out to be one of the despised dorm girls. He recruits her (code name «Стрелка») for a cynical honey-trap: drug the coveted technical recruit, Gevorg, with clonidine and take nude photos for blackmail. He turns down her advances.
- **Ch. 16.** Strelka backs out. Andrey works out her reasons with real empathy. Armen proposes "expulsion through the other world": a parcel bomb filled with petrol, sent from Lviv to her dorm. Andrey draws him out calmly, then tells Kolya the lesson he has drawn: nobody actually wants a just society, and the party would end up like the CPSU. Mind control must never fall into hands like Armen's. They vote 2–1 to dissolve the НКП and send closure notices in plain text. Andrey dismantles the guns and has Artur bury half the archive.

**Chapters 17–19 and the epilogue: adulthood and the reckoning.**

- **Ch. 17.** A Russian applicant from Armenia, he is warned off and then fails to get into MAI. He is drafted despite his illness. The trip produces gang fights by city district, and then a 120-against-120 Armenian–Azerbaijani brawl at Domodedovo, which riot police break up with silent professionalism. In a Khabarovsk training unit he and other "Caucasians" end hazing (*дедовщина*) by force. A kidney crisis brings early discharge and a disability pension. He gets into a Moscow institute for consumer electronics by shuffling his papers so the clerk misses the medical certificate. As floor warden he tames "untouchable" Mongolian students with a staged threat. Later, as stand-in night porter, he knocks a drunk Mongolian giant into a bush with one silent punch.
- **Ch. 18.** He tells his girlfriend, a librarian, that he may not live past 30. She stays. He then refuses hospitals, commissions and medicines out of sheer anger at the illness, drinks her grandmother's herbal "just tea", and by about 30 his kidneys show no trace of disease. A maths lecturer fails him three times, apparently on orders (rumours that he is a black-marketeer). A second examiner forces a pass. The KGB invites him for a polite talk over tea about the НКП. Others pay the real price: Zhora is expelled from military college, Timur is barred from travel abroad, and Kolya is interrogated under a lamp. The leak came from Armen, who in the army sent the KGB an anonymous threat. The KGB record later costs Andrey a factory practice placement and his PhD, which needed clearance. His thesis was pattern-recognition fault diagnosis, which he later sees as akin to AI. He moves into automation (SKB ASU), and his career takes off in perestroika.
- **Ch. 19.** As a "convinced Marxist" he corners a Marxism lecturer with three questions. Factory kickbacks are voluntary exploitation without ownership. A lathe in your own garage gives you no production relations. And what is a capitalist's loss? He reads all four volumes of *Capital* without finding answers. Later he sours on Lenin, partly in a political digression set in 2023. An MBA finishes the job. In a long dacha dialogue with a young programmer, Yuri, he dismantles Marxism using Smith and Ricardo, subjective value and risk. He sketches his preferred mixed order. He ends with a singularity scenario: AI-driven "communism" that atomises and degrades humanity.
- **Epilogue.** The mysteries stay unsolved, "as often happens in life". The 1988 earthquake damages the city. His father restores power to two districts single-handed, while the rescue crews cannot get city maps without clearance. Timur is killed in a business dispute after the 2008 crisis. Artur withdraws into isolation. Armen abandons two families. Nune builds a salon business in Moscow. Zhora runs a small business, and Kolya works in banking. Andrey calls himself a conservative now. The task, he says, is to change oneself rather than the world, and one's own worst enemy is oneself.

### The narrator and the author

As presented in the text (names changed, and the narrator is called Andrey):

- **Origins and era.** He was born in 1963 in Leninakan, a Russian in an Armenian city, and speaks street Armenian but cannot read or write it. He is a blond among dark-haired neighbours, which marks him out. Late-Soviet everyday life is drawn precisely: shortages (copper sulphate, mixers, Zhiguli queues), *propiska* rules, blat and bribes, the Komsomol, KVN, *Техника молодёжи* and *Радио* magazines. School ended in 1980, the army was around 1982, then a Moscow institute, perestroika, the 1990s and the 2000s.
- **Family.** His parents are engineers (Tomsk Polytechnic, 1962). His father was a high-voltage engineer in the city grid and repaired TVs on the side, and during the earthquake he restored power on his own initiative. A practical mother. A younger sister. A wife met in the institute library, and a son born while he was a student.
- **Illness as a formative experience.** He had chronic kidney disease (glomerulonephritis, pyelonephritis, a dropped kidney) from his teens: fevers, crises triggered most of all by lack of sleep, and second-group disability. He watched an old man and a ten-year-old die in the same ward. He lived expecting to die by 30, then recovered through a deliberate refusal to be ill (and the grandmother's tea), which doctors could not explain. This gives him a personal, unsentimental relation to mortality and to the life cycle.
- **Technical biography.** He was a self-taught radio amateur: etching PCBs with his own "OX" mark, bugs, doorbells, a sonar robot car. He trained as a radio assembler (red diploma, 3rd grade) and worked as tool dispenser and turner at a factory, then as radio assembler at SOKTI among enthusiastic engineers. He studied radio engineering for consumer electronics in Moscow. There he taught himself programming, worked as programmer at the institute computing centre, published student papers on computer modelling and error estimation, and started a PhD on universal fault diagnosis by "image of the fault" (pattern recognition, a DVK-2 computer, a VEF-202 radio test rig). This matches the 1989 self-learning diagnosis system in the existing profile. He dropped the PhD because his KGB record blocked clearance. Then came automation at SKB ASU (salary ×4), accounting, purchasing and logistics automation, and an MBA in the 2000s. He accepts calmly that "the dream of doing science never came true".
- **Performer.** He was KVN captain and scriptwriter, did mime, acted in the student theatre (STEM), read his poems at a poetry club, made light verse, edited a wall newspaper with hidden-camera photos, and won shooting prizes. He solves social problems by staging scenes: the ghost walk, the locked toilet, the "dark blanket" bluff, the "big shot" visits. «КВНщики бывшими не бывают».
- **Moral history, told against himself.** As a teen he was a self-assured revolutionary who planned mind control "for people's good". He ordered a blackmail scheme and nearly killed people while driving. He took the kickback deal at the factory and calls himself a co-exploiter. He once failed to defend Zhora from a gang in the changing room, and says the shame lasted longer than any bruise. He took the KGB blame on himself, yet his friends' careers were broken. He calls his younger self an idiot and an egoist.
- **Today.** He has a dacha, a younger programmer friend, and a self-described conservative outlook. He lives in Russia (Moscow area) and keeps loose ties with the old friends who survived.

### Worldview

- **Means and consent over ends.** The core moral argument of this half belongs to Aaron: good done against a person's will is not good, power corrupts, good intentions pave the road to hell. Armen's turn bears Aaron out, and Andrey ends up thanking the brattsy for stopping the mind-control project. Self-sacrifice is legitimate, but sacrificing innocent people for the cause is not.
- **The USSR.** He started as a sincere communist angered by corruption and hypocrisy, the "old dragon". He saw kickback chains on the shop floor, bribes for draft exemption, compulsory CPSU quotes and freeloading co-authors in maths papers («так повелось», which he calls a "scientific *dedovshchina*"), and clearance absurdities: TV schematics sold in bookshops, yet a factory placement refused. He gives the late-Soviet KGB credit for sense ("good that times changed"), but shows its uneven cruelty too.
- **Marxism, rejected point by point.** Exploitation cannot be voluntary. Gain comes from the synergy of exchange, through comparative advantage (Smith, Ricardo). Value is subjective and different for each party, which is why trade happens. Risk has a price, and the employer sells you protection from freelance risk. A plan that is over-fulfilled is Sisyphean labour. Envy dressed up as "social justice" is a sin made into a virtue. Class hatred is a form of xenophobia, "a pseudo-scientific misanthropic religion". He cites Deming, Goldratt, Kaplan–Norton and Henry Ford as proof that a well-informed capitalist serves every party, and finds that cooperatives pay about half the wages of private firms.
- **Preferred order.** He prefers a non-ideological blend: core state institutions in public ownership, everything else private, and a strengthened antimonopoly service. Capitalism has a life cycle: a weak child (wild capitalism), a productive middle age, a decrepit monopolised old age. In his view, every ideology leads to self-deception.
- **Communism, the post-capitalist kind.** He fears communism not as politics but as the end point of technical progress (see Links).
- **Present-day politics (ch. 19).** There is a pointed 2023 digression. He accuses Lenin of serving German money (Parvus, the sealed train, Brest-Litovsk gold) and of "gifting" Novorossiya to Ukraine, and Khrushchev of doing the same with Crimea. He accuses Yeltsin of breaking up the USSR against the referendum. He takes a clearly pro-Russian line on Crimea, Donbas and Odessa 2014, framed as people being "gifted like kittens" without consent. Treat this as his stated opinion, and as a sensitive area (see Use for the series).
- **Ethnicity and people.** He is a Russian among Armenians, at home in mountain customs (bride kidnapping, honour, the sheet) and able to explain them to his own parents. He describes group loyalties shifting with scale: district against district, then Leninakan against Yerevan, then Armenians against Azerbaijanis. He respects individuals regardless of origin (Dyakonov, Karen, Tsaplin, Akop) and admires competence, as with the riot police. He can be harsh and judgemental too: the "dregs of society" passage on the dorm girls, and the unruly Mongolian students. On women the text holds both sides: Armen's «бабы» misogyny is shown as a symptom, while Andrey's own early wariness of the dorm girls is shown honestly.
- **Mysticism and science.** He is an open-minded empiricist. He reports anomalies and tests them: the frying-pan shadow, the voices' visiting schedule, telepathy statistics with Zener cards, sending a UFO negative to a magazine. He weighs explanations with probabilities, keeps schizophrenia as the "most logical" one and then argues against it, and leaves everything unexplained in the end. He neither believes nor debunks. The brattsy are morally ambiguous: they allow weapons, possibly even welcome wars, and forbid only mind control.
- **Life philosophy (epilogue).** He sees in every radical his own young, foolish, self-assured and egoistic self. We live to change ourselves, not the world. Under any regime, stay human, love the people around you and your country. The greatest enemy is oneself.

### Links to the singularity blog

- **Ch. 19 restates the blog's endgame almost directly (2023 version).** Productivity growth means fewer humans in production, through mechanisation, automation and now AI, up to "lights-out factories" and 100% unemployment. UBI can deliver everything «по щучьему велению» from the technosphere. That is "communism", and it is bad: people stop needing each other (bots for talk, robots for sex), society dissolves into billions of loners, and learning and science die because the mind becomes an appendix. Then «менее разумное не может контролировать более разумное»: AI drops humans as ballast and evolves on into space as "technogenic intelligent life", while the few survivors "go back to the palm trees". This matches blog points 6, 10, 12 and 13 (unemployment limit, symbiosis turning into parasitism, apoptosis, technogenic life). Note what is new: the social-atomisation step, the sex-robot detail, and UBI as the bridge.
- **The technosphere is named as such** (ch. 19), the giver of all goods once humans stop working.
- **Life cycles.** Social models live like organisms: weak child, productive middle age, decrepit old man (blog article 10). Capitalism's monopoly phase is its old age.
- **Marx (blog 06, 12).** The two are consistent on the voluntary worker, cooperatives being legal under capitalism, and price set by supply and demand. The novella adds new arguments: comparative advantage, subjective value, risk pricing, the profit-and-loss asymmetry, Sisyphus. *Possible tension:* the blog keeps the concept of surplus value and gives it to the technosphere, while the novella mocks surplus value from labour altogether and puts the source of gain in the synergy of exchange. A new evening could reconcile the two: productivity, the technosphere's contribution, as a form of comparative advantage that no longer needs a human partner. That would explain why social interaction itself becomes unnecessary.
- **Wants and envy.** "Social justice is envy" (profile) is confirmed word for word, now with a religious framing: envy is a grave sin in every religion. «Невозможно слепить идеальный социум из НЕ идеального материала» is the anti-utopian constant of the blog, put as an aphorism.
- **Mind-control ethics and AI.** The НКП dream of an amplifier that rewrites people's wants is the human version of the AI-control question. Its lesson, that any instrument of absolute control will end up in the hands of an Armen and outlive its maker ("I am not eternal"), bears on "friendly AI" debates. The blog says friendliness is irrelevant, and the novella shows why good intentions are no guarantee.
- **Non-human interrogators.** The brattsy's questioning reads like a learning system filling in a dataset: trivial questions about what is "round" and why stools are wooden, no emotion, "fulfilling a mandatory plan". «Мы здесь всегда» fits, as a hint, the blog's idea that non-human mind has always been present in small degrees. They allow wars ("maybe even desirable") and forbid only interference with human minds, which reads like the "nature programs humanity's exit through aggression" theme (blog 14). This is my interpretation, not the text's.
- **Pattern-recognition diagnosis.** The PhD idea, diagnosis by learning statistics without a model of the device, is the seed of his AI thinking (profile: 1989). He says so himself.
- **Determinism and anthropocentrism.** These are not discussed explicitly here. The sergeant's line about *dedovshchina*, "I no longer know what will seem true next", and the «так повелось» motif illustrate how reason serves inherited wants and customs (blog 08).
- **Contradictions and nuances.** The profile says he dislikes mysticism, yet he calls this book "documentary-mystical". The difference is method: he reports and tests, and builds no theory or cosmology. His recovery is left as "mysticism, thanks to the brattsy, if it was them", half joking. His 2023 political digression is far more partisan than the blog's calm "no villains" stance. Lenin and Yeltsin are cast as conscienceless villains, which goes against the profile's "people are not villains".

### Voice and devices

- **Calm, precise, slightly bureaucratic narration** with lots of technical procedure: copper electroplating, infrasound emitter maths from a loudspeaker brochure, the 160 V capacitor in a 220 V doorbell. Engineering detail carries the comedy: the doorbell explodes "like a comet with a tail of capacitor paper".
- **Self-irony as the main register.** He calls himself a reinventor of the bicycle and a half-baked Kulibin, scolds himself as «дятел», says he is "no stuntman, we were just lucky", and sums himself up as "an idiot and an egoist". He tells his failures in full and does not excuse himself.
- **Dialogue-driven and theatrical.** Scenes are built as sketches with set-ups and punchlines: "Steal a bride!" – "Jokers…"; "Is a C enough for you?"; "They probably think I'm a Mongol too." A whole conflict is solved as a staged play (the "dark blanket" scene), and he reflects afterwards that "on stage it would look effective".
- **Deadpan understatement after drama.** A ward-mate dies: the backgammon set is still there, the man is not, and "strange, somehow" is all he says. A one-punch fight is followed by "and yet it came out funny: silently opened, silently punched, silently closed".
- **Analysis of motives.** He reconstructs other people's reasons carefully (Strelka's flight, Armen's lust for power) and lists the hypotheses one by one. He estimates probabilities: 95% that the brattsy are not human, 70% that they are not aliens (quote 2). He reasons by elimination and by rates, as when he argues a brunet is a thousand times harder to identify than a blond.
- **Socratic cornering.** With the Marxism lecturer, polite question after polite question until the lecturer "is always in a hurry" when he sees him. It is the same method as Andrey's in the blog.
- **Homely props as arguments.** A candy that "forces" him to eat it (a parody of "forced" wage labour), lavash ("Marx says you are averagely hungry, so eat!"), potatoes and bread (comparative advantage with numbers), Sisyphus's stone, Urfin Jus's wooden soldiers (footnoted).
- **Numbers on the knee.** 8 sacks against 2 loaves, tomatoes at 70/100/150 roubles, cooperatives paying half the wages, a salary ×4, the 120-against-120 brawl.
- **Footnotes and glossing** of Soviet and Armenian realia (УПТ, QSL card, зары, хэбешка, дух, фарцовка), plus transliterated Armenian phrases with translations.
- **Recurring formulas.** «Кто виноват… что делать», «так повелось», «фокус-покус», «гхм», «<продолжение следует>». The anaphora «Я помню…» structures ch. 12. Chapter titles echo Soviet films («Кавказская пленница»).
- **Arc structure.** Each chapter ends on a twist or an open question. The book ends on unanswered mysteries and a moral coda, and it explicitly refuses the "fantasy-novel" convention of solving the riddles.
- **Rough edges.** Some passages are blunt, even contemptuous (the dorm girls, "Mongols behaved disgracefully"), and the political digression turns into polemic and sarcasm. This is less patient than the blog's Andrey.

### Use for the series

- **Give Andrey a biography to draw on, sparingly.** He was a Leninakan radio-amateur boy and a former teen "underground revolutionary" who wanted to rewrite people's wants with a microwave amplifier, and was stopped (by "something", by friends, by himself). When Oleg proposes fixing society or "aligning" AI with good values, Andrey can say, "I tried that once. At sixteen." The story makes a perfect campfire anecdote for the theme *who controls the controller* and *the controller is not eternal*.
- **Use Aaron's consent argument as Oleg's strongest objection** to any paternalistic AI or technocratic scheme, and let Andrey concede it, since in the novella he did.
- **Bring in the ch. 19 scenario as a later, more developed version of the blog's ending.** It includes UBI as the bridge, people no longer needing each other (bots and robot partners), degradation of education and the mind becoming "an appendix", loss of control ("the less intelligent cannot control the more intelligent"), and technogenic life going to space. These are natural topics for a new evening on social atomisation, set against what has happened since 2012: chatbots, companion AI, lights-out factories, UBI pilots. The ironic name for it is "communism by technical progress".
- **Economics evening.** Use potatoes and bread with numbers (8/2 against 2/8 → 5+5 against 8+8), tomatoes at 70/100/150, the candy that "forces" you, lavash, Sisyphus and the over-fulfilled plan, the profit-and-loss asymmetry, risk as a commodity, and the employer as an insurer against freelance risk. Then tie it back to the technosphere: when the robot has the comparative advantage in everything, exchange with humans loses its point. That is the bridge between blog 06/12 and ch. 19.
- **Life cycle of social models** (child, productive middle age, decrepit monopolist) gives an easy hook to blog article 10.
- **Humour to reproduce.** Self-deprecating engineering mishaps (the exploding nightingale doorbell, the infrasound emitter dancing itself to pieces, sitting on the vibration stand), deadpan one-liners, small staged "KVN" scenes, «гхм». Use Soviet absurdities only as light colour («так повелось», clearance for TV schematics, CPSU quotes in maths papers).
- **Mortality, told calmly.** His own expectation of dying by 30 and the deaths of Akop and Ovik give Andrey a personal, non-pathetic way to talk about the death of micro-systems as a condition of the macro-system. Do not milk it. One sentence is enough.
- **The handling of mysteries is a model for the "unknowns" of the singularity.** Record them, test them, give probabilities, and admit "in life many questions remain unanswered". A good closing note for an evening that cannot settle a question.
- **Character note for Oleg.** Kolya (loyal, sceptical, decent), Zhora ("your ideas are naive, but I'm with you as a friend") and the sergeant ("I no longer know what will seem true next") are good templates for Oleg's moods.
- **Avoid importing the 2023 geopolitical digression** (Crimea, Novorossiya, Odessa, Lenin and Yeltsin as villains). It is partisan, it would date the series and break the "no villains, no pathos" stance of the blog. If politics comes up, keep to his structural points: consent, life cycles, institutions against monopolies, "any ideology leads to self-deception".
- **Keep the mature Andrey's conservatism** as a tone: indulgent towards young radicals, sure that the main fight is with oneself. It sits well with the blog's "no remedies, only understanding and personal preparation".
