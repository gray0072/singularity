# 04. In Search of the Source of Initiative — notes

## Summary
Andrey compares anthropocentrism to a wisdom tooth with curved roots that must be loosened and removed in pieces; tonight he wants one large piece out. A Socratic chain follows: a stone has no initiative, Oleg has, molecules have not, a bacterium has. Molecules are moved by external fields, fields have no mind, so initiative can exist without mind, and human initiative too may not come from mind. Oleg splits initiative into two "nationalities", spontaneous and purposeful. Andrey asks how, in the evolution of matter, initiative jumped from outside objects to inside them and turned from spontaneous to purposeful. His rule: initiative, if it exists, must be of one kind and always on the same side of all interacting objects, either always inside or always outside; anything else is "anthropocentric discrimination". Option "always inside" means atoms have senses, pattern recognition and a value system (ordered like the electrochemical series), "like" or "dislike" each other and "start a family" (a molecule); even dissociated ions in distilled water are ions with "other preferences". Oleg rejects this. Option "always outside" keeps physics intact, and Andrey shows it also fits humans: "I do what I want" proves subordination to one's wants, not freedom, because wants and emotions are not under our control. Will is just the dynamics of competing wants; a wavering would-be wallet thief is an unstable system, "watchdog emotions" force a decision, and conscience is a stabilizing mechanism. With enough measurement one could draw a force diagram and predict the act. Mind is an instrument of initiative, not its source: a supercar parked without a driver. The source is the laws of nature. Man never conquered nature or broke one of its laws, and the split between artificial and natural is contrived. Free will is a badly posed question: a human is nested in nature, so his will is both his own and nature's, the same will. Pressed, Andrey reveals the deeper view, that there is no initiative at all, anywhere, but keeps "always outside" as the working position. Three conclusions are fixed. Tomorrow's topic is artificial intelligence.

## Key theses
1. Initiative can exist without mind: physical fields "move" molecules without being reasonable.
2. Consistency principle: initiative must be located on the same side (inside or outside) for all participants of an interaction. Making some objects initiators and others passive is a logical error and anthropocentric discrimination.
3. "I do what I want" confirms subordination to one's wants and emotions, which themselves are not under one's control. It is not evidence of freedom.
4. Will is an anthropocentric name for the dynamics of competing wants. Behaviour is the resultant of field-like forces of wants, aversions and emotions, and is predictable in principle. Hesitation, anguish and conscience are mechanisms that stabilize the system.
5. The source of initiative is the laws of nature. Man never conquered nature, broke a law, or made nature obey a law of his own.
6. Free will is a badly posed question. Human will and the will of the laws of nature are one and the same will; if a choice is forced, human will is not free from the laws of nature.
7. At bottom, initiative does not exist anywhere. "Initiative always outside" is a safe interim model.
8. **Three fixed conclusions: (1) mind is not the source of initiative but its instrument; (2) man is part of nature; (3) the division of processes into natural and artificial is contrived.**

## Staging and devices
- Opening image: Andrey staring into the flames, comparing anthropocentrism to an impacted wisdom tooth with curved roots: rock it, saw it into pieces, extract slowly. The dental image recurs ("so I'll torture you a little longer, or the root will break").
- Long Socratic yes/no chain (stone, Oleg, molecule, bacterium, fields), with Oleg sensing "a trick question" and getting confused.
- Analogies: two "nationalities" of initiative put on separate shelves; atoms with a value system that judge each other as "a nice specimen", "yuck" or "don't care" and "start a family"; ions with "other preferences" (a risqué parallel with human sexual preferences); click-switching off love of sweets or sadness; stealing a wallet (greed vs self-esteem); a force diagram (эпюр) and its resultant; an expensive supercar parked with no driver; a scientist hunting the Higgs boson, then hungry for donuts, then meeting a girl and inviting her for donuts and a chat about the Higgs boson; Vasya and Petya as "freedom" between non-nested systems; "green: round or soft?" reused for free will; "do you want a compliment or the truth?", where the compliment is "you are a local god".
- Oleg's objections: "I feel the source of initiative in me"; particles' senses would have been discovered by science; particles would show variation in behaviour like people; "nobody controls me from outside"; a strong will controls wants; "you're dodging the question"; "I don't like that answer".
- Andrey's handling: lets Oleg choose between the two options, demolishes the one he chooses, then offers the other as "less painful". He reveals the radical position ("no initiative at all") only when pressed, and immediately withdraws to the safer interim view.
- Andrey jokes "an atheist, and look how he talks" when Oleg raises free will.
- Ending: three numbered conclusions. Tomorrow: artificial intelligence. Oleg slyly asks whether it is connected with the third conclusion, and Andrey praises his progress against anthropocentrism.

## The author in the comments
**Initiative as instability.**
- **Objection** (Im27th): initiative is just instability. Stable systems (a stone, a corpse) have none, unstable ones (bacterium, human) generate local initiative, and death is stabilization.
- **Answer**: initiative must ALWAYS and for EVERYTHING be placed on one side. Either all interactions are treated as field interactions (initiative outside), or all through the physics of "local creators and decision-makers". Mixing them mixes two immiscible physics and turns some objects into "gods" and others into "pawns". In one interaction the laws cannot differ between its participants.

**What field interaction means: the apples and the technosphere.**
- **Answer** (unprompted follow-up): the Earth and an apple attract each other; a falling apple shifts the Earth's orbit, microscopically. Habit treats this as negligible, until the apples pile up gradually into a heap many times heavier than the Earth, and then the Earth moves more than the heap, while habit still says the Earth is in charge. The same holds for humanity and the technosphere. While small, it depended on people. Growing smoothly and unnoticed, it now affects people more than they affect it, yet people still believe they control it. This is the first explicit statement of the technosphere thesis. (Ares_ekb calls it an interesting metaphor.)

**"One side only" is not an axiom of physics.**
- **Objection** (Im27th): physics has pressure from both sides, for example a balloon.
- **Answer**: the blog is written for ordinary people, simplified, "even excessively down-to-earth". With a scientist he would talk about determinism and the Lorentz invariance of the interval, and it would become clear that initiative does not exist anywhere: there is only the "physics-and-maths of the universe" defined in eternity. In field physics nothing acts one-sidedly; objects only inter-act. Anthropocentrism makes people forget that they are acted upon too. Even observation transfers non-zero energy and changes both observer and observed (attributed to Heisenberg). Hence no one can hold anything under control without being under control himself.
