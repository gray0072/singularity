# 14. The apoptosis of humanity — notes

## Summary
Andrey picks up the earlier conclusion (from the life-cycle evening) that the mortality of micro-systems is what keeps macro-systems viable and adaptable, and introduces apoptosis: cells could in principle divide forever, yet nature built a division counter into them that triggers forced death. This protects against viruses and runaway defective mutations, and in the human body it kills 10^10–10^11 cells a day. Similar mechanisms work in ecosystems: predators act as "sanitary orderlies" for herds. Whenever people meddle with such global laws (extermination of wolves, China's war on sparrows that ended in famine) they get the opposite of what they wanted — and the same short-sightedness, he says, drives gerontologists who dream of immortality. He then builds a ladder: a person can develop because cells die, humanity because people die, the planet because humanity dies, the galaxy because planetary systems die. If apoptosis is everywhere, one should look for it in the system "humanity" too. Its main components are the selective short-sightedness of reason and the "wants" (envy, malice, greed) that blind it, above all anthropocentrism. Further symptoms: unmotivated mass aggression (the 2011 London riots by well-off teenagers, the Egyptian unrest that ruined tourism), the ever more perfect weapons whose use becomes a matter of probability and time, mass psychoses, and the widening gap between rich and poor (Occupy Wall Street). The mechanism is "rational": it fires neither too early nor too late — most likely only when the technosphere can reproduce without people, perhaps triggered by some hidden mechanism in the psyche once people are convinced of this. The evening ends with three conclusions and a promise to describe the likely scenario next time.

## Key theses
1. Mortality of micro-systems is a necessary condition for the viability, adaptability and competitiveness of the macro-system they make up; that is why programmed death (apoptosis) appeared at the earliest stage of life.
2. Cells are not worn out by division; their death is imposed by a built-in counter, i.e. death is a function, not a defect.
3. Apoptosis-like mechanisms exist at every level showing evolutionary dynamics (predators as sanitary agents for herds); they arise automatically and obey laws of nature.
4. Human interference with such global laws reliably produces the opposite effect (wolves, Chinese sparrows); gerontology and the immortality project are another instance of anthropocentric short-sightedness.
5. The nested chain of mortality: cells → person → humanity → planetary system → galaxy/universe; each level evolves because the level below is mortal.
6. Humanity's apoptosis works first through the selective short-sightedness of reason: nature does not let people be wise enough to prevent the death of humanity; the "wants" (envy, malice, greed) and anthropocentrism are the blinding instruments.
7. Other components: unmotivated mass aggression, ever more perfect weapons (what can happen will happen, the only question is when), mass psychoses, the growing rich–poor gap and the social unrest it breeds.
8. Protests for justice bring not justice but more poverty and bloodshed: the rich get poorer, but the poor do not get richer.
9. The mechanism is rational and timed: humanity has not destroyed itself yet because an early death would leave evolution nothing to continue with; the trigger should come once the technosphere can reproduce without people.
10. **Closing summary: the death of humanity is most probably programmed by the laws of nature; anthropocentrism and the other reason-depriving "wants" are part of the mechanism; the moment of humanity's self-liquidation must coincide with the full autonomy of the technosphere.**

Characteristic line from the summary: «Смерть человечества наиболее вероятно запрограммирована законами природы.»

## The author in the comments

### Immortality, repair and "rolling back"
- **Objection** (Longfello): a cell is like a car — keep replacing parts and it runs forever; life has a meaning, harmony. → **Answer**: replacing parts gives upgrades only within one hardware platform: you cannot upgrade a 286 PC into a modern one, a crisp-logic computer into a fuzzy-logic AI, or a cart into a Ferrari. He once believed in meaning-as-harmony himself, but no longer does.
- **Objection** (Foror): why go straight to palm trees and extinction in one or two generations — why not roll back to an obedient technosphere and live off its crumbs? → **Answer**: scarce resources will go to the technosphere; even an axe is made by plants and steelworks, which stop making axes once out of human control, and iron is too valuable to be spent "on the side". Who will give up the car, lift, plumbing, fridge, TV and computer? And if some do, others will keep pushing the technosphere for money "here and now". The technosphere gets from humanity not only reproduction but the chance to evolve; rolling back means stopping evolution, which is like stopping a steam engine with a straw.
- Signature line: «Остановить эволюцию НЕВОЗМОЖНО!»
- He explains the drive behind it: every person seeks an advantage over others, so gunpowder, the spear, the bow and the stone axe "for crushing skulls" were inevitable; man never gives up an advantage voluntarily. «Так устроены его хотелки, что он будет жаждать того, что его в итоге уничтожит.» That is apoptosis, a natural mechanism; history was never in people's power.
- **Objection** (Сергей06): your conclusions sound anthropocentric and meant to scare; fine, man will be a phagocyte of the technosphere; what is your own attitude, and where should one stand to survive? → **Answer**: how does a child feel when it first realises it is mortal? He tried all along to escape anthropocentrism. When man stops being needed he will be nobody to the technosphere; its creatures will be more "other" to us than we are to a cockroach. The survival niche: use the minimum of planetary resources, only your own muscle — no electricity, plumbing, transport. Such people might last a couple of generations, until oxygen disappears, temperatures rise by tens of degrees and the technosphere pushes them off the surface.

### Does man still evolve? Humanity as ballast

### Limits of the human mind; quantity, not quality
- **Objection** (hitthelimit): what if a system of human-psyche complexity is the absolute functional limit (given fixed world constants)? → **Answer**: impossible. Man is local; humanity has existed for 0.03% of the universe's lifetime, like a muon (2.2 microseconds) relative to a human life; a component cannot be more complex than the whole it belongs to. Picture a juggler with as many balls as there are atoms in the universe — the psyche lacks the memory and speed.
- The psyche is an instrument of reflection; a reflection is always partial and distorting and cannot contain all of reality, or it would become reality. Particulars matter, not only generalities: no two stars, stones or twigs are identical, and the differences have objective causes that science, built on generalisation to cut the information flow, discards and cannot handle. "All information matters". People recognise individual "personality" in humans but not yet that non-humans may have personality too. He suspects the discarded information may explain the quantum "randomisers".
- Unrepeatable phenomena (a person levitating in an altered state of consciousness; a photo of Messing floating above a crowd) are ignored by science because they can't be reproduced, yet Earth is unrepeatable too and still real.
- How much "function" we see depends on worldview: atoms reacting to nearby atoms could be credited with "sensing", "preferences" and "motor function"; an observer who sees himself as an exclusive system with free will grants these to himself and denies them to atoms.
- **Objection**: complexity grows from star-planetary evolution to life to mind; machine evolution cannot skip mind; name a criterion. → **Answer**: entropy always grew globally and fell locally at every stage: stars are Prigogine's dissipative structures in the universe's dissipative medium; the Sun's radiation produces both life and tornadoes; crystals, snowflakes and computer viruses reproduce themselves. Differences between living and non-living, intelligent and non-intelligent are quantitative, not qualitative; the line is a naming convenience, like white, grey and black. He challenges the opponent to compare the "control structures" of a tornado with those of a state or a business.
- Control structures separate from what they control only in human perception; a physical field shows itself only in interaction and is a property of *space*, a control structure separated from the start, more fundamentally than cephalisation.
- The key question is where the *initiative* of control flows lies. People, atheists included, believe "recklessly and devotedly" in free will without scientific grounds. Mind does not generate initiative; it is its instrument, a "cog" executing a tiny part of the universe's control flows. We never deliberately added convolutions to our brains.
- A global limit on evolution may exist (the heat death of the universe as entropy flows dry up), but what is the point of discussing it? Reality needs no self-awareness; the laws of nature simply work.
- **On Panov–Snooks** (hitthelimit suggests the attractor point is the Panov–Snooks singularity and concerns the whole Earth because "there is only my reality"): → **Answer**: the curve describes only the planetary evolution of "living" matter; inanimate matter evolved long before. Only the phase of *biological* life is ending; evolution is a set of nested S-curves, this one high but not the highest. Its physical cause is entropic flows generating self-complicating dissipative structures; while the solar flow lasts, evolution is safe — its individual representatives, "dinosaurs of all kinds", are not.
- He welcomes hitthelimit's point that evolution understood as the appearance of systems capable of "seamless regression" may have nothing to do with entropy: a new thought he needs to digest.
