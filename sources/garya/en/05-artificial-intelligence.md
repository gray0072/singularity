# 05. Artificial Intelligence — notes

## Summary
Oleg opens: man cannot jump higher than his own head and create an intelligence greater than his own. Andrey replies that this would hold only if human initiative came from human mind. It comes from the laws of nature, in particular the global law of the evolution of matter. The lancelet could never have reasoned its way into a human, yet chordates became humans, so "artificial" intelligence is not quite artificial, and nature's trend from simple to complex should continue. Asking *when* AI will appear is again a binary question: AI has existed since windmills, mechanical clocks, wind-up toys and carts, devices with non-zero domain logicality, and the Antikythera mechanism was already an advanced analogue "computer". To Oleg's "but it needed a human to run" he answers with the cuckoo, which does not hatch its own eggs and is still a bird. He proposes a substitution method: replace the machine by a human helper and estimate how much extra brain activity is needed to give the same help. Computers are "slightly improved arithmometers", but that is how evolution works: small improvements accumulate into a new turn. Like human sequential thinking, computers compensate for scarce resources with serial processing and raise aggregate mind mainly through faster access to knowledge (local data, databases, the internet). Chess and strategy programs beat humans and have difficulty settings to lower their logicality, and humans improve more slowly than computers. One engineer with CAD replaces about ten without it: CAD takes over functions, not people, but nine engineers still become unneeded, a point he flags for later. Von Neumann computers will not surpass humans, because their parallelism is too weak. Emulating neural nets on them is like building a transistor radio from vacuum tubes, so the future is neurochips. Judge the dynamics, not the current state: neurochips went from primitive about 20 years ago to industrial 10 years ago to two orders more synapses now. Progress outruns people's ability to notice it, quantum computers already exist (one sold), and the theory of AI is basically complete, so only technology remains. Consciousness is defined as a self-model, and examples follow: the 2006 starfish robot with a self-model and a built-in "want", a 2009 robot scientist, IBM's 2009 cat-level simulation, and a DARPA SyNAPSE forecast of human level by the end of 2018, shown on a chart. Oleg is delighted that robots will work for us; whether that is good is left for next time.

## Key theses
1. Humans can create a mind greater than their own, because the initiative comes from the laws of nature and the evolution of matter, not from human mind (lancelet → human).
2. "Artificial" intelligence is not really artificial. Nature creates it through human minds and hands, continuing its trend from simple to complex.
3. AI has existed since the first mechanisms with internal logic (mills, clocks, the Antikythera mechanism). It differs only in degree, and "when will AI be created?" is badly posed.
4. Substitution method: estimate a machine's degree of mind by the extra human brain activity needed to replace its help.
5. Evolution works by small, seemingly insignificant improvements that accumulate into a qualitative leap. Computers raise aggregate mind mainly by speeding up access to accumulated knowledge (databases → internet).
6. Computers already exceed humans in particular domains (chess, strategy games). Humans learn more slowly than machines, and games in nature are training for real life.
7. CAD regroups functions: humans keep the harder tasks, machines take the systematizable mass work. Yet nine out of ten engineers become unneeded, the seed of the unemployment argument.
8. The von Neumann architecture is a dead end for human-level AI. Neural nets and neurochips are the way, and emulating nets on von Neumann machines signals a crisis of the old technology.
9. Look at the dynamics, not the current state. Progress accelerates faster than people can perceive it, the theory of AI is basically ready, and only technology remains.
10. Consciousness is the ability to build a model of oneself in the world. Robots with a self-model and a built-in "want" already reproduce the human scheme (a given intention, with the means found by the mind itself).
11. Forecast (cited from SyNAPSE/IBM): human-level AI by the end of 2018.
12. **Progress has not stopped: AI systems exist and they are developing.**

## Staging and devices
- Opening image: Oleg throws branches on the fire, settles comfortably and opens with the objection ("man can't jump higher than his head").
- Analogies: the lancelet evolving into man; windmills, clocks, wind-up toys and carts as primitive AI; the Antikythera mechanism; the cuckoo that doesn't hatch its own eggs but is still a bird; the substitution "helper" method; arithmometer → computer as gradual evolution; playing animals as training; difficulty levels in games; one engineer with CAD = ten without; a transistor radio built from vacuum tubes; neurons packed into neurochips.
- Props: Andrey hands Oleg his tablet to show a 2000 review of neurochips and IBM's 2011 neurochips, then a forecast chart ("look at the graph"). The modern gadget at the campfire signals the present tense of the argument.
- Chronology as rhetoric: 2006, 2009, 2009, then the forecast for 2018. Each milestone is linked to a news article.
- Oleg's objections: it's just a joke (mills as AI?); the mechanism didn't compute by itself; computers are only improved arithmometers; "these are only games"; CAD can't replace even one engineer; 10^15 synapses is far away; neural nets aren't visible anywhere; do they have consciousness?; they can't match humans.
- Andrey's handling: concedes the true part (CAD cannot replace an engineer fully; robots are not yet at human level) and then shifts to functions and dynamics.
- Ending: Oleg is enthusiastic ("people can relax, robots will work, great!"). Andrey defers "great or not" to next time, which sets up the economic and social articles (06, 07, 09). Summary line: progress has not stopped, and AI systems exist and develop.

**The film "Technocalypse 2 – Singularity".**

  - Too much about the human body. The talk of healthy food and morning exercise astonished him most: humans consume what the technosphere produces, not just lifts, fridges and cars but clothes, shoes, bread, paper, "everything". Why blow up machines while you keep using what they produce?

  - Recommends instead a YouTube lecture (link above) as "a completely different matter".

Tone: impatient with philosophizing and sharp toward sensationalism, but he defends ordinary people's intelligence. He sets his "serious" stance against hysteria.

## Voice and personality
- Evolutionary-naturalist frame: everything humans build is nature continuing its own trend. "Artificial" is a misnomer.
- Argues from trend and extrapolation ("judge the dynamics"). He cites dated milestones and news links as evidence and is willing to give a concrete date (2018).
- An IT engineer's taste for architectural judgements: von Neumann is weak at parallelism, emulation is a symptom of crisis, and he uses the tubes vs transistor analogy.
- Quick, homely counter-analogies (the cuckoo) that turn an objection into a demonstration of anthropocentrism.
- Plants economic consequences quietly ("nine engineers become unneeded") and promises to return to them, which builds suspense.
- Strong dislike of religious, hysterical or emotional treatments of the singularity. He wants it to be taken seriously by science.
- Not misanthropic: humans are smart, but nature selectively blinds them. Fatalistic about resisting the technosphere (futile).
- Characteristic quotes:
