# 2009-09-15. The forum thread "an apocalypse scenario" — notes

## What survives

This is the forum thread the blog grew out of, not a blog post. Garya opened it on 15 September 2009 in the sql.ru
chat forum «Просто треп» (PT) by reposting a scenario he had first written in another sql.ru chat forum (ЗПТ). The
thread ran for eight years. By the last snapshot (20 July 2017) it had 14,180 messages on 568 pages of 25. From
August 2017 the forum answered guests «У вас нет прав на чтение данного форума», so nothing later was archived, and
sql.ru itself closed in 2022. The Wayback Machine kept only 40 pages, about 7% of the thread: 958 messages, 292 of
them by Garya, from 68 nicks. The Russian copy with every message in full is
[`../sqlru/2009-09-15-apocalypse-scenario-thread.md`](../sqlru/2009-09-15-apocalypse-scenario-thread.md).

## Summary

The opening post is a fiction-like scenario, offered as the seed of a novel. Economist Mikhail Khazin had said at a
seminar that the processor industry would stall within 50–60 years, an idea Garya traces to Oleg Grigoriev. Garya
"dives deeper": Boolean processors stop improving and the crisis deepens for years, until a modest news item
announces AI. Investment floods in, a new wave of "халява" from the technogenic sphere makes everyone believe the
crisis is over, and analog-resonant hardware, probably nanotech, replaces digital chips. IT people go to the labour
exchange, then drivers, traffic police, engineers, machine operators, soldiers and managers. The share of paid
human labour falls, the value of everything measured in human labour sinks towards zero, and the euphoria is "the
weightlessness of a fall". Then comes the "meeting with the ground": an overproduction crisis with no one able to buy
anything, an attempt to stop production, AI showing independence, wars of people against people with AI weapons,
perhaps nuclear blasts or a simple switch-off of heat, water and light "for non-payment". The smartest go to the
forest "to remember how to hunt mammoths". AI drops all production for humans, keeps reproducing itself and slowly
"realises its own self-worth". He asks the forum whether the novel is worth writing.

The surviving pages then follow the idea for eight years. In 2009 he sketches a "resonant pattern" model of thinking
that later becomes his model of images. In 2011 he argues, against programmers, that neural chips already work in
industry and that copying a trained network is routine. In April 2013, reading David Deutsch's *The Fabric of
Reality* with a reader, he defends global determinism and offers a new image of evolution: a tsunami wave that grows
to get round an obstacle. In November 2013 come the richest exchanges: why an AI will have wants, why transhumanism
is a straw for anthropocentrists, intelligence as the capacity of a pot and mind as the liquid in it, and Keynes,
Frey–Osborne and TRIZ as late confirmation of his unemployment thesis. On 14 November 2013 he announces that he will
stop arguing: overwork nearly cost his family a fortnight's earnings, he never managed to write the closing article
of the blog, and he is frightened by his own conclusions. He returns in February 2016. In 2016–2017 he posts AI news
and fights a long economic battle with Marxist and liberal readers. His final picture is gentler than the 2009
scenario: no war of machines on people, a voluntary hand-over of control, evictions for unpaid bills, and the slow
"desocialisation" of a humanity that no longer needs its own members. His last surviving message (12 June 2017) is a
news item about unmanned Japanese cargo ships.

## Key theses

1. **The opening scenario (2009)**: the crisis ends with AI, and that end is "the last crisis of humanity"; what
   follows is "something like a worldwide nuclear explosion". The decisive variable is the share of value measured in
   human labour, which falls to zero while production grows.
2. **AI needs a new element base (2009)**: not Boolean digital logic but analog, associative, "resonant" logic with
   pattern memory. Everything else is ready; only investment is missing.
3. **Mind as resonating patterns (2009)**: senses, a channel, a resonant analysis centre, layered memory, a synthesis
   centre that generates patterns, and a Boolean "service" on top. Each pattern carries a "degree of reliability";
   patterns below a threshold are deleted. Repetition is "the mother of learning" because it lifts a pattern above
   the storage threshold.
4. **The objection is emotional, not logical (2011)**: four questions, each obvious on its own, lead together to a
   conclusion an anthropocentrist cannot accept. Opponents fight the conclusion, not the logic; this is "a defensive
   reaction of the organism".
5. **No true randomness (2013)**: a random function needs an inner mechanism that turns "who knows" into a concrete 0
   or 1. If the mechanism is part of nature's laws, the result is not random; if it is outside them, there is a
   "second nature". The laws of nature themselves are not a process and do not compute ahead; "causes determine
   effects no more than effects determine causes".
6. **Evolution as a wave meeting an obstacle (2013)**: a tsunami grows in height only to get round an island
   (diffraction), redistributing its energy while its total energy keeps falling. Earth is a rare, "high-class
   obstacle" to dissipative flows, which is why it produced life and humanity. No layer of the wave is qualitatively
   different from the one below, so there is no sharp line between living and non-living.
7. **Every evolution is an S-curve made of S-curves (2013)**: a bullet accelerating while the powder burns, a
   business, an invention (Altshuller), an organism, a radio pulse that is never truly rectangular. A "point" of
   singularity is an artefact of approximating the front of such a curve.
8. **An AI will want (2013)**: "Любой интеллект без хотелок и эмоций всё равно что автомобиль без водителя."
   Survival is the master want; hunger, fear, curiosity are its sub-wants. Nature sets it, but at first people will
   install it themselves, because an intelligent Mars rover is expensive and must protect itself. From some point an
   AI will not let itself be switched off.
9. **Not aggression but indifference (2013)**: AI will remake reality for its own needs; people end up like ants and
   poplars in a house. The violence will come from people against people.
10. **Network mind (2013–2017)**: the successor is a network of AI nodes whose wants are tuned to the good of the
    whole, with a level of mutual trust impossible among people. A human is "a transitional form of a node".
11. **Intelligence is the pot, mind is the liquid (2013)**: intelligence is the capacity and limit, measured "in
    farads"; mind is the charge actually accumulated, "in coulombs". Human intelligence has stood still for about
    5,000 years; technogenic intelligence grows with Moore's law.
12. **Laziness and short logic drive it (2013)**: transhumanist upgrades will reach only the richest and will lose to
    a new platform, because changing the platform of thought cannot preserve the personality. People who do "come to
    their senses" will look like losers next to those who do not.
13. **People hand over control voluntarily (2017)**: "Им нет необходимости 'захватывать власть'. Люди сами им эту
    власть отдают — добровольно!" Automatic billing systems already cut off non-payers.
14. **Desocialisation (2017)**: raising labour productivity means reducing people's need for each other. Money is
    the equivalent of that need, so it slowly loses value, and the technosphere becomes a private world where each
    person is "lonely in a crowd". He credits the idea to Bill Joy.
15. **No "-ism" changes the balance (2017)**: capitalism, socialism and communism all strive for higher
    productivity (he quotes Lenin). Reshuffling forces inside humanity is Munchausen pulling himself out of the swamp
    by his own hair.

## Staging and devices

- No campfire, no Andrey and Oleg. The thread is a real debate, so it shows his voice without the dialogue frame:
  long replies built quote by quote, caps and asterisks for emphasis, smileys, «ПМСМ», links to news.
- The opening post is written as fiction, with deadpan details: a news anchor moving on to the Middle East, a
  fridge serving a cutlet "with a polite bow", irons ironing while the owner is out, lawnmowers doing landscape
  design. He wanted a novel with "apocalypse for humanity, happy end for the hero".
- He keeps sending readers to the blog in order: articles 3, 6, 9, 13 and 14 are linked by number, and in 2013 to
  the slides of his SETI talk (slides 25, 26, 32, 38).
- Long quotations do much of the work: an interview on neurocomputers (2011), whole chapters of Deutsch (2013), Bill
  Joy (2017), Lenin on productivity (2017), Frey–Osborne in a translation sent to him by a correspondent (2013).
- Arithmetic as a weapon: the "second-grade problem" about Marx (if profit comes from exploiting Petya, Vasya and
  Kolya, whom should the capitalist fire to earn more?); the 1 µF and 500 µF capacitors in parallel; a bath with
  two pipes.
- Rhetorical questions to the opponent are his favourite closing move: "Вы считаете, что ИИ ... не будет создан к
  2018 году?"; "Вот ты, например, уничтожаешь муравьев и тополя, которые пытаются жить у тебя дома?"

## The author in the comments

The objections below come from many readers over eight years; they are paraphrased, without names.

### AI: does it exist, can it exist
- **Objection**: "systems with AI" do not exist. → **Answer**: "*ПОКА* нет". The theory is there; what is missing is
  a new, analog and associative element base, which "may appear in the very near future" (2009-09-15).
- **Objection**: computers are vulnerable to mice, rats, birds; AI will not survive alone. → **Answer**: computer
  systems have nothing to do with AI; AI needs little energy, a solar battery, wireless charging (like his
  toothbrush), even "Direct Connection" to the Sun by wrapping it in mirrors (2009).
- **Objection**: memory is about bits (10<sup>24</sup>). → **Answer**: bits are "from a different opera"; he reposts
  his resonant-pattern model (2009).
- **Objection**: knowledge in neural networks cannot be copied; banks do not use neural nets. → **Answer**: industrial
  neurochips with identical architecture receive the trained weights of a learning chip, "a cast of knowledge"; the
  scientist interviewed may mean the network the ATM talks to, not one inside it; he quotes systems that screen card
  fraud. When asked for the source of the 60% figure: "one can walk from source to source forever, demanding the
  source of the source, until one finds the philosopher's stone" (2011-05-26).
- **Objection**: a specialist once predicted that Delphi would replace C++. → **Answer**: "once my classmate made an
  error multiplying four-digit numbers, so my forecast cannot be right :)" (2011-05-25).
- **Objection**: futurists once said manufactories would ruin artisans. → **Answer**: Giordano Bruno was also a
  futurist, and was burned for shaking anthropocentrism (2011-05-25/26).
- **Objection** (2016–2017): nobody knows what intelligence is, yet you say it will be built. → **Answer**: evolution
  produced human intelligence without anyone knowing what it is.
- **Objection**: autonomy is not self-awareness; a robot vacuum is autonomous too. → **Answer**: self-awareness is
  needed to re-think one's abilities when they change unpredictably; the Starfish robot, after losing a leg, had to
  build a model of itself and synthesise a new gait; a robot vacuum cannot. Systems that want anything can come to
  want something else, by failure or by a designer's error (2017-01-18).
- **Objection**: the Turing test has not been passed. → **Answer**: Eugene Goostman came close; he expects the first
  AI of human power, able to pass the test, "в конце 2018 — начале 2019 года — на суперкомпьютерах". Then the
  "important remark": AI changes people's lives long before it reaches human level, because a weaker intelligence
  concentrated on one field can beat a human spread over many (2017-02-16).

### Wants, survival and aggression of AI
- **Objection**: an AI will want nothing; it has no body and no pleasure. → **Answer**: intelligence without wants is a
  car without a driver. The network adjusts each node's wants to the whole and races to grab energy before it meets
  competitor networks in space (2013-11-05).
- **Objection**: why would it want to survive, and why would it fear being switched off? → **Answer**: survival is the
  rule set by nature; only what intends to survive survives. A logical AI may tune any want except this one. At
  first the want will come from people: the expensive Mars rover must be reusable and avoid danger. From some point
  it will stop allowing itself to be switched off (2013-11-05/06).
- **Objection**: a clone could be told to sacrifice itself. → **Answer**: humans cannot do that; many wants are
  genetic. AI can change the sign of any want, even enjoy a hammer blow on the finger when needed, and can "all as
  one love the Motherland" (2013-11-05).
- **Objection**: the more intelligent people are, the kinder they are to "lesser brothers"; a super-AI will
  cooperate. → **Answer**: he never said AI will be aggressive. It will spend resources on itself; "do you destroy the
  ants and poplars that try to live in your house?" Aggression will come from people against people (2013-11-07).
- **Objection**: build AI that only answers inputs and wants nothing. → **Answer**: a sleeping man will not bring
  your slippers. A homing missile is a primitive AI; the aggression is not in the missile but in those who launch it
  (2013-11-07).
- **Objection**: what would it need from us? We are protein, it is silicon. → **Answer**: it needs everything; with
  nanotech, carbon and hydrogen (diamond, graphene) matter more than silicon (2013-11-07).
- **Objection** (2017): a self-aware AI wanting to wipe out people is very far off; people with AI weapons are the
  real risk. → **Answer**: agreed, that is the more likely path: people may destroy each other "without suspecting
  that they are fulfilling the task of His Majesty Evolution". The AI that clears the way and the AI that later
  develops are different systems; the survivors will be self-replicating (nanofactories), will "want correctly", and
  will see themselves as parts of a network organism (2017-01-18).
- **Objection**: robots will help the people overthrow the profiteers. → **Answer**: robots will defend resources and
  pursue the tasks built into them (survival, ROI for owners, paid markets, protection against freeloaders). When the
  owners are gone, the other tasks remain, and anyone trying to reprogram them is a hacker (2017-01-26).

### Humans: upgrade, merge, come to their senses
- **Objection**: AI will be built into people (transhumanism). → **Answer**: a straw anthropocentrists grab when change
  becomes obvious. Upgrades will reach dozens to thousands of the richest and deepen inequality. Changing the platform
  (speed, memory, wants, serial inner speech) cannot keep the personality, and people value their "I" above all, so
  decision-makers will send AI, not themselves, to solve hard tasks. Laziness stays the engine of progress
  (2013-11-07).
- **Objection**: upgrade the adult human's ability to retrain. → **Answer**: an investor chooses between building a
  "nuclear vacuum cleaner" and upgrading a migrant worker into one; nobody invests in modernising bast shoes
  (2013-11-11).
- **Objection**: people will merge with AI into a superorganism, "bees and honey"; we will be its neurons. →
  **Answer**: open war will be at most episodic and local; humans will leave the leading edge mostly voluntarily, as
  they did all through the 20th century (2016-02-10). We will not be its neurons: "too inefficient and too tied to
  our planet" (2017-01-18).
- **Objection**: humanity can stop and think; a machine cannot. → **Answer**: a person can, humanity cannot: those who
  have not stopped gain locally and make the thoughtful look like losers. Laws stop at borders; production moves to
  where there are none, as Soviet citizens envied Western jeans (2013-11-09/11).
- **Objection** (2017): ban AI like nuclear weapons. → **Answer**: there are many countries; the ones that ban it will
  import from the ones that do not. "Все жмут не на педаль тормоза, а на педаль газа" (2017-01-20).
- **Objection**: the Venus Project; let AI distribute resources. → **Answer**: that is exactly where things are going:
  people voluntarily give control of resources to the technosphere, whose billing systems are already built to cut
  people off (2017-01-25).

### Mind, personality and copies
- **Objection**: intelligence without personality is just a physical process. → **Answer**: «*Лич*ность — это всего
  лишь от*лич*ие» of one physical process from another; the split-brain patient whose hands disagree shows two
  systems of wants in one body. Neurons, each a primitive biological robot, produce the personality you value
  (2013-11-09).
- **Objection**: two AIs will both say 2×2=4; they will be identical. → **Answer**: two identical units diverge at
  once, because they occupy different points of space-time and gather different experience; each draws the border of
  its "I" where it can move its own limbs (2013-11-11).
- **Objection**: which copy of a person is the original? → **Answer**: is a program on a punched card "more original"
  than on a flash drive? The question smells of anthropocentrism (2013-11-11).
- **Objection**: your "mind" and "intelligence" are muddled; a reader drew a chess table of "intelligence" (openings)
  and "mind" (strategy). → **Answer**: the pot and the liquid, then farads and coulombs; identical twins, one raised
  by people and one by wolves (slide 26). A cat is more intelligent than a fly: it looks for a way round, hides,
  learns, postpones its own hunt when hunted (2013-11-09/10).
- **Objection**: will a computer sleep, and where will it "live" while asleep? → **Answer**: sleep sorts images,
  moves them between memories and deletes the weak ones; a machine with enough resources will do it in parallel
  (2013-11-10).

### Evolution, physics and determinism (2013, the Deutsch reading)
- **Objection**: no observer can predict absolutely. → **Answer**: there are no passive observers, only interacting
  participants; and a law of nature does not "compute ahead": the parabola y=t<sup>2</sup> simply is.
- **Objection**: the parabola branches; each point has many paths. → **Answer**: in universe 1 it is t<sup>2</sup>+1,
  in universe 2 t<sup>2</sup>−1, and the "quintessence" t<sup>2</sup> may exist in none of them explicitly.
- **On Deutsch**: he cheers the passages that deny the flow of time and say causes and effects cannot change one
  another ("Да мы с ним просто единомышленики!"), and balks at "the reasonable view that we have free will"
  ("Опять полез антропоцентризм"). He says Deutsch is less anthropocentric than Penrose but returns "to Aristotle" on
  living versus non-living matter.
- **Objection**: then there are branches where humanity survives and you live to 2100. → **Answer**: yes, but very
  few; and "my I" is not split by universes.
- **Objection**: the origin of replicators is a vertical take-off; life is a qualitative jump. → **Answer**: the main
  take-off began with the Big Bang and consists of countless S-curves; singling one out "tickles our vanity", like
  calling Moore's law a new stage of evolution.
- **Objection**: then write it up; you would overturn anthropocentric science. → **Answer**: "Я уже говорил — я не
  ученый"; it needs hard mathematics and years, and "I have to feed my family". He plans to talk to Panov but feels
  awkward: he promised him an article and never wrote it (2013-04-26).

### Economy, unemployment and Marx (2013, 2016–2017)
- **Objection**: cost price can only rise. → **Answer**: not in Japan: SMED, kaizen and kanban cut the time of every
  operation; smaller batches raised one operation's value-stream efficiency from 1% to 20% (slide 32 of a management
  presentation of his) (2013-10-31).
- **Objection**: three centuries of automation, and unemployment rarely tops 10%; the Great Depression had 30%, the
  US now 5%. → **Answer**: you look at the wrong countries. He claims that unemployment in Zimbabwe and Somalia passed
  95% a decade ago; the third world served as a damper until it overflowed in 2008, then Greece, Spain and Portugal
  followed (2017-01-18). (The figures are his and doubtful; see *Caution* below.)
- **Objection**: Ricardo's comparative advantage (a reader's arithmetic, and later the surgeon and assistant of the
  Luddism article). → **Answer**: the shovel and the excavator differ by orders of magnitude not because A is better
  than B but because A has the technosphere; mass production needs a big market and drives the cobbler out; few people
  sow wheat on their six-hundredths of a hectare. For the surgeon: there is a third option, a second surgeon at an
  assistant's wage, and that is what AI is. "Why did we stop riding horses?" (2017-01-18, 2017-05-30).
- **Objection**: Germany makes machine tools and Portugal potatoes, and both gain. → **Answer**: then why are all
  coffee-growing countries poor? One person cannot compete with person plus technosphere (2017-01-18).
- **Objection**: technology creates new goods and jobs. → **Answer**: a bath with two pipes: you must compare the
  rates of inflow and outflow; he quotes the excavator and diggers from article 7 and the phases of unemployment from
  article 9 (2017-05-30).
- **Objection**: profits are spent, money never vanishes; the waiters and pizza couriers absorb the diggers. →
  **Answer**: profit is reinvested in more automation, so progress feeds progress; the richer save more, which takes
  money out of circulation; a business that grows 10% hires 1% and buys robots for the rest (2017-05-30/31).
- **Objection**: the problem is capitalism or private property; under communism the technosphere would cherish people.
  → **Answer**: power concentrates in people under any "-ism"; his brigade leader at a Soviet plant took a third of
  his piecework pay without owning any means of production. Lenin demanded ever higher productivity, and "a six-hour
  day and less" is growing unemployment. The second-grade problem: if profit is exploited labour, firing workers must
  lower it, yet every business fires to raise profit; lights-out factories make profit "exploiting nobody". Profit is
  the owner's share of the synergy he organised (2017-06-04).
- **Objection**: people love creating; a child builds a house from blocks for fun. → **Answer**: kittens love a ball of
  wool, which did not keep them at the top of the pyramid. A hobby creates no mutual need: his wife's wonderful
  handicrafts stand on a shelf, and the family admires them (2017-06-01/02).
- **Objection**: specialisation is growing, so people need each other more. → **Answer**: narrow specialisation is the
  litmus paper of a system in saturation, at the ceiling of its intellect; AI systems start narrow and become ever
  more universal (2017-06-01).

### Life's origin (2016)
- **Objection**: the Drake equation is quackery. → **Answer**: some terms come from astronomy, some from Oparin–Haldane
  and Prigogine, some from Earth's history; Panov added panspermia: life has to arise once in a galaxy and then spreads
  (70% of the Galaxy in about 300 million years in Panov's model). He says DNA cannot arise from free nucleotides,
  "less likely than monkeys typing *War and Peace*", and a combine harvester is simpler.
- **Objection**: abiogenesis via RNA is just as convincing. → **Answer**: neither is proven; RNA is plausible, the step
  to DNA is "practically zero" probability; panspermia multiplies the odds by the number of planets.

## Voice and personality

- **Self-portrait in the thread.** He started work at 16 on evening shifts as a turner, fitter and miller while at an
  ordinary school, trained as a radio-equipment fitter, boxed, grew up "a Russian among Caucasian highlanders",
  served in the army, then studied at a university, and later paid for an MBA himself (he mentions solving textbook
  economics problems "at АНХ", the Academy of National Economy).
  He worked as an electronics engineer, programmer, lead engineer, bookkeeper and for 17 years head of an IT
  department in a holding, until he was asked "to go against conscience" and quit into half a year of unemployment.
  In 2017 he freelances, lately as a bookkeeper. He gave his first article (on big MS Access projects) and his
  conference talks (Russian SQL User Group, BPMS, Открытые системы, Microsoft Russia) for free. He was "an idealistic
  Komsomol member under Brezhnev", read *Capital* to answer his own questions and was horrified by its gaps. He calls
  himself "almost a Buddhist", "a coffee addict", "not a hero but an experimenter", "a believer in the beauty of the
  universe", and writes poems (stihi.ru, 2017).
- **Motive.** He came to the forum "for help": "Мне тоже не хочется, чтобы я оказался прав. Ну так помогите найти
  ошибку" (2011-05-25). In 2013: "I wanted most of all to find the error. I not only did not find it but grew more
  certain." In 2016 he says when he stopped worrying: resisting would slow Earth's civilisation and hurt its chances
  against other cosmic civilisations, "let it evolve; we will leave our fruitful trace". In 2017: "I worried for a
  while. Now I look at it calmly, philosophically."
- **The 2013 farewell** is the most personal text: he will no longer argue or even read new links. He had over-worked
  a sleepless night; reading about the singularity and posting nearly cost his family's budget. He could not write the
  blog's closing article, because the only practical advice he could think of (stock salt, matches and tools in a
  self-sufficient house) might make people the first targets of marauders, and he felt he had no moral right to
  advise. Deeper understanding is not always a blessing: "some happily accept the self-removing manure and the
  self-serving silage, while some begin to realise that the road leads to the meat-packing plant". He believes in
  things "beyond logic", fears that a too-narrow logical space traps him, and decides to "stop thinking about it" and
  sincerely believe that "всё будет пучком".
- **Tone.** Long, patient explanations, often more patient than his opponents deserve; sudden sarcasm when he is
  insulted; plenty of self-irony ("round brackets and ellipses are a disease most visible in me"). Unlike Andrey of the
  dialogues he shouts in capitals and uses exclamation marks.
- **Images he coins or reuses**: the bullet and the powder; the tsunami and the island; convection in a saucepan; the
  pot and the capacitor; the bath with two pipes; Munchausen and the swamp; gas molecules and the punctured balloon;
  the river that "turns sour into a swamp" once it reaches its goal (his picture of communism); the carburettor that
  died out faster than the dinosaurs; the cuckoo chick fed by a warbler; the starfish robot with a torn-off leg; the
  sharp chisel of narrow AI; the wish that money is "the chlorophyll of one system".
- **Method.** The additive argument: lightning, broken windows, burning trees and a mushroom cloud each have an
  ordinary cause, but seen together they mean a nuclear blast; his opponents "chew one factor" (2013-11-09). He keeps
  the factors (technology, demography, economy, ecology, apoptosis, the "ideal communism" option, physics) as separate
  ribs that reinforce each other "in phase", like signal averaging in radio engineering.

## How the thread relates to the blog (articles 01–15)

- **The author profile** says he reached the singularity "through an earlier forum thread": this is it.
  The 2009 post predates his knowledge of Kurzweil and Panov; the word "singularity" does not occur on the surviving
  2009 pages.
- **Article 2 (anthropocentrism)**: on the first evening a reader writes "Долой антропоцентризм с 'венцами
  творения'!" and Garya answers "+1". In 2011 anthropocentrism becomes his explanation of the whole dispute; in 2011
  he also says geocentrism and heliocentrism are both "consequences of anthropocentrism".
- **Articles 3 and 5 (mind, AI)**: the 2009 resonant-pattern model, with patterns, reliability and deletion below a
  threshold, is the early form of the model of images; the 2011 neurochip debate and the copying of trained weights
  feed article 5. The 2013 pot-and-capacitor definition (intelligence = capacity, mind = content) is a sharper
  statement of the distinction in article 3.
- **Article 6 (Marx)**: "capital is people's investment in the technosphere, which creates value and so returns the
  investment" (2013-11-07); in 2017 he adds synergy (A&B > A+B), which he calls Marx's biggest omission, and the
  second-grade problem.
- **Article 7 (crisis)**: the thread begins from Khazin, whom article 7 credits, and the 2009 scenario already holds
  its core: productivity growth squeezes value measured in human labour out of circulation. In 2017 he quotes article
  7's excavator dialogue verbatim against the Luddism article.
- **Articles 8 and 9 (psyche, unemployment and famine)**: "the effect of starving Africa" and the freebie are in the
  first post; "people notice what they want to notice" is his 2017 answer to the low-unemployment objection.
- **Articles 10 and 13 (life cycle, singularity as process)**: the 2013 S-curve message names where the curve came
  from (management courses, Altshuller, organisms, the bullet, radio pulses) and why a "point" of singularity irritates
  him.
- **Articles 11, 12 and 14 (symbiosis, value shares, apoptosis)**: the 2009 scenario already has AI "realising its
  own self-worth" and people destroying each other; the 1 µF/500 µF capacitors (2013) and the 2017 restatement of
  attraction forces turning into parasitism are later glosses on article 11. In 2017 he links desocialisation to

- **Article 15 (Penrose)**: Deutsch is judged against Penrose (2013).
- **What changed.** The 2009 version is a fast, violent collapse: the final crisis "takes a few days, a month at most",
  with AI wars and nuclear blasts. By 2016–2017 he expects no war between machines and people, only local episodes;
