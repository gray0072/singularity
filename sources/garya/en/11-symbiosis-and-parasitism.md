# 11. On the slowing growth of humanity's numbers, on symbiosis and parasitism — notes

## Summary
Oleg asks why population growth has slowed — was Malthus right? Andrey says Malthus would have been right had another limit not appeared first: the demographic transition. In agrarian societies children pay back their upbringing within 7–10 years and support parents in old age; in technological societies education takes decades, children are a cost until parents near retirement, pensions remove the motive, and childfree choices grow. The deeper cause is not economics (free education only moves the cost elsewhere — conservation laws) but that humanity has exhausted its resource of reasonableness: raising reason to the level progress now requires consumes most of a life, leaving no time to apply it. Intelligence (the ceiling of reason) cannot grow in an individual, cephalisation ended about two million years ago, and memory saturates with age (the Mowgli example); upgrading the biological "platform" would be like bolting chips onto valve electronics, and immortality would stop humanity's renewal. The rival is the technosphere, a younger system with a higher "rate of profit", alive in the way a cuckoo is alive even though others raise its chicks. A graph of mutual "attraction forces" tells the story: at first the technosphere needs humans much more than humans need it; the curves cross at a parity point of maximal "cohesion"; since then humanity's interest keeps rising while the technosphere's falls, so the symbiosis is becoming unstable (crises are its vibrations). When AI reaches human level the technosphere will no longer need people; humanity will try to hold it by force, turning symbiosis into human parasitism on the technosphere — a short phase, possibly never reached. Friendliness of AI is the wrong question: what matters is whose interests it serves; it can ruin humanity simply by taking the resources, which people are already handing over voluntarily through automation.

## Key theses
1. Malthus's food limit was overtaken by another limit: the demographic transition in technologically developed countries.
2. In archaic economies children are a profitable investment; in technological ones they are a decades-long expense, and social guarantees remove the old-age motive.
3. Today the population growth comes from the poorest, hungriest countries; food is no longer the main limiter.
4. The real limiting factor is the contradiction between the level of reasonableness society requires (through education) and people's ability to reproduce themselves.
5. Free education cannot fix it: "free" in one place means "paid" in another (symmetry/conservation laws); investment in education pays back less and less in energy terms.
6. Humanity has exhausted the resource of maximal human reasonableness; growth over the last three centuries came from enlightenment, which has now hit the ceiling of human intelligence.
7. Intelligence is the potential ceiling of reason; it does not grow within an individual, and biological growth of intelligence via cephalisation is exhausted.
8. Life extension helps only marginally because memory saturates with highly significant old images and loses the ability to learn.
9. The biological "platform" is morally obsolete; hybridising it with a non-biological one is energetically wasteful (valves plus VLSI chips).
10. Upgrading brains or achieving immortality would stop humanity's cycle of renewal and hand victory to a still-developing system.
11. The technosphere is a younger dynamic system (about 4 million years after humanity, ~1 million years ago) with a higher "rate of profit"; it is "alive" like a cuckoo that does not raise its own chicks.
12. Human–technosphere relations are a symbiosis driven by mutual "attraction forces"; synergy creates a "super-profit" split unequally between partners.
13. Initially the technosphere's interest in humans was large and humans' interest small; after the parity point of maximal cohesion, human interest keeps rising and the technosphere's falls, so stability declines faster than it grew.
14. When the technosphere no longer needs people for its self-reproduction (AI at human level), symbiosis ends; humanity will try to hold it by force — parasitism — but the technosphere's higher growth rate makes this phase short, and vibrations (crises) may break the union even earlier.
15. Whether superintelligence is "friendly" is irrelevant; what matters is whose interests it serves. It need not fight people — it only needs to take the resources.
16. Automation is the deliberate, voluntary transfer of control over resources to the technosphere, driven by human wants.
17. **Summary of the evening: population growth slows because humanity has reached the limits of its intelligence; humanity and technosphere are in a symbiosis that will soon end; it may turn into a brief phase of human parasitism on the technosphere, which may not even have time to begin.**

## The author in the comments
**Why should the technosphere spare or improve humans?**
- **Objection** (HoBTID): humans became smarter than monkeys yet did not take all their bananas; they protect nature. → **Answer**: only because humans NEED nature to exist; the technosphere does not need it.
- **Objection** (HoBTID): you assume hidden malice on both sides; a friendly technosphere could see options humans cannot and offer a DNA-level upgrade. → **Answer**: why would the technosphere bother? Friendliness is a wrong question: to destroy humanity it need not be hostile, only indifferent — or even sweetly friendly while using the resources mainly for itself. (He refers to "publication No. 12" for the friendliness point, though it is made in this article.) Why bolt a VLSI chip onto a cast-iron valve set rather than switch to chips?
- **Objection** (HoBTID): why can it improve itself but not man? Organic matter is more highly organised. → **Answer**: the IT-developer's rule "easier to rewrite from scratch than to fix someone else's code". A technosphere able to assemble anything from atoms, mix organic and inorganic, run neural or quantum models of reason and vary its own wants has no reason to modify an obsolete design with hard-fixed wants and one replication mode. Like building a walking excavator around a stone axe kept inside it. New replication technologies will erase the organic/inorganic boundary; the evolution of matter will enter a new turn.

**Is the author projecting human vices onto the technosphere?**
- **Objection** (vladK): you, who exposed anthropocentrism, attribute to the technosphere human "walking over heads"; can we even understand what it wants? → **Answer**: what mistake — developing itself instead of the "valve radio"? We cannot understand it, especially since it can modify its own wants and may have a totally different value system. But whatever its values, it will most probably choose maximal development at minimal energy cost — the principle that has held throughout the evolution of matter.

**Late comments (2015, no reply)**: ФортБоян argues abstraction and division of labour let humans keep progressing (Landau-style quote about schoolchildren and relativity), suggests Asimov's "zeroth law" as a built-in want, and that a superior technosphere would leave for space.
