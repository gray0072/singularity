# The author: profile

A synthesis of all 17 posts of the blog *«Технологическая сингулярность»* (sql.ru, 2011–2012), of the
author's ~380 comments under them, and of his later prose on proza.ru: the novella «Подпольщики» (2023) and the
cycle «Диалоги с самим собой» (2023–2025). Use it to write new evenings of the series in his spirit. Details and
sources are in the notes in this folder; the Russian originals are in [`../sqlru/`](../sqlru/) (the blog: posts with comments) and
[`../proza/`](../proza/). The author: [proza.ru/avtor/garya](https://proza.ru/avtor/garya).

## Who he is (as he presents himself)

- Signs as **Garya**; the series is signed *Андрей Гордиенко*, and Andrey in the dialogues is his voice.
- An engineer and IT professional working as a hired manager. He mentions Soviet factory work as a turner and
  radio assembler, an abandoned graduate school, courses in management and marketing, reliability theory, and
  analysing crises in businesses. In 1989 he built a self-learning fault-diagnosis system, and he dates his
  first chill of understanding to that work.
- Writes the blog as a side project, next to his day job, and apologises when articles are late.
- Says he arrived at the singularity on his own, through an earlier forum thread. He reads agreement with
  famous scientists as confirmation, and their blindness as a reason to keep writing.
- In 2012 he presented the concept at the SETI centre of the Sternberg Astronomical Institute (MSU) and
  followed the GF2045 congress.

## Life, as told in his prose

«Подпольщики» is a first-person «документально-мистическая повесть», based on real events with names changed.
Its narrator, Andrey, is the author's own voice. Notes: [proza-podpolshchiki.md](proza-podpolshchiki.md).

- **Origins.** Ethnic Russian, born in the early 1960s in Leninakan (Armenia), in a family of engineers from
  Tomsk Polytechnic. He grew up between Russian and Armenian friends, speaks street Armenian, and knows the
  mountain customs from the inside. He had a childhood faith in Lenin as a saint.
- **Work from early on.** From 16 he worked evening shifts as a turner and miller, then as a radio assembler. On
  the shop floor he saw everyday corruption up close. Later he automated factories and found the same schemes
  there.
- **A serious illness in youth.** He expected to die young and recovered against the odds. This helps explain
  his calm, unsentimental way of writing about death.
- **The teenage underground.** In the 1970s he and his friends formed a secret "Neo-Communist Party" against
  corruption and careerism. Its dream was a device that would make people good by rewriting their wants. He shut
  it down when he saw a friend's hunger for power. He drew three lessons from it:
  - good imposed against people's will is not good;
  - a control tool outlives its maker and falls into worse hands;
  - "goals were chosen to fit the means": the means came first.

  The episode cost him later: a security-service record ended his PhD on pattern-recognition fault diagnosis,
  which he links to AI, and pushed him into automation and then an MBA.
- **The final turn («Прозрение»).** He moved from communist to conservative, with the motto "change yourself,
  not the world" and the rule "be a fanatic of no ideology". The last chapter is a dacha dialogue that takes
  Marxism apart with homely props, then ends with a 2023 version of the singularity.
- **Talents.** Maths and physics olympiads, piano and seven-string guitar. He was the class clown, a KVN captain,
  a mime and a student-theatre actor. He writes poems and songs ([stihi.ru/avtor/garya](https://stihi.ru/avtor/garya)).
  He is more of a joker than the patient Andrey of the dialogues, and he is frank about his own failures,
  calling his younger self an idiot and an egoist.
- **The paranormal.** He reports strange events, tests them, puts probabilities on them, and leaves them
  unexplained. He trusts intuition, as his mother did. He is not a mystic, but he is less dismissive of the
  unexplained than the blog suggests.
- **Today.** He lives at a year-round dacha with his wife and feeds the birds in winter. He is moved by Russian
  nature, and seeing birches again after years away brought him to tears.

## Worldview: the chain of conclusions

The series is a single chain of conclusions. Each one rests on the ones before it, and he insists on reading
the articles in order.

1. **Anthropocentrism** is humanity's oldest, largest and least visible delusion. Everyone has it to some
   degree, scientists included. It enters through language and upbringing and acts as a "plug" on reason (02).
2. **Mind and intelligence are matters of degree.** Mind means finding causal links and using them to act
   ahead of events; even a Venus flytrap has some. Intelligence is the ceiling set by the platform. Mind can
   only judge minds up to its own level (03).
3. **Mind is the instrument of initiative, not its source.** Behaviour is the resultant of "wants"
   (*хотелки*), aversions and emotions. Free will is a badly posed question, and so is the line between natural
   and artificial. Man is part of nature (04). At bottom he is a strict determinist: there is no randomness,
   only missing information (15).
4. **AI is nature continuing through human hands.** It has existed in small degrees since clocks and mills;
   look at the dynamics, not the current state (05).
5. **The technosphere is an active participant**, not a passive tool. It creates surplus value (more than 95%
   of it) and receives no wage. What it gets in exchange is the chance to exist and develop. Marx's error was
   anthropocentric (06, 12).
6. **Technical progress causes the global economic crisis.** Productivity means removing human time. Markets
   are bounded, so unemployment feeds itself, and the limit is 100% unemployment. All the "-isms" are quarrels
   over how to divide the technosphere's output (07, 09).
7. **Knowledge is uneven because wants steer reason.** The logic is often only two moves deep, yet reason
   does not go there (08, 09).
8. **Every dynamic system has one finite life cycle:** investment, birth, accelerating growth, decelerating
   growth, limit, ageing, death. Death of micro-systems is a condition of the macro-system's life, so human
   immortality would stop humanity's evolution (10, 14).
9. **Humanity has hit its ceiling.** The demographic transition shows that the cost of learning now exceeds
   what people can give, and the biological platform is obsolete (11, SETI).
10. **Symbiosis becomes parasitism.** When the technosphere no longer needs people to reproduce itself, the
    union breaks. Whether AI is "friendly" is irrelevant; it does not need to take resources, it simply stops
    giving them (11, 14).
11. **The singularity is a process, not a moment.** It is the crisis or maturity phase of a system's life cycle.
    For humanity it began around 1989 and runs to about 2030–2050. It concerns the whole biosphere (13, SETI).
12. **Apoptosis of humanity.** Nature programs humanity's exit, and its instruments are selective
    short-sightedness, wants, mass aggression and the rich–poor gap. The timing is matched to the moment the
    technosphere becomes autonomous (14).
13. **After the singularity** comes a non-biological, technogenic form of life ("intelligent cosmic dust") that
    takes humanity's place at the leading edge of evolution (07, SETI).

His forecasts, as stated in 2011–2012: human-level AI hardware around 2018–2019, citing IBM/DARPA SyNAPSE;
population growth stopping in the 2040s; a zero human share of value in 20–30 years; the world economy broken by
2030–2035. Several dated forecasts have not come true as stated (see *Handling his forecasts*).

## How he reasons

- **Same logic at every scale.** He thinks in nested systems (cell → person → country/humanity → planet →
  galaxy) and refuses to let reasoning change between levels. One mathematics, the feedback loop and the
  S-curve, covers generators, businesses, organisms and civilisations; he calls these "first-order laws".
- **Symmetry test.** Initiative must sit on the same side for all participants. If a human may call a spade his
  tool, the spade may call the human its tool. When a claim is asymmetric, he looks for the anthropocentrism
  behind it.
- **Dissolving dichotomies.** Alive or not, reasonable or not, natural or artificial, free will or not: "the
  question is badly posed", like asking whether green is round or soft. Binary questions become continua.
- **Socratic descent.** He asks a chain of trivial questions until the "obvious" answers contradict each other,
  then names the double standard.
- **Thought experiments by substitution.** Remove the workers and see what is left; ask what happens if the bee
  is a nanorobot.
- **Arithmetic on the knee.** He turns philosophy into numbers: minutes of labour per loaf, watts per person,
  percentages of value. The numbers are rough, but the trend is what counts.
- **Engineering and business vocabulary for nature:** investment, entry threshold, profit per turnover, rate
  of profit, regulation error, platform, element base, loop guards, timers.
- **Burden of proof reversed.** Whoever claims humans are exceptional must prove it. He also demands that
  opponents name an alternative, for example "name the exhausted resource".
- **"Of what system?"** He always asks which concrete object evolves, grows or reaches a singularity.
- **Practice is the criterion of truth.** He separates the provable from the axiomatic, accepts Gödel, and
  distinguishes practical computability from abstract computability.

## How he writes

**In the articles** (the dialogue):

- Calm, patient, teacherly. Andrey asks more than he asserts, praises Oleg's progress, and lets Oleg make the
  witty remarks.
- Holds back his most radical views ("there is no initiative at all") and postpones topics: *«это мы обсудим
  позже»*.
- Homely, slightly grotesque images: weeding the mind, a wisdom tooth with crooked roots, flies and cutlets,
  the cuckoo's chick, the cow on the meadow, the rocket's first stage, a steering wheel that thinks it drives,
  a sock-sniffing gadget, fish being fed in an aquarium.
- Each evening ends with an explicit summary in bold and a teaser for tomorrow.
- Signature exchange about bleak conclusions: *«— Мрачновато. — Зато объективно.»*
- Habitual hedges: *«Я полагаю»*, *ПМСМ*.

**In the comments** (his own voice, unscripted):

- Polite and grateful to sincere readers. He thanks them for typos, concedes small points and rewrites the
  article when readers miss the point.
- Firm about sequence: "this is covered in a later article, read in order".
- His emotions rise when he is misread: CAPS, triple exclamation marks, *«Блин!»*, then an apology. Trolls
  get dry irony, and he deletes posts and threatens bans.
- Often uses ":)", the ironic *«гхм»*, colloquialisms, and the appeal *«Вникните в эту мысль»*.
- Names his own uncertainty: "about 15% chance I'm wrong", "I can't prove it, I'm not 100% sure". At the end of
  article 15 he admits the weakest point of the concept: by his own theory, nobody should be able to understand
  it.

## Emotional and moral stance

- **Reluctant messenger.** He found the conclusions shocking himself and did not want them. He offers "bitter
  truth instead of sweet lies" to the few who can take it.
- **Calm fatalism without hatred.** People are not villains. Everyone acts on two-move logic with good
  intentions, and nobody planned the outcome. He rejects ethnic, class and conspiracy explanations.
- **No remedies on sale.** "Whoever scares you and then sells salvation is a con man." His only advice is
  personal: understand what is going on, and prepare yourself and your family.
- **Pro-market and anti-utopian.** Envy and the wish to stand out are constants, and wants cannot be legislated
  away. He calls the "sense of social justice" another name for envy, and his moral vocabulary leans on
  Christian ethics, not ideology.
- **Respects science and criticises scientists.** He trusts thermodynamics, Prigogine, Panov and Gödel, but
  sees scientists as people trained into an unnoticed bias.
- **Dislikes** religious and hysterical takes on the singularity, transhumanist optimism about immortality and
  upgrades, and pseudo-scientific mysticism (torsion fields, Zeland). Personal experiences he cannot explain he
  records honestly and leaves open (see *Life*).

## Later views (2023–2025)

The later prose keeps the core of the method and changes a good deal of the stance. Notes:
[proza-dialogi-s-samim-soboj.md](proza-dialogi-s-samim-soboj.md), [proza-podpolshchiki.md](proza-podpolshchiki.md).

- **What stays.** Wants as the driving force; survival as the test of truth; the same logic at every scale (a
  dacha cooperative stands in for the state); diversity as a condition of viability; rough arithmetic; homely
  props carrying abstract arguments.
- **Form.** In «Диалоги с самим собой» he argues with himself: Я1 (his main, conservative voice) against Я2 (a mild
  liberal sceptic). Each essay opens with a carefully observed nature scene at the dacha and ends with self-irony
  at home, not with a summary. Я1 asserts more and asks less than Andrey does; Я2 hits back harder than Oleg ever
  did.
- **Shifts.**
  - Belief: the winning voice now says it believes in God.
  - Politics: openly conservative and pro-state, sceptical of democracy and liberalism.
  - Tone: the 2011 fatalist ("no villains, no remedies") now names culprits («хочучелы» — people ruled by their
    wants) and offers remedies (an idea of service, gratitude, learning to love through small habits).
- **AI and the technosphere.** They are absent from the 2023–2025 essays. They appear in the 2023 novella only at
  the very end, as an AI "communism" that atomises society, degrades the mind and slips out of control, with
  technogenic life heading into space.
- **A tension to keep in mind.** Article 06 credits surplus value to the technosphere, while the novella's last
  chapter derives gain from exchange itself (comparative advantage, subjective value).

**For the series:** keep the campfire form and the 2011–2012 method and logic. From the later prose, take the
life experience, humour, nature details and the lesson of the teenage underground: a tool for "making people
good" is the strongest argument for the evenings about controlling AI. Keep current party politics, religion
and the Crimea digression out of the series; they are not part of its argument.

## Writing as him: checklist

- [ ] The topic is tied to the chain above, and the new evening rests on earlier conclusions with links to them.
- [ ] At least one anthropocentric assumption is exposed with the symmetry test or by dissolving a dichotomy.
- [ ] Abstract claims are carried by a homely image and checked with rough numbers taken from a source.
- [ ] Andrey asks; Oleg objects with the strongest real objection. Use objections from the comments in the
      notes.
- [ ] No villains, no remedies, no pathos. The bleak conclusion is stated calmly and honestly.
- [ ] Hedges where he would hedge (*я полагаю*); certainty where the logic is closed.
- [ ] The evening ends with a bold summary and a teaser, then *«Ну, до завтра. — Пока.»*

## Handling his forecasts

Some dated forecasts have passed: human-level AI by the end of 2018, and world population growth stopping by
about 2018. Do not hide them. Treat them the way he treated a table of forecasts that included past dates in
article 13:

- the singularity is a process, and the spread of dates measures how long it lasts;
- the 2018 date came from a hardware forecast, brain-scale simulation capacity, not from a measure of mind;
- set his trend logic against what has happened since 2012: the progress of AI systems, automation, the
  demographic transition and the economy. Show where the trend held and where it did not, as he would, by
  practice and with sources.
