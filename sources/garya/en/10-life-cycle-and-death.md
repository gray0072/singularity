# 10. On the life cycle of dynamic systems and the meaning of death — notes

## Summary
This is the "theory" evening announced in the comments to article 09: a general model of how any system lives. Andrey defines a dynamic system as literally everything that interacts and changes in time, is localised in space, keeps a conservative inner structure and exchanges "matter" and energy with other systems; there are no non-dynamic systems. Therefore everything is either alive or the remains of something alive; the living/non-living split (protein synthesis as criterion) is anthropocentric, like defining radio as "anything with valves". Using a generic life-cycle graph he walks through the phases: a "plan" with no energy ("in the beginning was the word"), an investment that must exceed an entry threshold and hit a potential "well", birth, avalanche-like growth as a "vortex" that keeps a "profit per turnover", geometric growth set by a "rate of profit", deceleration as the potential is used up, a limit, a possibly very long "ageing" on stored energy, a sharp break of "death", and asymptotic decay that slowly frees space for new systems. "Matter" is itself made of lower-level dynamic systems: the organism lives because its cells die, so death is a necessary condition of life. Hence immortality of individuals (Kurzweil, Danila Medvedev) would stop the evolution of humanity and kill it, and a system "frozen in eternity" loses the war for resources to mortal, developing systems — the technosphere. Evolution is not an absolute: it is the S-shaped part of a concrete system's life cycle, it is finite, and it requires the destruction of other systems. A second graph compares humanity and the technosphere: human population growth has been decelerating since 1989 and is forecast to stop in the 2040s, so humanity may reach its limit within 20–30 years; the technosphere is far from its limit, and its oscillations (Great Depression, global crisis) come from its dependence on humanity.

## Key theses
1. Everything is a dynamic system; non-dynamic systems do not exist. Everything we see is either alive or the remains of something that was alive.
2. "Living vs non-living" is an anthropocentric, form-over-content distinction; the real content is a special kind of dynamics that all systems share.
3. All dynamic systems have one generic, finite life cycle: plan → investment (above an entry threshold, into a potential well) → birth → accelerating (geometric) growth → decelerating growth → limit → ageing → death → decay.
4. Growth is geometric because each cycle of energy throughput leaves a "profit per turnover" proportional to accumulated mass/energy; the "rate of profit" sets the growth rate.
5. Limits come from exhaustion of the potential (market limits for a business, ~10 m blood-lift height for organisms, food supply) and can act gradually or like a wall.
6. Ageing can last much longer than the growth phase (for businesses by orders of magnitude); death can strike at any phase.
7. A dead system's decay slowly releases space and creates new potentials for other systems.
8. Macrosystems consist of microsystems; death of microsystems is a necessary condition for the life and evolution of the macrosystem.
9. Making individuals immortal stops the evolution of humanity and leads to its ageing and death; systems that stop developing are destroyed by developing ones in a "war for resources".
10. Evolution is always the evolution of a concrete object, is finite, and the evolution of one system requires the destruction of others (conservation of energy).
11. Interacting systems distort each other's S-curves with oscillations.
12. Human population growth turned to deceleration in 1989 and should stop in the 2040s: humanity may hit its limit in 20–30 years and enter "ageing"; the technosphere has one forecast — continued accelerating growth.
13. The technosphere's growth oscillates because it still depends on humanity; it can escape either by becoming independent of humanity or by humanity learning to avoid crises and grow demand without limit.
14. **Summary of the evening: all dynamic systems share one type of life cycle; evolution is part of it; every life cycle is finite; death is a necessary condition of life.**

## Terms
| term (ru) | English | meaning as the author uses it |
|---|---|---|
| динамическая система | dynamic system | anything that interacts and changes in time, is localised, keeps a conservative structure and exchanges matter/energy; i.e. everything |
| жизненный цикл | life cycle | the universal curve every dynamic system passes through, from plan to decay |
| "слово" / план | "the word" / plan | pre-birth phase: a pure plan without energy or matter |
| инвестиция | investment | external energy poured into a potential to materialise a structure |
| порог вхождения | entry threshold | minimum investment needed to outweigh destabilising factors |
| потенциал / потенциальная "яма" | potential / potential well | an opportunity in space that, once filled with investment, can sustain a cycle of flows |
| вихрь / воронка | vortex / funnel | self-sustaining cyclic flow of energy and matter that constitutes a living system |
| прибыль с оборота | profit per turnover | share of throughput energy/matter the system keeps each cycle |
| норма прибыли | rate of profit | the exponent of geometric growth; set by the system's "physiology" |
| активная фаза | active phase | birth through ageing; includes growth and ageing |
| фаза старения | ageing phase | after the limit: system persists on stored energy, entropy rises |
| смерть | death | the sharp break when the vortex can no longer hold its structure |
| пассивная фаза / останки | passive phase / remains | post-death decay, giving energy to other systems |
| макросистема / микросистема | macrosystem / microsystem | hierarchy of dynamic systems (organism / cells) |
| эволюция | evolution | the S-shaped growth part of a concrete system's life cycle — never "evolution in general" |
| война за ресурсы | war for resources | interaction in which non-developing systems lose to developing ones |
