# 07. The True Causes of the Global Economic Crisis — notes

## Summary
Andrey claims the real causes of the global crisis lie outside economics, in plain sight but hidden by anthropocentrism. He credits the Russian economist Mikhail Khazin, while saying his own focus is on the evolutionary processes behind the economy. Labour productivity is measured only in human time, because only human time is paid, so raising productivity means cutting human labour. There are three ways to do that: specialisation, which needs ever larger markets (Khazin's cart-maker example), and mechanisation and automation, which replace people with the technosphere. Because markets cannot grow past the edges of the globe, rising productivity must end in layoffs. The excavator example shows that the people rehired in new industries must, taken together, earn less than the 100 diggers did, or there would be no saving. Unemployment therefore grows, only partly offset by new demand: the rich save more, and needs saturate while technical progress accelerates. Unemployment cuts demand, which causes more layoffs, and this self-feeding avalanche is the global crisis. It will end only when technical progress stops (it won't) or at 100% unemployment. AI will push out even scientists, artists and CEOs (SyNAPSE: human-level AI by end of 2018). Investors will hand control to AI and use combat robots against other people, and the robots win a war fought between humans. The technosphere is a new technogenic form of life, and humans will share the fate of the dinosaurs. Lehman Brothers was only a tiny push that tipped a system already in unstable equilibrium.

## Key theses
1. Productivity counts only human time, because only human time is paid. Raising productivity means removing human labour.
2. Specialisation needs a growing market. Mechanisation and automation replace people with the technosphere.
3. Markets are bounded by the globe, so rising productivity inevitably produces layoffs.
4. Hiring in new sectors never balances the layoffs. If every displaced worker were re-employed at no lower pay, there would be no economic saving at all.
5. Compensating demand is only partial. The rich save, and needs saturate while technical progress accelerates.
6. Unemployment, then falling demand, then more layoffs: a self-feeding avalanche, which is the current global crisis.
7. The crisis lasts as long as technical progress does, and its limit is 100% unemployment.
8. Intellectual and creative work is not safe. AI accelerates while human intellect has stopped evolving (human-level AI forecast for end of 2018).
9. Wars will be fought between people using robots, and the robots, that is the technosphere, win. People voluntarily hand over control of resources because the technosphere manages them better.
10. All "-isms" are quarrels among slave-owners over how to split the output of a silent slave, the technosphere. None of them gives up technical progress. Under socialism unemployment is just hidden.
11. Lehman Brothers was a trigger, not a cause: a tiny disturbance to a system that had lost stability.
12. Science is done by people with human psyches. Wants steer reason deep into some areas and leave it blind in others (developed in article 08).
13. **The cause of the global economic crisis is technical progress, the embryonic phase of a new technogenic form of life that replaces biological life. The phase ends when the technosphere controls the planet's material and energy resources and can reproduce itself without humans. The crisis ends with 100% unemployment and people leaving the leading edge of evolution.**

## Staging and devices
- Opening: Oleg asks whether the crisis also relates to the singularity. Andrey promises it will become clear "a bit later".
- Andrey positions himself honestly: his view is close to Khazin's but not identical, and the difference is the evolutionary angle.
- Rhetorical trap: "How do you feel about technical progress?" "Positively." "The extinct dinosaurs would hardly share your view." Andrey raises a finger at the key point that only human time is counted.
- Worked examples: the cart maker who makes wheels and then spokes (1, then 6, then 12 villages, with a 10-village market); 10 workers doubling output; 100 diggers at 500 roubles against one excavator (rent 9,000) plus an operator on double pay (1,000), five times cheaper.
- Oleg's objections in order: (a) unemployment from progress is "debatable"; (b) the fired will find jobs making excavators, fuel and so on; (c) engineers earn more than diggers; (d) the newly rich create new demand; (e) "I can't believe scientists and artists can be replaced"; (f) "this is just Terminator"; (g) "everyone knows it's capitalism's contradictions and Lehman Brothers"; (h) "why does nobody see it?"; (i) "modern science couldn't miss something so trivial".
- Andrey answers (f) with the key twist: in films people fight robots, but in his scenario people fight people and the robots win.
- A darkly comic picture of investors: they hand AI full powers and want only to "bathe in luxury"; war by robot while you lie on the sofa with popcorn.
- Oleg's reactions grow from a frown to stunned silence to "Sounds terrible". Andrey: "I don't like it either", but he is trying to be objective.
- Andrey's own origin story: "I was just lucky." A lot of chance information met a mind he had spent years carefully cleaning of anthropocentrism, after he once realised how badly it distorts logic. He did not expect to find an apocalypse.
- Ending: a summary paragraph, "time for bed", and a promise to discuss the human psyche tomorrow.

**Marx again: the spade and the wheel**
- **Objection** (ViPRos/Сахават: Marx didn't err. The spade is "materialised labour", and its maker should get a share while it works) → **Answer**: you are stuck on relations between people. I'm talking about relations between people and non-people. You can take money from someone, but do you know a way to give the technosphere back its share of earnings? Dropping "I don't believe it": just say whether bees create the value of honey.
- He announces a simpler example for later posts: a man makes a digging stick and uses it himself, and the time saved has to come from somewhere. Who gets dividends for the wheel when its inventor died thousands of years ago?
- **Objection** (ViPRos: the saving is the man's merit, and "the stick doesn't care") → **Answer**: the question isn't fairness or morality, and he agrees there's nothing immoral in using a spade. The question is whether the man digs alone or together with the spade. Anthropocentrists see "the great man plus a free attachment", and a "shovel-centrist" would see the reverse. Both are wrong: they interact on equal terms and influence each other. The man gets his foundation pit, and the spade gets to *develop*, into an excavator, an AI robot, and finally something that needs no human for reproduction.
- A long rescue-the-reader comment ("anthropocentrism is a terrible predator that has sunk its claws into your mind, hold on, I'm coming!"). People don't hold the helm of the universe. They move under wants just as an apple falls under gravity, and the apple could equally "believe" it chose to fall. In "inter-action" the falling apple also pulls the Earth a tiny bit. The spade's goal is simply "to be": to take up space, be part of reality, live. By making the stick, man helps it come into the world. Image: the flycatcher (a small songbird) feeding a cuckoo chick, sure it serves only its own ends. The spade *will* develop without man, because man will *delegate* that to the AI-spade, which does it better.
- He invites ViPRos to "imagine you're the highest spade in the universe" and the human a dumb energy supplier, the way "the stupid Sun" feeds "clever people". This is perfectly symmetrical to anthropocentrism.
- **Objection** (ViPRos: what isn't useful to man doesn't interest him; spades exist by man's will, here man is god) → **Answer**: "True, terribly true!" But usefulness is judged only one or two causal steps deep. Threats hide in more winding chains that reason avoids, and even when someone stumbles on them the knowledge is ignored, for psychological reasons covered in the next dialogue.
- To the joke "Garya on Red Square with a banner 'Tolerance for super-spades!'": why demand it when there's already more than enough? Why would a cuckoo chick shout "I demand the flycatcher notice I'm not her chick"?

## Voice and personality
- Builds from accepted textbook definitions (productivity, labour intensity) to a shocking conclusion, one small "agreed?" at a time.
- Uses plain arithmetic as rhetoric (100 × 500 = 50,000 against 10,000) and loves orders of magnitude (Lehman against world GDP), though the numbers here are wrong.
- Presents himself as a reluctant prophet. He takes no pleasure in the conclusion ("I don't like it either"), claims only honesty and objectivity, and attributes his insight to luck plus years of self-cleaning from anthropocentrism.
- Generous to sources he respects (Khazin) and careful to mark where he goes further.
- Satire of the comfortable investor class and of push-button war: sofa, popcorn, "let the robots handle it".
