## 18. The Anatomy of Wants

**Established**
- Wants are setpoints of a control system set by nature; mind is the computing block; emotions are error signals
  and watchdogs; behaviour is the resultant. One scheme from the thermostat to the human; only the number of
  setpoints and the depth of computation differ. The line between "set" and "wants" is drawn by anthropocentrism.
- Mind without wants cannot decide (Damasio's patients).
- Watchdogs: the torment of choice breaks ties (Buridan's ass) and then defends the decision (Brehm 1956);
  conscience is a late watchdog.
- Mind serves wants two moves ahead because it has no initiative of its own (resisting a want is another want;
  Ulysses pact) and because a want's force falls with distance in time (Thaler's apples).
- Wants cannot be legislated away (Prohibition; jeans, a ring through the nose); abundance does not remove them
  (the chaffinch at the feeder; Solnick–Hemenway; Easterlin); the setpoint rises once reached (Schultz's dopamine
  neurons; the hedonic treadmill; the tramp and the five thousand roubles).
- Setpoints were tuned for a world of scarcity (sugar, status); we are their carriers. The technosphere does not
  rewrite wants; it satisfies them, and each satisfied want hands it a little more control.

## 19. The Struggle of Images for Existence

**Established**
- Images are a form of life: they exist (occupy memory), reproduce (pass from head to head, are copied) and evolve
  (mutate, are selected). Memory is scarce, so they compete; a mind is their habitat, its wants are their selection.
  "Alive or not" is badly posed (gene vs image). Symmetry: if you keep images, images keep you.
- Forgetting is a function, not a defect (Ebbinghaus; synaptic pruning), the same rule as apoptosis.
- An image survives by being useful to its carrier as wants judge it, not by being true; truth wins in the long run
  only because it works in practice (caloric and Rumford). Superstitions live while wants shield them from checks.
- Images have a life cycle (born in the correlator, spread, limit, ageing, death) and move to a new platform when the
  old one hits its ceiling: speech → writing (Socrates in the Phaedrus) → print → internet → neural networks.
- Human memory is becoming a secondary carrier: images leave heads as soon as another platform holds them (Google
  effect, GPS). The new platform first feeds on human text (running out 2026–2032; Stack Overflow fell by a quarter),
  degenerates on its own output (model collapse: rare images die first, like rare languages), then gives birth to its
  own images (AlphaGo Zero), which flow back to people (Go players improved after 2016). "Human knowledge" is
  knowledge that lived in humans for a while; only the keeper changes.

## 20. What Consciousness Is

**Established**
- Consciousness is a system's model of itself inside its model of the world (the 2011 definition): one more image
  among images, the heaviest. It comes in degrees: the number of levels and the accuracy of the self-model.
- Ladder, not a switch: mirror test (Gallup 1970; children at 1.5–2 years; elephants, magpies; the cleaner wrasse
  2019, disputed); the Cornell "starfish" robot (2006) rebuilding its body model; a 2024 model predicting itself
  better than an outside model (simple tasks only). Butlin et al. 2023: no current AI is conscious, no obvious
  technical barriers; Chalmers 2023: somewhat unlikely now, possible for successors within a decade.
- Problem of other minds: we grant consciousness to others by behaviour and because they are "made like us";
  "made like me" is not a scientific criterion. The human self-model also invents reasons (Gazzaniga's chicken and
  shovel), so "real vs imitated" is badly posed; the line is drawn by anthropocentrism.
- Hard problem taken seriously (Nagel, Chalmers). Andrey's axiom, marked as such: feeling is how the recognition of an
  image looks from inside the recognising system; one thing seen from two sides. Orch-OR: he would be glad if true,
  still doubts; even special physics would only be a question of platform.

## 25. Freebies for Everyone?

**Established**
- "Free in one place means paid in another" (article 11) applied to basic income: every freebie has an address where it
  is paid for (Rome's grain dole paid by Egypt, Sicily and Africa; Alaska's dividend by oil; the American experiment
  by donors' tech fortunes; Finland by taxes). The experiments are accepted as honest: people barely work less.
- Arithmetic: $1,000 a month for 260 million US adults ≈ $3.1 trillion a year ≈ a tenth of GDP ($30.8T), nearly half
  of federal outlays ($7.0T).
- The circle: the technosphere pays (taxes) so that people can pay it (purchases). Only solvent demand brings profit
  (07); the freebie is the technosphere's cost of keeping a customer while it needs one (the near-zero rates after
  2008 were a disguised freebie). Machines increasingly buy from machines (Furman: IT investment ≈ 4% of GDP gave 92%
  of US growth in H1 2025; disputed because chips are imported); when the main customer is the technosphere itself,
  the human customer and his freebie become optional.
- Basic income as the likely form of the short parasitism phase predicted in article 11: humanity holding on to the
  technosphere's output by law, not by rebellion. Parasitism is a biological word, not a moral one (the mistletoe on
  the birch at the dacha). It holds while voters still control something the technosphere needs.
- A freebie does not calm wants (bread and circuses; the setpoint rises; equal money buys no status) and removes the
  need that held people together ("communism by technical progress", the author's 2023 phrase; the aquarium with a
  feeder and no owner).

## 26. Why People Stopped Having Children

  (1.87 in 2010 → 1.25 in 2024), Hungary (≈ 5% of GDP; 1.23 → 1.63 in 2011–2021 → 1.41 in 2024).
- A child changed from investment (hands at seven, support in old age) to expense; both returns passed to the

- Economists' reasons (opportunity cost, education, housing) are accepted as the mechanism; "of what system?": all are

- UN WPP 2024 data, counted year by year: the largest yearly addition was 93.4 million in 1989 (the author's 1989 in

- Honest limits: the new figures fit the "limit of reasonableness" explanation of article 11 but do not prove it; proof
  ends, conviction begins. Apoptosis goes softly: nobody dies, people are simply not born; no villains, no plan.

## 40. Why Firms Die and Cities Do Not

**Established**
- The answer to Oleg's question of 39 ("the plant is closed and the town stands; how is that?") and to the homework:
  by Oleg's count a mouse (25 g, 600 beats a minute, two and a half years) gets about 0.8 billion heartbeats and an
  elephant (5 t, 30 a minute, 65 years) about a billion; Oleg himself (70 a minute, 62 years old) has spent 2.3
  billion and "by the mouse's reckoning ran out at twenty-seven" ("thirty-five years on credit. — Nobody has asked
  for it back so far"). People and bats are named as exceptions; the rule holds "give or take a factor of two".
- The descent goes through the firewood: kindling burns faster than a log of the same weight because it has more
  surface per kilogram; "the mouse is kindling, the elephant is a log" (Rubner's surface law, 1883). The catch is in
  the numbers: by the skin an elephant should need about 3,500 times more food than a mouse, and it needs about
  10,000 (Kleiber's line: ten thousand times heavier, a thousand times more energy, not five hundred). A gram of
  elephant spends twenty times less than a gram of mouse and lives twenty times more slowly: "one clock run at
  different speeds; an elephant is a mouse played back slowly". The watts per kilogram of 35 are continued (a mouse
  about 8, an elephant under 0.5).
- West, Brown and Enquist: an animal is "not a bag" but a network of pipes whose last pipes (capillaries) are the same
  in a mouse and an elephant ("the same tap; the riser is different"). **Stated as argued about**: whether it is
  exactly three quarters and whether the pipes are the cause.
- Growth stops from inside: income comes through the pipes and lags behind the weight, upkeep does not; where they
  meet, growth ends. Oleg recognises the life-cycle curve of 10. Andrey adds to his own article: in 2011 he named
  ceilings that come from outside (the market, the food, the height to which blood can be lifted); this ceiling grows
  from inside, "the profit per turnover shrinks with every kilogram gained". First phrase to remember: **what grows
  more slowly than its own size**.
- "Of which system?": a town is not its people (half have left or died, new ones came), not its houses and not its
  plant, but what stays while they flow through it, "as water through the cells in the pot" (35) and atoms through a
  body. "A town is what remains when the people change. — And when the plants change." Oleg's town is three hundred
  years old and the plant lasted seventy.
- The Santa Fe count of cities: pipes (petrol stations, cables, roads) grow more slowly than the city (about an
  eighth less per inhabitant at each doubling), a person's own needs (housing, jobs, water) in step with it, and what
  happens between people (wages, patents, research jobs, and equally serious crime and new AIDS cases) faster: about
  15% more per inhabitant at each doubling. The explanation given is pairs (the 435 pairs of 39; sixty people make
  1,770): twice the people, four times the pairs, held back by streets and the hours of the day. What runs in the
  second network is the images of 19: "they breed where heads meet". Bigger animal, slower heart; bigger town, faster
  ("an elephant with a mouse's pulse"); the faster heart belongs to another system, the one that runs over the pipes.
  Second phrase to remember: **what grows faster than its own size**.
- Firms: they grow like animals (West's book, weighed as such), and of more than 25,000 traded North American
  companies half are gone in about ten years whatever the trade; the chance of dying does not depend on age. Andrey
  **does not stretch it**: a firm does not die of wear like an elephant, "it dies like a glass in the kitchen", and
  the animal part is only the growth that stops (10: what has stopped growing waits for its turn). "Died" mostly
  means bought or merged: "not buried but swallowed" (what becomes of the swallowed is deferred to 42). Towns are
  dropped too (the miners' settlement on one pit, "half a firm", one well); Oleg's town had four wells (the junction,
  the college, the market, the river). No town is known to have died of age.
- The mountain is brought in by Oleg, after the middle: Genka told him that the plant's foundry now holds racks of
  servers ("thick walls, its own substation, the river for cooling; a watchman and two engineers"). The lock with 37:
  what was built passes to the next owner at a discount; "the town is everything that was built, taken together, and
  the firms are tenants. — And so are the people." A forum reader's 2016 line is paraphrased without a name: towns
  are not where people live but the habitat of machines. Oleg: "too sharply; a town was built by people, for people.
  — Who goes in at the gates of your foundry now? — Electricity."
- The equation, counted as a column: gain proportional to size gives a geometric progression (13: the rate of profit
  is the exponent), a doubling every forty years (an example); with 15% more per head at each doubling every next
  doubling takes an eighth less (40, 35, 30, 26.5, 23…), and the whole infinite column sums to a little over three
  hundred years ("Achilles and the tortoise"). Panov's epochs of 29 are named as the same kind of column. Nothing real
  flies to infinity (13): before the wall growth stops and turns down, unless an invention moves the wall, and then
  the next one is needed sooner than the last ("a squirrel in a wheel, and the wheel speeds up by itself").
- The forbidden word: Andrey says only that physicists have a word for the point where a formula goes to infinity on
  a finite date and that it is printed in the paper "as an ordinary term, like boiling point"; Oleg, sure that it is

- Who makes the inventions "sooner every time"? In the paper, people. Oleg's doubt (he learned for fifteen years, his

## 41. Cheesecake for the Ears

**Established**
- The answer to Oleg's "What is it for at all?" (40). Andrey brings the seven-string, plays an old waltz and fluffs
  one note. Oleg's question in the terms of the cycle: "evolution the miser" (33, 36) pays pleasure only against
  goods; "I am no fuller and no warmer, and a wolf could have walked up behind me. What was I paid for?" Darwin's
  "most mysterious" is given in one sentence; Pinker's "cheesecake for the ear" (1997) is given fairly, and Andrey
  agrees "by half": the buttons were made for something else, but Pinker "was in no hurry to ask which of them is the
  main one".
- The descent, on the guitar: seven notes of a scale and a stop ("it is like a sneeze that won't come"; "Who told
  you there was one more? — Nobody. It was owed"); the fir-tree song with the last note taken up ("Wrong", though "a
  bear trod on my ear"); the fluff in the unknown waltz that Oleg took for intended (a weak forecast, a cheap miss);
  a new song is noise the first time, hummed "to my shame" the fifth, and "a dripping tap" the five hundredth.
  **Guess nothing: noise; guess everything: a dripping tap; between is the good part.** Oleg: "I sit there and guess,
  all the time, without noticing."
- The lock with 03: mind acts ahead of events; "a hunter throws his spear at the place where the hare will be"; the
  miser should pay for a forecast that came true.
- The measurement: music is paid for in dopamine, "the same coin as for food" (the monkey and the juice of 18 is
  recalled, not retold); there are two payments, one at the favourite place and one before it, in the seconds of
  waiting, "from another drawer of the same till". **Stated precisely**: the gooseflesh itself falls on the place;
  the paying begins earlier. Gooseflesh is the reflex that raised an animal's fur ("Lofty. With the hackles up").
  The pill experiment shows that the dopamine is a cause and not a bystander.
- Oleg's objection from 36 ("there the head pays for a wrong guess caught, here for a right one; a cashier who pays
  for hits and misses alike pays for everything") is answered through the monkey: a forecast that cannot fail earns
  nothing, a miss from which nothing is learned earns nothing; what is paid is **a forecast that has just become
  better**. A joke trips once at the end; a tune trips gently a hundred times and brings home each time ("the
  confectioner of that evening sells one sweet; this one keeps a shop"). The chords are measured (sure and deceived,
  or unsure and given the likeliest); **that the pay is for a forecast getting better is said to be a hypothesis**
  ("I like it, which is not an argument"), as is Huron's reading that we pass the thanks for our own guess on to the
  sound.
- "A training machine: there is no hare, the pay is real, nothing is at risk" (a simulator, as play in 36 is the
  place where mistakes are cheap). **Whether it trains anything but the guessing of music is not claimed**: the
  Mozart effect did not survive repetition. "Pinker keeps his half: a sweet. I only say which button it presses. Not
  a side one, for sounds. The main one."
- The flutes: three times older than bread, and the ivory ones cost "days and days of a man's work in the Ice Age,

## 47. Money as Memory, and What Will Come Instead

**Established**
- The answer to the teaser of 46 ("Bring a banknote tomorrow. We'll read what is written on it"). Oleg brings two:
  today's thousand ("Ticket of the Bank of Russia"; "A ticket to where? — It doesn't say"; it promises nothing and
  "only threatens") and a red Soviet ten found in the second volume of Chekhov ("backed by gold, precious metals
  and other assets of the State Bank"; "Did you ever go to the State Bank for your gold? — They would have called
  a doctor"). The English note promises to pay the bearer and pays "another ten pounds". Why the man with the birch
  took the thousands: "A circle. Everybody takes it because everybody takes it"; the red ten is whole, and the
  circle broke "in the summer of ninety-three".
- The circle where there is no paper: **the neighbour across the fence** (the roof of 43, the ploughed potato patch,
  the pump): "By my count, he owes me a little. By his, I suspect, the other way round... We don't sit down to
  settle." **"So you keep a book. — In the head. Both of us."** The man with the birch is "from the far end. I know
  him by his cap... With that one, money." (Two different neighbours.)
- The textbook story of barter (Smith's butcher, brewer and baker) against the ethnographers (Humphrey, 1985: no
  barter economy has ever been described, let alone money growing out of it). Oleg's counter-example is his own
  life: in 1996 the plant paid three months' wages in enamel saucepans (gearboxes → a combine works → a farm → "by
  the fifth link it came down to saucepans"). Answered with his own words: plants that had never heard of each
  other, middlemen nobody trusted, people who knew money when "there just wasn't any". **"Barter is not what came
  before money. It is what is left when money leaves."**
- **Yap**: stone discs cut on another island and rafted over; a big stone stays where it stood and everybody knows
  whose it is; **the stone that sank** and went on being a family's wealth ("A coin on the sea bottom. And they
  could pay with it? — They did. — Savages"); the German official's black crosses. The symmetry test: Oleg's
  pension "comes as a number and it leaves as a number"; the gold of the Bank of France relabelled in a New York
  vault in 1932 ("A black cross. From the other side"; Friedman's comparison). **Why the neighbour wants paper**:
  "He wants a stone that leans against his own wall. And my numbers are for him a stone on the bottom"; "He trusts
  his own pocket more than somebody else's book"; and paper "says only 'the bearer'" ("And the tax office need not
  know about the birch. — I did not say that").
- **Money is memory**: the stone is a mark, "this man has given and has not yet received"; with a book of
  everything anybody gave to anybody, open to all, the stone is not needed (Kocherlakota's theorem, 1998). **"Money
  is the book of the poor: one line torn out of it, to carry about."** The lock with 43 (the hundred and fifty; the
  prostheses; "a record that you once gave somebody something. I put it down").
- **The book came first**: the tablets of Uruk are accounts (barley, beer, sheep, cloth) and lists of signs, "not
  a verse, not a prayer, not a king"; Kushim, "a man from the supply department"; tens of thousands of people, "the
  book no longer fitted into a head. — And it moved onto clay"; the coin came twenty-seven centuries later: **"The
  ledger is as much older than the coin as the coin is older than you."**
- The second half of the banknote: the red ten's record is whole and "nobody honours it"; the paper holds "as long
  as people need something from one another" (Robinson and the drawer of coins: "O drug!", "go to the bottom", and
  "upon second thoughts I took it away": "One of ours"; with Friday there is still no money, "they see each other
  every day"). **The two conditions, named by Oleg**: "Somebody has to need something from you... And he must not
  be your own. Neither kin nor from your village. A stranger who needs you." **The author's 2017 definition (money
  is the equivalent of people's need for people) is kept as "your first finger"; the second condition is the
  continuation's** ("The second I did not see then: a need between one's own gets along without money"). The
  plan's epigraph: **"So what is money? — A record that somebody needed you. You carry it about because he was not
  your own, and will not remember."**
- **The table in the ash**, filled by Oleg: Robinson alone (nothing), his wife and he ("If she billed me for forty
  years of soup, I would be bankrupt"), the neighbour (a book in two heads), the man with the birch (money), the

  Could the head of the foundry take the takings home? — No. — Then it was a book, not money"; Coase, 1937, "a

- **The machines are brought in by Oleg, after the middle**: "I can see which row you want next, and I'll write it
  myself... Those ones. Copies: closer than sisters, letter for letter. One book for all, and nothing forgotten...

- **Oleg's first objection (the plan's)**: "It is the other way round. They are being given wallets" (the card
