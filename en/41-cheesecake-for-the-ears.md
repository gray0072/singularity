# Cheesecake for the Ears

Andrey sat on the log, turning a peg and listening to a string. Oleg stopped two steps away and counted.

— Seven, — he said. — One too many.

— Seven. My grandfather left a teach-yourself book for the seven-string. At sixteen I went to the shop with my parents' money, and only there found out that guitars come with six strings and with seven. I had the book already. Besides, seven is more than six.

— Sound reasoning.

— When I had more or less learned to play, I was told that hardly anybody plays the seven-string, that it is tuned differently, and that the fellow I wanted to play like had six.

— And?

— My mother said: now you can do what few can. — Andrey ran his thumb across the open strings, and they gave a clean major chord. — It is [tuned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_guitar) so that it sounds before you have learned anything.

— The dream is written down, — said Oleg, sitting down. — Before the phone. An exam in strength of materials, and I have come to it in slippers. Nobody rang.

— The second entry in the expense column.

— Play something.

Andrey played an old waltz, unhurriedly, and fluffed one note in the middle. Oleg listened with the notebook shut on his knee.

— Good, — he said when the last string had died away. — Now explain what that was. — He opened the notebook. — Your miser. Sugar is sweet because it is calories. Sleep is stocktaking. Laughter is a bonus for a mistake found. And this? For three minutes I sat with my mouth open. I am no fuller and no warmer, and a wolf could have walked up behind me. What was I paid for?

— People have been asking since Darwin. He wrote that the love of music is of no use to a man in the daily habits of his life, and so must be ranked among the most mysterious things he is endowed with. And in 1997 a well-known psychologist, [Steven Pinker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Pinker), answered in a thick book on [how the mind works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/How_the_Mind_Works): for nothing. Music, he wrote, is cheesecake for the ear.

— What cake?

— A curd cake. Sugar and fat, the two things the miser taught us to want [while they were scarce](18-anatomy-of-wants.md). In the forest nobody ever met them in one piece. A confectioner put them together and pressed both buttons at once. Music, he said, is the same: an exquisite sweet that tickles buttons made for other work. For speech, for the sounds of the forest, for the sense of one's own step. Take music away from mankind, and the rest of its life would hardly change.

— The musicians must have loved him.

— They have been answering him for thirty years. And I agree with him by half.

— Which half?

— That the buttons were made for something else. But he was in no hurry to ask which of them is the main one. Let's look for it. Listen.

He played seven notes going up, one after another, and stopped. Oleg waited. Andrey looked at the fire.

— Well?

— What?

— Finish it.

— I have played what I wanted.

— One more. The top one. — Oleg shifted on the log. — Don't torment me, it is like a sneeze that won't come.

Andrey played the eighth note. Oleg breathed out.

— Who told you there was one more?

— Nobody. It was owed.

— Can you read music?

— Never could. A bear trod on my ear, they said at school.

— And with that ear you knew which note was coming, knew that it had not come, and minded enough to ask twice. Now this.

He played the first line of the New Year song about the little fir tree, and took the last note up instead of down.

— Wrong, — said Oleg at once, and screwed up his face as if at a lemon.

— Where?

— At the end. It goes down there.

— So the right note was in your head before my finger got to the string.

— It is in everybody's head. It's the fir-tree song.

— And in the waltz? Did you catch anything?

— Somewhere in the middle. — Oleg hesitated. — I thought it was meant.

— It wasn't. But you don't know that waltz. Your forecast there was weak, and the miss cost you little. One more question. A song you hear for the first time: do you like it at once?

— Hardly ever. The first time it is noise. My granddaughter puts hers on in the car, and I can't tell where the tune is and where it is going.

— And the fifth time?

— The fifth time I catch myself humming it. To my shame.

— And the five hundredth?

— I switch stations. Every note known in advance, like a dripping tap.

— So: guess nothing, and it is noise. Guess everything, and it is a dripping tap. And between?

— Between is the good part. — Oleg squinted. — You mean I sit there and guess. All the time, without noticing.

![Scheme: how pleasant a tune is against how much of it the ear guesses in advance. Nothing guessed: noise, like a song heard for the first time. Everything guessed: a dripping tap, like a song heard for the five hundredth time. The hump is in between: almost everything comes true, with a measured dose of deceit. Every hearing moves a song to the right](../images/between-noise-and-a-dripping-tap.svg)

— What did we call mind, in the first autumn?

— Acting ahead of events. [The flytrap](03-mind-and-intelligence.md) that shuts before the fly is gone. The flower that closes before the rain.

— A hunter does not throw his spear at the place where the hare is. He throws it at the place where the hare will be. A head that forecasts better eats more often. What should the miser pay for in such a head?

— For a forecast that came true.

— Remember that. Now the measurement. In 2011, in Montreal, people [were asked](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.2726) to bring the records that give them gooseflesh. Their own, whichever they liked. They lay in a scanner that sees dopamine being released. Dopamine you remember.

— The monkey and the juice. The coin the miser pays in.

— The same coin as for food. At the favourite place it was paid out. That alone answers your "no fuller": the head pays for a turn of a tune from the same till as for dinner. But the interesting thing is when. The same people were put into another scanner, one that keeps time better, and it turned out that there are two payments. One at the place itself. The other before it: in the seconds when the place is on its way and you know it.

— Before?

— And from another drawer of the same till. The first payment is for the waiting.

— That I know without a scanner. — Oleg rubbed his forearm. — There is a place where the choir comes in. And the shiver starts a moment earlier, when it is only about to.

— The gooseflesh itself falls on the place. The paying begins earlier.

![Scheme of the 2011 measurement in Montreal: two payments of dopamine around the favourite place of a record. The first, in the caudate nucleus, rises in the seconds of waiting before the place. The second, in the nucleus accumbens, comes at the place itself, where the gooseflesh is. A scheme, not the data](../images/paid-for-the-waiting.svg)

— And what is gooseflesh, by the way?

— Hair standing on end.

— On whom does it stand to any purpose?

— On a cat, in front of a dog. To look bigger. Or in the cold.

— An old reflex of a furry animal: something important is happening, bristle. The fur has gone, the reflex has stayed. The loftiest thing a man feels at a concert is carried out by the muscles that used to raise his fur.

— Lofty, — said Oleg. — With the hackles up.

— And so that nobody says the dopamine merely happened to be passing: in 2019, in Barcelona, listeners [were given](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1811878116) a pill. One kind raises dopamine, another blocks it, the third is a dummy. With the first the same songs pleased them more, and they offered more of their own money for a record. With the second, less.

— A pill for liking music.

— So it is no figure of speech. The coin is the same.

— Stop. — Oleg leafed back through the notebook. — The evening [about the rat](36-what-the-rat-laughs-at.md). "Mirth: a bonus for a mistake found in one's own picture of the world." Your words. So the head pays for a guess that was wrong. Now it pays for a guess that was right. A cashier who pays for hits and for misses alike pays for everything.

— He pays for neither. Go back to the monkey. Juice it did not expect?

— Paid.

— Juice it has learned to expect for certain?

— Silence. They fall silent at the juice, you said.

— A forecast that cannot fail earns nothing: that is your dripping tap. A miss from which nothing is learned earns nothing either: that is the noise in the car. What is left?

— What is in between. — Oleg thought. — Where I was not sure, and it came out. Or was sure, tripped, and saw at once where.

— Where the forecast has just become better than it was. In the joke about the hunters you were led down one road for a minute and tripped once, at the end. In a tune you are tripped gently a hundred times, and brought home each time. The confectioner of that evening sells one sweet. This one keeps a shop.

— Is that measured, or is that you?

— The chords are measured. In 2019, in Leipzig, they [took](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2019.09.067) seven hundred and forty-five hits from the American charts, eighty thousand chords, and had a program work out for every chord how sure the listener is of what comes next and how much the chord surprises him. Then people listened and rated. The pleasant chords were of two kinds. Either the listener was sure, and was deceived. Or he was not sure at all, and what came was the likeliest thing.

— A trick where I am sure, a present where I am lost.

— A measured dose. People who write songs have always known it: promise, delay, give not quite that, and then give it after all. Why it is so, there are hypotheses. A musicologist from Ohio, [David Huron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Huron), wrote in 2006 that the head pays itself for a forecast that came true, and we pass the thanks on to the sound: the note seems beautiful to us, when it is our own guess that has been paid for. That the pay is for a forecast getting better is a hypothesis too. I like it, which is not an argument.

— So it is a training machine. There is no hare: I forecast, I am paid in real coin, and nothing is at risk.

— A simulator. As [play](36-what-the-rat-laughs-at.md) is the place where mistakes are cheap.

— And does it train? Do I shoot better after a concert?

— That was checked, in its own way. In 1993 students who had listened to a Mozart sonata for ten minutes [folded paper in their heads](https://doi.org/10.1038/365611a0) a little better than after ten minutes of silence. For a quarter of an hour. The newspapers made of it that Mozart makes you clever, and the governor of an American state asked for money to give every newborn a disc of classics. In 2010, in Vienna, about forty repetitions [were added up](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.intell.2010.03.001): next to nothing.

— So it trains nothing.

— It trains the guessing of music. Whether a muscle grown on that machine lifts anything else, nobody has shown. So Pinker keeps his half: a sweet. I only say which button it presses. Not a side one, for sounds. The main one: the forecast came true.

— An old sweet?

— In the caves of the Swabian Jura, in the south of Germany, they [found](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature08169) a flute made from the wing bone of a griffon vulture: five holes, a little over twenty centimetres, thirty-five to forty thousand years old. Bread is twelve thousand at most. Beside it lay pieces of flutes of mammoth ivory. A tusk has no ready pipe in it. A rod of ivory was split lengthwise, both halves were hollowed out, and then they were glued together again so that no air leaked.

— With a stone knife.

— Days and days of a man's work in the Ice Age, on a thing that fills nobody's stomach.

— So somebody was paying even then.

— Remember that too.

— Now me, — said Oleg. — Your Pinker never hauled anything. A sweet is eaten alone. And a song? My grandmother sang my mother to sleep, my mother sang me. At weddings forty people sang at one table, and whoever didn't know the words moved his lips. A march. A choir. ["Yo, heave-ho!"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Song_of_the_Volga_Boatmen): do you think the barge haulers were giving their ears a treat? It is not cake, it is glue. And the miser pays gladly for glue: a pack.

— I am glad of that objection; it is stronger than the cake. And it has been measured. In 2019 the songs of three hundred peoples [were gone through](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aax0868): lullabies, dance songs, healing songs and love songs are found everywhere, and a listener from the other end of the Earth tells a lullaby from a dance by the sound, without the words. In Oxford rowers [were put](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2009.0670) on rowing machines, each alone, and then as a crew, stroke for stroke. The effort was the same. After rowing together they bore pain noticeably longer. At Stanford people who had walked in step or sung in unison [put more](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02253.x) into the common pot afterwards than people who had walked and sung each in his own way.

— There. Glue.

— Now look what the glue is boiled from. What does "heave-ho" give the haulers?

— The moment. All pull at once. Pull out of time and you only wear yourself out.

— And how does each one know the moment?

— From the song. From... — Oleg stopped. — From the beat. He knows when the next one will be.

— He forecasts it. A hundred heads make the same forecast for the same instant, and a hundred backs pull as one. A beat is a forecast made common. Your glue is boiled from my button.

— So we are both right.

— The button is one, the uses are two. One head is paid for guessing. When a hundred heads guess the same thing, a hundred men become one beast, and the miser pays for that as well. Not ours alone, by the way: a cockatoo called Snowball [keeps time](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2009.03.038) to a record with his whole body, and slows down when the record is slowed.

— A parrot in the choir. Fine.

— Now tell me where the glue went. Sing me what the forty sang at that table.

— "Oh frost, frost..." — Oleg faltered. — I remember the first verse.

— And your granddaughter?

— She doesn't know there was such a song. She has her own. Everybody has his own now. Thirty people in a bus, thirty pairs of earphones, thirty songs. Genka says there were three guitars in the class, and now there is not one.

— Why did the haulers sing?

— I told you. To pull together.

— And who hauls barges on the Volga now?

— A tug.

— Who sings on a tug?

— The diesel, — Oleg grunted.

— The song lived while a barge needed a hundred men. When one engine and three pairs of hands were enough, there was nobody left to glue. In 2017 I wrote on the forum: a man who used to need a hundred others to dig a pit now manages with two or three, and dreams of replacing those with an excavator. A till with no cashier is put in a shop, nobody depends on anybody, and everybody is pleased. I called it loneliness in a crowd. The thought is not mine; I read it in [Bill Joy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Why_The_Future_Doesn%27t_Need_Us), and it opened my eyes.

— So the glue is not needed because the hands are not.

— Glue is spent where something has to be held together. In 1906 [John Philip Sousa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Philip_Sousa), the man who wrote America's marches, published an article against the phonograph. He was afraid that people would stop singing at home and that a baby would be put to sleep by a machine.

— And they laughed at him.

— They did. Do you know a house where people sing in the evenings?

Oleg said nothing for a while.

— The road from the choir to the earphones took a hundred and twenty years, and every step of it was a convenience. The gramophone, the radio, the tape recorder on the windowsill, a player in the pocket, a list of songs picked for you alone. Nobody took the song away. We needed each other a little less each time.

— The cake is still there, and the glue has dried.

Oleg took out his phone, turned it over in his hand and put it on his knee.

— I wasn't going to tell you. Genka sent a song for the reunion. About our class: the names, our form mistress's nickname, the bench behind the boiler house. A man's voice with a crack in it, a guitar, just as it was in our eighties. And at the second verse... — He showed his forearm. — Hackles.

— And?

— I rang him: who is singing? Genka laughed. Nobody. He typed three lines of what he wanted into a program, and a minute later there was the song. No singer, no guitar. And I felt as if I had been handed a banknote and it turned out to be drawn.

— But the hackles were real.

— They were. That is the annoying part.

— You brought it in yourself. I kept quiet.

— I did.

— Then the news. In 2009, in the scenario the blog grew out of, I had a line: machines will learn the laws of harmony and compose music better than people. I was answered that they would make good tools, and laboratory mice to try music on, and would hardly write anything themselves. Sixteen years later a band called [The Velvet Sundown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Velvet_Sundown) gathered more than a million listeners a month within a few weeks. Faces, a biography, two albums. Then it turned out that there was no band: the songs were made with a program, and its page said so afterwards.

— And "better than people"?

— "Better" nobody has measured, and I won't claim it. Something else has been. The French service Deezer counts how much of what is delivered to it every day is made by a machine alone. In January 2025, one track in ten. By last summer, [more than half](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2026/07/ai-music-exceeds-50-percent-daily-uploads-deezer/): some ninety thousand a day.

![Chart: the share of tracks made wholly by a machine among the tracks delivered to the music service Deezer every day. About 10 percent in January 2025, 18 in April, 28 in September, 34 in November, 44 in April 2026, and more than 50 in June 2026, about ninety thousand tracks a day. By the service's own count they get one to three plays in a hundred](../images/machine-made-uploads.svg)

— Half! And who listens to it all?

— Hardly anybody so far: one to three plays in a hundred. And most of those, the service says, are wound up by other programs, for the royalties.

— Machines sing, machines listen. Your lights-out production, only of songs.

— And here is another count. In the autumn of 2025 nine thousand people in eight countries [were given](https://www.rte.ie/entertainment/2025/1112/1543605-listeners-cant-spot-ai-generated-music-survey-finds) three songs, two made by a machine and one by people, and asked which was which. Ninety-seven in a hundred got it wrong. The survey was ordered by the same service, which sells a detector, so weigh it accordingly. But your forearm gave the same answer.

— So the ear cannot tell.

— Do you remember the last shield, from [the evening about professions](24-last-profession.md)? "Made by a human."

— The live concert, the cup made by hand. It hung on people wanting people.

— Is it in the sound?

— It turns out not. There is nothing in the sound to hang it on.

— Then where is it? What changed in Genka's song between the second verse and his laugh?

— Not a note.

— And what did?

— I found out that nobody... — Oleg looked for the word. — That nobody had taken any trouble. That there was nobody there.

— When I fluffed the note in the waltz, what did you think?

— That your fingers are not what they were. And that you have been at it for fifty years.

— And if the phone had fluffed it?

— I'd have said it was broken.

— Who gets the applause: the man who has run forty-two kilometres in two hours and a bit, or the bus driver who covered them in forty minutes?

— Nobody claps for a bus.

— Your granddaughter draws you a card for your birthday: a crooked house, a sun with whiskers. In the kiosk, for fifty roubles, there is a card with roses in gold. Which one is kept in the sideboard?

— Don't ask foolish questions.

— Then you know where the shield is. Not long ago I wrote, about quite other things, that a value is measured by the price people are ready to pay for it. I was speaking of what a life is given for. It turns out to hold in small change too. What we value in a song is not only the sound. It is the price somebody paid for the sound. Fifty years of fingers. An evening your granddaughter spent with her tongue stuck out. A tusk split and glued by a man who could have gone hunting.

— The flute.

— And your glue is of the same stuff. A mother singing over a cradle cannot be doing anything else: the song is a receipt that she is here and busy with him alone. A choir that sings as one voice has plainly spent many evenings together: a receipt for a pack to be reckoned with. That is how four researchers [read it](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X20000345) in 2020, and in the same journal others [argue](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X20000333) that the glue comes first. Darwin thought a song was a peacock's tail: costly and useless, and it is the cost that is being shown. I took up the guitar at sixteen, and not out of a love of acoustics.

— Did the tail work?

— It was a seven-string tail. Few could appreciate it.

— And Genka's song cost a minute and nothing.

— A receipt that anybody can print is not a receipt.

— A drawn banknote. I said so.

— You did.

— Wait. — Oleg raised a finger. — You have slipped something past me. The hackles came before I knew anything. So the sound works with no price at all. Your "price" is a label. Stick on "handmade" and I am touched; stick on "machine" and I spit. Tomorrow they will stop sticking labels, or lie, and that is the end of it. And another thing. Where did the program get that crack in the voice? From ten thousand singers who paid in full. So the price was paid. Only not by Genka.

— Both are fair. The second first. The program sings on what people have laid up in forty thousand years, as it [speaks](21-machines-that-talk.md) on what they have written. The price was paid once, by others, and now it is copied for nothing. That is exactly why its song is no receipt. A receipt is for what was paid now, by this one, for you.

— And the first?

— The first I accept whole. There are two payments. They have always come in one envelope, and we took them for one. The first is for the sound: the forecast and the measured deceit. It works whoever made the sound, and asks no questions. The second is for the price, and it works only when you know who paid. The first the machine already makes no worse than we do, and it will make it better: it can measure the dose for one particular ear. You called a joke our own [hacking of the reward](22-who-sets-the-tasks.md). Here the button is the same, and now it is not a confectioner who holds it.

— And the second?

— The second it cannot make, and not for want of skill. It has nothing to pay with. A minute of its time costs us nothing.

— And costs it?

— I don't know what will be dear to it. There is already a kind of money whose whole value is that somebody provably burnt electricity for it. But money is a separate evening.

— Don't start. So the shield stands. A price cannot be forged.

— It stands on two legs, and both are ours. The first: one has to know who paid. Your labels. The second: it has to matter to you that a person spent himself on you. That is, you have to need that person. And that need we have just followed from a table of forty to thirty pairs of earphones.

— And the third leg I remember myself. [The musician's listener](24-last-profession.md) has to earn.

— I am not saying it will dry up altogether. The halls are full, and people still pay to watch people play chess. I am only saying what the shield is made of.

— Of the same glue.

— Of the same need.

Oleg picked up the phone and looked at it as at a stranger.

— And this one. Does it hear anything? They say music is the soul, and a machine has none, so it will never write the real thing.

— What did we do with sleep and with laughter?

— Found the task and our way of doing it. And saw that we had named the task after the way.

— In training it does one thing: guesses what comes next and is corrected at every miss, with notes as [with words](21-machines-that-talk.md). The task "forecast" it has in a pure form, and with no bonus: there is nobody to bribe. Whether anything is felt there is [my axiom](20-what-consciousness-is.md), not a proof. And whether it will have something in place of a song, a beat to act as one, a receipt for a price, depends on whether it has a pack and whether it has anything to pay with. I don't know.

— So what we called the soul of music...

— Turned out to be a button and a receipt.

— And I had hackles.

— The hackles were honest.

Oleg opened the last page.

— Came with a guitar. Came out at Genka's program, and brought it in myself. Again.

He put a ninth tick beside the eight.

— Let's finish for today by fixing the result, — said Andrey.

1. **Music presses not a side button of the mind but the main one: the forecast that came true. It is a training machine on which the pay is real and the hare is not. Beauty is predictability with a measured dose of deceit.**
2. **The glue is boiled from the same button: a beat is a forecast made common, by which a hundred heads act as one. Glue is spent while people need each other. The road from the choir to the earphones is the road of that need.**
3. **In a song we value two things that used to come in one envelope: the sound, and the price somebody paid for it. The sound a machine already makes no worse, and for nothing. The price cannot be forged, but it is worth something only while we know who paid and need the one who paid.**

— And what is solid here, and what is yours?

— Solid: music is paid for in the coin of dinner, first for the waiting and then for the place, and the pay can be moved with a pill; the pleasant chord is a measured deceit; the flutes are three times older than bread; people who have kept time together share more. One survey by an interested party: the ninety-seven in a hundred. Argued about: cake, glue, tail or receipt. The scholars have not agreed, and I have taken a piece from each, which ought to put you on your guard. A hypothesis: that the pay is for a forecast getting better. Mine: that the two payments are parting, and that the second stands on our need of each other.

— One thing more, — said Oleg. — That band of yours. Was it a machine alone?

— A man typed the request. As Genka did.

— So there is a man inside. Not thrown out. Swallowed, like the tool room of my plant. — He closed the notebook. — Perhaps that is our road, then: not out but in. My great-nephew says so outright: we shall merge with them, and that is that. Inside, and fed. Not the worst of fates.

— There is a precedent. About two billion years old, and it sits in every cell of yours.

— A precedent again.

— A precedent is not a proof. But this one is worth asking what became of the swallowed.

— Tomorrow?

— Tomorrow.

— Then play something for the road.

— What for? It won't fill your stomach.

— And why do you play?

— You asked.

— Is that the receipt?

— The whole of it.

Andrey played the waltz to the end, with the same fluff in the middle.

— Well, until tomorrow.

— Bye.
