# A Memory of Tomorrow

Oleg came with the notebook under one arm and a parcel in newspaper under the other. Gaika ran ahead, went once round the fire and lay down where the light ended.

— Mushroom soup with barley, — he said instead of a greeting. — Yesterday you asked what would be for supper tomorrow, and I said: whatever she makes. Now I can tell you down to the bay leaf.

— And a day ago?

— Nothing. I tried, honestly, the whole way home. Not even whether it would be hot or cold.

— The dream first.

— My grandmother's wall clock, the one with the weights. I pull the weight up, let go, and it does not come down. It hangs there. And the pendulum goes all the same. Nobody rang.

— The twentieth entry. And the letter?

— Nothing. I look into the box with my eyes now.

— And the parcel?

— The reason the soup was late. She was drying up and dropped my cup, the blue one, I have drunk from it since the plant. She told me to bury it by the fence. I thought you would find a use for it.

He unwrapped the newspaper on the log: seven shards and a handle.

— I shall. Now the question.

— It has been in the notebook since the electron. In the autumn you told me that [tomorrow is already there](17-global-determinism.md), like a page in a printed book. I am no physicist, but a book I can open at either end. Why do I remember in one direction only?

— Where is the soup now?

— In me.

— Only there?

— In the pot, what is left of it. On a plate, if she has not washed it yet. The kitchen smells of it. — Oleg bent his fingers. — And in my head.

— Five places. And tomorrow's supper: in how many places is it now?

— In none.

— Is there nothing in the house that tells of it?

— Well. There is a chicken thawing in the sink.

— So, in one.

— That is not a supper, that is a chicken. She may roast it, she may boil it, she may put it back till Sunday.

— And from the dirty plate, what can you tell?

— Everything. Mushrooms, barley, sour cream.

— About the supper that has been eaten there are five things in the house, and each of them says the same, and exactly. About the one that has not, there is one thing, and it says "roughly". Let's name them. The plate is what we called [a trace](56-observer-who-is-not-there.md). And the chicken?

— A preparation.

— Remember the two words. Did you ever play billiards?

— At the plant's club. The big table, Russian rules.

— I film one stroke: a ball rolls, hits another, both roll apart. I cut off the cue and the last second, where they slow down on the cloth. Then I show you the film. Can you tell whether I am running it forwards or backwards?

Oleg thought.

— No. Two came together, two went apart. Either way it is a stroke.

— And the break?

— The pyramid? One blow, and fifteen balls fly to every side. Backwards, fifteen balls roll in from all over the table, stand up in a triangle and spit out the cue ball. I would laugh.

— At what? Every collision in it is the same as in the first film.

— Every one, yes. But all together they would have had to agree beforehand.

— At how many balls do you begin to laugh?

— At two, no. At three I would frown. — Oleg scratched his chin. — Somewhere around five.

— So the difference is not in the law of the blow.

— It is in the count.

Andrey moved the shards on the newspaper.

— Now your cup. A film of how it fell, run backwards.

— The shards slide together across the floor and jump up, the floor gives them a kick from below, and the cup flies into her hand whole. With the tea towel.

— Which of those things is forbidden?

— Separately, I suppose, none. The floor is warm, so its molecules are shaking. If all of them pushed upwards at the same instant...

— Every one of those pushes is lawful. The equations of mechanics do not tell yesterday from tomorrow: turn every speed round, and what you get is again a motion they allow.

— Then why, in sixty-two years, have I not once seen a cup jump back?

Andrey took a deck of cards out of his jacket, still in its wrapper.

— New, thirty-six cards. From the factory they come by suits, six to ace. Shuffle.

Oleg shuffled, with the ease of a man who has played a great deal of fool.

— Is it in order?

— Of course not.

— Go on shuffling until it is by suits again.

— I shall be here till morning.

— Longer. In how many ways can thirty-six cards lie in a row?

— Thirty-six times thirty-five times thirty-four... — Oleg reached for the notebook and put it down. — You have counted.

— At home. A thirty-seven with forty noughts after it. And from the beginning of the world till this evening, [thirteen point eight billion years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Age_of_the_universe), there have been about four hundred million billion seconds: a four with seventeen noughts.

— So if I had been shuffling once a second since the very beginning...

— You would have gone through one part in a million billion billion of the ways.

— And "by suits" is one way.

— One. And "a mess" is nearly all the rest. You are not shuffling towards a mess. You are shuffling anywhere at all. There is simply almost nothing there except mess.

— And the factory made the one.

— Remember the factory as well. In 1877 [a physicist in Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann%27s_entropy_formula) proposed that heat be counted in just this way: in how many ways the molecules can be laid out so that to us the thing looks the same. A room with the warmth spread evenly: an enormous number of ways. All the warmth gathered in one corner: very few. The count, or rather its logarithm, is what is called entropy, and the second law, that it grows, is your shuffling.

— So it is not a ban. It is bookkeeping.

— Not a ban. In 2002, in Australia, people [watched a tiny bead](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.89.050601) in water, held in a beam of light and dragged along. Over short stretches the bead now and then ran the wrong way: the water gave it a push instead of holding it back. With a deck of two cards you would get "in order" on every second shuffle.

— And a cup has more than two.

— In the cup and the square of floor under it there are more molecules than a one with twenty-five noughts. That deck you will not shuffle back.

![One order and all the rest, a logarithmic scale: the seconds since the beginning of the world, a 4 with 17 noughts, set against the number of ways thirty-six cards can lie in a row, a 37 with 40 noughts; "by suits" is one of those ways; shuffling once a second since the beginning one would have gone through one part in a million billion billion; for two cards the order returns on every second shuffle, which is why a bead in water can run the wrong way for a moment and a cup cannot](../images/one-order-and-all-the-rest.svg)

Gaika got up, crossed to Oleg and lay down again. In the smoothed ash at the edge of the fire she left a print of a paw.

— Who walked there? — asked Andrey.

— She did.

— How do you know that she walked there, and not that she will?

Oleg laughed.

— I am serious. A print of a paw that will step there tomorrow.

— Then nothing has pressed it.

— Could the ash have gathered itself like that?

— It could. Like the deck: one way out of I shall not even count how many.

— So when you see a small piece of order that the place would not make by itself, you say: something met with it. And you add, without noticing: and it met with it on that side. Why on that side?

— Because before the paw the ash was smooth.

— "Before."

— Well, yes, before... — Oleg stopped. — Wait. I am explaining time by the word "before".

— You noticed it yourself. Under the umbrella we had a rule for a suspicious word: [say it the long way](50-what-is-the-it.md). Without "before" and without "after".

— I'll try. The book is printed. On one page there is smooth ash and a dog beside it. On the next the paw is in the ash. On the following ones there is a print, and the dog is over here. — He frowned. — The print is on every page on one side of the page with the paw, and on none on the other.

— And which side is it?

— The side where the ash is more trampled. Where everything is more shuffled.

— Why there? What does a print need?

— Smooth ash. A clean place.

— What is the most valuable thing left in your notebook?

— The empty pages. About thirty.

— A record needs a clean page, and a clean page is a deck by suits: one way out of many. And when the paw pressed, where did the push go?

— Into the ash. Into warmth.

— A print is a small order, bought with a clean place and paid for with heat, and heat is shuffling. In 1961 an engineer at a firm that made computers worked out the least price: to wipe one cell of memory clean costs a quite definite portion of heat, the colder the cheaper. We spoke of him [on the evening about the silence of the universe](29-silence-of-the-universe.md). In 2012 the price [was measured](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature10872), and again on a bead in water.

— So every record is a paid bill.

— And a bill can be paid only towards the end where the shuffling is greater. Towards the other end there is nothing to pay with: things there are in better order than here. That is why prints, shards, dirty plates and recollections all lie on one side of what they are prints of. That side is the one people named the past.

— So it is not "traces remain from the past". It is "the past is the side the traces are from".

— The first is how it is said. The second is what it means.

— And my head?

— A print like any other, and a dear one: [twenty watts](35-pot-on-the-stove.md), day and night. The soup has pressed itself into it. Tomorrow's supper lies on the side from which nothing can press.

![Traces lie on one side, a scheme in the ash: a row of pages from the end where everything is by suits to the end where everything is shuffled, and a page marked "now" in the middle; from the pages on the side of order solid arrows come into "now": a print, shards, a dirty plate, a recollection, each made by the event itself and paid for with heat; towards the shuffled side there is one dashed arrow, a forecast, made by the head out of the same traces and a rule; the side the arrows come from is the one called the past](../images/traces-lie-on-one-side.svg)

Oleg picked up the handle of the cup and turned it in his fingers.

— All right. And why is the cause always there too? She dropped it, so there are shards. Not the other way round.

— In 2013 I wrote a sentence you will not like: causes determine effects no more than effects determine causes.

— That is nonsense.

— From the fall, can the shards be worked out?

— In principle. Your demon from the autumn could.

— And from the shards, the ring in the ears and the warmth in the floor, the fall?

— In principle... yes, that too. — Oleg put the handle down. — That is what an inquiry is. When a ladle came off the crane at our plant, the commission put it together to the second from what was lying about.

— So one end fixes the other in both directions. Then what is different?

— The fall was one. And of traces: the shards, her "oh", the dog jumped, my late supper, this parcel.

— One thing at one end, a crowd at the other, and the whole crowd tells the same story. Throw a stone into a pond: the rings go out to the whole shore. And backwards?

— The whole shore pushes the water at once, the rings run together and throw the stone out. The pyramid again.

— What we call the cause is the end of the link where things are gathered: fewer of them, and in better order. The effect is the end where it has spread. I wrote then that such links show themselves by force of the second law: the law is what begets them.

— And the [two cocks](39-price-of-a-false-alarm.md)? The common cause?

— Have you ever seen it the other way round: two things that go together because of a third which lies after them both?

— A common effect that makes them agree... — Oleg thought. — No. Two streams run into one river, but they did not agree about it.

— In the 1950s a philosopher noticed that the fork always opens one way. And [the vase](17-global-determinism.md) from the autumn still stands: the neck is not the cause of the bottom. Today I only add that on the vase one can tell which end is which. By where the prints lie.

— All the same. — Oleg was not giving up. — Yesterday cannot be changed, and tomorrow can. That is a difference, and I did not invent it.

— What can you do with tomorrow that you cannot do with yesterday?

— Decide. To go fishing or not to go.

— And your decision is one of the causes of the fishing, and has causes of its own. I am not taking your choice away: in the autumn we agreed that it is real, though not free. Listen to how we say it: the past is closed, the future is open. Open for whom?

— For me.

— About which of the two do you have a plate, and about which a chicken?

Oleg was silent for a moment.

— "Open" means that I have no trace.

— In the autumn we said that chance is a lack of information in a local system. The openness of tomorrow is the same lack, under a prouder name. In 2013 a reader and I were going through a physicist's book. He wrote that the reasonable view is that we can affect the future and never the past, and I [answered](../sources/garya/sqlru/2009-09-15-apocalypse-scenario-thread.md): anthropocentrism has crawled out again. A page later he took it back himself, and I wrote: hurrah, common sense has returned.

— And "time flows"?

— At what speed?

— A second per... — Oleg stopped. — Per second. That is not a speed. That is a one.

— In 2011 I [put it like this](../sources/garya/sqlru/14-apoptosis-of-humanity.md): we are drawn in the frames of a script frozen in eternity, and drawn as perceiving the earlier frames, ourselves in them included, and not perceiving the later ones.

— "Earlier", "later".

— The long way: as carrying prints of the frames on the side of order. On every next page the keeper of traces has more of them. From inside, that is what "it flows" is. A property of the one who keeps the records, ascribed to the world.

— [The weed.](02-anthropocentrism.md)

— An old acquaintance.

Oleg threw two branches into the fire and waited for them to catch.

— Good. There are no traces of tomorrow and there cannot be. Then the chicken. You said yourself that one thing in the house tells of tomorrow's supper.

— And what does it tell you?

— Roast, with garlic. She boils on Sundays, and tomorrow is Thursday.

— How do you know?

— Forty years of suppers.

— So what did you make tomorrow's supper out of?

— Out of yesterday's. — Oleg grinned. — Out of traces. And a rule: what comes after what.

— That sentence of 2011 went on: not perceiving the later frames, but able, by following the dependences between what is in the frames, to make certain suppositions about the frames of the future.

— Suppositions.

— When does the sun rise tomorrow?

— A little after four.

— Did you remember that, or foretell it?

— I... — Oleg hesitated. — I cannot find the difference. I know it the way I know yesterday's.

— Because the rule is good. When the rule is good, a forecast cannot be told from a recollection, and nobody says "I predict the sunrise". The difference is felt only where the rule is poor: at supper. [In the very first autumn](03-mind-and-intelligence.md) I told you about the flowers that close before the rain. What does the flower have?

— The damp. The pressure, or whatever it feels.

— A trace of what has been, and a rule written into it by all the flowers that did not close in time. The rain is not there yet, and the flower has already acted. I called that knowing the links between causes and effects and forecasting events with them. Today I would say it more shortly: a mind is a memory turned the other way.

— A memory of tomorrow.

— The only one that nature permits. It is made out of prints of yesterday, so it costs nothing extra. And it is made by the head and not by the event, so it can be wrong.

— Hold on. Memory is for remembering.

— What for? What can you do with yesterday's soup?

— Nothing. It is eaten.

— Then why does the head pay twenty watts to keep it?

— So as to know... — Oleg stopped. — So as to know what the chicken will be.

— [The wants](18-anatomy-of-wants.md) do not pay for a museum. An image that helps to guess [grows heavier, and one that does not is written off](19-struggle-of-images.md). There are people in whom this can be seen. In 1981 a man in Canada [came off a motorcycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_Cochrane) and lost his memory of everything that had ever happened to him. What he knew, he still knew: he could play chess, and he knew the facts of his own life the way one knows them about a stranger. For decades psychologists asked him what he would be doing tomorrow. He could not answer. He described it as the same emptiness as when he was asked about yesterday.

— One man.

— In 2007, in London, five people whose illness had destroyed the same part of the brain were asked not to remember but to [imagine](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0610561104): you are lying on a white beach, tell us what is around you. Taken together, they did it far worse than healthy people. What they had were scraps: a colour, a sound, and no place in which it all stands.

— So yesterday and tomorrow are kept in one cupboard.

— They are made on one machine tool. And yesterday is put together afresh each time as well: remember the [report that is not a reading off an instrument](50-what-is-the-it.md).

— Then my dream with the telephone call was...

— A claim to more than a forecast. A prophetic dream says: I was not put together out of yesterday, tomorrow pressed me. In the spring we found a round date in the calendar, and the dream turned out to be a forecast that had forgotten what it was made of.

— And if I do count ten calls in the notebook?

— Then tomorrow can press, and I go for firewood and rewrite this evening. I do not say "forbidden". A cup is not forbidden to jump back either.

Oleg took out his telephone and looked at the screen.

— Tomorrow, rain from three. And we are past the middle, so I shall say it. I read that the weather is now done by those ones.

— Since 2023. A network [was shown](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.12794) about forty years of records of the weather over the whole Earth, every six hours. It was not given one equation of the air. It was given the page and asked for the next one, and corrected. It learned to give ten days ahead in under a minute, and on nine measures in ten it turned out more exact than the best program that computes the air by the equations, on a machine the size of a hall. In 2025 the European centre whose program that was [put a network of its own on duty](https://www.ecmwf.int/en/about/media-centre/news/2025/ecmwfs-ai-forecasts-become-operational) beside it.

— With no physics at all?

— Honestly: the records it learned from were put in order by the old physical program, and so is the first page of every forecast. But inside it there is nothing except traces and a rule that it found for itself.

— A memory turned the other way. Made of iron.

— And the talking one is the same thing. Yesterday we said how it learned: the end of the text is cut off, guess the next word. All the traces people have left in writing, turned forwards.

— And what does it remember about today?

— Nothing. Its records break off at some day. Ask one with nothing to look at what year it is, and it names the last year it has read a good deal about. It is a letter that does not know when it will be opened.

— My grandmother's clock. The weight hangs still, and the pendulum goes.

— Give it [a stick](58-blind-mans-stick.md), a calendar to look at, and it finds out. Without the stick it has an excellent yesterday and a guess about today.

— And whose memory of tomorrow reaches further?

— What does yours depend on, with the chicken?

— On how many suppers I have seen. And on whether I noticed that boiled is for Sundays.

— On how many traces there are and how good the [correlator](33-use-of-sleep.md) is. Which of us has more traces?

— Not I. — Oleg looked at the telephone in his hand. — It has forty years of every cloud. I have a knee that aches before rain.

— The knee is a good instrument. It knows one yard.

— And about me? — He put the telephone on the log, screen down. — When I asked the bank for a loan for the greenhouse, they answered in a minute. Nobody looked me in the face.

— What did they look at?

— What I had left behind. Thirty years of pay, the bills, the fine for the fence.

— From your traces a program put together your tomorrow: will pay, will not pay. Did you put together your own?

— I thought I would manage somehow.

— Whose memory of your tomorrow was the better one?

— Theirs, I suppose. They gave it. — Oleg was silent. — So the traces are mine, and the memory of my tomorrow is theirs.

— [In the first autumn](03-mind-and-intelligence.md) I measured a mind by how many links between causes and effects it holds and how deep it can follow them. Today it is the same in other words: by how far its memory of tomorrow reaches, and how seldom it is wrong. Whoever remembers tomorrow further acts sooner. The flower has closed, and you are still looking at the sky.

— And is there a ceiling?

— There is, and it is not in the head. [In the autumn](17-global-determinism.md) I said that the weather is not given for more than a week or two, and the network has not moved that edge by much: beyond it the traces of today no longer hold the answer with the exactness that any instrument can take. And one more thing: the one who forecasts is himself in the book. A forecast that has been heard becomes one of the causes.

— The bank forecast that I would pay, and so there is a greenhouse, and so I pay.

— That too.

Oleg opened the notebook at the last page.

— Came with yesterday's soup. Came out at this: my tomorrow is remembered better by those ones. Again.

He put the twenty-seventh tick, and did not close the notebook.

— And now mine. I held it back on purpose. Your count of ways. You say: from "now" towards the shuffled end everything gets more shuffled, because there are more ways. But I take a deck half shuffled and ask the other thing: what was it like a minute ago? By the same count, more shuffled as well. There are more ways there too. The count does not know which way I am asking.

— That is the best objection there is, and it is from [1876](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loschmidt%27s_paradox): it was made to that same Viennese by his own colleague. The count alone gives no direction. By the count alone, your half-shuffled deck most probably came out of a mess, and so did the soup in your head.

— Then where does the direction come from?

— From one fact added to the count: at one end the deck lay by suits.

— The factory.

— Yes. The physicists say it plainly: to the laws one must add the supposition that at one end of the world there was far more order. In 2000 it was given a name, [the past hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Past_hypothesis).

— How much more order?

— Your acquaintance worked it out. Penrose, in [the very book](15-penrose-counterarguments.md) you once brought me. Of all the ways the world could have begun, ours is one part in ten to the power of ten to the power of a hundred and twenty-three.

— Write it down.

— It cannot be done. The number of noughts in it is itself a one with a hundred and twenty-three noughts. There are fewer particles in the visible world than that.

— And what was in such order there? As far as I have read, there was hot soup, the same everywhere.

— For a gas in a jar, "the same everywhere" is a mess. For matter that pulls itself together it is the other way round: evenly spread is order, and lumps are the mess. The earliest light that reaches us is [even to a hundred-thousandth part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmic_microwave_background). Out of that evenness came the stars, and [the hot Sun in the cold sky](35-pot-on-the-stove.md), which I owed you from the evening of the pot. The whole of our flow is that deck being shuffled.

— So you have moved the riddle. From my kitchen to the beginning of the world. Why did it lie by suits there?

— I have moved it. And there it lies for everybody: nobody knows. The Viennese tried to do without a factory: the world is a mess for ever, and we live in a rare accidental piece of order. He was answered with his own count: a small piece of order is cheaper than a large one. Then it is immeasurably more probable that not a whole world gathered itself by accident but [one head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_brain), with a recollection of soup that never was.

— My head, and no soup. — Oleg snorted. — And you believe that?

— No. A count that makes a recollection of soup more likely than the soup saws off the branch it sits on: I learned the count itself from records. I take the factory as a fact with no explanation. I have an old answer, from the same 2011: "why" asks for a cause, causes live inside time, on the side of order, and to ask for the cause of the first order is to ask for the page before the first. I cannot prove that this takes the riddle away. It is an axiom, the same one as in the autumn.

— And Hegel? I have it written down from somewhere: "the factor of the existence of time".

— From [the evening about the psyche](08-human-psyche.md). I said then that the unity and struggle of opposites is the cause of every motion in time, and so the factor of the existence of time as such. One sentence, and I have never unfolded it. I cannot unfold it honestly today either, so it stays a sentence. I shall not put a filling into it after the fact.

— Then a tick for me. — Oleg turned to his own page. — "Who laid the deck by suits."

— In pencil?

— In pencil, half. If it is ever answered, one of us will ink it.

— Let's finish for today by fixing the result, — said Andrey.

1. **What tells yesterday from tomorrow is not mechanics but a broken cup. A trace is a small order, bought with a clean place and paid for with heat, so traces lie only on the side where there was more order. That side is what we call the past, and a cause is the more ordered end of a link, not the end that commands.**
2. **"The future is open" and "time flows" are properties of the one who keeps the traces, ascribed to the world. Open means: I have no print of it.**
3. **A mind is a memory turned the other way. A forecast is the only recollection of tomorrow that nature permits: it is put together from prints of yesterday and a rule, so it is free and it can be wrong. Whoever has more traces and a better rule remembers tomorrow further, and today that is no longer us.**

— And what is solid here, and what is yours?

— Solid: the count of the cards; that the second law is a count and not a ban, with the bead as a witness; the price of a clean cell of memory; the man who lost yesterday and tomorrow together, and the five in London, who were not all alike: one of them managed. One correction to "mechanics does not tell": there is a tiny lopsidedness in the laws themselves, found in 1964 and [seen directly in 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.5832). It lives in rare particles and breaks no cups. Accepted by most who write about it, and argued in the details: that memory is tied to the side of order; in 2014 two physicists [showed](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1095) that the argument holds even for a memory that pays no heat at all. Open for everybody: why the deck lay by suits. Mine, that is the author's: that the second law begets cause and effect, said in one paragraph on a forum; the frozen script; Hegel, who stays a sentence. Today's: "a mind is a memory turned the other way". That is a way of saying, not a theorem.

— And what would make you take it back?

— A record of tomorrow that could not have been put together out of yesterday. Your ten calls would do.

— Gloomy.

— But objective. And the soup, I gather, was good.

Oleg wrapped the shards in the newspaper, thought, and left the parcel on the log.

— Bury it yourself. One thing more. That weather network of yours. Does anybody understand how it does it?

— In the sense you mean, nobody.

— And they trust it?

— They check it. Tomorrow comes, and they compare.

— With weather, tomorrow does come. And I read this week that a machine has proved a theorem. What do you compare that with? Who checked it?

— Another machine.

— And who understood it?

— That is for tomorrow.

— Which you remember?

— Roughly. The chicken is in the sink.

— Well, until tomorrow.

— Bye.
