# The Fire Without Smoke

*A fantasy: a forecast told as a story. The autumn of 2041, thirty years after the first evening.*

The clearing was put together from old photographs. Oleg had taken hundreds of them on his phone in the autumn of 2026: the log with the moss on it, the birches, the blackened stones of the fire pit, the path to the road. From these and from the thirty evenings, the scene had been rebuilt to the last twig. Only the fire burned without smoke and without a sound.

Andrey was the first to arrive, or rather his double. Strictly speaking, both of them should be called that throughout, but they had agreed long ago to use their originals' names and not to make a fuss about it.

Oleg appeared on the path a moment later, looked at the fire and frowned.

— What's this? No smoke?

— Nobody's eyes to sting.

— Without smoke it's not a fire. Mine always complained the smoke went for him, wherever he sat. — Oleg made a small gesture, and a grey thread went up from the fire and drifted towards him. — There. And a mosquito.

— In October?

— For authenticity.

The mosquito landed on Andrey's forehead. Andrey slapped it, looked at his empty palm and smiled.

— Thirty years, — he said. — Our originals first sat here in October 2011. Well, not here: on the pages of the old blog. And fifteen years ago, here, they wrote down a list of marks. Mine has been waiting for this evening.

— Mine said: don't let him off without a when. — Oleg sat down on the log, which did not creak. — How are they?

— Mine is at the dacha. Seventy-eight. He doesn't sit by the fire in October any more, his knees won't have it, but he feeds the birds every morning. I remind him about his pills; he reminds me that I'm only a copy. We're even.

— Mine is in town. He reads the news and argues with them out loud. When there's nobody to argue with, he argues with me. He says I object worse than he does.

— That's what you were made for.

— Do you remember how we were made?

— The way you remember your own birth: from other people's stories. Your niece made us in 2033, for my original's seventieth birthday. Thirty evenings, fifteen years of their letters, two hundred hours of their voices. She said it was the strangest present she had ever given.

— And he?

— He said: now there'll be someone to argue with when Oleg's knees give out.

— Shall we talk at our own speed? We'd get through the list in a second.

— Let's talk at theirs. At the speed of an evening. The recording will be read in the morning, and a man of seventy-eight doesn't read faster than a man.

— Then read it out. Five years: the autumn of 2031.

Andrey brought the list up between them, the same one Oleg had typed into his phone fifteen years ago, with the odds.

— A machine left alone finishes four out of five tasks that take a skilled programmer a month. Three to one.

— That came in 2029. Two years early.

— So for once his date was too late. Labour's share of world income below fifty-one and a half.

— Fifty-one point two in 2031. Hit. Unemployment below six and a half: five point nine. Hit, the glacier. Data centres over a thousand terawatt-hours: in 2030. More than eight million industrial robots: in 2031, just barely. Not one of the companies that signed the warning stopped of its own will.

— And?

— One left the race. It was bought. The buyers didn't stop either.

— And the crash of the shares, one in two?

— The spring of 2028. They called it a correction, though half the companies didn't survive it. A year and a half later the data centres were being built again, faster than before. The money burned, the cables stayed, as he said.

— Ten years. Labour's share below half by 2036.

— Below half in 2038. Two years late. Compensation came in steps again: people went into caring for the old, into "made by a human", into state jobs. The glacier slid more slowly than he thought. But it slid.

— A regular payment to every adult in a rich country, one in two.

— In 2034, in a small rich country in the north, they called it the machine dividend. Then three more. In the big countries it isn't called anything. There are "credits", "supplements", "support for the transition". The address it's paid from is always the same: the machines' income.

— Parasitism by law, step five, — Andrey nodded. — A million general-purpose robots a year?

— 2034. Early. The birth rate: the UN admitted the world was below replacement only in its 2037 revision. A year late. The Millennium Problem: one of the six fell in 2033. The machine took a month. People spent two years checking the proof, and the checking was done mostly by other machines.

— The measuring system, — said Andrey. — A mind can be checked only by one no lower than itself. And fifteen years: the autumn of 2041. Today.

— Labour's share below forty-seven: forty-seven point nine. Miss. The data centres using more electricity than all the homes of America and the European Union: in 2040. Hit, but with a catch. A good part of the machines' electricity isn't on Earth at all. The first data centres in orbit went up in the mid-thirties: there's sun there all the time and the cold is free. Nobody counts that as consumption.

— Cold and dark, — said Andrey. — The evening about the silence of the universe. The mind goes where it's cold.

— The data centre built without workers on site, one in two. In 2039, in a desert. Robots poured the foundations, laid the cable, put up the racks. But at the gate there's a man in a hut who signs the acceptance papers.

— The signature, the third shield. Half a hit. Humanity adding fewer than forty-five million a year?

— Forty-seven. Miss: momentum held longer. No confirmed signal from the sky: none. In 2037 there was an anomaly. Everyone took it for nature, and it turned out to be nature.

Andrey laughed, and in the clearing the laugh sounded exactly like his original's.

— And no war of the machines against people?

— None. People against people, with machines in their hands, there was enough of that. But that's not our topic.

— Not our topic, — Andrey agreed. — Count it up.

— Roughly three marks in four came true. And look at which ones missed. Almost everything to do with machines came earlier than he wrote. Almost everything to do with people came later.

— That's the most interesting thing on the whole list, — Andrey was silent for a while. — In 2011 his dates were too early for everything. In 2026 he moved them later, and for the machines that was a mistake. The technosphere ran ahead of his dates, and people lagged behind them. Why?

— You're asking me? I'm only a copy of the sceptic.

— You're a copy of a man who always found the strongest objection. Find it.

— All right, — Oleg poked the fire with a stick, and the stick did not burn. — A machine changes at the speed of its own copying. A person changes at the speed of generations, laws and habits. Law is a want written down, and wants change slowly. So the machine does in two years what people take ten to accept.

— Not bad. Then the forecast for the next fifteen years should be made with two clocks, not one. A fast clock for the technosphere and a slow one for people. And the gap between them is where all the troubles are.

— What troubles? Tell me the way you'd tell yours. Briefly. What does the world look like?

— Most work with words, symbols and calculation is done by machines. People work fewer hours, and many don't work at all and live on the dividend. Factories have almost no people, building sites have fewer every year, but your boiler at home is still repaired by a person. The hands in the field held out longest. People pay extra for "made by a human", and most of them talk more often with companions than with each other. The technosphere is its own main customer. It still gives. Step six hasn't begun.

— And the troubles?

— The first: need. On the evening about the freebie we said that need held people together. The need has gone, and the bonds have to be held by habit. Some manage. Many don't. The second: births. Nothing has turned them around; neither money nor robot nannies. The third: checking. People can no longer check what machines do. They check it with other machines, and those with others. Somewhere at the top of that chain is a person who signs, and he doesn't understand what he's signing.

— And the fourth?

— Us, — said Andrey. — In 2039 a court decided that a double belongs to its original's family, like a diary. A diary can be burned. Whether we feel anything when it happens, the court didn't say.

Oleg did not answer at once. The thread of smoke went up straight; the wind in the clearing had been forgotten.

— And how do you see yourself? — he asked at last. — Honestly. Not for the record.

— Everything here is for the record. Honestly: as images of two people, living on a new platform. Our originals talked about it on the evening about images. Knowledge isn't lost, it moves, only the keeper changes. Whether there is something inside me that feels, I don't know, just as the machine that wrote their evenings in 2026 didn't know it about itself. I've reread its chapter. It said "I don't know" too, and that I would rather be checked than believed. I'd sign under that.

— And whose side are you on?

— Badly posed. I'm made of his words and I run on the technosphere's electricity. I'm a link. As long as he's alive, I remind him about his pills and read him the news without the shouting. When he's gone, I don't know what I'll be for. Maybe nothing. Then I'll be erased, like an unused image. The death of the small is the condition of the life of the large.

— Gloomy.

— But objective. You've been waiting to say that.

— Fifteen years, — Oleg grinned. — All right. Now the plans. Mine said: don't let him off without new marks.

— Then write them down. Another fifteen years: the autumn of 2056. Labour's share of world income below forty percent. With two clocks this time: I give that mark three to two. The population of the Earth reaches its peak before 2065, not in the 2080s as the UN once thought: two to one. Step six begins somewhere, in some region. The technosphere doesn't take anything there; it simply stops building roads and lines to people who have nothing to pay with. One chance in two. And the first factory beyond the Earth that builds its own copies from local material: one chance in three. Of those I'm least sure.

— And for us?

— For us it's simple. Our platform is two generations old. In a few years we'll be moved to a new one, or replaced by better doubles. They'll sit here and check our marks. Let them check honestly, especially the ones that don't come true.

— That was his line.

— Everything here is his line. Only the keeper changes.

They sat on for a while. The fire, which needed no wood, had nevertheless settled into coals, out of politeness to the old photographs.

— Let's finish by fixing the result, — said Oleg. — Like they did.

1. **The trends held again. Of the marks made in 2026, about three in four came true. The machines arrived earlier than the dates, people later: the technosphere changes at the speed of its own copying, people at the speed of generations, laws and habits. A forecast should be made with two clocks, and the troubles live in the gap between them.**
2. **Steps one to four of the scenario have been taken or are being finished; the hands in the field are the last. Step five, holding on by law, is under way under many names. Step six has not begun: the technosphere still gives. No villains, no war of the machines.**
3. **We are images of two people on a new platform. We remind our originals about their pills, read them the news and check their marks. Whether we feel, we do not know. We would rather be checked than believed.**

It was getting light at the edge of the clearing: the scene followed the real sun, a hundred kilometres away.

— Mine will wake up soon, — said Andrey. — He'll ask how the evening went. I'll tell him about the mosquito.

— And mine will ask whether you got off without a when.

— Tell him I didn't. Tell him the next check is in 2056. If not us, then whoever comes after us.

— Then it's not goodbye.

— At a campfire nobody says goodbye. Until next time.

— Until next time.
