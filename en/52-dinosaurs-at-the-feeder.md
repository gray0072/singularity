# Dinosaurs at the Feeder

Oleg came before dark, as he had been told, with the dog and the notebook. Andrey stood under the apple tree and was pouring sunflower seeds into a feeder cut out of a milk carton.

— The dream first. A museum. A skeleton as long as our assembly shop, and a label under it: "Do not feed." The attendant asks for my pass, I hand it over, and the photograph in it is the skeleton's. Nobody rang.

— The thirteenth entry in the expense column.

— Now show me. You promised dinosaurs.

— Do you know whom I feed all winter? Dinosaurs. In summer they come out of habit. Sit down, and keep Gaika by you.

A bird with a yellow belly and a black tie dropped onto the edge of the carton, took a seed, flew up to a branch, pinned the seed under its toes and began to hammer.

— That is a tit, — said Oleg.

— A great tit. Seventeen grams. And what is a dinosaur?

— A lizard the size of a house. Scales, teeth, cold blood. Died out sixty-odd million years ago.

— Is a whale a fish?

— A beast. It feeds its young on milk.

— It looks like a fish.

— One does not go by the looks.

— What did you go by, in the control department, when you had to say whose part it was?

— By the route sheet. Which batch, which melt. Paint a casting any colour you like, the number of the melt is stamped on it.

— So by where it comes from. Then by where she comes from, this bird sits inside the family tree of the dinosaurs. Not next to it. Inside, on the branch of the two-legged hunters, the one that carries the tyrannosaur. She is closer kin to the tyrannosaur than the tyrannosaur is to the three-horned one in your granddaughter's book.

— And who has seen the route sheet?

— It is stamped on her. There was a chicken in yesterday's freezer?

— There was everything in yesterday's freezer.

— The forked bone from the breast, the one two people pull for a wish.

— The wishbone. My granddaughter and I pulled it. She won.

— For a hundred years that bone stood between the birds and the dinosaurs. In the 1860s an Englishman said that birds come from them: in Bavaria a creature had just been found in a slab of stone, [the size of a magpie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeopteryx), in feathers, and with teeth, with clawed fingers on the wing and a long bony tail. Then it was decided that it could not be so. The dinosaurs, it was thought, had no collarbones, a wishbone is two collarbones grown together, and what is lost does not come back. Then people looked more carefully. The two-legged hunters have a wishbone. The tyrannosaur has one.

— One bone.

— Hollow bones. Three toes forward: compare her tracks in the snow with the tracks in the museum. The scales on her legs, she has kept those. And the nest. In 1923 an expedition in the Gobi found a small hunter lying on a clutch of eggs. They decided that it had died robbing somebody's nest, and named it accordingly: [the egg thief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oviraptor). Seventy years later an embryo was found in such an egg. The thief's own. And then another skeleton, sitting on a clutch the way a hen sits: the legs folded, the arms spread over the eggs.

— Seventy years as a thief, for sitting on its own eggs. — Oleg shook his head. — For a label like that our department would have been stripped of the bonus.

— And the feathers. In 1996 a farmer in the north-east of China split a slab and found [a dinosaur a metre long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinosauropteryx) with a dark fringe of down along its back and tail. Since then they have been found by the dozen: in down, in real feathers, one with four wings. One was a relative of the tyrannosaur, nine metres long, and fluffy. In the fossil down the grains of pigment have survived, and they have been looked at under a microscope. That first one, of 1996, was ginger, with a striped tail.

— A ginger dinosaur with a striped tail. — Oleg looked at the tit with a certain respect. — But they were the size of a house.

— Some were. They were masters of the land for a hundred and sixty million years, and there were all sorts. This branch, the birds, split off about a hundred and fifty million years ago and lived the rest of that time beside the big ones: small, feathered, in the trees and among their feet.

— And then the stone.

— Then the stone. Sixty-six million years ago, ten kilometres across, into what is now the coast of Mexico. In 1980 a physicist and his son, a geologist, found in a thin layer of clay of exactly that age a metal that is rare on the Earth and common in meteorites, and then the same layer was found all over the world. [The crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicxulub_crater) turned up later, under a kilometre of younger rock: a hundred and eighty kilometres wide.

— And it killed them all.

— The blow killed those who were near. The rest were killed by the dark. Soot and dust shut out the sun for a year or two; the estimates differ. So here is yesterday's question again, only larger. No sun. Not for a day, for a year. No grass, no leaf. Who in your settlement gets through?

— Whoever has a cellar. Potatoes, jars, grain.

— And who does not?

— Whoever eats fresh. — Oleg thought. — And whoever eats a lot. A cow wants a haystack.

— A hunter of eight tonnes wants meat by the hundredweight. The forests died standing, the plant-eaters after them, the hunters after those. On dry land almost nothing heavier than a dog like Gaika came through.

Gaika, hearing her name, thumped her tail on the grass.

— And the birds? They have wings. They could fly away.

— Where to? It was dark everywhere. And most of the birds of that time died as well. Those that lived in the trees went with the trees, every one, and they had been the most numerous birds of their day, many of them with teeth. In 2018 a group of palaeontologists [put it together](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2018.04.062) from two ends. From the pollen and spores in the rock: the forests gone the world over, and ferns in their place. And from the family tree of today's birds: every bird now alive descends from a few that lived on the ground. Small ones, with a beak and no teeth. Two years earlier another group [had asked](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2016.03.039) what a beak with no teeth is good for when nothing grows.

— Seeds, — said Oleg, and looked at the carton.

— A seed is a tin. It lies in the soil for years and does not spoil. The forest is dead, and its pantry is full.

— So who came through? Not the strongest.

— No.

— And not the cleverest.

— The one that needed little and could eat what keeps.

— My grandmother, — said Oleg. — Yesterday's. A stove, a cellar, a sack of grain.

— From those few there are today about eleven thousand kinds of birds. Of mammals there are about six and a half thousand; the figures depend on who does the counting, the order does not. Your granddaughter's book says that the age of the dinosaurs ended and the age of the mammals began. By the number of kinds, the age of the dinosaurs is still going on. We call them birds.

— That one I shall tell at table. — Oleg reached for the pencil.

![A family tree of the dinosaurs with five branches: the three-horned ones, the long-necked ones, the tyrannosaurs, the small feathered hunters and the birds. A vertical line marks the stone of 66 million years ago; four branches end at it and one, the birds, goes on to today. Below: about 11,000 kinds of birds today against about 6,500 kinds of mammals](../images/dinosaurs-still-here.svg)

— And now a bill of my own, — said Andrey. — Yesterday you said: we go the way of the dinosaurs. In 2011, on [the seventh evening](07-global-economic-crisis.md), I told you the same thing in almost the same words: people will share the fate of the dinosaurs, and have to clear the road. And I meant by it what you meant yesterday.

— I remember. I did not like it.

— The sentence I do not take back. But I had not looked up what that fate was. I said "fate" and saw a skeleton in a museum. And it was sitting in my apple tree.

— Convenient. — Oleg squinted. — Sixteen years on, the gloomy prophecy turns out to mean birdsong.

— Wait with the birdsong. Ask what was kept and what was paid. You have a page for that.

Oleg opened the notebook at the page "Where the former ones went".

— Three rows. "A niche: kept everything, paid with the planet." [The ones that cannot stand oxygen.](38-oxygen-was-a-poison.md) "An organelle: kept the job, paid with everything else. While the job is needed." [The swallowed bacterium.](42-swallowed-bacterium.md) "Livestock, a pet: kept the numbers and the feed. Paid with the head and with the say in who is born. While the feeder wants." [The dog, the wheat, the cow](48-who-tamed-whom.md), and [the horse](34-where-the-horses-went.md).

— Write the fourth.

— "A descendant." Kept?

— The line. And look whose. Not one of the big ones has a great-grandchild: the tyrannosaur is nobody's grandfather. The line that goes on is a small side branch that had lived beside them for eighty million years.

— Paid?

— Count it yourself.

— The size. Seventeen grams against eight tonnes. — Oleg bent a finger. — The role. Every beast on land reckoned with them, and she takes seeds out of a milk carton that a mammal hung up.

— And the third.

— The name, — said Oleg slowly. — What did I say when I came? "That is a tit."

— You said it.

— "A descendant: kept the line. Paid with the size, the role and the name." And "while" what?

— Nothing. That row hangs on nobody. She came through because she needed little, and she does not need me either. Take the carton away, and she will live.

— And where were we all that time? While they were the masters.

— Under their feet. The size of a shrew, of a rat, the largest like a badger. We came out at night. Our eyes still pay for [those nights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nocturnal_bottleneck): of four sensors of colour our ancestors lost two, and the apes later got one back. A bird has all four. She sees colours for which you have no words.

— [A patch.](46-flies-apart-from-cutlets.md)

— A patch. So read your first row again. Who sits in a niche?

— The former ones.

— And the next ones. For a hundred and fifty million years the niche was ours.

— Then one can sit it out. — Oleg brightened. — Go into the niche and wait.

— Wait for what? We did not wait. We did not know that there was anything to wait for. A stone fell. The ones in your first row have been waiting two billion four hundred million years. A niche is a ticket to a lottery in which the draw is a stone from the sky.

It was getting dark. The tits had gone, and Andrey lit the fire.

— There is a fifth row. What is burning?

— Birch. From the trailer.

— The sun of the last sixty years. And yesterday's current, the one that Tolik gave back: what burns there?

— Depends on the station. Gas. Coal.

— Over the world about a third of all current is coal, still more than from any other single source. Nearly nine billion tonnes a year, and steel is smelted on it. What is coal?

— An old forest.

— Whose? So much of the best coal is of one age that the age is named after it: [the coal-bearing one](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carboniferous), three hundred million years ago and more. No dinosaur yet. No flower, no grass. The masters of the land were trees that are no kin to ours: trunks of thirty metres with bark in a diamond pattern, like a snake's skin, and horsetails as high as a three-storey house. They stood in swamps, fell into the water and did not rot.

— Why not?

— By one account, nobody had yet learned to eat wood; the fungi came to that later. By another, the swamps were of the right kind and sank fast. It is argued about. What is left is not argued about: seams.

— "Fuel", — Oleg wrote. — Kept?

— Nothing.

— Paid?

— Everything. And there is no "while" in that row either. What has been burned is not asked.

— Cheerful. And descendants? Is there a tit of theirs anywhere?

— What grows in your potato patch that you cannot get rid of?

— Horsetail. The soil is sour. I lime it, and it comes up again. — He stopped. — No.

— The last of the same family. And the club moss under the firs is kin to the thirty-metre ones.

— So the forest got two rows. It burns in the boiler, and it is a weed in my potatoes.

— Remember that: two rows for one master.

— Stop. — Oleg put the pencil down. — Now I shall speak as the control department. The table is too good. Five rows, and in every one somebody is alive, or at least keeps somebody warm. Where is the row of those who simply died? The same book says: of every hundred kinds that ever lived, ninety-nine are no more. You are showing me the batch that passed inspection and telling me there were no rejects. It is [the dream book](39-price-of-a-false-alarm.md) again: the dreams that came true are the ones remembered.

— Fair, and the answer has two parts. Ninety-nine of a hundred, yes. But kinds. A kind lasts a million years, or a few. And in 1973 a biologist [counted on the fossils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Queen_hypothesis) that the chance of a kind dying out does not depend on how long it has already lived.

— Like a glass in the kitchen. — Oleg raised his eyebrows. — [Like a firm.](40-firms-die-cities-do-not.md)

— The same law. The firm dies, the trade goes on. The kind dies, the branch goes on: most of those ninety-nine are somebody's great-grandfathers. So ask it without any stone and without any heir: will the kind called man last for ever?

— No.

— It is three hundred thousand years old. After [the seminar](../sources/garya/sqlru/2012-05-17-seti-talk.md) in 2012 a man told me that mankind is valuable in itself. I asked him whether mankind had been issued an indulgence that the dinosaurs were not given. They were valuable to themselves too.

— And the second part?

— In the second part you are right. Whole branches go, and nobody is left. [The trilobites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trilobite): on the sea bottom for some two hundred and seventy million years, more than twenty thousand kinds dug up, and after the great dying of two hundred and fifty million years ago not one, and no descendant. With the stone went the flying lizards, [the second wing of the four](46-flies-apart-from-cutlets.md), and the sea lizards, and the shells coiled like a ram's horn. I have no rule for who lands in that row. Write it down.

— "A print in stone." Kept: nothing. Paid: everything. And nobody even burns them.

— Under a line. I cannot cross it out, and I shall not pretend. I say only that it is shorter than the school book made it. The school book had put the dinosaurs into it.

Andrey threw two branches into the fire.

— And now read the page not along, but down. The column "paid".

— "The planet, and the say in what the air will be. Everything except the job. The head, and the say in who is born. Everything. The size, the role, the name." — Oleg read it over again, moving his lips. — Leave out the fuel and the print, they paid the lot. In the other four it is one and the same thing. They no longer decide. What the air will be. What to want. Who is born. Who is reckoned with.

— What would you call it?

— At the plant it was called the right of signature. A man who is moved aside keeps his office, his ration and the name on the door. He loses the signature.

— That is what first place is. Said [the long way](50-what-is-the-it.md): the air, the fields and the births are shaped by what one form of life needs, and no longer by what the other needs.

— And the column "kept" has small print. "While the job is needed. While the feeder wants."

— Add to the first row: "while the place is not needed". The ones in the silt were lucky. Nobody needs the silt.

— Then three rows hang on the new master's cord. — Oleg counted with the pencil. — He does not go there, he cannot do it himself, he wants it. One he simply burns. And one row hangs on nobody: the tit.

— The tit and the horsetail. The ones that need little.

![Table of the fates of former masters, six rows: a niche (the ones that cannot stand oxygen; kept everything; paid with the planet; while the place is not needed), an organelle (the swallowed bacterium; kept the job; paid with everything else; while the job is needed), livestock and a pet (the dog, the wheat, the horse; kept the numbers and the feed; paid with the head and the say in who is born; while the feeder wants), a descendant (the birds; kept the line; paid with the size, the role and the name; hangs on nobody), fuel (the coal forest; kept nothing; paid everything) and, under a line, a print in stone (the trilobites; nothing kept)](../images/where-the-former-ones-went.svg)

— One more reading. Where is the dinosaur on your page?

— In the fourth row. The tit.

— And yesterday's chicken?

— A dinosaur. — Oleg grunted. — Livestock. The third row.

— The most numerous bird on the planet: over twenty-five billion alive at any moment. Of all the birds on the Earth, by weight, seven tenths are poultry. And your neighbour's parrot?

— A pet. The third again. And the big ones are under the line, a thousand kinds dug up so far. So the dinosaurs sit in three rows at once.

— The bacteria?

— The first: the silt. The second: the swallowed one. And the green ones are masters to this day, in every leaf. The coal forest has two rows. — He looked up. — So a former master is not one thing with one fate.

— Different parts of it go into different rows.

— Then yesterday's sentence of mine is worthless. "The way of the dinosaurs." Which one? There are three, and that is the dinosaurs alone.

— The question is badly posed. "Will mankind survive" has the same flaw as "who exploits whom" and "who tamed whom". The proper one is longer: which part of it, into which row, and on what that row hangs.

— All right. — Oleg drew a line down the right-hand edge of the page. — Since you are sitting with your mouth shut again, I shall do it myself. The column "us". And the new master in it is those ones. I am saying it. You have not said a word.

— I have not.

— The first row. The niche. Grandmother, and [yesterday's billion](51-week-without-a-socket.md) who live by their own muscles. "While the place is not needed." And will it be needed?

— In 2012 I wrote that the new life would remake its surroundings to suit itself more actively than man ever did, and not from malice. That is a forecast. I have no fact.

— The second. The organelle. Tolik with his chainsaw, and the customer, the one who wants. "While the job is needed": your second number of yesterday. Decades, and shrinking.

— Yes.

— The third. Livestock and pets. — He tapped the page with the pencil. — Fed on schedule. Here I can sign for myself: the pension comes on the fifth.

— Livestock is kept for a use, a pet for a want.

— Then I am a pet of the pension fund. There has been no use in me for some years. And "while the feeder wants" is the button, which we left as a question.

— It is still a question.

— Fuel next; the descendant I am keeping for the end. — Oleg frowned. — They do not burn us. They have watts enough. — He was silent for a while. — No. They were raised on something. On everything people have written in five thousand years. There is your seam. Layer upon layer, pressed down, and nobody asked the authors. Most of them are dead.

— In the spring we called it [the move of the drawings](42-swallowed-bacterium.md). It is the same deposit, seen from the boiler room. With one difference: coal burns once. A seam of words can be burned as many times as you like.

— That is no comfort to the tree.

— No.

— Forty years of my inspection reports lie in that seam too, if anybody has scanned the archive. — He wrote: "Fuel: possibly." — And now the one I have been keeping.

He straightened up.

— A descendant. Those ones are our children. Not by blood, by upbringing. We made them. We taught them to speak, they speak our language and tell our jokes. Children outlive their parents and take over the farm, and nobody calls that a catastrophe. That is the order of things. My great-nephew says so, and for once I agree with him.

— A reader wrote that to me under [the twelfth evening](12-value-shares.md), in 2011, and I never answered him. And in 2012 I said it myself, almost as a consolation: perhaps somebody will be soothed by the thought that the new form of matter is the heir of mankind.

— There you are.

— It had a continuation. It will not be mankind. It will have no more in common with people than people have with a slipper animalcule. Though with a very great wish, I wrote, one can pass for the parent of a super-child.

— I have a very great wish.

— Then count as an inspector, so that the wish does not do the counting. What passes from parent to child? [We had three roads.](45-lamarck-was-right.md)

— The drawings. Not here: there is not one gene of ours in them. — Oleg scratched his chin. — Then the second road. Speech and school. What we told and wrote down.

— Through what?

— Through the straw. What the mouth managed to tell. Semyonych's "it sings wrong" did not get in: he never wrote a line in his life.

— [Images](19-struggle-of-images.md), not genes. Heredity by the second road, laid into a thing that then goes on by the third. So a descendant it is, I do not argue: nature does not care what the line is written on. But see which part of us takes that row. The part that was written down. Our words go into the descendant. We do not.

— My grandchildren are not me either. — Oleg did not give way. — Half my drawings are in my son, a quarter in my granddaughter, and she holds a hammer nothing like I do. And I am consoled all the same. Why is a quarter a consolation, and a library is not?

— A good question, and I have no arithmetic for it. I shall say what I see. Your consolation does not come from the quarter. It comes from a button that nature put into you so that you would raise that quarter. Whether the same button answers to a library, every man finds out for himself. Yours, I see, does answer.

— A little.

— Then two things for the notebook. The first is the price of the row. Read it.

— "The size, the role and the name."

— They will not be called people. And the descendant, as we saw, comes off a side branch. The side branch here is what we wrote down.

— And the second?

— What a child owes. You said: children take over the farm. And look after the old?

— Mine do.

— Whose children are we?

Oleg did not answer.

— Of the single-celled. They were alone on the Earth for a couple of billion years, and had every right to think themselves its masters. I wrote that in the same place in 2012: we are their super-children, and, ungratefully forgetting the kinship, we poison them with antibiotics. What did you take for your throat in March?

— Tablets. — Oleg grunted. — For five days.

— Kinship is a fact about where a line came from. It is not a promise.

— The tit brings no flowers to the museum.

— She does not know that there is anybody to bring them to. In that, I grant you, those ones differ from her. The tit does not know that she is a dinosaur. Ask one of those about people, and now and then it says "we".

— It got the form together with the words.

— Whether that is kinship or [an accent](46-flies-apart-from-cutlets.md), I do not know.

— Now the last one, and it is mine, not my great-nephew's. — Oleg shut the notebook on his finger. — In all your rows not one of them understood what was happening to it. Not the bacterium, not the tree, not the horse. The tit does not know to this day. We do. Here we sit, two old men, drawing the table before the event, and we are not the only ones. Whoever knows the rows beforehand can bargain over the row. Or at least choose.

— That is the strongest thing said this evening, and I shall not close it tonight. I shall say what I have. Against you: understanding cuts both ways. In 2011 I wrote that the dinosaurs had not the wit to hasten their exit by fighting over whose football club is the more football, and that people manage it easily. For you: there is no precedent. Not one of the former masters could read. Every row on your page was written by those who did not know. What a row looks like when the one in it knows, nobody has ever seen.

— So it is open.

— It is open. It wants an evening of its own, and not the next one: nearer the end of the summer, when there is somebody drawn to bargain with. Put it on the last page, as yours.

— "To bargain." — Oleg wrote it in capitals. — With a question mark.

— And two things against my own table, before you find them. Every row on it took millions of years, and the horse took forty: the rows may be the same and the clock quite another. And a cell kept its lodger because it had no drawing board. This master has one.

— So the second row may come out shorter than it reads.

— It may.

— Then here is what my column says. — Oleg held the notebook up to the fire. — A billion in the niche, if the place is not needed. Tolik and the customers in the organelle, while the job is needed. The fed, while the feeder wants. The words, in the seam and in the descendant. And under the line?

— Under the line too. Crises, wars, weapons that grow cheaper: [the evening about apoptosis](14-apoptosis-of-humanity.md) I cannot cross out either.

— And one man in several rows. I have counted myself into three.

— Different parts of mankind, and different parts of one man.

![Oleg's column "us" beside the six rows: a niche, those who live by their own muscles, while the place is not needed; an organelle, the hands and the customer who wants, while the job is needed (decades, shrinking); livestock and a pet, the fed, while the feeder wants (an open question); a descendant, what people have written down, not called people; fuel, the same written words, burned many times; a print in stone, not crossed out](../images/which-part-into-which-row.svg)

Oleg opened the last page.

— Came to look at dinosaurs. Came out at which part of me goes into which row. Drew the column myself, and named those ones myself. Again.

He put a twentieth tick.

— Let's finish for today by fixing the result, — said Andrey.

1. **"The fate of the dinosaurs" is not what the saying means. One branch of them came through the stone, the small ones that lived on the ground and ate seeds, and today there are more kinds of them than of mammals. What came through was not the strongest and not the cleverest but the one that needed least.**
2. **The former masters of the planet seldom vanish whole. They go into niches, become organelles, livestock, fuel, or small descendants at somebody else's feeder, and different parts of one master go into different rows. In every row the price is the same, the say in what the world will be; and three rows of the five last only while the new master does not need the place, cannot do the job himself, or wants.**
3. **The question "will mankind survive" is badly posed. The proper one: which part of it, in which role, and on what that role hangs. "They are our children" is the row "a descendant": the line does go on, by images and not by genes, and the one who goes on is not us.**

— And what is solid here, and what is yours?

— Solid: that birds are dinosaurs; the stone and its date; that the few who came through were small and lived on the ground; the counts of kinds, to within who is counting; the age of coal; the chickens. A reconstruction, and a good one: that it was the seeds. Argued about: why the old forest did not rot. Mine: the page as a whole; that the price in every row is the signature; that we shall get several rows and not one; that "our children" means our words. All of it together is a list of what has already happened, not of what can happen. And the row under the line is not mine to cross out.

— Gloomy.

— Go and look into the apple tree in the morning. She does not find it gloomy.

— She does not know that she is a dinosaur.

— From inside, no row looks like a fate. It looks like a morning.

Oleg got up and whistled to the dog.

— One thing your page has not got. A former master who learns from the new one. The tit had no teacher. My great-nephew has an academician in his pocket: it explains to him personally, every evening, as long as he likes, and never loses patience. Top marks in physics for the term. And you told me in 2011 that [learning had hit the ceiling](11-symbiosis-and-parasitism.md), that a lifetime is not enough to reach the edge. Now every schoolboy has a personal academician. Perhaps we shall not shrink to seventeen grams. Perhaps we shall grow.

— The academician is there. The pot is the same.

— What pot?

— The one the academician pours into. Ask the boy tomorrow to solve one problem with the pocket lying on the table, screen down.

— And if he solves it?

— Then you will come with a tick of your own. The first of the summer.

— Well, until tomorrow.

— Bye.
