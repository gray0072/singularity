# Science Advances from Funeral to Funeral

Oleg sat down on the log without a greeting and for a while poked the coals with a stick.

— The dream first. Before the phone. I was standing at the blackboard in a chemistry lesson and could not remember what valency is. The teacher was Genka. Nobody rang.

— The fifth entry in the expense column.

— Now the quotation. — He opened the notebook and read without pleasure: — "The children now love luxury. They have bad manners, contempt for authority; they show disrespect for elders and love chatter in place of exercise." Socrates.

— And the place?

— There is no place. — Oleg shut the notebook. — I sat till midnight. A hundred sites, every one says "Socrates", and not one says where. One refers to another, that one to a third, and the third to a tear-off calendar.

— The place is Cambridge, 1907.

— Socrates got as far as Cambridge?

— [Kenneth Freeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_John_Freeman) did. A student, twenty-four years old. He wrote a dissertation on the schools of ancient Greece, and in one paragraph he summed up, in his own words, what the Greeks of that time held against their young: luxury, bad manners, contempt for authority, crossing their legs, gobbling the dainties at table. No quotation marks and no Socrates. He died before [the book](https://archive.org/details/schoolsofhellase00freeuoft) came out, and his friends printed it. In 1911 an American educator quoted the paragraph in a speech, honestly, with the name. In 1921 a magazine reprinted it from him. In 1922 a California newspaper put the name of Socrates under it. The whole road [has been traced](https://quoteinvestigator.com/2010/05/01/misbehave/), step by step.

— Fifteen years.

— From a student's summary to the wisdom of the ancients.

— So a boy wrote it. About boys. — Oleg grunted. — And I have been beating boys with it.

— There is something better. What was Socrates put to death for?

— Something about the gods. And... — Oleg stopped. — For corrupting the young.

— In 399 before our era, at seventy. He was not the one who grumbled. He was what the fathers grumbled about. Twenty-four years before [the trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trial_of_Socrates) Aristophanes had put him on the stage, in [a comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clouds): a windbag to whom a decent man sends his son to be taught to argue. The son comes home, beats his father and proves that he is right to.

— All right, the quotation is a fake. — Oleg was not going to give in. — But the complaint? Did the Greeks scold their young, or did your student make that up as well?

— They scolded, and I owe you the honest part. In Plato's "Republic" Socrates does describe a city where the father is afraid of his sons, the teacher flatters his pupils, and the old men joke along with the young so as not to seem dull. Only he is describing the illness of a state that has had too much freedom, not the boys of his own street. Aristotle wrote that the young think they know everything and are always quite sure of it. And Horace, in Rome, twenty-odd years before our era: our fathers' age, worse than our grandfathers', bore us, who are worse again, and we shall soon give the world offspring more worthless still.

— There. — Oleg raised a finger. — Two and a half thousand years, and they all see the same thing. That many people cannot be wrong.

— Let's count. A generation is twenty-five years. How many of them since Aristophanes?

— A hundred, near enough.

— Suppose each was worse than the one before by a trifle. Two per cent: nobody would even notice. What is left after a hundred steps?

Oleg took out his phone, tapped at it and frowned.

— Thirteen per cent. An eighth of a Greek.

— And at five per cent a step?

— A little over half of one per cent. — He put the phone away. — With that we could not have lit this fire.

— And it is lit. So the young were not getting worse, and all those people were not lying either. Each reported honestly what he saw. What we must look at is what they saw it with. Yesterday you said: in my day a team was a team. How old were you, in your day?

— Twenty-five. I told you.

— And music? When was the music good?

— Don't start. — Oleg waved his hand. — The music was good when it was music. Seventy-nine, eighty.

— How old were you?

— Fourteen. Fifteen.

— In 2018 an American economist took the records of a music service: who listens to what, by year of birth. A song is loved most by the men who were about fourteen when it came out, and by the women who were about thirteen. And ask a man of seventy to tell you his life: most of the stories [will fall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reminiscence_bump) between ten and thirty.

— Naturally. Everything was for the first time.

— Say it in the language of [the evening about images](19-struggle-of-images.md).

— The shelves were empty. — Oleg thought. — Whoever came first took the place. No queue and no fight.

— And whoever comes later?

— Has to push somebody out.

— Whom? An image you have used for forty years, or one that came on Tuesday?

— Tuesday's will lose. You told me in the spring: [every night a stocktaking](33-use-of-sleep.md). What was used is kept and grows heavier. What was not is written off.

— How many stocktakings have you had?

— Sixty-two years. — Oleg moved his lips. — Over twenty-two thousand.

— Twenty-two thousand times the heavy was kept and the light was written off. Now the arithmetic of one fact. Suppose you judge a thing by the average of all the times you have seen it. You have seen it nine times, and the tenth time it comes out differently. How far does the average move?

— By a tenth.

— And if you have seen it nine thousand nine hundred and ninety-nine times?

— By a ten-thousandth. It won't move at all.

![Chart: what one new fact weighs for a mind that judges by the average of everything it has seen. The second fact weighs a half, the tenth a tenth, the hundredth a hundredth, the ten-thousandth one part in ten thousand: the curve falls to the axis and stays there](../images/weight-of-a-new-fact.svg)

— The thermometer outside your window shows frost in July. Do you believe it?

— I throw the thermometer out. I have seen sixty Julys.

— And your granddaughter, when she was five?

— She would have run for her mittens.

— Who is right?

— I am. — Oleg hesitated. — Nearly always.

— Nearly always, and that is the whole of it. A mind that has seen much gives one new fact little weight, and it is right to, while the world stays as it was. It is the screw from [the evening about your dream](39-price-of-a-false-alarm.md). Turn it one way, and you rebuild your whole picture for every fluke. Turn it the other, and the world has changed while you were throwing out thermometers.

— A false alarm and a miss.

— The young stand at one end, the old at the other. And nobody put them there out of spite. The screw turns by itself, with every fact added.

— So an old man is not a fool. He is full.

— He is full. Now honestly. When the first machines with program control came to your plant, who cursed them?

— Semyonych, our foreman. He said a part that no hand had touched had no soul in it. — Oleg smiled. — He could tell a bad bearing by ear from the far end of the shop.

— And who learned to run them?

— We did. The boys.

— And when the stock ledgers went into the computer?

Oleg was silent for a while.

— I was thirty-eight. I kept a paper ledger on the side for two years. In case it all vanished.

— Did it vanish?

— Not once. — He looked at the notebook in his hands and put it down on the log. — This is different.

— I haven't said a word. What did Semyonych say about your team, when you were twenty-five?

— That we were a lost lot. Hair down to the shoulders, a tape recorder instead of a head, and not a grain of conscience.

— In 2019 two American psychologists [asked](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aav5916) some three and a half thousand people about the young. Those who read a great deal said that the young have stopped reading. Those who respect authority said that the young respect nobody. The clever ones said that the young have grown stupid. Each found the young lacking in exactly the thing he is strong in himself.

— Why?

— Because he does not compare them with the young of his day. He compares them with himself, and he remembers himself as he is now. You set a lad of twenty-five beside yourself at twenty-five, as a foreman of sixty-two remembers him.

— And that one already had a conscience. — Oleg scratched his head. — And carried nothing through the gatehouse.

— Did he?

— Let's not.

— About conscience there is a separate count. In 2023 two psychologists [gathered](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06137-x) the surveys of sixty countries, the oldest from 1949. Every year most people say that people have become less honest and less kind than they used to be. And every year, asked about today (were you treated with respect yesterday, would a stranger help you), they answer as their grandfathers answered.

— Morals have been falling for seventy years without moving from the spot.

— Like the Greeks at two per cent a step.

Oleg threw a branch on the fire and watched it catch.

— Or perhaps the head simply wears out. Like a bearing. And all your shelves have nothing to do with it.

— In the sixties two physiologists at Harvard closed one eye of a kitten for its first months. Then they opened it. The eye was sound, and the cat was blind in it for life: the brain had given that eye's place to the other one. They did the same to a grown cat, for a whole year. Nothing happened.

— So there is [a window](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critical_period), and it shuts.

— And in 2002, in Pisa, they took grown rats and [dissolved](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1072699) the nets that grow round the nerve cells when the window shuts. The window opened again. A grown brain relearned like a kitten's.

— So nothing had worn out.

— It had been locked, and the lock comes off. Why would nature lock up a thing that works?

— So that the new does not wipe out the old. — Oleg answered at once. — That was your sleep. The shop that shuts for stocktaking.

— The same screw again. What has been learned must be guarded from what is being learned. When we like the guard, we call it maturity. When we don't, stubbornness.

— Then I'll say this. — Oleg straightened up. — You have put up a monument to the boys. And an old man knows more. Semyonych heard a bearing through the wall, and not one of us could. Verdi wrote "Falstaff" at nearly eighty. Goethe finished "Faust" past eighty.

— All true, and it has been measured. In 2015 some fifty thousand people of every age [were tested](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614567339). Speed of thought is at its best at eighteen or nineteen. Memory for the new, at about twenty-five. And the stock of words, at sixty-five and later. In 2016 physicists [went through](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaf5239) the careers of thousands of scientists: a scientist's best paper is as likely to be his first as his last. The old write less. Their shot is no worse.

— Well then.

— So I am not saying that the old are worse. I am saying one thing: their images are heavy. Inside the old picture that makes a man fast and nearly always right. He is slow in one place only, where the picture itself has to be changed. "Falstaff" is the best Italian opera of a man who wrote Italian operas all his life. The summit of a road, not a turning off it. That one is mine, don't look for it in a journal.

— All right. In one head the screw turns one way. — Oleg picked up the stick again. — Then how does a village of such heads ever learn anything new?

— How do you think?

— New heads come.

— [Max Planck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planck%27s_principle) wrote it at the end of his life: a new scientific truth does not triumph by convincing its opponents and making them see the light, but rather because its opponents eventually die, and a new generation grows up that is familiar with it.

— From funeral to funeral, then.

— An economist retold it in just those words. And Planck knew what he was speaking of from both sides. As a young man he spent years trying to persuade the grey heads of his science, and persuaded nobody. In 1900, at forty-two, he brought the quantum into physics himself. He called it an act of desperation and spent years trying to get rid of it. And in 1913, recommending the young Einstein to the academy, he apologised for him: that he sometimes misses the target, as with his light quanta, should not be held against him.

— He opened the door and stood in the doorway.

— Darwin wrote the same in 1859, on the last pages of his book. He did not expect to convince the experienced naturalists, whose minds were stocked with a multitude of facts all viewed, for long years, from a point of view directly opposite to his. He looked to the young.

— Stocked. That's what I said: full. — Oleg squinted. — Beautiful. Has anybody counted it, or is this one more Socrates?

— Twice. In 1978 they [took](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.202.4369.717) Darwin's own readers, the British scientists of his day. Within ten years most of them had accepted evolution, the old ones too. Age explained less than a tenth of the difference.

— So Planck laid it on thick.

— About people he did. People do change their minds. And in 2019 three economists [took](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20161574) four hundred and fifty-two eminent American biologists and physicians who had died in the middle of their work, at about sixty on average, and looked at their fields before and after. Those who had written together with the star began to write in that field markedly less. Those who had never written with the star came into the field and wrote more, by about a twelfth. And it was their papers that were later cited most. They brought other methods and read other books.

![Scheme of the 2019 study: papers in the field of an eminent scientist before and after the scientist's early death. The line of the co-authors falls after the death; the line of those who never wrote with the star rises by about a twelfth, and their papers are the ones cited most later](../images/after-a-star-dies.svg)

— About people he laid it on, and about fields he was right.

— That is how I read it.

— And I read it more simply. — Oleg shook his head. — A man died. His posts came free, and his money. People ran to where the money was. It is not ideas, it is a vacancy.

— Perhaps. The authors looked at what they could. The stars seldom sat at the tap themselves: few of them edited journals or shared out the money. And where the star's pupils sat at the tap, fewer strangers came. So it is partly the posts. Telling the one from the other is the work, and it is not finished. But notice what kept the strangers out while the star was alive. Mostly nobody drove them away. They did not go.

— A chair that is taken need not be defended.

— And it is vacated later and later. At the American institutes of health the average age at which a scientist gets the first large grant of his own was thirty-six in 1980. Now it is past forty-two.

— Then tell me who does the cleaning. — Oleg thought aloud. — In one head it is clear: the stocktaking, every night. And in the village?

— What is forgetting in one head? We said it once.

— A function, not a fault. The unused is written off, or there is no room for the new.

— And who writes off a heavy image in the common memory, if nobody can reach into another man's head?

— Nobody. — Oleg was silent for a moment. — It leaves together with the head.

— Death is the cleaner of the common memory, as forgetting is of one's own. I said it long ago in other words. A man can grow from an infant into an old man because [his cells are mortal](14-apoptosis-of-humanity.md). Humanity can develop because a man is mortal. The death of the small systems is [a condition of the life of the large one](10-life-cycle-and-death.md). And in 2017, on the forum: the mortal cells, with a term of life shortened on purpose, outplayed the ancient immortal ones for one plain reason. They changed faster.

— On that forum of yours a man wrote the opposite, I marked it. So much knowledge has piled up that there is no pouring it into the youngsters, and half of it goes into oblivion. So life must be made longer, and best of all a way to immortality found. Otherwise man will stay something like an ape.

— He saw the trouble rightly, and I'll come back to it. The cure is wrong. Make him immortal. Who is he in two hundred years?

— A man who has seen it nine million times.

— With a thermometer he does not believe. An immortal mind is a chair that is never vacated. And not one chair: all of them. Under [one of the articles](13-singularity-as-process.md) I answered it in a line: immortality automatically lowers the ability to survive.

Oleg put the stick down.

— Stop. And those ones? They don't die. You told me yourself in the spring: a talking machine is taught once, and after that it only talks. It does not learn while it talks. So what is it, young for ever or old for ever? I asked, not you.

— You asked. Both, and both badly. If nobody tells it the date, it will name the year in which its schooling ended. It lives in its day.

— In my day a team was a team.

— Just so. Twenty-five for ever, with the certainty of sixty.

— Then teach it further. It doesn't sleep, it doesn't get tired.

— They tried. In 2024 a group in Canada, led by one of the founders of the trade, [published](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07711-7) this. A network was given task after task, thousands in a row, each one new. The first ones it learned well. Then worse and worse, and in the end no better than the simplest network there is, with no depth to it at all. Nothing in it wears out: it is numbers. Some of its units had fallen silent for good, and the rest had set so hard that a new task could not shift them.

— Full.

— Full. The engineers call it loss of plasticity. And now guess the cure.

— Shake it?

— At every step a tiny share of the units, the ones used least, are wiped clean and started from nothing, like the newborn. With that the network went on learning for as long as it was taught.

— Small funerals.

— Inside one head. It is what a body does with its cells. And there is a second cure, a coarser one. A network that learns by trial and error clings to its first experience: engineers [call it](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07802) the primacy bias.

— My seventy-nine.

— So from time to time the network is wiped, in part or altogether, and the diary of everything it has lived through is kept. The new one reads the old one's diary and comes out better than the old one was.

— The head is changed and the notebook is left.

— The makers of the big talking machines do much the same. They do not top one machine up for ever. Every year or so they raise a new generation, on everything written before it. There are several reasons for that, and I am not an engineer. This is one of them.

— So it is the same with them. From funeral to funeral.

— With two differences. Planck's turn took a generation, thirty years. Theirs takes a year. And the second is what passes from the old head to the new. With them the diary stays whole. With us most of it leaves together with the head.

![Table: how the old makes room for the new. The cells of a body: the order to die, tens of billions a day, the body stays. The images in one head: the nightly write-off, only the light ones go and the heavy grow heavier. The heads of a science: a funeral, once in about thirty years, books and pupils stay and the skill leaves. The units of a network: the least used are wiped at every step. The generations of machines: a new one is raised every year or two and the whole diary stays](../images/how-the-old-makes-room.svg)

Oleg looked into the fire for a long time. Then he turned to Andrey.

— Now about you. You are sixty-four. Fifteen years ago you saw your mountain, and since then every thermometer of yours shows it. I ask about ants, and it is there. I ask about Socrates, and it is there. How much does that image weigh by now? By your own arithmetic of tonight no fact of mine will shift it.

Andrey did not answer at once.

— It weighs a great deal. And from inside a heavy image cannot be told from the truth: both feel like "obviously". I have put a heavy one down once. [At fifteen](23-machine-for-making-people-good.md) I believed in an idea firmly enough to get into trouble for it, and it took me years to let it go, and I was young then. The second time the calendar did it for me. I promised a thinking machine by 2018 and did not give the date up until [it had passed](16-fifteen-years-later.md).

— So not by your own strength.

— Not by my own. I know one cure, the same as for everybody: somebody from outside, and a date. That is why the marks lie in your notebook, written down beforehand. And the last check will most likely be made without me. By tonight's logic that is as it should be.

— Don't be in a hurry to vacate. — Oleg tapped the notebook. — I have half of this left.

— I am in no hurry. I only say that the chair is not mine.

— I'll put it beside the pigeon. On the last page. — He wrote. — And if their lines do flow into one, as you said yesterday, and that one never changes its head?

— Then it is the same chair, the size of a planet. Whether there is a cure for that is for the end of the summer.

Oleg turned to the last page.

— Came with Socrates and the spoiled young. Came out at a network in which they hold small funerals, and brought it in myself. Again.

He put a twelfth tick beside the eleven.

— Let's finish for today by fixing the result, — said Andrey.

1. **The complaint about the young is as old as writing, and it measures the one who complains. He compares today's young not with the young of his day but with himself, and remembers himself as he is now.**
2. **A mind ages not because it wears out but because it fills up: images used for decades grow so heavy that a new one cannot shift them. This is the price of not forgetting. One screw sets both, and it turns by itself with every fact added.**
3. **Nature mends this not by repair but by replacement: the cells of a body, the heads of a science, the generations of machines. Death is the cleaner of the common memory, as forgetting is of one's own. An immortal mind is a chair that is never vacated.**

— And what is solid here, and what is yours?

— Solid: that Socrates did not say it; the window that shuts in a kitten and opens again in a rat; that the stock of words grows to sixty-five and the speed falls from twenty; the network that stops learning, and that small funerals cure it. Argued about: how much of what happens after a star's death is ideas and how much is vacant posts; how far Planck was right about people. Mine: that a mind ages by filling; that death does for the common memory what forgetting does for one head; that the summit of an old road is not a new road. And about my own image you have heard.

Oleg stood up and put the notebook in his pocket.

— All the same, it is a pity. Semyonych listened to bearings for forty years. And all of it went into the ground with him. The boy who came after started from the alphabet. A blacksmith's son is not born with calluses.

— The son is not.

— And who is?

— Tomorrow.

— Well, until tomorrow.

— Bye.
