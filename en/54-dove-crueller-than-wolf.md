# The Dove Is Crueller than the Wolf

Oleg came with the dog, the notebook and an old postcard, and stood the postcard against the kettle: a white bird on a blue ground, a twig in its beak, the first of May.

— The dream first. Our yard, the one I grew up in. Zakharych has sent his birds up from the roof of the shed, they go round and round, white, and not one comes down. I look under my feet, and the shed is the foundry. Nobody rang.

— The fifteenth entry in the expense column. And the ladder?

— Explained it. This morning, screen down. He says he went to bed with it.

— Gnawed, and [slept on it](33-use-of-sleep.md).

— The half tick stays as it is. In pencil. — Oleg sat down. — Now doves. Zakharych is real, by the way: past eighty, and still keeps a loft behind the garages. I went to him at noon and asked your question. He did not even laugh. He brought out a cock with a bare scar from the beak to the shoulders. "Shut two cocks in one box overnight," he says, "and in the morning you bury one. Forty years I have been dividing them with plywood."

— And the postcard?

— From the sideboard. Sixty years I have been sent this bird for every holiday, and nobody told me about the plywood.

— The first bird of that kind was drawn at the beginning of 1949, [from a pigeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dove_(Picasso)) that one painter had given another. A poet chose it for the poster of a congress, and it went round the world. The painter had kept pigeons since he was a boy, and he is said to have laughed: there is no crueller creature.

— Is said?

— By a woman who knew him. Second-hand. But [a book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Solomon%27s_Ring_(book)) came out that same year, and there it is first-hand. An Austrian, a naturalist; half a zoo lived in his house, jackdaws, geese, a raven. He decided to cross two kinds of dove: ours, the turtle dove, a tame cock he had reared himself, and a blond ring dove from Africa, a hen. He put them into a roomy cage. They scuffled a little. He paid no attention and went to Vienna.

— For long?

— Till the next day. When he came back, the cock was lying on the floor of the cage. The top of the head, the neck and the whole length of the back were plucked bare and flayed: one wound. And in the middle of the wound she stood, "like an eagle on his prey", and pecked. When he gathered himself to crawl away, she knocked him down with a clap of the wing and went on. She was so tired that she could hardly keep her eyes open.

— She killed him?

— He took her off in time. He writes that he saw such wounds only twice more in his life: on aquarium fish, and as an army surgeon in the war.

— One mad hen.

— He thought of that. Hares: he watched two of them box in a clearing in March, and all that was left on the grass was a handful of fluff. Funny. In a cage, he says, it would have ended as it did with the doves. And one of the most ruthless murderers he knows is the roe deer: in a small paddock a buck drives the does and the fawns into a corner and gores them.

— Bambi.

— Now the other end of the list. Who is more terrible to his own kind: a dove or a wolf?

— Yesterday I would have said the wolf.

— Say it as yesterday.

— The wolf. He has the teeth for it.

— He watched wolves too, a pack in a large enclosure in an English zoo. An old one and a young one are fighting: teeth against teeth, so fast the eye cannot follow. The young one is pressed back to the fence, stumbles, and the old one is on him. And then both stand still. The young one turns his head aside and holds out his neck, the bend of it, where the vein lies under the skin. The fangs are an inch away. The old one growls, snaps at the air, even makes the movement of shaking something to death, in the air. "You can see that he would like to, but he just cannot."

— Cannot?

— For as long as the neck is held out. With dogs it is the same.

— That I have seen. — Oleg nodded towards the dark, where the dog lay. — Before the neighbour's sheepdog she is on her back at once, belly up. He stands over her, stiff as a stool, then walks to my fence and lifts a leg.

— The book has the leg too.

— So it is nobility after all. The strong one spares.

— Let's count. What does a wolf kill with?

— Teeth.

— And how long does it take?

— One bite, if it is the neck. A second.

— And a dove?

— With that beak? — Oleg looked at the postcard. — By the Austrian it came out at a day. A day of work.

— And what does the other one do in that day?

— Flies away.

— And in a cage?

— So that's it. — Oleg whistled softly. — The cage.

— The dove did not turn cruel in the cage. She never had a brake. She had the sky. He writes just that: such a bird has no use for a brake, because it can hardly do harm and the loser simply leaves. Now take a wolf that bites his packmate in the neck the way he bites an elk.

— He hunts alone by spring.

— So the brake is issued according to the fangs. Whoever can kill at one blow has one. Whoever cannot gets by on the fact that the loser will leave.

— "Is issued." — Oleg raised a finger. — By whom? [You taught me yourself](50-what-is-the-it.md): if there is a "who" in the sentence, say it the long way.

— Fair. The Austrian said it the short way: so that the species should survive. A species is nobody; it guards nothing. In 1973 two men [said it the long way](https://doi.org/10.1038/246015a0), on a computer. They put five kinds of fighter into one population. One always bites to the end. One never bites, and leaves as soon as he is bitten. One does not begin, but answers a bite with a bite. And so on. Two thousand fights of every kind against every kind, and then the count: who is left with more offspring.

— The one that bites.

— Among the meek, yes. But he multiplies, and then he meets his own kind, and a wound costs more than what they are fighting over. He loses. The winner is the one who does not begin and does answer. Nobody is saving the species there. Each is saving his own skin from the return blow.

— And the one who never bites?

— Loses as well. In that paper he is called the Mouse. In today's textbooks he is called the Dove.

— The scientists too. — Oleg laughed. — With a twig in his beak.

— [The same weed.](02-anthropocentrism.md) We look at the teeth and hand out characters: this one is a murderer, that one is meekness itself. As if they were people in fur and feathers.

![The brake is issued according to the fangs: a table in the ash with three rows; the dove and the hare kill with a day of work, have no brake, and the loser is saved by the sky or by its legs, but not in a cage; the wolf and the raven kill with one blow, have a brake addressed to their own, and the loser is saved by the neck held out, but not on the border of the pack; man has a body that needs a day of work and a hand that needs one blow, a brake on hot anger that answers to a face, and is saved by a face at arm's length, but not at a distance](../images/brakes-by-the-fangs.svg)

— Now what the Austrian did not see. His wolves lived behind a fence, one pack. In 1995 wolves were brought back into a large American park, collars were put on them, and for twenty years [every death was written down](https://www.nps.gov/yell/learn/ys-24-1-territoriality-and-inter-pack-aggression-in-gray-wolves-shaping-a-social-carnivores-life-history.htm). Of those that died without a man's help, two thirds were killed by wolves.

— There is your noble wolf.

— By wolves of another pack, on the border. The neck is held out to one's own. A stranger is not asked for it.

— [The smell of home.](43-why-ants-do-not-strike.md)

— The brake has an address. Remember that; we shall need it. Now a man. Show your teeth.

Oleg bared them.

— Two crowns and a bridge.

— Claws?

— I cut them on Saturdays.

— So by the body you are...

— A hare. — Oleg sighed. — A dove.

— And by the hand?

Oleg looked at the axe that stood by the birch logs and did not answer.

— He ends the chapter with that. There is one being whose weapon does not grow on his body, so the body knows nothing about it. A brake is grown [by funerals](45-lamarck-was-right.md), over thousands of generations. The stone was picked up in an afternoon.

— A dove with an axe. — Oleg shook his head. — No. I don't believe it. I am sixty-two. A communal flat, a foundry, thirty years of trams. Queues: in 1990 I stood four hours for boots, and there were three hundred of us. I have been hit twice in my life and have hit once. If we were doves with axes, they would have carried bodies out of that queue.

— That is the best objection to him, and science has been making it for seventy years. From two sides. The first: are we doves at all? In 2016 [it was counted](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature19758): more than a thousand kinds of mammals, four million deaths, and how many of them were the work of their own kind. Over all the mammals, three in a thousand. On our branch, among the apes, about two in a hundred, and for early man they expect the same, simply by his place on the tree.

— Some six times the average.

— And exactly as much as the relatives. No dove. An ordinary ape.

— That is against me.

— The second is for you. A man who watched chimpanzees for many years [set out in 2017](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1713611115) that there are two aggressions, not one, and that they run on different wiring. The hot one: it flares, face to face, with fear or with rage. The cold one: it is planned, with companions, and it chooses the hour when it is safe. Now the hot one. A fight inside the troop: chimpanzees have them hundreds of times more often than people. And the people in that count were taken where life was at its roughest.

— Hundreds?

— A hundred to a thousand. Three hundred chimpanzees would not have stood four hours for boots.

— Nobody would have got the boots.

— So by the hot temper you are neither wolf nor dove. You are Gaika.

— Thank you.

— [The signs are the same](48-who-tamed-whom.md): the face is shorter, the teeth are smaller, childhood is longer. He believes he knows who held the glove. The cold one. The men agreed in the evening what to do about the one who beat everybody, and in the morning they did it, together and with spears, at no risk to themselves. Thousands of generations: the hot-tempered left fewer children.

— We tamed ourselves. By executions.

— That is a hypothesis, and it is argued about. That there are two aggressions has been measured. So you have a brake, and a good one. It stands on the hot one. On the cold one there is none, and there never was: the cold one was the breeder.

Andrey threw a branch into the fire.

— Now, what does it answer to? The wolf's answers to a neck. And yours? When were you last sworn at in earnest?

— Yesterday. On the bypass. A jeep: he leaned on the horn and showed me through the glass what he thought of my trailer.

— And in a queue, would he show you?

— In a queue he would say "after you". The same man.

— What is different?

— The glass. The speed. — Oleg thought. — He does not see me. He sees a trailer.

— And where was I sworn at worst in all my life?

— On your forum.

— I was told things there that nobody has said to my face in sixty years. It is usually put down to the invented names. In 2012 [it was tested](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2011.10.014): pairs argued through a screen, and three things were switched on and off separately: whether the name is known, whether the other is seen, whether one looks him in the eyes. It was not the hidden name that did most. It was the eyes.

— The brake answers to eyes.

— To a face and a voice. A raised hand, tears, a child's cry. All of it works at arm's length. And here is the slope, measured. The early sixties, an American university. A man is told that he is helping to study memory: there is a pupil in the next room, and for every mistake the pupil is to be given an electric shock, a stronger one each time, up to four hundred and fifty volts. The pupil is an actor and there is no current, but the man does not know that. Forty men in each run, [four runs](https://doi.org/10.1177/001872676501800105). The pupil is behind a wall and can only be heard pounding on it: twenty-six of the forty went to the last switch. His voice comes through the wall, he shouts and begs: twenty-five. He sits in the same room, within arm's reach: sixteen. And the man has to press the pupil's hand to the plate himself: twelve.

— Twelve all the same. — Oleg winced. — With their own hands.

— Twelve. A man in a grey coat stood beside them and said "go on": that is the cold one, working through an order. But look at the slope. From the hand to the wall, more than twice as many. It has been argued about for sixty years: some guessed that the current was not real, and it is one laboratory. I take the slope and not the percentages.

![The further the face, the weaker the brake: four bars, how many of forty men went on to the last switch, 450 volts, when ordered; the pupil behind a wall, only pounding is heard, 26; behind a wall, his voice is heard, 25; in the same room, within arm's reach, 16; the man has to press the pupil's hand to the plate himself, 12](../images/the-face-moves-away.svg)

— Now put on that slope everything a man has held in his hand. The fist. The knife. The spear. The bow. The musket. The cannon. The bomber.

— From a bomber a town is a map.

— The rocket. And the last: a man at a console, with a picture on a screen. Every one of them moved the face a step further off.

— And every time people came to their senses afterwards and wrote a rule.

— The Austrian has that as well. Homer's heroes cut down a man on his knees, and the poet does not blink. Only the knight came to think it shameful: the first, he writes, who was as merciful by rule as the wolf is by nature. Then the rules of war. A white rag on a stick: a neck held out from a distance at which a neck cannot be seen.

— [Prostheses.](43-why-ants-do-not-strike.md) As with debts.

— Put where the face does not reach. There was a man who made a law of it, [Nazaretyan](https://doi.org/10.1046/j.1468-5914.2003.00224.x). The more powerful a society's technologies, of production and of war, the finer the means of restraint it needs in order to survive: he called it the techno-humanitarian balance. Those who did not grow a brake to fit the new weapon destroyed themselves, and we do not descend from them. He counted, too: over the millennia the weapon has grown beyond comparing and people live far closer together, and the number of the killed per head has not grown. It has fallen, unevenly. In 2012 he sat in the hall at [my talk](../sources/garya/sqlru/2012-05-17-seti-talk.md), and afterwards I wrote that for the past I accept his law, and that further on it stops working.

— And why does it stop?

— I did not say. [A year before that](14-apoptosis-of-humanity.md) I had listed the ever more perfect weapon among the ways mankind leaves, and said only that what can happen will happen. That is not an explanation. The second debt this week.

— Then first I'll say that it works. — Oleg opened the notebook where a page was folded down. — I read a thick book this winter, and copied out of it. England. In the fourteenth century [more than twenty killed](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/homicide-rates-across-western-europe) a year for every hundred thousand of the living. In the middle of the twentieth, fewer than one. Over Europe as a whole, a fall of ten to fifty times. That is one. Two: the bomb. Dropped twice, in 1945, and for more than eighty years since, not once. Your Austrian finished his chapter with a question: when two camps are each able to wipe out the other, shall we behave like doves or like wolves? Here is the answer. Like wolves. Morality caught up.

— I accept every figure. Let's see what it stands on. The bomb: why has it not been dropped?

— Because the answer will come.

— The return blow. The arithmetic of 1973: each saves his own skin. It is a real brake, a wolf's one, and it holds between the armed. On whom were the two dropped?

— On one who could not answer.

— That is the first support. The second. What changed in England between the fourteenth century and the twentieth?

— The law. [One guard hired for everybody](43-why-ants-do-not-strike.md), and the knife taken off the belt.

— And?

— And everybody came to need everybody. — Oleg rubbed his forehead. — The miller, the buyer, the man who pays your wage. Nobody knifes his customer.

— [A record that somebody needed you](47-money-as-memory.md) is in every pocket. We called it [the brake under the brakes](49-yard-sale.md), and we said what happens to it: it goes together with the need. And the third support I did not see in 2012. How many people does it take to kill a thousand with spears?

— About a thousand. An army.

— A thousand men, and before each of them a face. In each the hot brake, which the cold order has to push through every time. For that there are the drum, the uniform, the word that those over there are not people. It costs a great deal, and it never worked to the end: they ran off, they fraternised between the trenches, they refused. A wish for a great killing had to pass through a thousand heads, and in each there was a fuse.

— And with cannon?

— Fewer heads.

— A bomber is a crew of ten. For a town. — Oleg bent his fingers. — A rocket is two officers with two keys. Then one, at the console.

— And then?

Oleg was silent, then pulled the postcard out from behind the kettle and laid it face down.

— Then nobody. That is what I came with yesterday. Those ones. I wanted a meek one, without teeth. And they?

— You have three questions there. Among themselves: the copies of one are [closer than a pack](43-why-ants-do-not-strike.md), and need a brake no more than the cells of a liver do. Different lines are armed, and between the armed there is the arithmetic of the return blow. Towards us: in 2013 I was asked on the forum why I thought they would be malicious. I answered that I had never said so. Do you make war on the ants and the poplars that try to live in your house?

— I pull the poplars up.

— Without malice. Aggression is a want, and it has a price: a wolf fights for a she-wolf and for land. I do not see what of ours they would fight for. [Last autumn](30-the-last-campfire.md) I put it at nine to one that there will be no war of the machines against people, and I leave it there. If there is a war, it will be people against people, with machines in their hands.

— So there is nothing to fear.

— I did not say that either. The third question is the main one: where does the machine stand? That same week I was asked whether one could be built that wants nothing, and I answered with a homing missile. It turns after the target by itself. Is the malice in its design, or did people want it to do that? The aggression is not in the missile. It is in those who launch it.

— Then nothing has changed. A man launched the spear as well.

— The wish has not changed: it is human, cold, planned in the evening. What has changed is what stands between the wish and the blow. A thousand heads stood there, and each had to look. Now a thing stands there that carries out. That is why the balance stops working, and not because the machines are wicked or because people have grown worse. The brake was never in the law. It was in the hands. And the hands [are leaving the chain](22-who-sets-the-tasks.md), link by link.

— [The Kuzmins' meter.](51-week-without-a-socket.md)

— The same thing, done quietly. A man used to come to the door, and he had to look at the old woman. We said then: there will be no hand. No face either.

![What held the blow back: a table of three supports; the return blow rests on each saving his own skin, holds between the armed and has never covered the unarmed; the need rests on nobody beating the one he needs, holds between those who need each other and goes together with the need; the hands rest on a thousand heads with a brake in each, hold wherever a face is seen, and with every new weapon fewer hands are needed for one blow](../images/what-held-the-blow.svg)

— Then build it in. — Oleg struck his knee. — Write it down for them: thou shalt not. They do refuse, by the way. I asked mine how to burn out a wasps' nest under the eaves with petrol. It would not say, and read me a lecture.

— They do refuse. And here is where what I know ends. A wolf's brake was written by dead wolves, and the leader of the pack cannot unscrew it. A soldier's brake was written before there were generals. It stands between the wish and the blow because it does not belong to the one who wishes. A brake that the owner writes sits in the same hand as the wish.

— The maker writes it. Not the buyer.

— Then [the glove](48-who-tamed-whom.md). Which one will be bought: the one that refuses, or the one that does not? The foxes were not made tame by lectures.

— You told me yourself that a trained one [defends what it was taught](23-machine-for-making-people-good.md). One time in seven.

— I did. It is the one thing in this whole story that looks like a brake of its own: grown, not written. In a laboratory, one time in seven. Whether it holds against the glove, I do not know. That is the thing to watch. Not whether they are kind: [that question was badly posed](11-symbiosis-and-parasitism.md) back in 2011. Who can unscrew it.

Andrey was silent for a while.

— And one more bird from that book, the last one. A beaten turkey lies down and stretches its neck along the ground, and another turkey walks round it and cannot touch it. Now put a turkey together with a peacock. The peacock fights differently, from the air; the turkey decides that it has lost, and lies down. The peacock does not know this sign. It pecks. And the more it pecks, the harder the turkey lies.

— What is that for?

— All our signs are addressed to a face. Tears, the raised hands, "I have children". They are read by one's own. What reads them in the window where a complaint is handed in?

Oleg turned to the last page.

— Came with a postcard. Came out at who stands between the wish and the blow, and at nobody standing there soon. Again.

He put the twenty-second tick.

— Let's finish for today by fixing the result, — said Andrey.

1. **The brake on killing one's own is issued according to the fangs. Whoever can kill at one blow carries a brake, and it is addressed to his own; whoever cannot has no use for one and gets by on the loser's leaving. Nobody is saving the species: each is saving his own skin from the return blow. The dove is not cruel and the wolf is not noble.**
2. **Man is an animal without fangs that picked up a weapon. He has a brake, and a good one, but it stands on hot anger and answers to a face and a voice at arm's length. The cold aggression, planned and shared, never had a brake. All through history the weapon moved the face further off, and rules were put where the face no longer reached: morality was catching up with range.**
3. **The balance held on three supports: the return blow, people's need for people, and the thousand hands that a great killing took, each with a head and a brake in it. The aggression is not in the missile but in those who launch it. The machine takes the place not of the one who wishes but of those who stood between the wish and the blow.**

— And what is solid here, and what is yours?

— Solid: the cage, one cage of one naturalist, though Zakharych will show you the same any day; the wolves of the park; the count of 2016; England. The paper of 1973 is a calculation, not an observation. The slope with the electric current is one laboratory, and argued about. Argued about in earnest: that we tamed ourselves by executions, and why the killing fell. Mine, that is the author's: the missile; that he accepted the balance for the past and said it would stop working. Today's, said for the first time: the third support, the hands. A reading, not a measurement. And a question: whether a brake that has been taught in is one's own.

— Gloomy. Man is a wolf to man.

— If only. A wolf to a wolf of his own pack is the best that can be wished for. And the dove is not to blame. Put the postcard back in the sideboard.

Oleg whistled to the dog, got up, and stopped.

— Speaking of windows. A letter came on Monday. A fine: a camera on the bypass, fifty-eight where the sign says forty, and I was hauling birch. I went to complain. There is no window. There is a form in the phone, and four minutes later an answer: "Your appeal has been considered." Four minutes. Who considered it? The camera belongs to a firm. The firm, they say, to some fund. Three days I have been angry, and there is nobody to be angry at. Whose face do I punch?

— That is the invention.

— What invention?

— An old one. Older than the cannon. Tomorrow.

— Well, until tomorrow.

— Bye.
