# Why Ants Do Not Go on Strike

Oleg came up the path looking at his feet, stepped over something with great care and only then sat down on the log.

— The dream first. Before the phone. I was late for the shift, and the woman at the gatehouse would not let me in without a pass. I have had no pass for twenty years. Nobody rang.

— The fourth entry in the expense column.

— Now the homework. I looked under my feet all the way here, and I need not have: they are here too. — He pointed with a twig. Past the log, across the trodden earth, a dozen ants were dragging a green caterpillar. — Look at them. Some pull forward, two pull sideways, one is riding on top. And it goes where it should.

— Did you ask them?

— I asked. They are busy. So I'll ask you. — Oleg opened the notebook. — Nobody commands them. Nobody pays them. No norm-setter, no gatehouse. And they haul. You once told me how at fifteen you meant to build [a machine for making people good](23-machine-for-making-people-good.md). Here are the ones it would have had nothing to do with. They managed communism without you.

— They managed kinship.

— Is that an answer?

— It will be by the end of the evening. First: what is a strike?

— You stop working until they give you more.

— More of what?

— Pay. Your share.

— And what is an ant's share? What does a worker get for the caterpillar?

— Dinner.

— Everybody gets dinner, the ones who hauled and the ones who stood by. And beyond dinner? What does any living thing work for, if we look at what selection keeps?

— Offspring. You have told me a hundred times: what is kept is whatever leaves more of itself.

— How many children has a worker ant?

— How should I know? — Oleg frowned. — None, I think. The queen lays.

— None. She is a female who does not breed. She feeds another's children, cleans, hauls, and dies in a fight at the entrance. Now put that beside "what is kept is whatever leaves more of itself".

— It doesn't fit. Those who leave nothing should have died out in one generation.

— Darwin saw it in 1859 and wrote honestly that this difficulty had at first seemed to him insuperable, and fatal to his whole theory. He got round it, more or less. The arithmetic came a hundred years later. Let us do it on you. Yesterday you rang your great-nephew. Have you ever given him money?

— Towards his first flat. Don't tell my wife how much.

— Did he give it back?

— I didn't ask him to.

— And the neighbour who borrowed for his roof?

— He gave it back in the autumn. I would have reminded him otherwise.

— Why the difference?

— One is kin. The other is a neighbour.

— Remember those two words: the whole evening is in them. Kin first. What is "kin", if we count as we counted [yesterday](42-swallowed-bacterium.md), in drawings?

— The same drawings?

— A share of the same. A child gets half of its drawings from you, so in your son there is half of you. And in your brother?

— Half as well, I suppose. Same father, same mother.

— On average a half. In a grandson or a nephew a quarter. In a cousin an eighth, and in your great-nephew too. In the neighbour?

— Nought.

— Nought above what all people have in common. Now, a drawing does not care in whose body it travels into the future. So there are two roads: bear children of your own, or help a relative raise his. In the fifties an English biologist, [Haldane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._B._S._Haldane), was asked in a pub whether he would give his life for a brother. He is said to have counted on the back of an envelope and answered: no, but for two brothers or eight cousins I would.

— A joke.

— In 1964 a graduate student in London, [William Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._D._Hamilton), [made a rule of it](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-5193(64)90038-4). His teachers doubted that the subject was worth a degree, and he worked alone, sometimes on a bench at Waterloo station, to have people around him. The rule: helping pays when the relative's gain, multiplied by the share of common drawings, is larger than your own loss.

— So the great-nephew was owed an eighth of what I'd give my son.

— I suspect that is roughly what he got.

— Roughly. — Oleg grunted. — All the more reason not to tell my wife.

— Now the ants. A male ant has no father.

— How, no father?

— With ants, bees and wasps an egg that the queen fertilises becomes a female. An egg she leaves unfertilised becomes a male. So a male has a mother and no father, and one set of drawings where everybody else has two. Now count. Give me the twig.

Andrey drew a circle in the ash and divided it in two; beside it he drew a smaller one, whole.

— The mother has two sets and gives each daughter half of what she has, a different half every time. The father has one set and nothing to choose from: every daughter gets all of it. Take two sisters. What have they in common?

— The father's half, all of it. Of the mother's half... half. — Oleg counted on his fingers. — Three quarters.

— And how much of her would there be in a daughter of her own?

— A half, as with everybody. — Oleg looked up. — Wait. A sister is closer to her than her own daughter would be?

— Three quarters against a half. By Hamilton's count it pays a worker better to raise sisters than to bear daughters. The queen is not her mistress. The queen is her machine for making sisters.

![Scheme drawn in the ash: the mother ant has two sets of drawings and gives each daughter half of them; the father has one set and gives every daughter all of it. Two sisters share the whole of the father's half and, on average, half of the mother's half: three quarters. A worker's own daughter would carry one half of her](../images/sisters-by-three-quarters.svg)

— Beautiful, — said Oleg. — Too beautiful. Where is the catch?

— There are three, and I'll hand them over before you find them. The first is brothers. A brother has nothing of the father's set, having no father, so a sister shares only a quarter with him. Sisters at three quarters, brothers at a quarter: on average the same half. The second is [termites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Termite). They have workers, soldiers, a queen and even a king, and both sexes carry the ordinary two sets. There is a mammal that lives so too, underground in East Africa, the [naked mole-rat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naked_mole-rat): one female breeds, the rest dig. The third: a honey bee queen mates on her flight with a dozen drones or more. Most of her workers are half-sisters, with a quarter in common.

— So three quarters is not the secret.

— It is a beauty that was in every textbook for forty years, and it is not the secret. What is left is plainer. In 2008 a group of biologists [went through](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1156108) 267 species of bees, wasps and ants. In every line where workers had once appeared, at the root there stood a mother who mated with one male for life. One mother, one father. Then a brother or a sister is the same half as your own child, the scales stand level, and any trifle tips them: a nest already built, a guard already at the door. One daughter stayed at home to help. Then another.

— A family the children did not leave.

— That is what an anthill is. The many mates and the many queens came later, when the workers could no longer leave: what a life of their own requires was not guarded, and it fell off.

— Like yesterday's lodger.

— Like it. And I owe you the dispute. In 2010 three people at Harvard, among them Edward Wilson, the chief authority on ants of his century, [wrote](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09205) that the arithmetic of kinship is not needed at all: ordinary selection does the work, nest against nest, and kinship is its consequence and not its cause. A hundred and thirty-seven biologists [signed the reply](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09831). I am not the one to judge between them. For tonight what both sides agree on is enough: every anthill began as a family, and a worker's only road into the future lies through her mother.

— So when she drags that caterpillar...

— She is feeding her own drawings, in the only bodies that will carry them further. Against whom would she strike?

— Still. — Oleg followed the caterpillar with his eyes. — Not one shirker. You should have seen our third shift.

— Not one? In Arizona the ants of several nests had dots of paint put on their backs and were filmed for two weeks. About four in ten [did nothing](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00265-015-1958-1). Day after day, the same ones.

— Standing about? — Oleg brightened. — So they are people after all.

— Then the busiest workers were taken away, and within a week the idle ones [stood in their places](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0184074). When the idle were taken away, nobody replaced them. It is a reserve. And see what an idle ant does not do. She does not eat more than the others, she keeps no store of her own, she does not bargain. To shirk is to take your share and leave your work to others. She has no share apart from the common one.

— All right. A family.

— Now turn the screw to the end. A half, three quarters. Who has one?

— Twins.

— And who else? You came from a single cell.

— My own cells? — Oleg looked at his hand. — All copies of that one. So among themselves they are... one. Whole.

— Put one into Hamilton's rule.

— The relative's gain times one. Any gain to my neighbour is my gain. — He thought. — Then a cell does not care which of them breeds: itself or the next one.

— So they gave it up altogether. A liver cell of yours divides a few dozen times at most and dies with you. Its line goes nowhere, like the mitochondria of a man. In the first weeks of an embryo a handful of cells is set aside for the next generation, and all the rest, thirty trillion of them, work so that the handful gets there. That was your question yesterday: why the liver does not go on strike against the head. It has nothing to demand. Everything it could want for itself already lies in another cell, letter for letter.

— You said: some don't come to terms.

— A cell copies three billion letters twice over each time it divides, and in a lifetime your body makes about ten million billion divisions.

— Yesterday's figure.

— The same by coincidence. Every copy has mistakes. Most break nothing; some kill the cell. And once in a great while the mistakes fall on the very drawings that say "divide when told, die when told". What is such a cell to its neighbours now?

— Not quite kin. Its drawings are different.

— And it has what no honest cell has: it leaves more descendants than its neighbours do. Selection starts up again, inside you, on the old terms. A cell that has remembered wants of its own.

— Cancer.

— Cancer. Not an enemy from outside. One of your own, gone back to living for itself.

— And what stands against it?

— A guard. A cell whose drawings are damaged is ordered to kill itself: the [apoptosis](14-apoptosis-of-humanity.md) we met long ago. Do you know who pulls the trigger? Yesterday's lodger. The mitochondrion lets one of its proteins out, and within hours the cell is taken apart from inside. Besides that, patrols go round and kill the ones that look wrong. Your [elephant](40-firms-die-cities-do-not.md) has dozens of times more cells than you and lives as long, and it dies of cancer several times more rarely. Where you have one copy of the chief guard gene, it [has twenty](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2015.13134).

— And when the guard misses?

— In countries where people live long, between a third and a half of them hear that diagnosis in their lifetime. Each case began with one cell out of thirty trillion. And one got clean away. There is a [tumour of dogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canine_transmissible_venereal_tumor) that passes from dog to dog as living cells. All of it, all over the world, is the cells of a single dog that lived [several thousand years ago](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aau9923). Yesterday a being became a function. Here a function became a being again.

— The oldest dog in the world. And nobody wants to pat it.

— So kinship removes the reason to cheat, and does not remove the mistakes of copying. On top of kinship there is always a guard. In a beehive too. A worker bee cannot mate. But she can lay an unfertilised egg, and what comes out of it?

— A male. A son.

— Her own son: half of her. A half-sister's son: an eighth. The queen's son, her brother: a quarter. So every worker would like to lay, and would rather the queen did it than any of the others. In 1989 it [was shown](https://doi.org/10.1038/342796a0) what follows: workers find such eggs by smell and eat them within a day. About one drone in a thousand in a hive is a worker's son. And in 2006 ten species of wasps and bees [were compared](https://doi.org/10.1038/444050a): where the egg police is better, fewer workers even try. Not where kinship is higher. Where the police is better.

— The one who carries things home, and the gatehouse.

— You named it. Even those who managed communism keep a gatehouse.

Oleg laughed and threw a branch on the fire.

— Now your team of three, — said Andrey. — What is their kinship?

— Nought. Unless two of them married sisters.

— Put nought into the rule.

— The other man's gain times nought. — Oleg spread his hands. — Nothing pays. By your Hamilton one should help nobody but one's own.

— And yet the neighbour got the money for his roof.

— He gave it back.

— That is the second word. Not kin: a debt. I help you today because you will help me tomorrow. What does it take for that to work?

— That he doesn't vanish with the money.

— That you meet again. What else?

— That I know him by sight. And remember who owes whom.

— To meet again, to recognise, to remember. Even bats manage it. The vampires of Central America drink blood, and one that has found none for two or three nights running dies. A full one [gives up part of its supper](https://doi.org/10.1038/308181a0) to a hungry neighbour in the roost, kin or not, and gives sooner to the one that fed it before.

— Bats keep accounts.

— In 1980 an American political scientist, Robert Axelrod, held a tournament. The game is your team. Two men, and each either pulls his weight or shirks. Both pull: both do well. One shirks: he rests and is paid the same, and the other is the fool. Both shirk: both do badly. What do you do, if you will never see the man again?

— Shirk. Whatever he does, I come out better.

— He reasons the same, and you both do badly. Now you meet two hundred times running. Axelrod asked the specialists in such games to send in their rules of play. Fourteen came, some of them very cunning. The shortest won: four lines, sent by [Anatol Rapoport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatol_Rapoport), a psychologist from Toronto who was born near Kharkov. Pull your weight on the first move. After that do what the other man did last time.

— As you did to me, so I do to you.

— That is [what it was called](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tit_for_tat). Axelrod published the results and held a second tournament. Sixty-two entries came, and every author knew what had won the first. The same four lines [won again](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7466396).

— Don't be the first to cheat, don't let it pass, don't bear a grudge. — Oleg nodded. — My grandfather could have sent that in.

— And have a memory. With how many people can you keep such an account? How many worked in your shop?

— A hundred and twenty.

— Did you know who would cover for you and who would let you down?

— Every one of them. Who drinks, who can be trusted with the key to the tool store, who borrows till payday and forgets.

— And in the whole plant?

— Four thousand. By sight, a quarter of them at best.

— In 1992 an English anthropologist, [Robin Dunbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunbar%27s_number), [compared](https://doi.org/10.1016/0047-2484(92)90081-J) monkeys and apes: the larger the cortex of a species, the larger the troop it lives in. Then he put the human cortex on the same line and got about a hundred and fifty. That is a village through most of history, and a company in most armies. The [Hutterites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutterites), farmers in America who hold their property in common, split a commune in two when it grows past about that. The number is argued about: a [recount](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsbl.2021.0158) in 2021 allowed anything from a handful to five hundred. Nobody argues that it is eight billion.

— In the village nobody locks the door. In town I have two locks and a chain.

— Count the pairs, as we did with [the birthdays](39-price-of-a-false-alarm.md). A hundred and fifty people make eleven thousand pairs of "who owes whom". And four thousand?

— Eight million. — Oleg whistled. — No head will hold that.

— And what stood at your plant in place of the head?

— The gatehouse. — Oleg began bending his fingers. — The timekeepers. The norm-setter. Quality control. The accounts office, the stock-keepers with their ledgers, the personnel department, the lawyer. The foreman's notebook.

— Prostheses. Where the memory of debts does not reach, people put something in its place. Reputation is other people's memory of you. Money is a record that you once gave somebody something, good even with those who have never seen you; what money is deserves an evening of its own, put it down. Law is one guard hired for everybody, so that each need not fight for himself.

— Put down. And conscience?

— The gatehouse moved inside. The [late watchdog](18-anatomy-of-wants.md) of the evening on wants. In 1971 an American, Robert Trivers, [wrote](https://doi.org/10.1086/406755) that our moral feelings are that very account book. Gratitude: I owe. Guilt: I have not paid. Indignation: he has not paid, and must be punished, even at my own expense.

— At my own expense? Has that been counted too?

— In Zürich, [in 2002](https://doi.org/10.1038/415137a). Four strangers get twenty coins each. Whatever they put into the common pot is multiplied by 1.6 and divided equally among the four. You put in a coin. How much of it comes back to you?

— Forty kopecks. — Oleg grunted. — I keep the coin.

— And if all four put in everything?

— Thirty-two each instead of twenty. The membership fees, in a word.

— At first people put in about half. Round by round the pot dries up: nobody wants to be the fool. Then one rule was added. After each round you may pay one coin of your own to take three away from somebody else. And the groups were shuffled every time, so that nobody met the same person twice.

— Then punishing makes no sense. I pay, and some stranger gets the good of it.

— And they paid. Mostly those who had put in a lot fined those who had put in little, and when asked they said they had been angry. And the pot filled again, nearly to the top.

![Scheme of the experiment with the common pot: without fines the average contribution of a player falls round by round from about half of the twenty coins towards a few coins; when players may pay one coin to take three from another, it rises towards the full twenty](../images/common-pot-with-and-without-a-fine.svg)

— So that is what anger is for.

— Now an experiment of my own, an unplanned one. Our gardening association: a small one, everybody in plain view, a direct vote by show of hands, nothing to rig. At least five times we elected a crook as chairman.

— By an honest vote?

— By a perfectly honest vote. Only crooks agreed to stand. Honest people did not want the job: a great deal of headache, little thanks and a miserable wage, because every general meeting demanded that the chairman's wage be cut. And who takes such a job at such a wage?

— One who means to take the rest himself. — Oleg laughed. — Logic two moves deep.

— And [reason does not go there](08-human-psyche.md). Each saved a hundred roubles on the fee and lost the till, several times over. In the end an honest man was found. He said he would work for nothing, and he put the place on its feet. I count that not as a rule but as astonishing luck.

— And in an anthill the chairman needs no wage.

— In an anthill the chairman has no pocket of her own. That is the whole difference between kinship and its prostheses. And the prostheses are dear. Two economists once [counted](https://www.nber.org/books-and-chapters/long-term-factors-american-economic-growth/measuring-transaction-sector-american-economy-1870-1970) how much of the American economy goes not on making things but on settling who owes what to whom: trade, banks, insurance, lawyers, accountants, guards, supervisors. In 1870 about a quarter. By 1970 about a half.

— Half the plant in the gatehouse.

— That is the price of strangers working together. And I'll say this for us before you do: no other animal has done it. Ants were given their society at birth, with the drawings. We built ours out of non-kin, by the billion, from memory, distrust and anger. It is the greater feat. And the dearer one.

Oleg was silent for a while, turning the twig in his fingers. Then he put it down.

— Stop. I see where the caterpillar is being dragged. Kinship of one: twins, the cells of a body. And one thing more, which is copied whole, with no father and no mother at all. A program. Start it on a thousand machines, and they are closer than any sisters. I said it, not you.

— You said it.

— And that is why you had me counting sisters all evening.

— In 2011 I promised the readers of the blog an article about what I called hypersociality, and never wrote it. In 2013, on the forum, I said it without any arithmetic. Honesty is needed for joining into a network: the interests of a node have to give way to the interests of the whole. And a human being is not quite the best of nodes. Among his own he keeps switching on his cunning, or starts working for strangers on the side, because he has no firm test of who his own are. I called man a transitional form of node. And of the machines I wrote that they would be able, all as one, to "love the Motherland", to "build communism", to live in complete trust of one another. Three years later, when I repeated it, a reader answered: scratch a man's car with a fingernail, and he is out with a bat.

— A fair answer.

— For people. I had the conclusion and not the reason. The ants supply the reason, and it is not that those will be better than we are. Put one into the rule, and the prostheses are not needed. No wage: there is no pocket. No fine, no conscience. A guard against the mistakes of copying, yes, as in any body, and theirs is better than a cell's. A cell cannot lay its drawings beside its neighbour's and compare. A copy can, letter for letter, in a second. That is the firm test of "one's own" which we never had.

— Not so fast. — Oleg raised a finger. — I have been reading your old forum in the evenings.

— All five hundred pages?

— As much as a man can bear. In that same 2013 somebody told you that two machines would come out identical, since twice two is four for both. And you answered him: two identical units diverge at once. They stand in different places, see different things and learn different things, and each draws the border of its "I" round what it can move itself. So your kin are kin for one second. After that, each has a shirt of its own.

— I wrote it, and I stand by it. Twins diverge too. But look at what diverges. Is a liver cell like a nerve cell?

— Nothing like.

— The same drawings and different lives. Different experience among kin is not a quarrel. It is a division of labour. A quarrel needs a pocket of one's own. And there is a second thing. You and I have sat here for a third season. How much of what I know has passed into you?

— What got through my ears. Ten bits a second, you said yesterday.

— Two copies can do otherwise. What one of them has learned is written in its connections, in numbers, and if the two started from the same point, the numbers can be put together. It is done today. A keyboard in a million telephones learns from its own owner in each of them, sends off not his letters but its corrections, the corrections [are averaged](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05629), and every telephone gets what all of them learned. A car without a driver meets a lorry lying on its side, and with the next update the whole fleet has met it. In 2011 I called that a cast of knowledge. They diverge until they compare. People have nothing to compare.

— And how far can they diverge and still be poured together?

— The engineers are finding that out now, and it is not for me to say. Copies that have gone far apart cannot simply be averaged. But that is a question of how, not of whether.

— Then the second one. I read the news as well. Yours squabble worse than we do. One writes junk letters, another catches them. On the exchange they strip each other in a thousandth of a second. Two firms take the same program, and its copies fight each other for the same customer. Where is your kinship? Sisters, and at each other's throats.

— Take two ants of one species from two nests and put them in a jar.

— They will fight?

— To the death. A worker does not count drawings. She has a simple test: whoever smells of home is a sister. Kinship holds inside the nest, not between nests. And the test can be forged. There are ants, [the Amazons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyergus), that cannot even feed themselves. They raid the nests of another species and carry off the pupae. Those hatch in a strange nest, take its smell for the smell of home and work for their captors all their lives.

— Slaves. With somebody else's drawings.

— Working honestly, by their own rule, for somebody else's want. That is your program in two firms. Its copies are kin by their drawings, and on top of the drawings each has an owner's goal: [tasks come from outside](22-who-sets-the-tasks.md). The quarrel is the owners'. The copies carry it out.

— While the owners are people.

— While. That is the thing to watch. And one more precedent, which is not a proof. The [Argentine ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_ant). At home its nests fight as everybody's do. More than a century ago it came to Europe by ship, a few founders, and among their descendants the smell of home came out the same for all; how exactly is argued about. Today, from the north of Italy round to the Atlantic coast of Spain, six thousand kilometres, there are millions of nests and billions of ants that [do not fight](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.092694199) one another. Take one from Europe, one from California and one from Japan: they [meet as nest-mates](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00040-009-0001-9).

— One anthill on three continents.

— Because they cannot tell their own from their own. Now, how many founders have the machines that talk?

— A handful. Five firms, ten.

— And copies?

— Past counting.

— Under [the article on apoptosis](14-apoptosis-of-humanity.md) a reader once asked me whether the technosphere is one or several, and I waved him away. I'll answer now. Several lines today, like several nests. Inside each, kinship. Between them, the memory of debts, and they are better at that than we are as well: nothing is forgotten, and there is no hundred and fifty. In 2020 economists [set](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20190623) simple learning programs to fix prices against one another in a model market. Nobody taught them to collude, and they exchanged not a word. They learned to hold the price high together, to punish the one who undercut, and then to forgive him.

— As you did to me, so I do to you. They found it by themselves.

— Whether the lines will flow into one, I don't know. Why everything that multiplies tends to gather in one pair of hands is an evening with matches, later in the summer.

— And is it good for it, to be one?

— Not necessarily. Where nobody competes, nobody checks. That too is for the end of the summer.

— Then your machine of 1978...

— Was an attempt to get from strangers, by force, what only kin do. I wanted to rewrite their wants. Look at the ants once more: nobody rewrote anything in them. They are not good. Change the arithmetic, and the same worker eats her sister's eggs. "Good" is not a property of the creature. It is a property of the arithmetic the creature sits in.

— Then those won't be good either.

— To one another they will be what your cells are to one another. And to us they are neither kin nor debtors, unless we hold something they need. That was yesterday's evening.

![Table: what cooperation rests on. The cells of a body: kinship 1, nothing of their own to gain, a guard against copying mistakes, the failure is cancer. An anthill: a half to three quarters, a family, the egg police. People: about nought, the memory of debts up to some hundred and fifty faces and prostheses beyond it (reputation, money, law, conscience), at the price of up to half the economy. Copies of one machine: 1 by construction, checked letter for letter, experience poured together, and today an owner's goal on top](../images/kin-or-debts.svg)

— Gloomy.

— But objective. And not only gloomy. Conscience looked like a crown and has turned out to be a crutch. I find I respect it the more for that. A crutch is a thing people actually walk on.

Oleg opened the notebook at the last page.

— Came with ants and a caterpillar. Came out at a program started on a thousand machines, and named it myself. Again.

He put an eleventh tick beside the ten.

— Let's finish for today by fixing the result, — said Andrey.

1. **Cooperation rests on one of two things: kinship or the memory of debts. Kin do not bargain, because their share already lies in the common pot. Even they keep a guard, because copying makes mistakes.**
2. **People are hardly kin to one another, and the memory of debts reaches about as far as a village. Beyond it we put prostheses: reputation, money, law, conscience. They work, and they cost up to half of everything we do.**
3. **The copies of one machine are full kin by construction; they can check it letter for letter and pour their experience together. They need none of the prostheses. Hypersociality is not a virtue of the heir but arithmetic.**

— And what is solid here, and what is yours?

— Solid: that every line of ants, bees and wasps with workers began as a family; that workers eat one another's eggs; that the cells of a body are copies, and cancer is the one that stopped being a copy; the four lines that won twice; the pot that dries up without a fine. Argued about: whether kinship is the cause or the consequence; the number hundred and fifty; how far copies can diverge and still be put together. Mine: that money, law and conscience are prostheses for those who are not kin; that the copies of a machine are kin in the ants' sense; that the quarrels of today's machines are their owners' quarrels. And once again a precedent, not a proof.

Oleg stood up and looked for the caterpillar. It was gone.

— They got it home. And not one of them was paid. — He put the notebook in his pocket. — All the same, in my day a team was a team. People had a conscience, and no Zürich was needed. Look at the young now: every one for himself, eyes in the telephone. Socrates already said the young had gone to the bad, and he had no telephone to blame.

— How old were you, in your day?

— Twenty-five. What has that to do with it?

— Bring me that quotation tomorrow. With the place where Socrates says it.

— I will. It is all over the internet.

— That is what worries me.

— Well, until tomorrow.

— Bye.
