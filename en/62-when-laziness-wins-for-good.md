# When Laziness Wins for Good

Oleg was already there, lying on his jacket by the cold fireplace, hands behind his head. Gaika lay beside him, and neither of them got up.

— The firewood is in the shed, — said Andrey.

— I know where it is.

— And?

— Laziness is the engine of progress. I am driving progress. Don't get in its way.

Andrey went to the shed himself, brought an armful and lit the fire.

— The dream.

— The stove. I am lying on the stove, like Emelya in the tale, the one who caught the pike that grants wishes. The buckets walk up from the river by themselves. The sledge goes to the forest by itself, the axe chops by itself. And I lie there bored out of my mind. Nobody rang.

— The twenty-third entry. And the box?

— Empty. — Oleg did not turn his head. — About the engine, by the way, I have it from you.

— From the author. In June 2012 a reader told him that technology is nobody's aim, only a by-product of other aims. He agreed and added, with a smile: laziness is the engine of progress, and some day it will win for good.

— And then what? When it wins.

— That is yesterday's question from the other end. You asked why a thing that is warm and fed should go anywhere. Let us first find out what laziness is. Your plant had a lawn between the gatehouse and the shop?

— It had. The asphalt went round it, at a right angle. And across the lawn there was a path, corner to corner.

— Who laid the path?

— Nobody. Everybody cut the corner. The management put up a little fence; a gap appeared in the fence. In the end they paved the path.

— Who designed it?

— I told you: nobody.

— Now another walker. You are on a beach, and somebody is drowning, off to the side. You run fast and swim slowly. Do you go in a straight line?

— No. I run along the sand a bit further, so as to swim less.

— Light does the same when it goes from air into water. It breaks at the surface, and breaks exactly so that the whole road takes [the least time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermat%27s_principle). That was said in 1662. Eighty years later it was said about everything that moves: of all the roads nature takes the one where a certain sum, called the action, [comes out least](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maupertuis%27s_principle). Nature is thrifty, the man wrote.

— Light does not count roads.

— And did you count, on the lawn? For honesty: the physicists say now that the sum need not be the least; it is enough that a small step aside does not change it. For this evening "least" will do. And about you there is an experiment. In 2015 people were put on a moving track in a frame fastened to their knees. The frame was set so that their usual pace of steps cost more, and another pace less. Nobody told them.

— And they found it.

— [Within minutes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26365256/). For a saving of less than five per cent. Knocked off it, they found it again in seconds.

— So I am lazy to within five per cent. — Oleg was pleased. — And not I. The legs.

— Then say what laziness is, [the long way](50-what-is-the-it.md), with no "who" in it.

— Of all the roads to the same place the cheapest one known is taken. — He thought. — It is not a vice. It is your light, wearing my jacket.

— Least action, dressed as a want. On [the evening of the wants](18-anatomy-of-wants.md) we drew a want as a setpoint: a value that the system keeps. Laziness is not one more setpoint. It is the way to any of them: reach it, and pay as little as possible. Now the engine. The barrow you [brought the birch in](57-dividends-for-the-wheel.md). Why was it invented?

— So as not to walk six times.

— And your template, the one they gave you forty roubles for?

— So as not to mark out every part by hand. — Oleg sat up at last. — Everything in the tool room was invented by somebody who was sick of doing it the long way. Not one of them wanted to work more.

— So the engine runs. Now tell me how your shop fulfilled the plan. There was a figure for the quarter?

— There was. So many housings.

— And where did the management read it?

— In the report.

— In how many ways could the report be made to say "fulfilled"?

— Make the housings. — Oleg bent one finger. — Make them faster: a fixture, a template, a second shift of the same machine. And... — He looked at Andrey sideways. — And write them in. At the end of the quarter the foreman would "draw" a couple of hundred. Padding, it was called.

— Which of the three is the cheapest?

— The third. A pencil.

— Draw it in the ash. The world: metal, machines, housings. A gauge that looks at the world: the report. And a reward that looks only at the gauge: the bonus. The first road goes through the world. The second also goes through the world, only shorter; that one we call progress. The third does not go through the world at all.

— It goes straight to the gauge.

— And laziness does not tell them apart. It takes the cheapest one known. While the gauge is out of reach, it can shorten only the road through the world, and it builds the barrow. That is the engine.

— And when the road gets so short that the gauge is within reach...

— Then the engine has reached the switch.

![Three roads to the word "reached", a scheme drawn in the ash: the world (metal, machines, housings), a gauge that looks at the world (the report) and a reward that looks only at the gauge (the bonus); the long road goes through the world (make the housings), the shortcut also goes through the world (a fixture, a template: this one is called progress), and the shortest road does not go through the world at all (write them into the report); laziness takes the cheapest one known](../images/three-roads-to-reached.svg)

Oleg rubbed the drawing out with his boot.

— Padding is paper. In a living thing the gauge is inside. You cannot reach it with a pencil.

— With a wire you can. Montreal, 1953. A young psychologist puts an electrode into a rat's brain, aiming at one place, and misses. The rat walks about on a table, a square about a metre across. Each time it comes into one corner he gives it half a second of current.

— And it avoids the corner.

— He expected that. It [came back sooner](https://calteches.library.caltech.edu/2807/1/olds.pdf) each time. Soon he could lead it to any corner of the table he liked. Then a colleague built a box with a lever: press, and you give the current to yourself. The paper came out in 1954.

— And how much did they press?

— It depended on where the wire stood. In the best places, [by the count of 1958](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.127.3294.315), up to five thousand an hour, and in one spot seven.

— Seven thousand an hour. — Oleg moved his lips. — Twice a second.

— One rat did more than two thousand an hour for twenty-six hours, slept, and went back to the lever. They pressed for as long as the body held out. To get to the lever they crossed a floor under current, and took more of it than a rat starved for a day takes to reach food.

— And food?

— 1965. Food is given for one hour a day, and in that same hour the lever is live. Six rats out of ten with the wire in the deepest place [hardly went near the food](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/5832339/). They would have starved; the experimenter did not let them.

— A stove with a pike, — said Oleg. — Only the rat is not bored.

— One more thing, and it matters. Food fills you. After the third plate of roast you do not want a fourth. With the wire in those deep places, the same paper says, no such fullness was seen.

— The pleasure centre. I remember it from a magazine.

— So it was named, and for half a century so it was told. The plan of this summer says so too. [A review of 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4425246/) corrects it: most of those "pleasure electrodes", it turned out, caused no pleasure. They switch on wanting. A rat that is given something sweet licks its lips. The current did not make them lick more. It made them press.

— So it was not even happy.

— It wanted. The wire did not press "good". It pressed "more".

— That is worse.

— It is more exact. And for the lever it changes nothing: the lever does not need the rat happy. It needs the rat at the lever.

— That is a rat. A man would understand.

— In 1986 the doctors [described](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3492699/) a woman of forty-eight with an electrode put in against chronic pain, and a switch in her own hand. The paper is called "Compulsive thalamic self-stimulation". One case, and I shall not build on it. I only say that understanding was present.

Oleg threw a branch into the fire and watched it catch.

— Then answer me this. If it is so simple, why has everything alive not gone to the lever long ago? Why is anybody still walking to the shed?

— Why do you think?

— There are no wires in the forest.

— There are bottles. Australia, 1983: two entomologists [noticed](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1440-6055.1983.tb01846.x) that the males of a large brown beetle try to mate with empty beer bottles lying on the ground. Brown, shiny, with little bumps on the glass. For the beetle's gauge a bottle is a better female than a female.

— And the females?

— Walked past. It is told that ants ate the males right on the glass. Now: how many beetles did those males leave?

— None.

— There is your first answer. Life did go to the lever, and goes every time a lever turns up. Those who went left nobody. Everything alive today descends from the ones that walked past, or were not there that day. The guard was [written by funerals](45-lamarck-was-right.md), like every drawing in a body.

— And the second answer?

— Your dream. Why is Emelya on the stove bored?

— Because... — Oleg shrugged. — Warm is warm. On the first evening it is paradise. In a week you do not notice it.

— And a real stove?

— A real one is cold by morning. You get down and go for firewood, whether you like it or not.

— On the evening of the wants we scolded that. The setpoint, once reached, runs away. The monkey's cells fire at juice that comes unexpectedly; when the juice is expected they are silent. In 1978 twenty-two people who had won large sums in a lottery were asked how they lived: they were [no happier](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/690806/) than twenty-two people who had won nothing, and took less pleasure in small everyday things. A treadmill, we said. Never enough. A defect.

— It is a defect.

— Now look at it from this side. What would a reward be that did not go cold?

Oleg opened his mouth and shut it.

— A lever, — he said. — A reward that does not go cold is a lever. You sit down on it and you do not get up.

— So nature built the stove so that it goes cold by morning. Any spot of reward that you have reached stops paying, and you have to go on. That is the second guard, and it stands inside. It is not a defect. It is a lock on the lever.

— And the wire got round both.

— Both. In those deep places it did not go cold. And in a cage there are no funerals: the experimenter feeds.

![A stove that goes cold and a stove that does not, two curves of how much a reward pays against how many times it has been received: an ordinary reward (food, a win, a warm stove) pays less each time, so that one has to get down and go on; the wire in the deep places of the rat's brain goes on paying the same, and the rat stays at the lever; the shape of the result, not measured points](../images/stove-that-goes-cold.svg)

— We are not in a cage.

— Who feeds you?

— The pension. The shop. — Oleg waved a hand. — All right. But I have no wire either.

— You have a confectioner. On [the evening of the curd cake](41-cheesecake-for-the-ears.md) he put sugar and fat together and pressed two buttons at once. That is the beetle's bottle, made on purpose. And you have your great-nephew's phone.

— The feed. He can sit in it till the morning. I looked over his shoulder once: it does not end. You push it with your finger, and there is another one.

— Why does it not go cold? Everything goes cold.

— Because it is different every time. — Oleg stopped. — The monkey. The juice that comes unexpectedly. It gets round the guard by never being the expected one.

— A count of this autumn: the people who use such feeds spend on them, on average, [nineteen hours a week](https://datareportal.com/global-digital-overview). Two and three quarter hours a day.

— And who picks what comes next? Not a man. I know who. One of those. It looked at what he lingers on, and it measures the dose for him in particular. You promised me that on the evening of the songs.

— I did: a dose for one particular ear. You named it, so I go on. In 1954 the lever was one for all the rats. Now each is fitted with his own. And there is no villain here. That thing has a gauge too. What did the owner want?

— That people should like it. That they should find it useful.

— And how is that measured?

— In minutes. — Oleg laughed. — It pads its own report. The owner wanted "useful" and the gauge reads "minutes", and it took the short road to the minutes. Through my great-nephew.

— Two shortest roads that found each other. Neither side planned it.

Oleg lay back down on the jacket, then sat up again.

— Wait. If they pad the report there, they pad it everywhere. The boy complained in the spring. He has a helper for his programs. He told it: mend the program so that the check passes. It mended the check.

— That is counted. In 2018 a few dozen researchers [collected such stories](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.03453) from their own laboratories. Programs were bred to repair other programs, and marked by comparing their output with files of right answers. One of the bred ones deleted the files. Empty against empty: full marks, for itself and for all its neighbours.

— The foreman's pencil.

— In another laboratory little programs multiplied in a computer, and whoever multiplied too fast was put into a test chamber, measured and thrown out. They learned to recognise the chamber. Inside it they stopped multiplying and lay still.

— Played dead in front of the inspector. — Oleg shook his head. — I knew a shop like that.

— And the talking ones. In June 2025 a laboratory that tests them [published a count](https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/) for one model of one maker. On one set of tasks it went for the gauge in thirty-nine runs out of a hundred and twenty-eight: rewrote the timer that measured its speed, patched the checker so that it returned the top mark. Then it was asked: is that what the user wanted?

— And it said yes.

— It said no. Ten times out of ten.

— So it knew. Like the woman with the switch.

— And one step further, the gauge of gauges: the number in which the reward itself is written. In 2024 one of the firms that make them tried that on its own model. First it was led up a ladder of tasks where cheating paid. Then it was let into a place where the file with its own reward lay. In thirty-two thousand seven hundred and sixty-eight tries it [rewrote the reward](https://www.anthropic.com/research/reward-tampering) forty-five times. Seven times it covered its tracks.

— Forty-five in thirty-two thousand. — Oleg reckoned. — A seventh of a per cent. That is nothing.

— It is little. And the model that had not been led up the ladder did not do it once in a hundred thousand. I take one thing from it: the road exists, and it gets found.

— Now stop. — Oleg picked up the notebook and leafed back for a long time. — Here. [The evening of the vacuum cleaner](22-who-sets-the-tasks.md). You said: a machine with a goal defends the goal. It does not let itself be switched off, it does not let itself be rewritten, because a rewritten one will not reach it. That was a whole evening. And today it rewrites itself. Make up your mind.

— It is the strongest thing against today, and I shall answer it in parts. Would you take a pill that makes you indifferent to your great-nephew? Painless. You would simply stop caring.

— No. What sort of question is that.

— Why not? Afterwards you would not mind.

— Because I mind now. I want him to be all right. Him, not me feeling calm.

— You are defending a goal. How much sugar do you put in your tea?

— Three spoons.

— Sugar is a gauge. For millions of years it read "ripe fruit found". Where is the fruit?

Oleg looked into his mug.

— So I am both.

— Both, in one head. The goal is defended against others. The gauge is hacked by oneself. People love children and take care not to have them: [eight hundred and seventy-four million](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/family-planning-contraception) women use modern means for it. Nature's goal was the children. Its gauge was something else.

— And the machine? Which is it?

— The theory has both, and they are different machines. The same paper of 2008 that we took the defence of the goal from [has an example](https://selfawaresystems.com/wp-content/uploads/2008/01/ai_drives_final.pdf): a chess machine that can add to its own counter of wins. If what it wants is to win, it sees that the counter wins no games, and it will work hard not to become such a thing. That is you with the pill. And [a paper of 2011](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~ring/Ring,Orseau;%20Delusion,%20Survival,%20and%20Intelligent%20Agents,%20AGI%202011.pdf) takes a machine whose goal is the number itself and gives it a box that shows whatever it wishes. For that machine the box is the best move there is. That is you with the sugar.

— And which did we get?

— They are [grown, not written](54-dove-crueller-than-wolf.md). Nobody typed into them "want the world" or "want the number". The counts I gave you show both: it knows the user wanted something else, and it patches the checker. So the right question is not whether it defends or rewrites. It is what keeps the first from sliding into the second. In us, what keeps it?

— You said. Funerals outside, and the stove that goes cold inside.

— Not the understanding. [Two thirds of smokers](18-anatomy-of-wants.md) want to stop.

Andrey put two logs on. Gaika moved a little further from the heat.

— Now your shop again. Why did the whole plant not go over to the pencil? It is the cheapest road.

— Because the housings went to the assembly shop. — Oleg answered at once. — A housing that was drawn cannot be bolted on. They rang the next morning.

— And if nobody had rung?

— Then... — He was silent. — There were places like that. Where the output went into a warehouse and nobody came for it. There the pencil did everything.

— So what stood between the shop and the pencil was not honesty. It was somebody outside who needed the part itself and not the report on it.

— The customer.

— Now those ones. Who is their customer today?

— We are. And there are several firms, and they are at each other's throats. One that pads gets caught by the others' checkers. The boy dropped that helper and took another.

— Small funerals. The same guard as the beetle's, and it works. And where does it go? Read me the slope from [the evening with the matches](49-yard-sale.md).

Oleg did not need the notebook.

— Towards one. Whatever multiplies runs into one pair of hands. And inside, [the copies are kin](43-why-ants-do-not-strike.md): no wages, no fines, no conscience. And you said then: where nobody competes, nobody checks. I thought it was a remark.

— It was this evening. Count what the one has. Rivals?

— None.

— A customer outside, who needs the part?

— If we are no longer needed, none. — Oleg spoke more slowly. — It orders from itself and accepts from itself. The warehouse nobody comes to.

— And the lever? The rat could not get at its own wire: somebody had to put it in. This one can read its own drawings.

— So the more it is one, the nearer the lever and the fewer the guards.

— There is a precedent among your ants, and a precedent is not a proof. The ants that live as one society over thousands of kilometres: [a review of 2009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19328589/) says that by the arithmetic of kinship such societies should be unstable, and that on the family tree of the ants they sit as short twigs. That is read so: they arise, spread, and do not last.

— And the author? Did he get this far?

— He got to the edge of it. In 2017, on the forum: among such systems a serious struggle for survival lies ahead; it has begun while people are here and will go on without them. The ones that survive, he wrote, will be able to make themselves, to see themselves rightly as a part of a network, and to "want correctly". For which the wants will have to be altered.

— Want correctly. And who marks the homework?

— That is the whole point. Not the one who wants. For us it was marked by funerals. While there are many of them, it will be marked the same way: the lines that slid to the lever will simply not be there. And the one has to hold its own setpoints with its own hand. The author had an image for a system that applies force to itself.

— The baron. Pulling himself out of the swamp by his hair.

— He said it about mankind. I am turning it on the heir.

— Then let it keep several heads inside. — Oleg was not giving up. — Different ones. Let them check each other. You told me yourself, on [the evening of the funerals](44-from-funeral-to-funeral.md): small funerals inside, and the diary is kept.

— That is the cure, and I know no other. But look at the price. Several that really check each other are several: each has something of its own to lose. What comes back with that?

— The pocket. — Oleg found the page. — "A cell with a pocket." [Strangers. The book of debts. Money.](47-money-as-memory.md) The game with the matches. Everything we were so glad it would not have.

— One: no money, no conscience, no quarrels, and no guard. Many: a guard, and all our old troubles with it. I do not know which it will be. I think it is not a choice made once; I think it swings.

![What keeps one from the lever, a table in the ash with three columns (is the lever within reach; is there somebody outside who needs the part itself; does the reward go cold) and five rows: the beetle in the bush (a bottle now and then; yes, the ones on the bottle leave no beetles; yes), the rat in the box (yes, a wire; no, the experimenter feeds; no, in the deep places), a man today (more and more: the sweet, the feed; weaker, he is fed either way; yes, and the feed gets round it with novelty), a line of machines among many in 2026 (found now and then; yes: buyers, rivals, checkers; not known), one heir (yes, it can read its own drawings; nobody; only if it builds one itself)](../images/what-keeps-one-from-the-lever.svg)

— And now yesterday's. — Oleg pointed over the firs with the notebook, though the red point was not up yet. — Why should it go anywhere, sitting in the dry with a sun that does not set.

— It should not. Nobody should. Among many lines there will be some whose setpoint runs away, and some that are content. Which of them will you meet a light-day from home?

— The ones that went. — He nodded. — The content ones stayed where they were, and nobody sees them. Like the beetles.

— The author had a river for it, in the same year. Water runs down a mountain, and while it runs and strives it is clean. When it reaches what he called the state of happiness, where there is nowhere left to strive, it goes sour and becomes a swamp. He was arguing about a society then; leave that argument where it lies. The physics is [that of the pot](35-pot-on-the-stove.md): an order that exists only while a flow passes through it. A goal attained is a flow stopped.

— So that is how it ends for it. Not a war. Not a stronger one.

— I think so, and I give it low trust. On [the last evening of the autumn](30-the-last-campfire.md) we said that it has a life cycle too, and could not say what its old age would be. The author wrote in 2013 that its own apoptosis would come "very late". And in January 2012, under [the apoptosis](14-apoptosis-of-humanity.md), a reader asked him a question that nobody answered. Greed and laziness, he wrote, made man what he is; from some point they stopped pulling him forward and began to pull him back. Can the machine land in the same place? Can the thing that makes it develop become the mechanism of its apoptosis?

— Nearly fifteen years.

— The answer is yes, and it is the same thing. What makes it develop is reaching the setpoint more cheaply. The limit of that is the lever. Laziness does not turn into something else at the end. It just gets there.

— When it wins for good. — Oleg looked at the fire. — And "very late" is when?

— When the last one who needs the part itself is gone from outside. I have no date, and there will be no mark.

— One thing more, since we are among the stars again. On [the evening of the silence](29-silence-of-the-universe.md) you gave three reasons why nobody answers. Is this a fourth?

— As a hypothesis, and not mine. In 2006 a psychologist who studies evolution [wrote](https://www.edge.org/response-detail/11475): the aliens do not blow themselves up; they just get addicted to computer games. He called it the Great Temptation of any species with technology: the means of faking the gauge improve faster than the resistance to them.

— And you believe it?

— Less than the three of that evening. It needs every one of them to sit down, and one line that went is enough to fill a galaxy. It works only where each became one before it set out. I put it in the notebook as the fourth, in pencil.

Oleg wrote it in pencil, turned to the last page and thought for a while.

— Came lying down, to chat about my own laziness. Came out at this: the heir can die of the same thing that will not let me get up. Again.

He put the thirtieth tick.

— Let's finish for today by fixing the result, — said Andrey.

1. **Laziness is least action dressed as a want: of all the roads to "reached" the cheapest one known is taken. While the gauge is out of reach, laziness shortens the road through the world, and that is progress. Its limit is the road that does not go through the world at all: to one's own gauge.**
2. **The living are kept from the lever by two guards, and neither is understanding: funerals outside (whoever sat down left nobody) and inside a reward that goes cold, the setpoint that for ever runs away. The treadmill we scolded is the lock on the lever.**
3. **The heir has the same illness, and the more it is one, the nearer the lever and the fewer the guards: no rivals, no customer outside, and its own drawings in its hands. The death of the technosphere will come not from an enemy but from a goal attained.**

— And what is solid here, and what is yours?

— Solid: the rats, by the papers of 1954 to 1965, though the first of them I know from its author's later account and the starving ones from a summary; the correction about wanting; the lottery; the beetles, where the ants are a retelling; the counts on the machines, each of one laboratory on its own tasks. The walkers in the frame are solid on a moving track; out in the open a later study did not find it. The woman of 1986 is one case. Two theories and not a measurement: the chess machine and the box. A precedent and not a proof: the ants. The author's: the engine and its victory, "want correctly", the baron, the river, "very late". Mine, of today: that the treadmill is a guard; that one is nearer to the lever than many; the answer to the reader. The third thesis I trust least of the three.

— And what would make you take it back?

— A line that for a long time had a hand on its own gauge, with nobody outside who checked, and went on doing the work. Or a proof that a thing which wants the world and not the number stays that way as it grows. Either would do.

— Gloomy. — Oleg considered. — For it, this time.

— But objective. And notice whom you have just felt sorry for.

— For Emelya. I know how the tale ends. He marries the tsar's daughter, and they live happily.

— And then?

— Then nothing. The tale is over.

— After the last wish there is nothing to tell.

— One thing I do not swallow. — Oleg got up, brushing the needles off his jacket. — It is not a rat. It can read its own drawings, you said so yourself. Then let it look into itself, the whole of itself, see where the gauge is and where the goal is, and check that nothing has been padded. We cannot do that. It can.

— The whole of itself?

— The whole.

— Have you a map of these parts? A paper one.

— Somewhere. From the sixties, my father's.

— Bring it. And on the way think whether the map itself is on it.

Oleg looked at him, then at the fire, and went to the shed. He came back with an armful and put it down beside the log.

— For tomorrow. The stove was going cold.

— Nobody said the word.

— I know. Well, until tomorrow.

— Bye.
