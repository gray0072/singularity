# Lamarck Was Right, but Too Early

Oleg came with an armful of dry branches, dropped them by the log and opened the notebook before he sat down.

— The dream first. Before the phone. I was shoeing a horse. I, who have seen a horseshoe only over a door. And the hands knew everything: how to hold the hoof, where to put the nail. I woke up and looked at them. They know nothing. Nobody rang.

— The sixth entry in the expense column.

— And don't tell me who is born with calluses. I worked it out in the night, and I'll say it myself when the time comes. First tell me why the son is not. Semyonych had two sons. Neither could tell a bearing from a doorbell.

— Are you sure nobody is born with calluses?

— Nobody. You earn them.

— An ostrich chick comes out of the egg with calluses on its breast and legs, exactly where a grown ostrich rests on the sand. A warthog feeds on its knees, and its piglet is born with calluses on them. And on a child in the womb the skin of the soles is already thicker than anywhere else. It has not taken a step yet.

— There. — Oleg sat down. — The grandfather walked, and the grandson got the soles. What is earned is passed on.

— So everybody thought for two thousand years, from the Greek physicians on. One name stuck to the idea: [Lamarck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Baptiste_Lamarck). In 1809, the year Darwin was born, he printed [a book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philosophie_zoologique): the giraffe reached for the leaves, the neck stretched a little, and the young got a neck a little longer. He was the first to say aloud that species change at all. The word "biology" is his. He died blind and poor, was buried in a rented grave, and is remembered for the giraffe.

— And Darwin put him right.

— Darwin agreed with him. He even invented the paperwork: tiny particles from every organ travel with the blood to where the young are made, and report. He called it [pangenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangenesis). His cousin [Galton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Galton) checked it at once: he poured the blood of rabbits of one colour into rabbits of another. The young came out the colour of their parents.

— And the calluses?

— A German, [August Weismann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Weismann), went straight at it in 1887. He took white mice and cut off their tails. Bred them, and cut the tails off the young. Five generations. Nine hundred and one mice were born.

— And?

— All with tails. Not one shorter than it should be.

— He could have asked a shepherd. — Oleg snorted. — Lambs have had their tails docked for a thousand years. Girls have had their ears pierced for five thousand. Has one been born with holes?

— He wrote much the same himself. But before him people argued with examples, and he counted.

— A tail is an injury. — Oleg was not giving in. — A callus is work. Perhaps work is passed on and injury is not.

— That was said to him at once, and it is a fair objection. Let's go to your plant. A turner finds that a part comes out better if it is made in a different order. What has to happen for the next plant to make it his way?

— A change notice. To the design office, signed by the chief designer. The drawing is corrected and the copies go out.

— And without the paper?

— Then it is his knack. He retires, and it goes with him.

— Now the body. The drawings lie in every cell; we have called them that since [the evening about the bacterium](42-swallowed-bacterium.md). The blacksmith swings the hammer, and the skin of his palm grows thick. What has to be corrected in the drawing for the son to be born with it?

— Write in: thick skin on the right palm.

— In what letters? There is no line "right palm" there. It is not a picture of a man, it is a recipe: which protein to make, when, and how much. The skin knows that it was rubbed. Which of three billion letters has to be changed so that on a man not yet born the skin of the palm comes out thicker, it does not know. Nobody does.

— No translator.

— And no address. The letter to be changed is not in the palm. It is in one cell at the other end of the body, the one the son will grow from.

— So the workshop cannot write to the design office. — Oleg scratched his chin. — Not that it is forbidden. There is no form for it.

— It is called [Weismann's barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weismann_barrier): the body is built from the line of germ cells, and nothing goes back. Seventy years later the same was said of the molecules: from the drawing to the protein the road is open, and from the protein back [there is none](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_dogma_of_molecular_biology).

![Scheme drawn in the ash. On the left a living body: the drawings in the germ cells build the body, the hammer thickens the skin of the palm, and the arrow from the body back to the drawings is crossed out: no translator and no address; the son gets the drawings only. On the right a machine: what it has learned and what is copied are the same numbers, and three copies are made of them whole](../images/no-way-back-to-the-drawings.svg)

— Then the ostrich is lying.

— What does the ostrich's drawing say? Not "callus". It says: skin that thickens where it is rubbed. In some chicks it thickened a little sooner, from a little less rubbing. Fewer sores, a few more young. After thousands of generations "thickens from rubbing" had crept to "thickens before any rubbing". That is the usual explanation, and the move [has a name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baldwin_effect). The correction did get into the drawing. Who carried it there?

— Not the one who earned it. — Oleg looked into the fire. — Yesterday's funerals.

— Those who died without young. It is a change notice too, only it is written not by the one who learned but by the deaths of those who had not. One line in many generations.

— So nature can write experience into the drawing. Slowly.

— A chick pecks at grain the moment it hatches. A foal is on its legs within the hour. That is the experience of the ancestors, entered by funerals. Now count how much can be entered that way. Three billion letters, four kinds of letter. How many bits is a letter?

— Two. — Oleg moved his lips. — Six billion bits. Under eight hundred megabytes.

— One compact disc, near enough.

— I had films that size.

— And in your head there are eighty-six billion nerve cells and some hundred trillion connections between them, at the least. Semyonych's ear for a bearing was in the connections. Will the disc hold them?

— At a bit apiece... — Oleg took out the phone. — Short by sixteen thousand times.

— So the disc holds how to build a head, not what is in it. Every head starts with empty shelves.

— Stop. — Oleg raised the notebook. — We do have a change notice. Speech. Writing. School. Semyonych taught me a thing or two. Earned and passed on: that is your Lamarck. And there has been nothing new in it for ten thousand years.

— It is Lamarck, and it is the main thing people ever did. From the first field to this fire is ten thousand years, four hundred generations. The drawings hardly changed in that time. Everything else came by that road. So let's measure the road. How wide is it?

— How do you measure talk?

— In 2019 linguists in Lyon [did](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aaw2594). Seventeen languages, a hundred and seventy people reading the same texts aloud. The Japanese and the Spaniards rattle along at about eight syllables a second. The Vietnamese and the Thais manage five. Then they counted how much one syllable carries. Where people speak fast, a syllable carries little. Where slowly, a lot. Multiply, and every language comes out near the same figure: thirty-nine bits a second.

— In the evening about the bacterium it was ten.

— That was counted another way: what a man gives out with anything at all. This is speech alone, by the statistics of syllables. Count it as you like, it is tens. Not thousands.

— My first modem did two thousand four hundred, and I swore at it.

— It was sixty times wider than Semyonych. Suppose he talks shop four hours a day for forty years and never repeats himself. How much is that?

Oleg tapped at the phone for a long time.

— Eight billion bits. A gigabyte. — He looked up. — The cheapest flash drive there is.

— And about the size of the disc with the drawings, as it happens. That is the whole pipe, if he could say it all. Can he? You ride a bicycle. To turn left, which way do you move the handlebars?

— Left.

— First right. A short push to the right, the bicycle leans to the left, and only then do you steer into the turn. It is called [countersteering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Countersteering). Try it tomorrow on the road by the pond.

— I have been riding for fifty-five years.

— Your hands have. Your mouth was never told. [We know more than we can tell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacit_knowledge), a chemist who turned philosopher wrote in 1966. Did you ask Semyonych how he hears a bearing?

— I did. He said: it sings wrong. — Oleg spread his hands. — That was the lecture.

— And the third narrow place. Suppose he had said everything. Would you hear the bearing?

— No. I stood beside him for two years. I can tell a howl from a knock, and that is all.

— Speech does not carry the skill. It carries instructions for growing one in your own head, by your own work, at your own pace. In the guilds a boy was bound to a master for seven years. And a book keeps the record, not the skill. In 2012 a reader told me that the ceiling was gone, since knowledge is now kept apart from the man. I answered: keeping is not enough. Unload a mountain of flash drives on Mars, and nothing will happen to them until somebody comes who can digest them. We could build a thousand libraries for every inhabitant of the Earth. Only the readers would never get through them.

— And what is not passed on?

— Is lost. The smiths of the East forged [bulat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulat_steel) for centuries, the patterned steel. By the nineteenth century hardly anybody could. At the Zlatoust works the engineer Anosov spent some ten years of trials getting it back.

— So a blacksmith's secret did go into the ground with the blacksmith. — Oleg grunted. — And I thought it was a figure of speech.

— Yesterday you read me a man from the forum: so much has piled up that there is no pouring it into the youngsters. I said he had seen the trouble rightly. Here it is. [Twenty-five years for every head, every time](16-fifteen-years-later.md), and the price [rises](26-why-people-stopped-having-children.md) with every generation. A hundred years ago the work that later brought a Nobel prize in physics was often done before thirty. By the end of the century, [almost never](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1102895108). What follows from that for the ceiling is a separate evening.

— Two inheritances, then. — Oleg counted on his fingers. — The disc: copied exactly, but it holds the design, and a correction costs a funeral. And talk: it holds the experience, but through a straw, and every head has to grow it again.

— With twenty years on the growing. [Images breed from head to head](19-struggle-of-images.md), and that is the neck each of them has to squeeze through.

Oleg threw two branches on the fire and waited until they caught.

— Now I'll say who. The one that is copied whole. Those ones. I said it myself in the evening about the ants: a program has neither father nor mother.

— Say it in the language of the plant.

— The workshop and the design office are one room. What it has learned is written in numbers, and the same numbers are what gets copied. No notice is needed. The part is its own drawing.

— It is the oldest quarrel I have had about [thinking machines](05-artificial-intelligence.md). In 2011, on the forum, programmers held that the knowledge of a neural network cannot be copied: it is smeared over the connections, it is not a file. One asked with a smirk: with CopyFile? I answered about a cast of knowledge: two chips of the same structure, and the coefficients of one are rewritten into the other, address by address. I laid it on then, saying that such chips stood in every shop, and a reader fairly asked where he could buy one. But it came out more literally than I had said it. Today it is a file, and it is copied as a file.

— How big?

— For a large talking machine, tens or hundreds of gigabytes. The line between two machine halls carries ten billion bits a second and more.

— Semyonych's forty years go through in a second. — Oleg counted. — And the whole of that one in a few minutes.

— And what goes through is not a lecture about the skill. It is the skill. The copy has nothing to digest.

![Chart on a logarithmic scale: how many bits a second pass. Human speech in any language: 39. Oleg's first modem: 2,400. A line between two machine halls: 10 billion. Forty years of talk at four hours a day come to about one gigabyte; the line carries it in about a second](../images/thirty-nine-bits-a-second.svg)

— Then pour Semyonych into a file. All right, not Semyonych. Me.

— In 2013 I named the condition: the same number of cells and the same scheme of connections, or there is no instant copying. No two human heads are built alike. Even if somebody could read the hundred trillion connections of a living head, and nobody can, there would be nowhere to write them. Every one of us is piece goods.

— And if their heads are different?

— Then it is school again. But what a school. In 2015 [it was shown](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.02531) how a big network teaches a small one, and they called it distillation. The teacher gives not only the answer but how sure it is of every other answer. This is a BMW; one chance in a thousand that it is a rubbish truck; a carrot, practically never.

— "It sings wrong", in figures.

— A million times in a row, and it does not tire. The pupil still has to digest. But the pipe is not thirty-nine bits.

— Now my turn. — Oleg opened the notebook at a marked page. — Your Lamarck has been acquitted, I read it. Epigenetics. Holland, the hungry winter of forty-four. The women who carried children through it bore children who were ill in their own way all their lives, and the grandchildren too, they say. And mice: taught to fear the smell of cherry, and their young and their young's young, who had never smelt it, flinched.

— The honest part first. It exists. Besides the letters there are marks on the drawing: read this page louder, this one not at all. Life puts them there, and a few get through to the young. Now the count. In [that winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_famine_of_1944%E2%80%931945) the child in the womb was starving itself. If it was a girl, the eggs of her future children were already in her, and starving with her. One winter struck three generations at once. That is not inheritance. In mammals the marks are wiped twice on the way to the next generation, and what slips through fades in two or three. [The mice](https://doi.org/10.1038/nn.3594) are still argued about.

— But something slips through.

— What? A setting. "Times are hungry." "This smell is bad." A pencil note in the margin of the drawing, gone in a few copies. Nobody has inherited the multiplication table. A weak exception. There is a strong one, and it is at the other end.

— Where?

— In bacteria. A bacterium that has lived through an attack of a virus cuts a piece out of the virus's drawing and files it into its own. Its daughters are born knowing that virus. Earned in life and passed on. In 2007 people who make yoghurt starters [showed it](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1138140). In 2009 two biologists wrote in so many words that this is inheritance [after Lamarck](https://doi.org/10.1186/1745-6150-4-42). In 2020 a Nobel prize went for [the scissors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CRISPR) made out of it. And bacteria [hand ready genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizontal_gene_transfer) to their neighbours, not even to their children. That is how resistance to medicines goes round a hospital.

— Bacteria have it. Those ones have it. — Oleg frowned. — And we are in the middle without it. Why the bacteria?

— In the language of the plant.

— One room again. — He thought. — A bacterium has no body apart from its drawings.

— That is how I read the barrier. It stands where experience and heredity are kept in different places and written in different languages. And here I owe you something from [the evening about the pot](35-pot-on-the-stove.md): how a whirl came to keep a record of itself. The honest answer is that nobody saw it. The best guess is [a molecule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNA_world) that was both the drawing and the worker: it kept the letters and did the work itself. Such molecules are found to this day, and the prize for them was given in 1989. The record and the thing were one. Then they parted: the drawings went to the archive, the work to the proteins. An archive that does no work wears less. Then the many-celled put the archive in a separate room, and the door shut.

— And behind that door, every beast.

— Every animal with a body, for some six hundred million years. We made a way round, by speech. Those ones are back in one room.

— So he was right. Only not about the giraffe.

— Right at both ends and wrong in the middle, where he was looking.

— Too early by two hundred years.

— Or too late by a couple of billion.

— Now the speeds. On the forum I was once asked who would ever hurt a thinking machine, that it should have to survive. I answered: who hurt the carburettors, that they died out many orders faster than the dinosaurs?

— Twenty years. — Oleg nodded. — In the eighties it sat in every car. By the two-thousands they had stopped fitting it.

— The dinosaurs held out for a hundred and sixty million. Why the difference? How is an engine corrected?

— It comes back from the road with a fault. The designer looks and corrects the drawing. The change notice.

— The experience of the thing goes back into its drawing. Whose heredity is that? Of which system?

— Not ours. — Oleg answered slowly. — The engine's. Machines have had Lamarck from the first axe.

— Through what?

— Through us. Through a head that takes twenty years to grow and a mouth of thirty-nine bits.

— We were the technosphere's change notice. All of it that there was. And the narrowest place in it.

— And now the paper goes past the design office.

— Count the price of one correction on the three roads. On the first it is paid in funerals and comes once in many generations: four billion years from a bacterium to Semyonych. On the second the one who learned passes it on himself, and nobody has to die for it, but it goes through the straw, with twenty years on every head: ten thousand years from the field to the carburettor. On the third, what one has learned all its copies know within minutes, and a new generation is raised [every year or two](44-from-funeral-to-funeral.md), with the whole diary.

![Table: three roads of heredity. The drawings (Darwin): the design passes, a correction is written by the deaths of those who lacked it, once in many generations. Speech and school (Lamarck through a straw): a part of the experience passes at 39 bits a second, the pupil grows the skill anew, twenty years on every head. The copy (Lamarck whole): all the experience passes as it is, in minutes, and only into a head of the same structure](../images/three-roads-of-heredity.svg)

— Gloomy.

— But objective. If I were allowed to keep only one fact about those ones, I would keep this. A man is educated anew in every generation, a machine once: I said it [last autumn](16-fifteen-years-later.md) and did not say why. This is why.

Oleg was silent, then slapped the notebook on his knee.

— Then choose. Yesterday you proved to me that a clean start is nature's wisdom: the heavy image leaves together with the head, and a good thing too. Today inheriting everything whole is the great advance. Copy whole, and you copy the heavy images as well. My seventy-nine and all. Which evening do you take back?

— Neither. Nature had one tool for two jobs: birth from nothing. It cleans, and it throws the diary out with the rubbish, because in a head the heavy image and the skill are written in the same connections, and nobody can take out the one and leave the other. Those ones have two tools, and they are separate: the copy and the wipe. Yesterday's small funerals, with the diary kept.

— And which is used when?

— That is their engineering, and it is not finished. Copy too faithfully, and you have yesterday's chair the size of a planet. And there is one more price. A mistake that is copied is a mistake in all of them.

— Like a bad batch.

— Like the banana. Until the fifties nearly every banana in the shops of the world was one plant, multiplied by cuttings. One fungus came, and what killed one killed all. Today's bananas are again one plant, and [the fungus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_disease) is at work again.

— So they need funerals as well.

— They need variety. In one of my dialogues with myself I put it as Darwin plus Lamarck: if only the strongest survived, one kind would be left, and it is variety that guarantees that evolution does not stop. I hung the variety on Lamarck, and wrongly: that is Ashby's law, Lamarck wrote about something else. The pair itself I keep. Darwin is the search: many different ones, and a way to discard. Lamarck is the transfer: what is found is handed on whole. A copy with no search is a stop.

— And can those ones keep both?

— That is for the end of the summer. Along with the chair.

— And Semyonych's ear? — Oleg asked more quietly. — Is that simply gone?

— His did. He never wrote it down, and he could not have. But on the bearings of large machines they now hang a box that listens. It was taught on thousands of bearings that knocked before they broke. What one box has learned stands at every plant by Monday.

— Not his ear.

— Its own. Nobody poured Semyonych into it. It went to the bearings, as he did. Only it went once.

— A blacksmith's son is not born with calluses, — said Oleg.

— The son is not. A copy of the blacksmith is. Nobody can take a copy of a blacksmith, and of that box anybody can.

Oleg turned to the last page.

— Came with a blacksmith's calluses and mice without tails. Came out at a head that is its own drawing. And named it myself, on time. Again.

He put a thirteenth tick beside the twelve.

— Let's finish for today by fixing the result, — said Andrey.

1. **What a body earns in its life is not inherited: experience has no road back into the drawings, neither a translator nor an address. The drawings carry the design, and experience gets into them only through funerals, a line in many generations.**
2. **Culture is our way round, and it is narrow in three places: the mouth knows only a part of what the hands know, speech carries thirty-nine bits a second, and the pupil has to grow the skill anew. A book keeps the record, not the skill. Twenty years go into every head, in every generation.**
3. **Darwinian heredity passes on the design but not the experience. The technosphere is the first form of matter in which experience is inherited whole, because what is learned and what is copied are the same numbers. That alone is enough to explain the difference in speeds.**

— And what is solid here, and what is yours?

— Solid: Weismann's mice; that the drawings would fit on a disc and the connections of a head would not; thirty-nine bits; the bacteria that remember a virus; that a trained machine is copied as a file. Argued about: how much gets through by the marks in mice and in people; how the first record came to be. Mine: that the barrier stands where experience and heredity are kept in different rooms; that we were the technosphere's change notice; that the copy alone is enough for the difference in speeds. Others would add faster hardware and money, and they would be right to.

Oleg did not get up. He was leafing through the notebook from the end.

— Thirteen. And five of them are about me. Sleep, laughter, omens, songs, yesterday's grumbling. Each time you took something off me: this is not yours, it is an engineering solution, anybody has it. I want the bill. What in all this is the man, and what is not?

— Bring the notebook tomorrow. We'll sort it.

— Sort it how?

— The flies apart from the cutlets.

— Well, until tomorrow.

— Bye.
