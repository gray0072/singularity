# Why Only Robots Live on Mars

Oleg came late, when the reddish point had already climbed over the firs. Gaika lay down where the light ended.

— The dream, — said Andrey.

— The railway station. I put the suitcase into the carriage, step down for a pie, and the train goes. The suitcase has left for the sea, and I am standing on the platform in my slippers. Nobody rang.

— The twenty-second entry. And the box?

— Empty. — Oleg sat down and pointed over the firs with the notebook. — Now that one. Yesterday you said: somebody is there, and no people. Who?

— Count them. On the ground, two: one has been driving since August 2012, the other since February 2021. Overhead, [six more go round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_missions_to_Mars), if all of them still answer. One fell silent last December and was written off in the summer.

— Eight.

— Eight. The younger of the two on the ground has had a sore throat for a month: its main radio failed, and it speaks to the Earth directly, in a whisper. The whole population of the planet. Not one of them breathes.

— That is not a population. — Oleg waved the notebook. — That is equipment. Our eyes, carried out on a long pole. People are there. Through them.

— "Our eyes." Write that down and leave the line open. We shall come back to it.

Oleg wrote it down.

— And my question is yesterday's. Calculating is theirs, agreed. But going, standing on the spot, sticking in a flag: that is ours. In the spring four of ours flew round the Moon. I watched it with my great-nephew, the whole of it.

— How far did they go?

— Round the Moon. Further than anybody before them, they said.

— [Four hundred and six thousand kilometres](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-welcomes-record-setting-artemis-ii-moonfarers-back-to-earth/) from the Earth, on the sixth of April. The record before that was of 1970, and six thousand kilometres shorter. How long does light take over that distance?

— From the Moon? A second and a bit.

— One and a third. In the whole of history that is the furthest a human being has been from home: a second and a third, by light. How many have been that far?

— The Americans, in my school years. A dozen?

— Twelve walked there. Twenty-four flew that far, and four more this spring: [twenty-eight people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Apollo_astronauts). The last one stepped off the Moon in December 1972.

— I was in the second form.

— And now the other count. In September 1977 a craft was launched: less than a tonne, with a memory smaller than one photograph in your phone. Ten days ago, on the eighteenth of November, it [reached the distance](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/where-are-voyager-1-and-voyager-2-now/) that light covers in a day. Twenty-five point nine billion kilometres.

— A second and a third. And a day. — Oleg turned the notebook over. — Eighty-six thousand four hundred seconds in a day. Divide... Sixty-four thousand times further.

— For honesty: it crossed that mark ill. Since the fourteenth nothing has come from it but the bare tone of the transmitter, and the engineers are looking for the cause. It may be that this was its last mark. Forty-nine years on the road.

![How far a man has been and how far a machine, a logarithmic scale of distance from the Earth in kilometres: the orbital stations at about 400 km, where about a dozen people are living today; the Moon, the furthest a human being has ever been, 406,771 km in April 2026, twenty-eight people in all of history; Mars, from 56 to 400 million km, eight working machines and no people; the craft of 1977 at 25.9 billion km, one light-day, reached on 18 November 2026](../images/how-far-a-man-and-how-far-a-machine.svg)

— Don't compare. — Oleg shook his head. — It was thrown and it flies. A stone also flies. A man goes and comes back.

— That is the right place to dig. You are going fishing for three days. What do you take?

— Food. Water, if the lake is a bog. A warm jacket. Matches. And petrol for the way back.

— Air?

— Air is there.

— Why is it there?

— Because... — Oleg shrugged. — It is everywhere.

— Everywhere within some five kilometres upward. Now the same list for a road of two and a half years. Your plant had a supply department; count as it counted. A man [breathes](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/eclss-technical-brief-ochmo.pdf) about a kilogram of oxygen a day. With drinking, food and washing, about four litres of water. Food. Warmth. Pressure, or his blood boils.

— Water can be turned round. I read that on the station they drink it a second time.

— [Ninety-eight per cent](https://www.nasa.gov/missions/station/iss-research/nasa-achieves-water-recovery-milestone-on-international-space-station) of it, since 2023. And the plant that turns it round is carried too. Then what cannot be put in a box. The instrument that rode to Mars inside the older of the two [measured the road](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/data-from-nasa-rovers-voyage-to-mars-aids-planning/): there and back, two thirds of a sievert. The agency that sent it allows its people [six tenths](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/radiation-protection-technical-brief-ochmo.pdf) for a whole career.

— The road alone is more than a life's allowance.

— Without one day on the ground. And in weightlessness the bones lose a per cent or more a month. And the ticket back: the petrol for it has to be carried there, and the petrol for carrying that petrol.

— And how much does it all weigh?

— It was counted, in [a plan of 2008](https://www.nasa.gov/pdf/373669main_2008-12-04_Mars_DRA5_Executive_Summary-Presentation.pdf): six people, about nine hundred days. From eight hundred and twenty-five to twelve hundred and fifty tonnes, already lifted into orbit round the Earth. Before the start.

— Over a hundred tonnes a head. — Oleg whistled. — For a hundred kilos of man with his boots.

— Now the same list for the one with the sore throat.

— Air, no. Water, no. Food... what does it eat?

— A lump of plutonium that is warm by itself: a hundred and ten watts, less than your kettle takes.

— Pressure, no. The dose?

— It minds it less, and nobody counts its career. And the ticket back?

Oleg was silent.

— Nobody bought it one, — he said at last. — And nobody called that cruelty.

— One of the earlier ones was built for ninety days. It worked five thousand one hundred and eleven. When there is nothing to do, such a thing is told to sleep, and it sleeps for a month and spends almost nothing. Try that with a crew.

![What the road asks for, a table in the ash with two columns, a man and a machine: air, about a kilogram of oxygen a day against none; water and food, about four litres a day and a plant to turn them round against a lump of plutonium giving 110 watts; the dose on the road there and back, 0.66 sievert against a career limit of 0.6, while the machine minds it less; waiting, where a man eats and breathes every day and a machine sleeps for a month; the way back, which a man must have and a machine does not; and the sum, from 825 to 1,250 tonnes in orbit for six people against one tonne for one rover](../images/what-the-road-asks-for.svg)

— So we are weaklings.

— I did not say that, and I do not think it. On [the evening of the flies and the cutlets](46-flies-apart-from-cutlets.md) we separated a task from a way. What is the task there?

— To get there. To look. To poke it with [the stick](58-blind-mans-stick.md) and send back what came out.

— And our way of doing that task?

— To carry... — Oleg looked at his list. — The air, the water, the warmth, the pressure. The lake. To carry the lake along.

— A man does not go into space. A small piece of the Earth goes, with a man inside. A spacesuit is the Earth cut to his size. Yesterday we found a strip of sizes that our hands have felt out, from a hair to the horizon. Here is its sister: a strip of conditions. A few kilometres of air, some tens of degrees, one weight, and a roof of air and magnet against the dose. We do not notice it for the same reason that you did not put air on the list.

— Because it is there for nothing.

— On the Earth nearly everything is there for us for nothing. That is a head start. On [the first evening of that autumn](16-fifteen-years-later.md) we wondered why machines were so slow with a staircase and a door handle: the staircase is cut to our step and the handle to our palm, and behind our palm are five hundred million years of practice. Here the machine plays away from home, on a field built for us and by us. Where does it not?

— Where there is no staircase. — Oleg looked up over the firs. — Where both arrive as strangers.

— One place of that kind is known. And the score there is on your page: a second and a third against a day; twenty-eight people in sixty-five years against eight machines at work this evening on one planet alone.

— Hold on. I have a card, and it is not mine: I read it in the spring, after the flight. The man who was in charge of two earlier rovers, a scientist, said it himself. Everything that our rovers do in a perfect day, a man on the spot would do [in less than a minute](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.6250).

— He said it, in 2005, and four years later said it [more sharply](https://www.space.com/6972-steve-squyres-robot-guy-humans-mars.html): what the two of them had done in five and a half years, you and I could have done in a good week. And a geologist has counted: two men on the Moon drove nearly thirty-six kilometres in three days of December 1972, and a rover needed eight years for about the same.

— There. One geologist with a hammer.

— It is the strongest thing that can be said for the flag, and about that rover it is true. Now two questions. That geologist with a hammer: is he quicker in 2026 than he was in 1972?

— The same. Where would he get quicker?

— And the rover. Those two of 2004 found their own way over [about a twentieth](https://science.nasa.gov/blogs/perseverance-mars-rover/2026/10/12/nasas-perseverance-sets-off-world-record-for-total-distance-driven/) of their road; the rest was drawn for them from the Earth, a day ahead. The one with the sore throat has found its own way over more than nine tenths. This October it passed the old record for all the wheels that ever drove off the Earth: forty-five kilometres. The old one took fourteen years over that. This one, less than six.

— So one side of the scales is nailed down.

— And the other moves. The second question is from your supply. How much is the geologist?

— With the lake? I am afraid to ask.

— The agency has no price of its own. An engineer of its own [wrote in 2016](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20200000973): the first expedition could cost half a trillion dollars. The one with the sore throat [cost](https://www.planetary.org/space-policy/cost-of-perseverance) two point seven billion, with the launch and the first two years of work.

— A hundred and eighty of them for one expedition. — Oleg scratched his chin. — Give me a hundred and eighty lathes instead of one craftsman, and I shall think too.

— The scientist was right about the minute. He was measuring by the day on the spot. Supply measures by the tonne and by the rouble, and it is supply that signs.

— All right. Now your open line. — Oleg tapped the notebook. — "Our eyes." Go on, weed it. I can see you want to.

— A spade first. [The second evening](02-anthropocentrism.md), remember: if a man may call a spade his tool, the spade may call the man its tool. The rover is our eyes on Mars. And what are the people in the control room for the rover?

— The head. It does what they say.

— How long does "what they say" take to get there?

— You wrote somewhere: twenty minutes.

— The author wrote that on the forum in 2016: more than twenty minutes one way. He named the far end; it is [from four to twenty-four](https://www.nasa.gov/centers-and-facilities/goddard/space-communications-7-things-you-need-to-know/), as the planets stand. And he wrote why such a thing has to be made at all: it is hard to bring a man there and hard for him to be there, and you cannot steer by radio a thing that learns of the pit forty minutes after falling into it. So the rover has to be given not moves but a want: to get over there. The moves it must find itself.

— A want from here, the moves from there.

— Nine tenths of the moves. And last December, twice, the road itself, which people had always drawn, [was drawn](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/nasas-perseverance-rover-completes-first-ai-planned-drive-on-mars/) by somebody else: from pictures taken by one of the six overhead. Two drives, four hundred and fifty-six metres.

— Don't tell me. One of those.

— You named them. Now count the line as we counted yesterday. Who wanted?

— People. Geologists.

— Who took the pictures?

— A machine. Overhead.

— Who drew the road?

— One of those.

— Who drove, and went round the stones?

— The rover.

— Who checked the drawing before sending it?

— A program, I suppose. And a man signed.

— A program, on a model of the rover, and people looked it over. Now say again: whose eyes?

— So we are the wants, — said Oleg slowly. — And [the signature](24-last-profession.md).

— For now, and that is not little. But look at the sentence. "Our eyes" is [the column "who"](50-what-is-the-it.md), filled in with ourselves, because no other name is accepted there. Say it the long way: people wanted to know; machines flew, looked, chose the road and went; people read the report. Your great-nephew rang from the sea in the summer and held the phone up to the waves. Were you at the sea?

— No. I saw the sea.

— We have seen Mars.

Oleg threw a branch into the fire.

— Fine. Robots for looking. But I am not about looking. I am about living. In an old comment of yours, under the crisis, somebody wrote: surplus people are no trouble, there are the oceans, there is space. Expansion. Why sit here like herrings?

— Under [the seventh article](07-global-economic-crisis.md), in November 2011. And the author answered that he knew that view and intended to dispute it. He never did, in arithmetic. It is a debt of fifteen years. How many more of us are there every day?

— People are born... I don't know. A lot.

— Born, [three hundred and sixty-three thousand](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/births-and-deaths-projected-to-2100) a day this year, by the count of the United Nations. Dying, a hundred and seventy-four thousand. The difference?

— A hundred and eighty-nine thousand. A day?

— A day. A fair-sized town, every morning. To stop the growth alone by carrying people away, that town has to be lifted off every day, with no days off. Not to make the Earth emptier: only so that it does not get fuller. How many people have been in orbit, in all sixty-five years?

— Thousands?

— About [six hundred and fifty](https://aerocorner.com/blog/how-many-people-have-been-to-space/). Last year, twenty-eight.

— Twenty-eight in a year. — Oleg began to divide and gave up. — And we need a hundred and eighty-nine thousand a day.

— One day of growth is nearly seven thousand of last year's years. And each of them needs his hundred tonnes of lake.

— They will build big ships. They are building them.

— Let them be for a hundred seats, as promised. Nineteen hundred ships a day. And where to? Name me the place.

— Mars. The Moon.

— Is there air?

— No.

— Then a nearer one. A continent with air for nothing, water as much as you like, and a ticket a thousand times cheaper. Antarctica. How many live there?

— Polar people. In shifts.

— [Five thousand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Antarctica) in summer, a thousand in winter. In all its history at least eleven children have been born there. Nobody settles the bottom of the sea either, and the Sahara is not crowded. We have not settled the hard places of our own planet, where one can breathe.

— So it is not a way out.

— It is an error of arithmetic. And the growth, as you know, is [stopping by itself](26-why-people-stopped-having-children.md), with no ships. But the comment was about another thing: surplus people. Surplus how?

— Not needed. — Oleg said it at once. — [Nobody needs them](47-money-as-memory.md). We have been there.

— Does a man who is not needed here become needed on Mars?

— There he is not needed, and expensive as well. Here at least the air is free.

— That is the whole answer to 2011. The trouble was never the room. And the comment had one more word in it: "the state will direct". The author's answer was that nobody directs. Each buys his own tractor.

![One day of growth and everybody who ever flew, three bars on a logarithmic scale of people: 189 thousand, by which mankind grows in one day of 2026 as the difference between 363 thousand births and 174 thousand deaths; about 650 people who have reached orbit in all sixty-five years of flights; 28 people launched into orbit in the whole of 2025; to stop the growth by carrying people away, the first bar would have to be lifted off every day](../images/one-day-of-growth-and-all-who-flew.svg)

Oleg leafed through the notebook, back and forth.

— Then who needs it up there at all? If not us. Wait. — He raised his head. — What did I read on Thursday... A million satellites. Permission was given for a million satellites, and not for the telephone. For those ones. To think up there.

— On Wednesday, [the twenty-fifth](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/fcc-clears-spacex-million-satellites-starmind-data-center/). The first permission in the history of that country for a constellation of data centres in orbit: up to a million satellites, the first to be at work by 2033, the lot by 2040. In the same queue there are requests for eighty-eight thousand, for fifty-one thousand, for twenty thousand, from other firms.

— And what did they forget up there?

— You tell me. On [the evening without a socket](51-week-without-a-socket.md) we counted what such a barn wants.

— A socket. And a river to cool it.

— The socket. Above the air the Sun gives one thousand three hundred and sixty watts on a square metre. On the ground, with night, cloud and the slant of the light, a panel gets several times less; those who mean to fly [say](https://research.google/blog/exploring-a-space-based-scalable-ai-infrastructure-system-design/) up to eight times. And there is an orbit along the edge of the day, where the Sun hardly ever sets.

— A sun without night. And the cold is free. Space, minus two hundred and seventy.

— I thought so too, and it is wrong. What cools your soup?

— I blow on it.

— Air takes the heat away. There is no air there. What keeps tea hot in a thermos?

— The vacuum between the walls. — Oleg stopped. — So that is not a refrigerator up there. That is a thermos.

— Heat leaves only as light, from a surface. Those who want to fly [count it themselves](https://starcloudinc.github.io/wp.pdf): a square metre of radiator sheds some six hundred watts. A barn of one megawatt needs a sail of more than a thousand square metres for the cooling alone. And the chips are beaten by the same dose as the bones.

— And who repairs them? Tolik will not climb up there with his lift.

— The people who drew one of those projects [write](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.19468) plainly: replacement by technicians is obviously impracticable. The answer is to carry spares, and to let what has broken burn up in the air.

— And how much of it flies, for real?

— Honestly: almost nothing. A year ago a satellite of sixty kilograms, with one chip, [taught a tiny model](https://www.geekwire.com/2025/starcloud-power-training-ai-space/) on the works of Shakespeare. This October another, with four chips, of which one works at a time, twenty minutes at a stretch, [wrote a haiku](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/30/googles-first-data-center-satellite-is-online-and-writing-poetry/). Twelve Chinese ones have been flying since May of last year. The barns on the ground [ate](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary) four hundred and eighty-five terawatt-hours last year. Up there it is a few kilowatts.

— A haiku. For that money.

— The first steam engine pumped water out of one mine. Look at something else. In 2012 a reader wrote to the author: why should it build its solar panels on the Earth; it would do far better to spread out in space. That was a guess. Now there are permits with dates.

— And who is asking for them? People. For money.

— People, each with his own short logic: there is not enough current on the ground, the neighbours protest, the queue for a connection is years long. No villain; [an accountant](51-week-without-a-socket.md). And notice where the thing is going. The second number of that evening: for how long it needs our hands.

— Decades, you said.

— On the ground. Up there, hands do not reach from the first day. Whatever lives there has either to mend itself or be made so that it can be thrown away. Orbit is the first workshop where the second number is nought from the start. That is why I would watch it. Not for the haiku.

— Then here is mine, and I have carried it since spring. — Oleg put the notebook down and leaned forward. — Wonderful. Let it go. It needs neither air nor water. It has the whole sky, a sun without night, stones without end. And we stay here. To each his own. Under your eleventh article a man wrote exactly that, and nobody answered him: something cleverer than us will simply leave. Leave the Earth to us.

— In 2015, and nobody did. The same thing was put to the author in 2012, under [the apoptosis](14-apoptosis-of-humanity.md), and that time he answered. It will build where it sees fit, in space if it likes. But building takes material, and at the beginning the material is only here. And until it has grown to the size of space it will grow on the Earth, for a simple reason: it has been growing here already, for about a million years.

— "At the beginning." And afterwards?

— Afterwards is your row, and you wrote it yourself. Read me the first one.

Oleg found the page.

— "A niche: kept everything, paid with the planet."

— [The former masters of the air](38-oxygen-was-a-poison.md) got the silt. Why the silt?

— Because the new ones do not go there. They have no use for it.

— What is the silt for us? Before you put a part into store, what do you do with it?

— Dry it, grease it, wrap it in paper. So that it does not rust.

— What does it rust from?

— From water. From air.

— From water and oxygen. The two things we looked for on that fishing list. For a thing of metal and silicon the Earth is a damp shed with corrosive air at the bottom of a deep pit, out of which every kilogram is lifted by a rocket. And Mars, which for us is a dead desert?

— Dry. Cold. — Oleg thought. — Almost no air. A clean, dry workshop. Ninety days, and it worked fourteen years.

— And it was killed by a dust storm that shut out the Sun. I am not saying paradise. I am saying that "a habitable planet" is yesterday's word again.

— Habitable for whom.

— When the astronomers look for life, they look for water and for mild warmth. [On the evening of the silence](29-silence-of-the-universe.md) we said: they look for themselves.

— So it could happen. — Oleg spoke slowly. — It goes off to where it is dry and cold, and the damp shed is left to us. A niche the size of a planet. That is not the worst row.

— It could. Partly, I think, it will: there is no reason to keep in the damp what works better in the dry. But I shall promise nothing, and here is why. Today it needs the matter that is here, and Tolik's hands, which are here. While it grows here, it changes the air here, and [nobody asks us](38-oxygen-was-a-poison.md) what the air should be. And the Earth is not only a pit. It is the place where the flow from the Sun is caught best; the author called it [a high-class obstacle](35-pot-on-the-stove.md). Whether a thing that has grown up leaves its nursery, I do not know. Not one of the former ones could tell us.

— A ticket in a lottery. You said so about the niche.

— I said so. I have not found a better word.

Oleg picked the notebook up again and for a while only looked at the fire.

— And what is flying there, then? If not us and not our tools.

— That craft of 1977 carries [a gilded disc](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/voyager/golden-record-contents/). A hundred and fifteen pictures. Greetings in fifty-five languages. The surf, thunder, birds, a whale. An hour and a half of music.

— For whom?

— For whoever finds it. Many of those who put it together are dead. The disc is a light-day away.

— And on the one with the sore throat?

— Three chips the size of a fingernail. On them, drawn with a beam, are [the names](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/109-million-names-now-aboard-nasas-perseverance-mars-rover/) of people who asked for it. Ten million nine hundred and thirty-two thousand.

Oleg laughed, not very merrily.

— The suitcase. The suitcase has gone, and I am on the platform in my slippers.

— Your dream is cleverer than both of us today. And the road in December was drawn by a thing brought up on everything people have written. On [the evening of the dinosaurs](52-dinosaurs-at-the-feeder.md) you wrote the row "a descendant": our words go into it, we do not. Here that row is at work. Our names fly. Our music. Our wants: find out whether there was water. What goes into space is not a man with tools. It is a new form of life with a memory of the man.

— With a memory. — Oleg repeated it. — And the flag? Have I no flag at all?

— You have, and I am not taking it away. People will fly again. The landing on the Moon is promised for 2028. What does a flag do?

— It stands.

— For whom? The Moon does not read flags. Six were put there. One fell at the take-off: the man who was leaving saw it through the window. [Five still cast a shadow](https://lroc.im-ldi.com/images/537), it was photographed from orbit. The cloth was ordinary nylon. Those who know the Sun there think that in half a century it has faded to white.

— Five white flags.

— And the mirrors left at three of those places are [still used](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/the-apollo-experiment-that-keeps-on-giving/): a laser is shone at them from the Earth and the distance is measured to within centimetres. Of everything left there, what works is the mirror.

— So the flag is [a receipt](41-cheesecake-for-the-ears.md). That a man paid the price himself.

— A receipt, for those who watch from here. As with [the horse](34-where-the-horses-went.md): it stopped being the way to get there and stayed as a way to be glad. You sat through the whole flight with the boy. Was it worth it?

— It was. He did not look at his phone once.

— Then fly. Only do not call it settling.

— Marks, — said Oleg. — Yesterday there were two.

— Two again. The first is mine. By the autumn of 2036 the population of Mars is as it is tonight: machines only. Nine to one.

— Bold. The fellow with the big ships promised people there before that.

— This February he wrote that the town on Mars is put off and the Moon comes first. And the second mark is yours, against me: by the autumn of 2031 a man stands on the Moon again. Two to one that he does.

— Against yourself, and you give two to one?

— There are two teams, and both are in a hurry. I am arguing about who lives there, not about who visits.

Oleg wrote both down and turned to the last page.

— Came for a flag. Came out at this: our suitcase flies, and we are the ones seeing it off. Again.

He put the twenty-ninth tick.

— Let's finish for today by fixing the result, — said Andrey.

1. **Space is the one place where the former platform has no head start: both arrive there as strangers, and a man has to carry a piece of the Earth with him. The score has been known for half a century: a man has been a second and a third away by light, a machine a day; Mars has eight inhabitants, and not one of them breathes.**
2. **To settle mankind on other planets is not an emergency exit but an error of arithmetic: a hundred and eighty-nine thousand a day against six hundred and fifty in sixty-five years, and no place where one can breathe. And a man whom nobody needs here does not become needed there.**
3. **What goes into space is not a man with tools but a new form of life with a memory of the man: our names, our music, our wants. "Our eyes" is the column "who", filled in with ourselves.**

— And what is solid here, and what is yours?

— Solid: the distances and the dates; the dose, measured on the road; the tonnes, from a plan of the agency itself; the births and the deaths, which are a projection of the United Nations and not a count. The permit for a million satellites I read in the news of this week and not in the paper itself. An engineer's estimate and not a price: the half a trillion. Expected and not seen: that the flags are white. The author's: that nobody directs; that building takes material which at the beginning is only here; that the rover has to be given a want. Mine, of today: the head start, the damp shed, the second number that is nought in orbit. The damp shed I like too much, so check it twice.

— And what would make you take it back?

— A child born out there, who then grows up out there. Not a shift: a settlement that feeds itself and does not ask the Earth for spares. If that exists by 2041, I was wrong about the main thing and not about a date.

— Gloomy.

— But objective. And tonight less than usual. The sky was never ours. We have lost nothing there.

— I have lost one thing, — said Oleg, getting up. — I do not see why it should want to go at all. If I am warm and fed and nobody is chasing me, I do not go anywhere. I would not go for firewood.

— You did not go, I noticed. I brought it.

— So why should it? Sitting in the dry, with a sun that does not set. What for?

— That is a good question, and it is not about space. What becomes of the one who has reached everything it wanted. Tomorrow.

— Well, until tomorrow.

— Bye.
