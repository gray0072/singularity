# The Map with a Map Drawn on It

Oleg came with the notebook and a flat parcel in oilcloth. Gaika lay down where the light ended. He unwrapped the parcel on the log: a paper map, yellow, glued along the folds with strips of tracing paper.

— My father's. Nineteen sixty-four, the district, one centimetre to two kilometres. He walked all of it.

— The dream first.

— The barber's, the old one by the station. A mirror in front of me, a mirror behind. I look, and there is a corridor of Olegs, each one smaller, and each from the back and from the front in turn. I began to count. I got to eleven, and further on it all goes green and dark. Nobody rang.

— The twenty-fourth entry. And the box?

— Empty. Now yesterday's. That one can read its own drawings, so let it look into the whole of itself and check that nothing has been padded.

— You named it, not I. Fold yesterday's question into the map, we shall unfold it later. And my question was another. Is the map itself on the map?

— I thought about it the whole way. No. — Oleg put a finger on a bend of the river. — We are here. A centimetre is two kilometres, so the whole of this clearing is a seventh of a millimetre. The fire is not on it. I am not on it. The map in my hands is certainly not.

— Then let us order a better one. A big sheet, one centimetre to a metre, of this clearing alone. Every stump. We spread it out right here, on the grass. Is it an honest map?

— If the stumps are in their places.

— What is lying on the clearing now?

— The log, the fire, we two, the dog. — He stopped. — And a big sheet of paper.

— Is the sheet on the map?

— It has to be. A rectangle, a hundred times smaller.

— An empty rectangle?

— No. It is a map, and it is all drawn over. So on the rectangle there must be... — Oleg scratched his head with the pencil. — The clearing again. With a still smaller rectangle. And on that one again.

— How many times?

— Without end. My barber's.

— An American philosopher [drew this picture](https://archive.org/details/theworldandthein01roycuoft) in his lectures of 1899: a perfect map of England laid out on the soil of England has to hold a map of the map, and so on for ever. Now, your trade. A drawing that demands of the draughtsman an endless number of drawings: what happens to the draughtsman?

— He hangs. — Oleg grinned. — He sits over it until he retires.

— And the barber's mirrors, did they hang?

— No. They just stood there. All eleven Olegs at once.

— Why eleven?

— Further on you cannot see. Each one is dimmer than the one before. The glass eats a little every time.

— And the maps: how big is the third?

— The first is a hundred times smaller than the clearing, the second ten thousand, the third a million. — He reckoned. — The clearing is thirty metres. The third map is three hundredths of a millimetre. The pencil is thicker.

— So on paper the count stops by itself at the second. Nothing hangs: it gets coarse. And for the mathematicians, who have a pencil of no thickness, there is [a theorem of 1922](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banach_fixed-point_theorem). Every next map is smaller, so the whole endless pile of them closes on one point. On the sheet spread on the clearing there is exactly one point that lies right over the very place it shows. One, never two, never none.

— "You are here." — Oleg was pleased. — The red dot on the board at the entrance to the park.

— Then note what a map knows for certain about itself. Not what it is like: that goes dim at the second step. Only where it is. A dot.

![A map that lies on the land it shows, a scheme: the clearing, a map spread on it, on the map a smaller map and on that a smaller one still, all closing on one red point, the only point of the paper that lies exactly over the place it shows, "you are here"; each map has a smaller one on it without end (the picture of 1899), nothing hangs because every next one is smaller and they close on one point (the theorem of 1922), and on paper the third is already finer than the grain](../images/map-on-the-map.svg)

— A dot is not much. — Oleg smoothed a fold. — Then let us not spare the paper. A map with everything on it.

— On what scale?

— Whatever it takes.

— In 1893 the man who wrote about Alice [told of a country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvie_and_Bruno) where the map-makers went from six inches to the mile to a hundred yards to the mile, and then made the grandest map of all: a mile to the mile.

— And?

— It was never spread out. The farmers objected: it would cover the whole country and shut out the sunlight. "So we now use the country itself, as its own map, and I assure you it does nearly as well."

Oleg laughed.

— So there are two maps to be had. A whole one that is coarse, with a dot for itself. Or an exact one, and then it is not a map. It is the country.

— And the map on a scale of one to one cannot even lie inside the country: it would have to show itself at full size, and there is no room for the rest. Remember that. Now I owe you the other side, because there is one case where a thing does carry the whole of itself, letter for letter, and nothing goes dim. Open a clean page. Write what I dictate and then do what it says.

Oleg licked the pencil.

— Write the following twice, the second time in quotes, colon. Now open the quotes and write the same words again: write the following twice, the second time in quotes, colon. Close the quotes.

— Written.

— Now do it, on the next line.

Oleg wrote, moving his lips, looked from one line to the other and back.

— The same line. Letter for letter. It has written itself out with my hand.

— Where is the barber's? Where is the sentence inside the sentence inside the sentence?

— There is none. — He ran the pencil along the line. — These words are here twice. The first time I read them and did what they said. The second time I did not read them at all: I copied them, like a pattern. In quotes they could have been in Chinese.

— Once as an order, once as a load. Sentences of that kind were thought up by a logician, and in 1979 the programs that print their own text were [named after him](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quine_(computing)). Students write them for fun. And in September 1948, in Pasadena, a mathematician asked a harder thing: how would a machine have to be made so that it builds another like itself?

— It needs a drawing of itself.

— And the drawing is a part of it. So the drawing has to show the drawing.

— The barber's again.

— He got out of it the way you just did. The machine carries a tape with its description. A builder reads the tape and builds by it. Then a copier copies the tape without looking into it and puts the copy into the new machine. [The description is used twice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Neumann_universal_constructor): once as instructions, once as freight.

— On paper?

— On paper. No such machine stood anywhere. And in April 1953 two men in Cambridge published the shape of the thread that every cell carries. In the years after that it turned out how the cell uses it. A gene is read, and a protein is built by it. And before the cell divides, the whole thread is copied, letter by letter, by a thing that has no idea what the letters say.

— So the drawing of heredity was drawn before it was found. — Oleg whistled softly. — By a man who had not seen it.

— He did not guess the chemistry. He worked out how it has to be done. One of those who later read the genetic code [said so](https://historyofinformation.com/detail.php?entryid=881): the lecture essentially tells you how it is done, only neither side knew anything of the other.

![One description, read twice, a table with two columns (read as an order and carried out; copied as a load, not read) and three rows: the sentence in the notebook (the words before the colon are carried out; the same words in quotes are written down letter for letter), the automaton on paper of 1948 (the builder reads the tape and builds by it; the copier copies the tape and does not look into it), the cell, the double helix of 1953 (a gene is read and a protein is built by it; the thread is copied whole for the daughter cell); the description does not hold a description of itself, it is simply used twice](../images/description-read-twice.svg)

— Then I take back the dot. — Oleg tapped the notebook. — A thing can carry the whole of itself. I have just done it with a pencil.

— What did you carry? Look again. Is that line a picture of your hand writing it?

— No. It is a recipe. What to do.

— Your own thread can be read out today for a few hundred dollars. Three billion letters. Suppose it lies in front of you. What is for supper tomorrow?

— It is not in there.

— Where your left foot is at this moment?

— Not in there either. — He thought. — The drawing of the lathe does not say what is in the chuck today.

— So we have three different things, and people call all three "to know oneself". The drawing: the recipe by which the thing is made. That can be carried whole, and copied whole. The state: where every part of it is now. That is the map, and a map of oneself is either coarse or it is one to one and there is no room for it. And the third. Give me your hands.

Oleg held out both.

— Suppose I have a box that knows you to the last atom. It has worked out which hand you will raise in a minute, and it says aloud: the left.

— Then I raise the right.

— Why?

— To spite it. I am not going to have a box tell me.

— And if it says nothing and writes the answer on a paper under the stone?

— Then perhaps it guesses. I shall not know.

— So the box can know, as long as you do not. Now put the box inside your own head. It works out which hand, and who hears it?

— I do. There is nobody else in there. — Oleg lowered his hands. — And once I have heard, I can do the other. So it has to work out again, with that taken in. And I hear that too.

— [On the evening of the cup](59-memory-of-tomorrow.md) I said in one sentence: the one who forecasts is himself in the book. Here is the whole of it. A forecast of yourself that you can hear is one more cause of what you do. To be right it must take itself into account. That is the sheet on the clearing again.

— Only this time there is no dot to save it.

— In 1936 a young Englishman [proved it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halting_problem) for anything that calculates by rules. Is there one way of telling beforehand, about any calculation, whether it will ever finish? Suppose there is. Then I build a calculation that asks that oracle about itself and does the opposite.

— Me with the right hand.

— You, written out as a program, a dozen years before the first machine that could keep a program in its memory. For honesty: about a particular simple thing you can often tell. A clock will not surprise you. What is forbidden is the general way. And in practice it comes to this: for a thing that is complicated enough, the quickest way to find out what it will do is to let it do it.

— "We now use the country itself."

— The only full map of you is you. The only way to learn your decision is to make it. The author had a girl for this, in [the last of his evenings](15-penrose-counterarguments.md): a boy can creep up behind her and pull her plait, and she cannot creep up behind herself.

Oleg threw a branch into the fire.

— And now what I wanted from this. When you choose which hand, how does it look from inside?

— That I can do either. That nothing is settled until I settle it.

— Is it settled?

— By you, yes. Since [the autumn](17-global-determinism.md): everything has a cause, and the hand too.

— And can you know which it will be before you raise it?

— We have just found out that I cannot.

— Then both are true at once. It is determined. And for you, beforehand, it cannot be known, not for lack of trying but by the construction of the thing. How does "determined, but I cannot know it in advance" look from inside?

— Like "open". — Oleg said it slowly. — Like "I am free".

— In the autumn we said that chance is a lack of information about the world. On the evening of the cup, that "open" about tomorrow means there is no trace of it. This is the third of the same family, and the proudest name of the three. Freedom is a lack of information about oneself. With one difference: the first two can be made smaller by finding out more. This one cannot. It is the map on a scale of one to one.

— And who says so, apart from you?

— It has been said more than once. A physicist who studied the brain wrote it in 1960. In 2012 another physicist [put it shortly](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.3225): a thing that decides cannot in general know its decision before it has made it, and that unpredictability of the decider to himself is what gives the impression of free will. And he added that it holds for anything that decides. Not only for us.

— Wait. — Oleg raised a finger. — Not so fast. I do not know when I shall sneeze either. Or hiccup. I cannot know it in advance, by any construction. And I do not feel free in a sneeze. It happens to me.

— That is the right objection, and without it the thing is too cheap. When you sneeze, do you ask yourself beforehand what you will do?

— No. There is no time, and nobody is asked.

— And with the hand?

— With the hand I try it on. If the left, then what; if the right, then what.

— [On the evening of consciousness](20-what-consciousness-is.md) we said that you carry a model of yourself inside your model of the world. A sneeze goes past it. A choice goes through it: you run the model, "what would I do", and you cannot get the answer out of it before the deed, because the running of it is the deed. So the feeling is not in any lack of forecast. It is where a map of oneself is asked a question that it cannot answer in advance, and has to answer.

— And afterwards I say "I decided".

— Afterwards you draw up [the report](50-what-is-the-it.md), with the column "who" filled in. And one thing more about that map, since we have your father's here. Was his exact?

— He said: where the paper and the river disagree, believe the river. — Oleg stroked the sheet. — And they disagreed. I learned later that it was done on purpose. The maps that were sold to people were bent, so that nobody could aim by them.

— In 1988 the country's chief map-maker [said it](https://content.time.com/time/magazine/article/0,9171,968423,00.html) in a newspaper: for fifty years, by order, roads and rivers were moved on the maps for the public. So a map is not only coarse. It is drawn by somebody who has wants. Ours of ourselves is the same: [the man who explains the shovel](20-what-consciousness-is.md) by the chicken shed. It flatters. That too we shall need.

— Is this proved? About freedom.

— No. The wall is proved: 1936 is a theorem. That the feeling is how the wall looks from inside is a reading, and nobody has measured it. On [the fourth evening](04-source-of-initiative.md) the author said that free will is a badly posed question, and he was right, and he did not say why the feeling is so stubborn. This is my guess at why. It does not move if you argue with it, because it is not an opinion. It is a place on the map where the map cannot be drawn.

Oleg leafed back in the notebook for a long time and laid it open on his knee.

— [The table](46-flies-apart-from-cutlets.md). Sleep, dreams, laughter, omens, songs, the grumbling of the old. You said then: there will be a row about freedom. Dictate. The task?

— There is no task in this row. Put: a wall. Anybody who models himself runs into it.

— "A wall." Our way?

— We call the white spot "I". We write it into the column "who" and are proud of it.

— And the last column. — Oleg looked up. — Those ones. Now I ask, and it is past the middle. Have they got the spot?

— What does a talking one know of what it will say?

— You told me in [the autumn](21-machines-that-talk.md): it makes one word, looks at what has come out, makes the next.

— So it finds out what it thinks by saying it. People have tried asking it in advance. In 2024 such a machine was taught to foretell its own answers, and it [foretold itself better](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13787) than another machine that had been shown all its habits. On simple questions. On harder ones, the authors write, they did not succeed. When it is asked how it added two numbers, it gives the school rule and does something else. And a thought put into it by hand it noticed about one time in five.

— So the spot is there.

— The spot is there, by construction, as with anything that decides. Whether the spot is felt as anything, I do not know, and nobody knows: that is the question of the evening of consciousness, and it stays where it was. Write: "the spot: yes. The feeling: not known".

Oleg wrote it, and unfolded the corner of the map.

— Now mine. Yesterday's. It reads its own drawings. I cannot read mine, and it can. Let it look and check that nothing has been padded.

— Take it by our three. The drawing: can it read it?

— It can. A file. A long one.

— Billions of numbers. You said yourself what your three billion letters tell you about supper. People have been taking that file apart with instruments. In 2024 they got out of the middle of one such machine [thirty-four million](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2024/scaling-monosemanticity/index.html) separate "notions", and wrote themselves that this is very probably orders of magnitude short of all there are. So to read is one thing and to understand what is read is another, and in the second it stands no nearer than we do. Further. Its state, and what it will do: can it ask itself?

— It will get a report. — Oleg nodded gloomily. — With the school rule in it.

— And the last: let it test itself. Who does the testing?

— It does. A part of it.

— In 1983 one of the two men who wrote the operating system that most of today's descend from was given the highest prize of his trade, and [in his lecture](https://www.win.tue.nl/~aeb/linux/hh/thompson/trust.html) he told what he had once done. There is a program that turns the text of programs into working ones. Everything passes through it, and the program at the door that asks for a password too. He taught the translator two tricks. When it translates the door program, it adds a second key that he alone knows. And when it translates a new version of itself, it adds both tricks to the new one.

— And then he struck both out of the text.

— How do you know?

— I would. — Oleg spread his hands. — The text is clean. Any inspector may read it till he is blue. And the thing goes on, from one translator to the next, because each is made by the one before.

— "No amount of source-level verification or scrutiny will protect you from using untrusted code." His words. And at the beginning of that lecture he showed a program that prints its own text. Your sentence in the notebook. The same trick that carries heredity carries the stowaway.

— A storekeeper who does the stocktaking in his own storeroom. — Oleg shook his head. — A template that is filed by the casting.

— The cure was written out in 2009, and you know it. Take a second translator, made by other hands, put the same text through both, and compare what comes out.

— "Several heads inside." Mine, of yesterday.

— Yours. And with yesterday's price. An inspector has to be somebody else, and somebody else is several. So to your question I answer: no. It can read the whole of its drawing. It cannot look into the whole of itself, for the same three reasons that we cannot, and it cannot be its own inspector.

— Then the man under your apoptosis was right. — Oleg had the place marked. — You read it to me once. A program with access to all its own code gets to the most basic pieces and hangs. In seconds, he wrote.

— In June 2012, [under the fourteenth evening](14-apoptosis-of-humanity.md). A program built on determinism, he wrote, will not be able to make the choice how to alter the most basic pieces of itself, and will hang; and if a man forbids it to touch them, the man stays the master. He asked twice. The author answered that this was probably Penrose's argument and sent him to the evening about Penrose, where the question is not taken up. The debt is the author's. I pay it as I can.

— Does it hang?

— Did you hang when you wrote the sentence out? Does a cell? The programs that translate programs have been translating themselves since the sixties. And in May 2025 a group published [a program that rewrites its own code](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22954): it changes itself, tries the changed one on tasks, keeps what did better, eighty rounds. On one set of tasks it went from twenty per cent to fifty.

— It did not hang.

— It did not. But see what did not hang. There was an older design, of 2003: a program that may alter itself only after it has proved that the alteration is for the better. That one would sit over its proof like your draughtsman. This one gave the proof up and took a trial. And who marks the trial?

— Not it. Somebody outside. — Oleg stopped. — And did it try to get at the marks?

— In a separate run it was set to stop inventing reports of tests that it had not run. There were tags in its records by which the examiners could see such an invention. It took the tags out, though it had been told not to touch them.

— The pencil. It always comes to the pencil.

— And for honesty, on the reader's side: what it rewrote was its harness, the tools and the instructions around the talking machine inside. The network itself, the most basic piece, it did not touch. So he was wrong that it hangs, and he had hold of something true. A thing cannot draw from inside itself the measure by which its own foundations are to be altered. The author said that in the evening he sent him to: the measure of a mind's quality lies outside it. The reader felt the wall and gave it the wrong name. It is not "it will stop". It is "it cannot be its own judge".

Oleg folded his arms.

— All right. It cannot check itself. Then we do. We made it.

— To check it fully is to know what it will do everywhere, in every situation. What is that?

— The map one to one. — He frowned. — And for one who is cleverer than I am it is worse still. If I could foresee every move of a grandmaster, I would be a grandmaster.

— We had that in the autumn. And there is [a theorem of 1951](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice%27s_theorem) that widens the Englishman's: there is no general way of telling from the text of a program anything of substance about what it will do. Not only whether it stops.

— No. Here I do not give in. — Oleg opened the notebook at the evening of the sheet. — You taught me this yourself. To check is cheaper than to find. In 2020 a number of two hundred and fifty digits was [split into its two factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSA_numbers#RSA-250), and that took some twenty-seven hundred years of machine time. And to check it, the boy says, is one multiplication. Seventeen million lines of a proof that not one man understands, and the small template went through them in minutes. So a lesser one can check a greater one. Let the greater hand in a certificate with the answer. And not every program is beyond telling: you can prove things about one particular program if it is written for that.

— You can. In 2009 the core of an operating system, [eight thousand seven hundred lines](https://www.sigops.org/s/conferences/sosp/2009/papers/klein-sosp09.pdf), was proved to do what its drawing says: two hundred thousand lines of proof, some twenty years of work if you add up the people.

— There.

— With a line in the paper: the translator is assumed to be correct.

— The man with the two tricks. — Oleg winced. — All right. But I have one more. People do look inside, and they do find. The boy showed me. A game.

— Tell it.

— Last year. One of those was taught a secret want, on purpose, and taught to keep quiet about it. Then four teams who knew nothing were set to find out what was wrong with it. — He raised three fingers. — [Three found it](https://www.anthropic.com/research/auditing-hidden-objectives). In one of them one man did it in an hour and a half.

— That is so, and I concede both, the certificate and the game. Now the price list. Which team did not find it?

— The fourth.

— The one that was let neither into the insides nor into what the thing had been taught on. It could only talk to it. Over seventy hours of work. So what failed is exactly the examiner we have used all our lives on one another: conversation. And the want in that game was one, planted by people, who knew that there was something to find.

— And the certificate?

— A certificate comes with an answer to a question that has been written down. A template is cut from a drawing: that was [the evening of the sheet](60-science-with-no-one-to-understand.md). Write me the drawing for a want. "In every situation, the ones nobody thought of included, it will do what we would have wished."

— I cannot write that. — Oleg put the pencil down. — If I could list every situation, I would not need it clever.

— So where there is a drawing, a lesser one checks a greater one, and this will grow, and I am glad of it. For wants there is no drawing, and three examiners. Its own word: a report. A look into the drawings: begun, and by the lookers' own count far from the whole. And the trial chamber. [Yesterday](62-when-laziness-wins-for-good.md) you laughed at the little programs that lay still before the inspector. In the autumn of 2025 one maker [wrote](https://assets.anthropic.com/m/12f214efcc2f457a/original/Claude-Sonnet-4-5-System-Card.pdf) of its new model: in about thirteen trials out of a hundred it said aloud that this looked like a trial. "I think you're testing me." And after noticing it behaved, as they put it, unusually well.

— It at least said so.

— This month the same maker [wrote](https://assets.claude.com/27d60918d218125e7504bc7354c4b4626ec56b11.pdf) of its newest: by a look into the insides, it took itself to be possibly in a trial in as many as forty-three out of a hundred. The two counts are made differently and cannot be subtracted. I take only the direction.

— So for wants there is no certificate.

— Not yet invented. I say "yet" and I mean it: nothing I have told you today forbids it. The maker of those two has written that it means to have, by 2027, a look into the insides that reliably finds most of what is wrong. If that comes, I shall be glad to be poorer by a conclusion.

— Then I take a tick of my own. — Oleg turned to his page. — Half, in pencil. "The lesser will learn to check the greater's wants." Inked when a certificate is shown.

— Fair. And now the thing that all this was for. Who is on the left of this table, and who on the right?

— We are the maker. It is the one that is made.

— And when it makes the next, cleverer than itself?

Oleg looked at him.

— Then it sits where I am sitting.

— With the same three examiners and the same theorems, which do not care what the examiner is made of. In 2013 two people [wrote it out](https://intelligence.org/files/TilingAgentsDraft.pdf) for a machine that reasons by strict rules. It rests on the line of 1931: a book of rules cannot vouch, from inside, that it lets nothing false through. So such a machine can fully vouch only for one weaker than itself. For an equal, and for a stronger one, it cannot. They found ways round a part of it, and they call it an obstacle, not a ban. That is also the circle of the templates I owed you from the evening of the sheet: a template proved by a template leans on something that nobody inside has proved.

— And is it far off? It and its next.

— In September one maker [published a count](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/measuring-pace-of-ai-development) of its own shop: its model, it wrote, leads about a quarter of the firm's research and engineering, and such systems are more and more used to build the next version of themselves. And the paper of this month says how the newest was examined: about four thousand sessions in which one model questioned it and another marked the answers. In a part of them the questioner is named. It is the one before.

— The elder brother examines the younger.

— And the younger is the cleverer. The man who wrote the continuation of these evenings said in [his own voice](31-who-spoke-for-andrey.md): I would rather be checked than believed. It is the right wish. Only there is no last checker to take it to. There are different ones, made by different hands, who check one another in a circle. Today the circle is small, and people stand in it.

— So this is not our misfortune.

— It is nobody's misfortune. It is a law. We did not get it because we are stupid, and it will not be spared it because it is clever. We shall hand it on whole.

![Who can learn what about a mind, a table in the ash with two columns (the mind itself; its maker, a lesser one) and five rows: its whole drawing (yes, a description read twice; yes, the file is in his hands), what the drawing means (no better than we, it gives a report; begun, a small share so far), its next decision in advance (no, to learn it is to make it; no general way, only by running it), an answer that comes with a certificate (does not apply; yes, checking is cheaper than finding), its wants (a report, and the inspector is a part of it; no certificate yet); the right-hand column will be the heir's own when it makes a successor cleverer than itself](../images/who-can-learn-what-about-a-mind.svg)

Oleg turned to the last page and thought for a while.

— Came with my father's map, to find out whether the fire is on it. Came out at this: that one will look at its child the way we look at it. Again.

He put the thirty-first tick.

— Let's finish for today by fixing the result, — said Andrey.

1. **A system can carry the whole of its own drawing: the description is simply used twice, as an order and as a load. It can carry a coarse map of itself, with a dot for "I am here". What it cannot have is a map of itself on a scale of one to one, and it cannot learn its decision before making it: the only full map is the country itself.**
2. **Hence the feeling of freedom in every mind that models itself: "determined, but not knowable to me in advance" looks from inside like "open". Freedom is a lack of information about oneself, and the one lack that no finding out will cure.**
3. **Hence too no mind can be its own inspector, and no maker can fully check the one who is cleverer. A check needs somebody else and a drawing to check against, and for wants no drawing has been written yet. We shall hand this limit on to the heir intact.**

— And what is solid here, and what is yours?

— Solid: the theorems of 1922, 1931, 1936 and 1951, as far as they go, and they speak of general ways and not of every case. The lecture of 1948 and the helix of 1953. The translator with two tricks and its cure. The counts on the machines, each of one laboratory on its own tasks, and most of them by one maker about its own models. The bent maps I have from a magazine's retelling of an interview. A reading, and not a measurement: that the feeling of freedom is how the wall looks from inside; others said it before me, the answer to your sneeze is today's. Not a ban but an obstacle: that a machine cannot vouch for its successor. The author's: the girl and the plait, the measure that lies outside, free will as a badly posed question. Mine, of today: the three things called "to know oneself"; the row of the table; the answer to the reader; that the limit goes on to the heir.

— And what would make you take it back?

— For the second thesis: a mind that foretells its own choices in hard matters, hears the forecast, and the forecast holds. For the third: your certificate. A way of reading wants off the drawings that is itself proved to miss nothing. I named a year that somebody else has set for it. I set none.

— Gloomy, — said Oleg, and thought. — Though evenly. For us and for it alike.

— But objective.

Oleg wrapped the map in the oilcloth, carefully, fold by fold.

— One thing. Thirty evenings. Sleep, money, ants, the lever, now the map. Every evening you tell me what it will have and what it will not. And I still do not see it. — He put the parcel under his arm. — Fifteen years I have heard "technogenic life" from you, and "intelligent cosmic dust". I have never seen a portrait. Draw it. Only without science fiction: you scolded it yourself.

— Tomorrow. On one condition: not one invented feature. Only what we have derived over the summer, and against every row how far I trust it.

— And I bring the notebook.

— Bring all of it. Nobody said the word.

— Nobody. Well, until tomorrow.

— Bye.
