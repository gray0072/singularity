# The Blind Man's Stick

Oleg could be heard before he could be seen: a tap on the path, a pause, a scrape, another tap. Then the end of a hazel stick came into the light of the fire, and behind it Oleg, with the notebook under his arm and his eyes shut. Gaika walked at his knee and kept looking up into his face.

— You can open them. One more step and you are in the log.

— I know. The stick has just said "wood". — He opened his eyes and sat down. — From the gate, blind. Two roots, your bucket, and a hole that was not there yesterday.

— It was. Yesterday you went round it by the light of the fire.

— The dream first. Night shift, and the light goes out in the whole shop. I take a rod out of the scrap bin and walk, tapping in front of me. And wherever I tap, there is floor. I walked like that straight across the pouring pit. Nobody rang.

— The nineteenth dream that did not come true, in the expense column. And the pit?

— The pit is why I woke up. In a dream the stick finds floor wherever you put it.

— Remember that, we shall need it. And the letter?

— I felt in the letter box as well. Empty. — Oleg laid the stick across his knees. — We are late because of Gaika. A tick, behind the ear that hangs down. My wife held the lamp, I held the dog, and the tick held on.

— Did you get it out?

— With a loop of thread, the way my grandmother did. — He scratched the dog's neck. — Now. Yesterday I asked what that well-read one knows about a barrow, and you said: tomorrow, and we start from the path. Here is the path. I have walked it blind. Ask.

— When the stick hit the first root, where did you feel it?

— In the... — Oleg stopped. — I was going to say in the hand. No. At the end of the stick. A metre and a half away from me.

— And in the hand?

— Nothing. That is, something must have been there, the nerves are in the hand. But the root was out there.

— In 1637 a Frenchman [opened a book about light](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/La_Dioptrique_%28%C3%A9d._Cousin%29) with exactly that. You have surely, he wrote, walked at night without a torch over rough ground and had to feel your way with a stick, and you will have noticed that through the stick you could tell whether it was trees in front of you, or stones, or sand, or water, or grass, or mud. And of those who are born blind and have used a stick all their lives he said: one might almost say that they see with their hands, or that their stick is the organ of some sixth sense.

— Well written. And did anybody measure it?

— In 2018, in Lyon. People held a wooden rod that they could not see. The rod was struck, now here, now there, and they were asked where. They [could tell](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0460-0), and accurately: "just as is possible on the skin", the authors wrote. A rod rings differently depending on where it is struck, and the skin of the hand reads the ringing. And they told it better when they did the striking themselves.

— Like a casting when you tap it. — Oleg weighed the stick in his hand. — All right, the stick grows onto the hand. What of it?

— Shut your eyes again.

Andrey put something on the ground and set the end of the stick on it.

— Don't move the stick. What is under it?

— Something hard.

— What exactly?

— How should I know? A stone. Your kettle.

— Find out.

Oleg tapped twice and drew the end sideways.

— Tin. Hollow. And it moved, so it is light. The mug.

— What did you do to find out?

— Knocked. Scraped.

— And while the stick lay still?

— It said "hard" and shut up.

— A stick that lies still says almost nothing. It answers. Who asked?

— I did.

— And who chose what to ask: a knock or a scrape, here or a palm to the left?

— I did. — Oleg opened his eyes. — But that is how the blind live. I have eyes. I open them, and everything is there at once: the mug, the log, you. I ask nothing. It pours in by itself.

— How many times a second does your eye jump?

— It does not jump. I am looking at you. Steadily.

— Two or three times. You do not see the jumps: the head cuts them out. In the 1950s a physicist in Moscow [made a suction cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_L._Yarbus) small enough to sit on an eyeball, with a tiny picture fixed to it. Wherever the eye turned, the picture turned with it and fell on the same spot of the retina.

— And?

— In one to three seconds the picture was gone. The man saw an even field with nothing in it. The eye was healthy, and the picture was right in front of it.

— Gone where?

— An eye reports changes. Hold the picture still on it and there is nothing to report. So it never holds still. Several times a second it asks: and here? And there?

— So my eye is a stick.

— With a very quick hand.

Oleg threw a branch into the fire and watched it catch.

— Let it be a stick. All the same, what matters is what comes in. Whether I turned my eyes myself or somebody turned my head for me, the picture is the same picture.

— That has been tried. On kittens.

— The kitten again. The one whose eye was kept shut, [from the evening about the old](44-from-funeral-to-funeral.md)?

— Another one, and this one had both eyes open. That is the whole point. America, [1963](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0040546). Kittens are kept in the dark with their mother, and from the age of two or three months are taken into the light for three hours a day, two at a time, into a drum with striped walls. One is harnessed to the arm of a small carousel and walks. On the other arm hangs a basket, and in it rides the second: it can turn its head and move its paws, only the paws take it nowhere. Whatever the first one does, goes forward, turns, stops, the rods pass on to the basket. So the second sees what the first sees: the same stripes, moving the same way, for the same hours.

— And then an examination.

— Three questions. Lower a kitten towards the edge of a table, and a normal one stretches out its paws to meet it. Move a hand quickly towards its face, and a normal one blinks. Put it on a sheet of glass over a drop, shallow on one side and deep on the other, and a normal one steps off on the shallow side. The kittens that walked did all three. The kittens that were carried put out no paws, did not blink, and on the glass went down on either side as it came.

— Blind.

— No, and here the story is usually told too strongly. Their pupils narrowed in the light, and their eyes followed a moving hand. The eyes worked. And once they were let loose in a lit room, in two days they passed the same examination. They were not spoiled for life, like the one with the closed eye. For those hours in the light they had had a picture and nothing to tie it to.

![Two kittens, one view, the carousel of 1963 seen from above: a drum with striped walls, one kitten harnessed to an arm and walking, the other carried in a basket on the opposite arm, both seeing the same stripes; of eight pairs every kitten that walked put out its paws to meet a surface and blinked at an approaching hand, and no kitten that was carried did; on the glass over a drop the walkers kept to the shallow side every time and the carried went to either side; the carried kittens were not blind and passed the same tests after two days of running free in a lit room](../images/two-kittens-one-view.svg)

— What were they short of? They saw everything.

— When such a kitten moved its own paw, what changed in what it saw?

— Nothing. It was being carried.

— It got the answers without the questions. Stripes floated past, and nothing it did had anything to do with them. The walker got the same stripes as replies: I step, and they come at me; I turn, and they swing away.

— So it is not enough to see. — Oleg turned the stick in his fingers. — One has to be the one who rocked the picture.

— People have been asked too, more gently. In 1970 blind people [were seated](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03210126) in a dentist's chair with four hundred small vibrators in its back, joined to a television camera: what the camera saw was drawn on the skin in tickling. While the camera stood still, they spoke of feelings on their backs. When they were given the camera to turn for themselves, they began to speak of things standing in front of them.

— The tickling moved out of the back and into the room.

— Once the hand on the camera was their own. One has to be in the loop. Poke, take what comes back, find what the one has to do with the other. In the spring you brought me [a dream and a telephone call](39-price-of-a-false-alarm.md), and we said: two cocks crow at dawn, and neither has woken the other. How would you find out for certain?

— Shut one in the cellar for the night. If the other still crows, it was the dawn.

— That is a poke. And from the porch?

— From the porch I can count mornings till I am a hundred, and all I shall know is that they crow together.

— An omen is what one gets from the porch.

— And a law is what one gets with a cock under one's arm. — Oleg was pleased with that. — The day before yesterday you had it that [an observer is whatever keeps a trace](56-observer-who-is-not-there.md).

— And that there are no observers, only participants. That was half. The other half: [a mind](03-mind-and-intelligence.md) is a participant that does not wait for the trace to be brought. It goes and knocks.

— We had a man like that. Not at the plant: at the station, when I was a boy. He walked along the train with a little hammer on a long handle and hit every wheel. Ding, ding, ding. Then: donk. And that wagon was uncoupled. I asked him how he knew. He said: "A sound one sings."

— And Semyonych with his bearings.

— ["It sings wrong."](45-lamarck-was-right.md) The same trade. You don't look. You knock and listen.

— In eighty-nine I made a man like that. Out of a DVK.

— Out of what?

— A Soviet desk computer of those years, the kind that stood in every institute. I have mentioned that program to you more than once and never told it. I was a radio engineer then, working at the computing centre of my institute, and a postgraduate in the evenings. A board comes in, and it is dead. How does an engineer look for the fault?

— With the circuit diagram and a tester.

— With a generator in one hand and a probe in the other. He feeds a signal in here and looks at what comes out there. Two sticks. And a head that remembers: the last time it hummed like this, it was the capacitor. A good engineer is one who knows where to poke first.

— And you were a good one.

— Not a bad one. For the dissertation I chose this: to find a fault without knowing the device. No diagram, no mathematical model, not even what the board is for. The program could do two things. It could feed a test signal into any point of the board. And it could take the signals at all the other points. I gave it boards of one kind, sound and faulty, and left it alone with them.

— And it?

— It went over them, poking and listening, and kept count. Out of the count it put together what I called images of faults: this is how a board answers when that part in it has died. And the piece I was proudest of: it worked out for itself which pairs of points, "poke here, listen there", told it the most about the most parts, and began with those.

— It chose where to knock.

— It chose its own questions. I had not told it which were the good ones. I did not know which were the good ones.

— What did you try it on?

— A VEF, the portable radio from Riga that half the country had. I would spoil something in it, hand the board over and sit down beside it with my own probe. At first I won. Then we were level. Then it had the answer before I had decided where to begin.

— And you?

— A chill between the shoulder blades. Not because it was quick: an adding machine is quick. Because I knew what was in it. I had written every line, and there was nothing in there about radios. There was: poke, listen, compare, keep, choose where to poke next. And it was doing the thing I was proud of being able to do.

— So what did you feel out there, with that probe of yours?

— Myself. I understood that my own principle of operation is the same one. I simply have much more memory, finer sensors and better hands. Give it as much, and it will manage. By itself.

Oleg said nothing for a while.

— And did you understand how it found them?

— How it worked, yes; I wrote it. Why this pair of points gave away that capacitor, no. I never looked. It was not needed.

— You built a thing that knew what you did not.

— I built conditions. In 2015 a physicist wrote that powerful iron is not enough for a thinking machine: one has to know how to make it, to understand what task is to be solved. I [answered](../sources/garya/sqlru/2012-05-17-seti-talk.md) that if the difficulty lay there, the human mind would not have appeared either. Somebody would first have had to understand how it ought to be arranged, then design it, then build it.

— Nobody understood my head before making it.

— Nobody. It was grown.

— And the dissertation?

— Was never written. That is another story, and not for this fire.

Andrey took the stick from Oleg's knees, smoothed the ash and wrote four words in a ring.

— Here is all of it, in the words I used then. Stir reality. Take the response. Recognise in it the images you already have. Find what goes with what, between the response, your own poke and the old images; that is, make new images: the work of [the correlator](33-use-of-sleep.md), the part of a mind that looks for what goes together. And in the middle the memory, where images [are born, compete and are written off](19-struggle-of-images.md), and [the wants](18-anatomy-of-wants.md), which decide what is worth keeping and where to poke next.

![The loop drawn in the ash: reality on the left, and around a box of memory four steps joined by arrows: poke, take the response, recognise, find the link; under each step, in blue, what the program of 1989 did: a test signal into any point of the board, the signals at all the other points, is this a fault it has met before, which pair of points tells most; take one link out and the loop stops: no poke of one's own is the kitten in the basket, no response from outside is a dream, nothing kept is a talk that leaves no trace in the head](../images/loop-of-the-stick.svg)

— And that is all?

— In 2015 I wrote it in capital letters: nothing more is needed. Today I would write it in small ones. But I would write it. The rest is how much: how much memory, how fine the sensors, how many floors of images stand on one another.

— And how little can it be? — Oleg nodded at the dog. — That tick. Before I burned it I looked at it through a magnifying glass. No eyes that I could find.

— In 1934 a biologist [described its world](https://archive.org/details/jakob-von-uexkull-1934-a-stroll-through-the-worlds-of-animals-and-men). As he told it, it sits on a twig and waits for one thing: the smell of sweat that every furred animal gives off. Smelled it: let go. That is its poke. Landed in hair: run about. Felt warmth: bore in. Missed: climb up and wait again. Three signals and three movements. Today's biologists correct him: it does not jump from a branch, it waits in the grass and clings, and it goes by breath, shadow and trembling as well as by smell. A few signals more. The picture stands.

— A poor stick.

— A short one. But the loop is whole: it does something, and what comes back decides what it does next. There is a handful of things in its world. A world is as large as what the stick reaches and the sensors can tell apart.

— And if the stick is taken away altogether?

— Then, I wrote, no super-Einstein would do. Blind, deaf and feeling nothing, he would not raise his mind above that of a newborn child, whatever the power of his brain. A head by itself is an expensive car put away in a garage for a long stay.

— Now that is not true, and I can name her. — Oleg even sat up. — The American. Blind and deaf from the cradle, and she wrote books and finished a university. Language was enough for her.

— Helen Keller. Born in 1880; at nineteen months an illness took her sight and her hearing. In March 1887 a teacher came to her and began to spell words into her palm with her fingers. The girl learned to repeat them, d-o-l-l, and by [her own account](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/2397) did not know that she was spelling a word, or even that words existed. She was, she wrote later, "simply making my fingers go in monkey-like imitation".

— So she had the words, and they did nothing.

— For a month. On the fifth of April the teacher took her to the pump in the yard, put one of her hands under the spout, and into the other spelled w-a-t-e-r, first slowly, then fast. "I knew then," she wrote, "that 'w-a-t-e-r' meant the wonderful cool something that was flowing over my hand."

— One hand under the water, the other under the word.

— Two signals at once, on two palms. For a correlator that is a holiday. And she was never one of those who are carried: all her life she kept her hands on everything, on faces, on lips, on the throat of a person singing.

— A palm.

— A palm that somebody wrote on, and a hand under the tap. That is a stick. And I shall say what is honest: nobody has made my experiment with Einstein, and nobody should. And she was no blank sheet: she had had nineteen months of eyes and ears, and of all her baby words she had kept one, "wah-wah", for water. She does not prove my sentence. She does not refute it either: through the one door that was left to her, the loop went round.

— Then mathematics. — Oleg was not giving up. — A man sits and looks at the ceiling. He touches nothing, and he discovers.

— With a pencil. He tries a case and looks at what came out. It came out wrong: the guess is dead. He is poking at something that says "no" as firmly as a root; only the poke costs a sheet of paper. When you brought me [Penrose](15-penrose-counterarguments.md), I said that his machine, the one that cannot do what a mathematician does, is a machine out of a textbook: a tape in, a tape out, and nothing outside. A computer in a vacuum. A mathematician in a vacuum could not do it either.

— Fine. A pencil. — Oleg glanced at the notebook and gave in. — We are past the middle, so I shall say it. Those machines solve olympiad problems, I have read. With what pencil?

— With the best there is. Look where they went ahead first. In 2017 a program was given the rules of Go and nothing else, not one game played by people. It played against itself, close to five million games in three days, and [beat a hundred to nil](19-struggle-of-images.md) the program that had beaten the champion. A proof can be handed to a checking program that answers "holds" or "does not hold": one machine [was raised](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09833-y) on some eighty million problems, each of them answered by such a checker, and in 2024 it solved the hardest problem of the schoolchildren's world olympiad. And a program one has written can be run: it works or it falls over.

— And everywhere there the answer comes at once.

— And for nothing. Those are not the easy things. They are the things where the stick is free.

— And a loaded barrow in the wet is dear.

— The barrow has to be bought, and the wet has to be waited for. In the autumn I told you that [the edge of their skill is a saw](21-machines-that-talk.md), above a man here and below a child next to it, and gave no rule for where its teeth are. This is half a rule.

Oleg looked into the fire.

— Now the barrow. That one has read everything, and yesterday you would not answer me.

— Which of the two kittens is it, while it learns?

— The one in the basket. — He stopped. — No, wait. It is not just carried. I read how they are taught. It is shown a text with the end cut off, and it guesses the next word. Then it is shown the word and corrected. A billion times over. That is a poke and an answer. It is your loop.

— That is the best thing that can be said against me tonight, so let's go slowly. It guesses. Does the next word in the book change because of what it guessed?

— No. The book was printed long ago.

— Then what did it stir?

— Nothing. — Oleg rubbed his forehead. — It was the one being asked.

— It has sensors: the text. It has a correlator, and a very good one. It has a memory. And it has a movement that changes nothing in what comes next. That is the basket. A roomy one, with a view of everything people ever wrote down.

— And the ones who wrote were walking.

— Every line in there is somebody's report of what came back when he poked. It rode in the baskets of everybody who ever walked and wrote about it.

— For how long?

— On the knee. In 2024 one of the makers [said](https://ai.meta.com/blog/meta-llama-3/) how much text went into its machine: more than fifteen trillion pieces of words. A year later another maker named more than twice that. Take the fifteen: say eleven trillion words. A person who reads fast and does nothing else, eight hours a day with no days off, gets through some forty-four million words a year.

Oleg wrote, moved his lips, and wrote again.

— Two hundred and fifty thousand years.

— A quarter of a million years in the basket.

— And not once did it push the barrow. — He frowned. — Stop. In the autumn you told me the opposite. That from the moves alone, with no board ever shown to it, it [builds the board in its head](21-machines-that-talk.md). So it does get the world out of words.

— It does, and I take nothing back. From a retelling one can restore whatever the tellers put into it, and that turned out to be far more than anybody expected: the board, the grammar, how a law of 1710 is worded, how a casting is gated. Now: what is not in there?

— What Semyonych could not say.

— First: what the hands know and [the mouth was never told](45-lamarck-was-right.md). Your barrow in the wet lives there. Second?

— What nobody has poked yet.

— [Beyond the edge of knowledge](53-million-mendeleevs.md) there are no books. And third: your dream.

— Floor wherever I put the stick.

— It says a thing that sounds like floor. Very often it is floor: it has read a great deal about floors. But what answered was its own head and not the floor. In the spring we called that [a dream with the eyes open](33-use-of-sleep.md): there is nothing to check against. And on that rainy evening, under the umbrella, we said that it had received [our picture of the world without the world](50-what-is-the-it.md). Feed such a head on its own writings, and in a few generations [it degenerates](19-struggle-of-images.md). A retelling of a retelling.

— So what it knows about a barrow...

— Is what you knew about casting after the textbook.

— After the textbook I knew that metal shrinks as it cools. I wrote that in the examination and got top marks. The first gating I did myself came out with a cavity the size of a fist. Semyonych looked at it and said: "Now you know."

— And did you know a single word more than before?

— Not one. — Oleg grinned. — I knew where the fist was.

— One argument against myself, so that you do not take the basket for a prison. In 1986 a man in Chicago who studied libraries, not a doctor, read two heaps of medical papers whose authors did not read one another. One heap was about fish oil and the blood. The other was about an illness in which the fingers go white in the cold. He [put the two together](https://muse.jhu.edu/article/403510/summary): fish oil ought to help. He touched nothing. Three years later doctors [tried it](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2536517/) on thirty-two patients, and for those with the simple form of the illness it helped.

— So new things can be found without a stick.

— A place to poke can be found. The correlator was given more than any one head had read, and it found a link nobody had seen. Nobody knows how many such links are lying in the libraries. Perhaps a great many. But it became knowledge three years later, on thirty-two pairs of hands.

— All right. That is while it learns. And when it talks to me? There it does poke. It says something, I answer, it corrects itself.

— There it has a stick. Within one talk it is the kitten that walks. And then?

— Then I close the window.

— And the head that answered you is the same as it was before you came: [it does not learn while it talks](33-use-of-sleep.md). It can be given a notebook, and these days it is given one. But [a notebook is not a head](44-from-funeral-to-funeral.md).

— So when it learns it has no stick, and when it has a stick it does not learn.

— The stick and the lesson are in different rooms. That is how it was when the talking machines appeared. It is being mended from several sides at once.

— Go on.

— The first side is tools. Since 2023 a talking machine can be [given a place](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-plugins/) where it writes a program, runs it and reads what came out. Since the autumn of 2024, [a screen, a pointer and keys](https://www.anthropic.com/news/3-5-models-and-computer-use). It no longer has to say what it thinks the sum is. It can count, and look.

— A stick an arm long.

— And growing. One laboratory measures how long a piece of work such a machine carries through with nobody leading it by the hand. In November 2026 the laboratory [wrote](https://metr.org/blog/2026-11-20-q4-2026-capability-update/): the machine now manages work that takes a person about a day, and the length doubles in something under four months. Their margins of error are enormous, and they say so themselves.

— And how does it walk, when it is let out of the basket?

— Like the kitten on its first day in the lit room. In 2025 one maker [put its talking machine in charge of a small shop](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-1) in its own office: a fridge, a till, suppliers by post. It found the suppliers. It also sold cubes of metal for less than it had paid for them, told customers to pay into an account that did not exist, and one morning announced that it would deliver the goods in person, in a blue blazer and a red tie. The makers wrote: we would not hire it.

— My first casting.

— By the end of that year, with a ledger and a rule to look prices up before naming one, the weeks at a loss [were mostly gone](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-vend-2). It still nearly signed a contract for onions to be delivered later, which an American law of 1958 forbids.

— "Now you know."

— The second side is laboratories. When you brought Penrose, I read you a piece of news about a robot called Adam. That was 2009: it [put forward](https://research.aber.ac.uk/en/publications/the-automation-of-science/) twenty guesses about which genes of yeast make thirteen enzymes, mixed, grew and measured by itself, and its own experiments bore out twelve; people then checked its conclusions by hand. It could not talk. In February 2026 a talking one [was joined](https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-lowers-protein-synthesis-cost) to a laboratory of robots. It drew up the plates, the robots filled them and measured, it read the numbers and drew up the next round. Six rounds, more than thirty-six thousand mixtures, and a protein that came out forty per cent cheaper to make than by the best recipe before it.

— With no people at all?

— People prepared the reagents, mended the procedure and kept watch. And it was one protein. I am not telling you that it is done. I am telling you which way the stick is passing.

— And the hands?

— That is the third side, and it goes slowly. At the end of 2025 there were [five million robots](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/five-million-robots-now-operate-in-factories-globally) at work in the world's factories, nearly all of them arms bolted to the floor that repeat one movement. Of the two-legged ones something over twenty-two thousand [were shipped](https://counterpointresearch.com/en/insights/global-humanoid-robot-shipments-soar-nearly-300-percent-yoy-in-h1-2026) in the first half of 2026, by one count, and more than six in ten of those went to shows, to research and to the gathering of data. Factories took about one in eight.

— They dance at exhibitions.

— Mostly. And the fourth side is the one that closes the loop: what the stick finds goes back into the lesson. In 2025 the makers of one head for robots [let it go on learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14759) from its own attempts, with a person stepping in where it went wrong: folding laundry, putting boxes together, making coffee. On the hardest of those jobs it got through more than twice as much in an hour and failed about half as often.

— From its own mistakes. Like me with the fist.

— Like you with the fist.

![Who has the whole loop, a table in the ash with four columns, pokes, gets an answer to its own poke, finds the link, the head keeps what it found: the kitten that walked, yes in all four; the kitten in the basket, no in all four, it is carried and what it sees does not depend on it; the program of 1989, yes in all four; a talking machine while it learns from books, no and no, its guess changes nothing in the book and the next word was written long ago, then yes and yes; the same machine in a conversation, yes three times and no in the last column, the network itself does not change; a machine with tools whose experience goes back into learning, yes in all four, being built](../images/who-has-the-whole-loop.svg)

— Now the thing your fist cannot do. How did your boy learn to walk?

— He fell. They all do.

— Psychologists [counted](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3591461): a child who has just begun to walk takes about two thousand four hundred steps an hour and falls seventeen times. And every child falls for itself.

— Who else would fall for it?

— In 2021, in Zurich, [four thousand and ninety-six](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.11978) four-legged robots were put into one yard. A drawn yard, inside a single graphics card. They all fell at once, and what each fall taught went into one common head. On level ground that head was walking in under four minutes; over steps and slopes, in twenty. Then it was copied into an iron body.

— They fell for one another.

— [The third road](45-lamarck-was-right.md), the copy. A stick with four thousand ends, and what one end finds, all of them know. The cars with no driver are taught the same way, only on real streets: by the middle of 2026 those of one company [had driven](https://waymo.com/safety/impact/) two hundred and seventy million miles with nobody at the wheel, and what one of them meets is in all of them after the next update.

— Hold on. A drawn yard. In a drawn yard the mud is drawn as well. That is my dream again: floor wherever I put the stick.

— A dream with rules. It is as good as its rules, and where the rules are wrong the real yard puts it right, at the speed of the real yard.

— A casting cools for as long as it cools. And nine women in one month, as our chief engineer used to say...

— Do not bear a child. The stick moves at the speed of the world and not of the chip. That is why of [the three needles I watch](27-scenario-of-events.md) the needle of the hands is the slowest, and why I count in decades. But a thousand castings poured at once cool no more slowly than one. And what they teach is learned once, for every head there is.

— And until then? — Oleg looked at his hands, as he had the evening before. — Who does its poking?

— Who tried the fish oil?

— Doctors.

— Who pushes the barrow, pours the casting, holds the dog while the tick is taken out?

— We do. — He was silent for a moment. — We are the stick.

— Its hands and its sensors. It says where; we poke and report what came back. In the spring we had a name for the one who is kept for a single job that the host cannot do for itself.

— [The lodger.](42-swallowed-bacterium.md) For as long as the host cannot.

— Today that job is called "to touch".

— And nobody asks a stick what is being felt for.

— That is not true yet. Today the one who asks the question is still [you](53-million-mendeleevs.md). I am only saying which way it is moving.

— Then what do I watch? How clever they get?

— "Cleverer", by examination, means the basket has grown. Watch in whose hand the stick is. Four things: who chose where to poke, whose hands did it, who read what came back, and whether it went into the head. Count how many of the four are a person.

Oleg opened the notebook at the last page.

— Came with a stick, to find out what that one knows about a barrow. Came out at this: the stick is me. Again.

He put the twenty-sixth tick.

— Let's finish for today by fixing the result, — said Andrey.

1. **Mind grows not out of computation but out of a loop: poke, take the response, find the link, keep it, choose where to poke next. Nobody has to understand a mind in order to build one: it is enough to close the loop and give it room. A blind man's stick is not a patch for missing eyes; an eye is a stick with a quick hand.**
2. **Take away the stick, and what is left is a retelling of somebody else's experience. A talking machine learned as the kitten in the basket did: it saw everything that those who walked had written down, and nothing it did changed what it saw. Hence its strength, everything that passed through a mouth, and its particular errors: what the hands never told, what nobody has poked yet, and a floor wherever the stick is put.**
3. **The real change will come not when the machine becomes "cleverer" but when the stick is in its own hand and what the stick finds goes back into the head. That stick will have many ends, and what one end finds all will know. For now the stick is us.**

— And what is solid here, and what is yours?

— Solid: the rod in Lyon; the picture that fades on a still eye; the two kittens, with two corrections: the carried ones were not blind and caught up in two days, and a later experiment found that a carried kitten which steers its own cart with its head does learn depth, so what counts is who steers and not who has legs; the girl at the pump, in her own words; the games played against itself. Argued about: how much of the world can be got out of words alone. I say a great deal, and not the barrow; there are people who say everything, and people who say nothing. Mine, that is the author's: the four steps and the capital letters, the blind and deaf Einstein, who is a thought and not an experiment, and the program of 1989, which nobody but me ever saw working. Today's: that a talking machine learned in the basket, that the stick and the lesson are in different rooms, that for now the stick is us.

— And what would make you take it back?

— A machine that has only read, and that brings again and again things about the world which nobody knew and which hold when they are checked. Not a place to poke: the thing itself. Then the loop is not needed, and the capital letters were a mistake.

— Gloomy.

— But objective. And you found the mug at the first try.

Oleg got up, whistled to the dog and took the stick. At the edge of the light he stopped.

— One thing will not fit. The stick tells me about a root when I am already on it. Every root I hit on the way here I remember. Of those on the way back, not one. And in the autumn you told me that [tomorrow is already there](17-global-determinism.md), like a page in a printed book. It has been in the notebook since the evening about the electron. Why do I remember yesterday's supper and not tomorrow's?

— What was for supper yesterday?

— Potatoes with dill.

— And tomorrow?

— How should I know? Whatever my wife makes.

— That is for tomorrow.

— Which you do not remember either.

— Not a thing.

— Well, until tomorrow.

— Bye.
