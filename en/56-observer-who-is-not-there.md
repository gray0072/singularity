# The Observer Who Is Not There

Oleg came with the dog and the notebook, holding his phone out in front of him like a summons.

— The dream first. The doorbell goes. I look through the peephole, and from the other side an eye is looking at me. I open the door: nobody.

— So somebody did ring.

— In the dream. In the morning nobody rang.

— The seventeenth dream that did not come true, in the expense column. And the court?

— I took the paper in this morning. There is a window there, and in the window a girl. She looked at me, stamped it and said: wait for a letter. — Oleg sat down on the log. — [A face.](55-person-without-a-face.md) I even took my cap off, would you believe it.

— You behave differently when you are looked at.

— Don't start. Start with this. — He turned the phone to the fire. — "At the moment of observation the electron stops being a wave and becomes a particle. Physicists have proved that the consciousness of the observer affects the outcome of the experiment." Physicists, Andrey. Not the women on the bench. And yesterday you asked me "looked at with what", as though it mattered.

— It is the only thing that matters. Have you any sausage on you?

— For you?

— For Gaika. Put a piece on the end of the log and tell her "no".

Oleg unwrapped a paper, laid a round of sausage on the log and said "no". The dog sat down and looked from the sausage to Oleg and back.

— Now look at me, not at her. Does the electron know that it is being watched?

— How should it... — Behind him there was a short wet sound. Oleg turned round. The log was empty, and Gaika was studying the fire with great interest.

— She behaves differently when she is looked at, — said Andrey.

— The thief.

— What did she have to know to do that?

— That I had turned away.

— That you have eyes. Where they point. What follows if they see. It has been tested, in Leipzig: dogs that had been told "no" [took the food](https://doi.org/10.1037/0735-7036.117.3.257) far more often when the person shut her eyes, turned her back or left the room. And [in the dark](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10071-012-0579-6) they steal more than in the light. To behave differently under a gaze one has to carry inside a picture of the one who gazes.

— [The mind that moved into one domain.](48-who-tamed-whom.md) Into one skill: to understand the master.

— Now the electron. What does it know about your eyes?

— Nothing.

— Then when the article says "it behaves differently when it is looked at", whom is the sentence describing?

Oleg was silent for a while.

— It says it about the electron. And I heard it about Gaika. — He frowned. — And about our shop. While the foreman was looking, everybody worked.

— That is why the sentence travels so well: there is a somebody in it. Let's take the somebody out and see what is left.

— Something is left. The stripes do vanish. They did not make that up.

— They did not. Tell me the experiment as you understood it.

— A plate with two slits. Electrons are shot at it one at a time. Behind it a screen, and each one makes a dot. And the dots gather into stripes, the way waves on a pond do behind two gaps in a fence. So each one went through both.

— That was done one electron at a time in Bologna in 1974, and [filmed in Japan](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.16104) in 1989: hardly ever more than one electron in the apparatus at a time, and after some tens of thousands of dots the stripes stand there. In both a thin charged wire stood in for your plate; the picture is the same. Go on.

— And if you find out which slit each one went through, the stripes are gone. Two heaps, as from pellets.

— "Find out." How?

— Look.

— It is a pipe with the air pumped out of it, and it is dark in there. Look with what?

— Shine a light.

— And what is light, to an electron? Light comes in grains too, photons. A lorry does not notice your torch. To an electron one photon is a cobblestone.

— So it gets knocked. — Oleg brightened. — Then it is our micrometer. A thin-walled ring breathes under the screw: you measure it, and it is no longer the ring that was lying on the bench. Any inspector knows that. A rubber gasket you do not measure with a calliper at all.

— And any radio engineer knows that a voltmeter is a load: connect it, and the circuit is another circuit. In 2011, in the comments under [the fourth evening](../sources/garya/sqlru/04-source-of-initiative.md), I wrote exactly that to a reader. It is impossible to observe a thing without acting on it. Information does not pass without energy; the energy may be very small, but it is not zero, and it changes both of them, the observed and the observer. And I added: this was proved by Heisenberg.

— And was it not?

— There are two mistakes in those lines, and both are mine. The first. Heisenberg did tell it that way in 1927, with a microscope that looks at an electron through gamma rays. Bohr showed him that same year that the explanation did not hold, and another physicist [derived the inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncertainty_principle) with no microscope in it at all. It is about the wave itself. Knock on the log.

Oleg knocked.

— What note was that?

— No note. A knock.

— And a note has to last, or it is not a note. What is short in time is smeared in pitch. Nobody need be listening; it is so all the same. That is the famous uncertainty: a property of a wave, not the damage done by a spy. And the story about the damage was put to the test a year after my comment, [in Toronto](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.109.100404) with photons and [in Vienna](https://doi.org/10.1038/nphys2194) with neutrons: by their count, a careful measurement spoils less than the formula of 1927 allowed. How the damage should be counted is argued about to this day. The inequality for the wave nobody argues about.

— And the second mistake?

— That the push is the point at all. In 1998, in Germany, they sent atoms along two paths and made each atom carry the record itself: [a tick inside it](https://doi.org/10.1038/25653), which path it had taken. The push from putting the tick was far too weak to smear the stripes. The stripes went.

— With no push.

— With a record. Now say it [the long way](50-what-is-the-it.md): what is "to observe"?

— Something that met the electron keeps a trace by which the slit can be told. — Oleg thought. — And somebody reads the trace.

— Let's see whether the second half is needed. Vienna, [1999](https://doi.org/10.1038/44348). Not electrons: molecules, each a football of sixty atoms of carbon. They come out of an oven at some nine hundred kelvin, six hundred and some degrees on your scale, fly through a grating of slits, and at the far end there are stripes.

— A football through two gates at once.

— Through several. The balls have grown since: in 2026 the same Viennese [did it](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09917-9) with lumps of sodium of more than seven thousand atoms each. And here is the part I like. They come out of an oven. They are hot, and what is hot gives off light: heat light, the kind the eye does not see. Each of them could throw off two or three photons on the way. Why did the stripes survive?

— Nobody caught the light?

— Nobody was trying to. They survived because the waves of that light are far longer than the distance between the paths. By such a photon you cannot tell left from right. A trace that tells nothing is not a trace.

— And if the waves were shorter?

— [2004](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature02276), the same laboratory. Seventy atoms this time. Before the gratings the molecule is heated with a laser, and the laser has a knob. The hotter the ball, the more photons it throws off and the shorter their waves. The stripes grow paler. At about three thousand kelvin, as hot as the filament of a light bulb, there are none.

— Who was looking?

— Nobody. The light went into the walls of the chamber. No eye, no counter, no film. The people who did it [wrote afterwards](https://physicsworld.com/a/probing-the-limits-of-the-quantum-world/): contrary to popular belief, it is irrelevant whether a person actually makes the effort to find out.

— It gave itself away. — Oleg whistled softly. — Like a sentry who lit a cigarette.

— A molecule can be "seen" simply by heating it. And mind the knob: it is turned smoothly. Paler, paler, gone. What would half an observer's consciousness be?

— Me, before the first tea.

— A year earlier they had [let a little gas](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.90.160401) into the chamber instead. More gas, paler stripes: a molecule of the gas bounces off, flies away and carries the trace with it. And the sentence in your article has a grandfather, [a press release of 1998](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/1998/02/980227055013.htm) headed "Observation Affects Reality". It says in so many words that the "observer" in that experiment wasn't human: it was a tiny electronic detector beside one of the paths, and the "watching" was turned up with the current through it.

![A molecule can be seen simply by heating it: three panels with the same molecule of seventy carbon atoms; not heated, it throws off a rare photon of heat light with a long wave that cannot tell which path it took, and the screen shows stripes; heated more, it throws off more photons with shorter waves, and the stripes are paler; at about 3,000 K every photon could tell where the molecule was, and there are no stripes; the light goes into the walls of the chamber, and no eye, counter or film catches it](../images/stripes-fade-with-heat.svg)

— So the observer...

— Is whatever carries the trace away. A photon, a molecule of air, the wall. A physicist in Heidelberg [said it in 1970](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00708656), and for ten years hardly anybody listened: a large thing cannot be kept apart from the rest of the world. It is "looked at" all the time: by the air, by the light, by its own warmth.

— So that is why the Moon...

— Einstein once asked a colleague on a walk whether he [really believed](https://doi.org/10.1103/RevModPhys.51.863) that the Moon exists only when he looks at it. On the sunlit side of the Moon there fall, every second, more photons than a one with thirty-four noughts after it. It has not been left unobserved for one instant in four and a half billion years.

— And a speck of dust between the stars? In the dark?

— There is no dark. The glow left over from the beginning of the universe is everywhere, and by [a calculation of 1985](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01725541) that glow alone finds a speck of dust a hundredth of a millimetre across in about a second. In the air by this fire, sooner than any clock can tell.

— That is why Gaika is never in two places at once.

— Where there is sausage I would not be so sure.

Oleg threw a branch into the fire.

— All right. Nobody need read the trace. Then explain to me why the physicists themselves have this word on every page. The boy's textbook: "an observer on the platform", "an observer in the carriage". If nobody is needed, whom are they writing about?

— Let's go through the places where the word stands. The platform. What does the observer on the platform have?

— A watch. A ruler, I suppose.

— Does he do anything but read them?

— He stands.

— Then leave the watch and the ruler on the platform and send him home. In 1971 two Americans [did just that](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafele%E2%80%93Keating_experiment). They took four atomic clocks, strapped them into seats on ordinary passenger flights and flew them round the world, once eastwards and once westwards. The story goes that the tickets were made out to "Mr Clock".

— And?

— The clocks that flew east came home fifty-nine billionths of a second behind the ones that had stayed, and the clocks that flew west, two hundred and seventy-three ahead. As the formulas said. The observers of that experiment had seats and no eyes.

— A clock is a made thing. People made it.

— Then take one that nobody made. High above us the rays from space knock particles out of the air, each of which lives two millionths of a second. In that time, even at nearly the speed of light, it ought to cover some six hundred metres. They are born some fifteen kilometres up, and they reach this log: about one a second goes through your open palm. Their own clock runs slow for us, and that was [counted on a mountain](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.1969508) in 1963. A stone will do for an observer. This stone lives two millionths of a second.

— And nearer home?

— The clocks on the satellites that tell your phone where it is run fast by thirty-eight millionths of a second a day, [by the same formulas](https://doi.org/10.12942/lrr-2003-1), and are set slow on the ground before the launch to make up for it. Otherwise the map would drift by some ten kilometres every day. Nobody is up there. Take the word "observer" out and write "a clock that moves so and so": not one letter in the formulas changes.

— That is one place.

— The second is my own trade, and that observer I met before I met the physicists'. In 1964 an engineer at Stanford needed a name for a circuit that works out what is going on inside a machine from what can be measured on its outside. [He called it an observer](https://doi.org/10.1109/TME.1964.4323124), and so it is called in every textbook on control. A relative of it [flew to the Moon](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19860003843) in the ship's computer and worked out where the ship was, in case the radars on the ground lost it.

— Three men on board, and the observer was the fourth.

— A few dozen lines of arithmetic. Nobody ever asked whether it had [a consciousness](20-what-consciousness-is.md). The third place we have been through: whatever keeps the trace.

— There is a fourth. — Oleg scrolled down the phone. — The article ends with it. The whole universe is fitted to the observer. Make gravity a hair stronger, or the charge of the electron a hair different, and there are no stars, no carbon and no us. "The universe is tuned for man."

— Where were you born?

— In the settlement. The maternity ward by the station.

— Not at the bottom of the sea? Not on the Sun? What luck.

— I could not have been born on the Sun.

— When your class met, forty years on, how many at the table were alive?

— All of them. — Oleg snorted. — I see. The others did not come.

— Whoever is [at the feeder](52-dinosaurs-at-the-feeder.md) in March may conclude that winters are made for tits. In 1973, in Kraków, at a meeting for the five hundredth birthday of Copernicus, a physicist from Cambridge [put it carefully](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0074180900235638). Copernicus taught us not to assume that we stand in the middle. Only do not overdo it: our place is "necessarily privileged to the extent of being compatible with our existence as observers".

— Privileged the way everybody at a reunion is alive.

— Twelve years before him an American had [explained the age of the universe](https://doi.org/10.1038/192440a0) in the same way. The question "why is it so old" cannot be asked before the stars have had time to cook carbon, because, he wrote, it is well known that carbon is required to make physicists.

— A joker.

— Note what kind of explanation it is. It explains our "special place" by selection, with no purpose in it. It works against the man in the middle, not for him.

— Then where does "tuned for man" come from?

— From the next step. The same physicist offered a strong form as well: the universe "must be such as to admit the creation of observers". A book of 1986 built a staircase on that "must": the universe must have the properties that allow life to develop; one step up, observers are necessary to bring the universe into being; and on the top step mind must appear in the universe and, once it has appeared, will never die out. A reviewer [gave the top step its full name](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/1986/05/08/wap-sap-pap-fap/): the completely ridiculous anthropic principle.

— "Compatible with" became "for". — Oleg nodded. — The same old thing in a white coat.

— An English writer [told it best](https://web.archive.org/web/2005/http://www.biota.org/people/douglasadams/index.html), about a puddle. The puddle wakes up one morning and thinks: this is an interesting hole I find myself in. It fits me staggeringly well. It must have been made to have me in it.

— And the constants? Why are they what they are?

— I don't know. Nobody does. Selection explains them only if there were many draws: many universes, or many regions of one. Nobody has seen a second universe, and I do not lean on it. I only note what the careful form needs for its "observer". The man who named the principle [was sorry afterwards](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.1983.0096) about the word he had chosen: it sounds as if the principle were about mankind, and all it needs is anybody who is there to ask.

![What stands where the word observer stands: a table in the ash with four rows; in relativity the sentence says an observer on the platform, and what is there is a clock and a ruler, for example four atomic clocks in aeroplane seats in 1971; in engineering it says an observer of the state, and what is there is a formula that works out what cannot be measured, as in the computer of the ship that flew to the Moon; in quantum mechanics it says the electron is observed, and what is there is something that keeps a trace of the path, for example light that went into the wall in 2004; in cosmology it says a universe fit for observers, and what is there is whoever is there to ask the question, as everybody at a class reunion is alive](../images/what-stands-behind-the-observer.svg)

— Four places, — said Andrey, — and in each of them the word can be changed for a thing: a clock, a formula, a photon, whoever happened to be at the table.

— Then who put it there?

— Physics is written in formulas, you said so yourself [on that evening in the rain](50-what-is-the-it.md), and a formula has no column "who". But a formula has to be told: to a student, to a reader, to oneself. And a sentence...

— ...is not accepted with the column empty. — Oleg grinned. — The deputy for general questions. On the platform, with a watch.

— With a watch and a diploma. Einstein's paper of 1905 has him [in its first section](https://www.fourmilab.ch/etexts/einstein/specrel/www/): at the point A there is a clock, and "an observer at A" finds the positions of its hands. The clock would have done. And in quantum mechanics the word went into the very name of a letter in the formula: since [a textbook of 1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observable) the quantity that is measured is called "an observable". That is the debt of [the first evening](02-anthropocentrism.md). In 2011 I told you that even the exact sciences bring in "the observer" and that some of their conclusions give grounds for doubt, and I did not say which. This is which. Not the formulas. The word they are told in. People chose it and recognised themselves in it.

— They had to call it something.

— They did, and I am not blaming them. One thing more about that word. The photon struck the molecule and flew off changed. Which of them observed which?

— The light observed the molecule.

— And the molecule was struck and flew on changed as well. [The spade's turn to speak](02-anthropocentrism.md): tell the same from the molecule's side.

— The one we are interested in is the observed. The other one is the observer.

— "Interested" is a want. So the difference between the two is in the head of whoever writes the report. In 2013, [on the forum](../sources/garya/sqlru/2009-09-15-apocalypse-scenario-thread.md), I said it more rudely: the very notion of an observer is the fruit of a wild fantasy. There are no observers. There are only interactions between active participants, who throw away some of what passes between them as unimportant, being quite sure they know what is important and what is not.

— A participant, then. But not an equal one. — Oleg shook his head. — The football did not heat itself. A man turned the knob. The clocks did not buy the tickets. We set it all up, and we choose what to ask.

— At [the talk in 2012](../sources/garya/sqlru/2012-05-17-seti-talk.md) I asked the hall to picture an observer of a world in which nothing moves. He decides that rest is the natural order of things. Then he notices that some things move steadily, and calls that unnatural, until he gets used to it. Then he notices that they speed up. Every time, the highest change he has noticed, the one his own doings are tied to, he calls artificial and done on purpose, and himself he calls the one who aims it.

— And the man at the knob?

— Sees one link up the chain: his own hand. What turned the hand? A rival's article, a grant, the want to know. [A chain with no first link.](22-who-sets-the-tasks.md) The knob is in his hand, I do not argue. Whether the hand is where it begins is the question of [the fourth evening](04-source-of-initiative.md).

— No. — Oleg laid the phone on the log, screen up. — You are going too smoothly. I had the boy dig for me this morning, on purpose, for what can be said against you. Two things. First. Your heated football. The stripes are gone, fine. But on the screen every football lands in one place. One. And the formulas, he says, give "here and there" for the football. After the photon flies off, "here and there" for the football and the photon together. Then for those two and the wall. Where does the "and" turn into an "or"? It has a name, the measurement problem, and it is not solved.

— It is not.

— Second. There are physicists, and not small ones, who say that it turns when somebody finds out. A Nobel laureate said in so many words: consciousness. There is a whole school today for whom the wave is the bets of whoever uses the theory, and he sits in the middle. And one very famous man drew the universe as a big letter U with an eye on one end of it, looking at its own tail.

— All of it is true, and the boy is good. In order. The fading of the stripes has a name, decoherence, and the name explains exactly as much as you said: where the stripes go, and not why the football lands in one spot. That is the honest state of things. Now, where the "and" turns into an "or". In 1932 a mathematician [walked the whole chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heisenberg_cut): the thing measured, the instrument, the light from its scale, the eye, the nerve, the brain. And he showed that the line between "what is observed" and "who observes" can be drawn after any link, and the numbers come out the same.

— After any?

— After the instrument. After the eye. After the brain, leaving on the far side only what he called the abstract "ego". Your plant: where did the foundry's responsibility end and the machine shop's begin?

— Where the transfer note was signed.

— Could the note be signed at another door?

— The chief engineer moved it twice in my time. The castings did not notice.

— That is the line. The article draws it at the far end, at the "I". The heated football shows that the stripes go at the very first link. And between the first link and the last no laboratory has ever found anything to happen.

![Where does what is observed end and who observes begin: a chain of seven links, the molecule, a photon, the wall or a counter, the record, the eye, the brain and the I, with a dashed line between each pair; the line can be drawn after any link and the numbers come out the same, as von Neumann showed in 1932; the stripes go at the first link, as the heated molecule of 2004 shows, and the popular article draws the line at the last; between the first link and the last no laboratory has found anything to happen; why the molecule lands in one place is not solved](../images/the-movable-line.svg)

— The laureate.

— In 1961. He put [a friend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigner%27s_friend) into the laboratory: the friend looks at the instrument, and he himself stands outside the door. For him, by the formulas, the friend is "saw the flash and did not see it" until the door is opened, and that seemed to him absurd: so the wave must give way in the friend's mind. He said it with all his weight. And some twenty years later, past seventy, he [took it back himself](https://www.unil.ch/files/live/sites/philo/files/shared/DocsPerso/EsfeldMichael/1999/SHPMP99.pdf). He gave two reasons. He had read the man from Heidelberg: a friend is a large warm thing, and a large thing cannot be shut off from the world. And his own answer led to a world in which only he himself was certainly there.

— Took it back. Alive. — Oleg raised his eyebrows. — Changed his mind [without a funeral](44-from-funeral-to-funeral.md).

— It does happen. The letter U with the eye. That man loved a loud phrase, and "the participatory universe" is his. But ask him what "observed" means, and he answers [in his own words](https://gwern.net/doc/science/physics/1983-wheeler.pdf): consciousness "has nothing whatsoever to do with the quantum process"; what counts is "an irreversible act of amplification", "an indelible record".

— A record. Your word.

— His. And the school of bets. I shall not refute it, and it is the cleverest of the three. For them the wave is not out there in the pipe at all. It is [the notebook](https://doi.org/10.1119/1.4874855) of whoever uses the theory: what he expects, written in figures. He learns the result and corrects the notebook. What happens to the electron at that instant?

— Nothing?

— This morning nobody rang, and you entered your dream as an expense. What happened to the telephone?

— Nothing happened to the telephone. — Oleg looked at his notebook with some respect. — So I am a physicist of that school.

— For them the user does sit in the middle of the theory, and they say so with pride. Whether they are right I cannot tell you. I only note what it takes to be their user: a notebook and bets. Not a diploma. They write themselves that [their definition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.03572) of the one who acts does not rule out a dog.

— Gaika. A physicist.

— One who acts. To be a user she would have to keep the notebook. The physicist whose inequality you threw at me [in the autumn](17-global-determinism.md) asked it best: was the wave of the world waiting to jump for thousands of millions of years until a single-celled living creature appeared? Or did it have to wait a little longer, for some better qualified system, [with a PhD](https://doi.org/10.1088/2058-7058/3/8/26)?

— Bell.

— Not the one with the telephone, this time.

— And how many of them think the way the article does?

— In 2011, at a conference of people whose trade is exactly this question, thirty-three of them [filled in a form](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.1069). That the observer plays "a distinguished physical role", by consciousness for instance: two. In 2025 a journal [asked more than a thousand physicists](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-025-02342-y) whether a measurement needs an observer. In the answers it published, more than half said: it does, but consciousness has nothing to do with it, the surroundings will serve. That the observer must be conscious: fewer than one in ten.

— One in ten is not nobody.

— It is not. It is an opinion, held by a few, and not one of them would say "proved".

— And you? Where do you stand?

— Where I stood [on the evening with the coin](17-global-determinism.md). There are several ways to read the formulas, the laboratory cannot yet tell them apart, and I have an axiom: the world is one and lacks no information about itself, so the "or" is what a part sees from inside the whole. An axiom. I mark it as one, as I did then. And one more witness, the man I argued with on [our last evening of 2012](15-penrose-counterarguments.md). He does think the wave gives way in earnest and by itself. Only for him it is gravity that does it, and a mind is made out of such events, [not the other way about](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2013.08.002). Even he does not put the one who looks above the electron.

— "Cannot yet tell them apart." — Oleg picked the phone up again. — By the way. I asked one of those last night, in the phone. I'll say it myself, since you won't. I typed in: does the observer's consciousness affect the electron? It says no. An instrument is enough, any interaction will do, consciousness is not required.

— And?

— Well, it would say that, wouldn't it. It is an interested party.

Andrey laughed.

— It read that in our textbooks; [it has nowhere else to get it](50-what-is-the-it.md). But since you have named them: who looks at the electrons nowadays? In the ring near Geneva the bunches of protons meet forty million times a second. Iron and a program [decide](https://atlas.cern/Discover/Detector/Trigger-DAQ) within millionths of a second which hundred thousand of those meetings to pass on, and another program keeps a few thousand. The rest nobody will ever see: not a man, not a disk.

— Inspection by sampling. And [the stamp is put by a machine](55-person-without-a-face.md).

— If a thing needed a mind to look at it before it could happen, then nearly everything that happens in that ring did not happen.

— That is the stamp. And the question itself? "Cannot yet tell." How would one tell? Put a man in the box, like that friend?

— That is exactly what is needed, and exactly what cannot be done. The friend, laboratory and all, has to be handled as that football was: not one trace may leak out. And a man is warm. He gives off hundreds of watts of heat light. He gives himself away sooner than the football at three thousand degrees.

— Then it is just talk.

— In 2019 and in 2020 it was done with photons in the place of the friends, [in Edinburgh](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aaw9832) and [in Brisbane](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-020-0990-x), and the answers came out as the formulas said. The authors wrote themselves that a photon is not much of a friend. So the Brisbane people [wrote down](https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2023-09-14-1112) what an honest experiment would need. A friend that can think. That can be sealed so that nothing leaks. And whose thinking can afterwards be run backwards, wiping the record.

— Run backwards. — Oleg looked at the dog, at the fire, at his own hands. — Not me.

— Not you.

— One of those. — He said it without pleasure. — A program.

— One that thinks no worse than a man, on a quantum computer far larger than any that exists. Neither has been built. But look who got the part. For a hundred years the observer of this argument was a man with a diploma. And the only observer on whom the question can be put to nature at all is not a man.

— And if it turns out that a thinking one does matter?

— Then the article is right and I am wrong, with one correction to the article: not "man", but "whatever thinks". I would bet against it. I name no odds, because there is no date. But that is the experiment that could catch me, and I would sooner know of it than not.

Oleg opened the notebook, not at the last page.

— Two for later. A trace is always of what has been. Why is there never a trace of tomorrow?

— A separate evening, and not the next one.

— And the one who draws the line: is he on the drawing himself?

— Also a separate evening.

Oleg wrote both down and turned to the last page.

— Came with an electron that is shy of being looked at. Came out at this: the one to sit in the box is one of those. Again.

He put the twenty-fourth tick and did not close the notebook.

— And one for me. "Why one place." You did not answer it.

— I did not. Nobody has.

— Half a tick.

— In pencil. If the box is ever built, one of us will ink it in.

— Let's finish for today by fixing the result, — said Andrey.

1. **"To observe", in physics, is to leave a trace: any meeting after which something in the world keeps a record of what happened. The stripes behind two slits go when the record exists, whether or not anybody reads it: a molecule can be "seen" simply by heating it. And looking is never one-sided: there are no observers, only participants.**
2. **The "observer" of physics is not the one who understands but whatever keeps the trace: a clock and a ruler in relativity, a formula in engineering, a photon or a wall in quantum mechanics, whoever is there to ask in cosmology. People chose the word and recognised themselves in it. Take it out, and the formulas do not change. This is the purest specimen of anthropocentrism: it sits not in nature and not in the equations but in the name of a letter in a formula.**
3. **What is not solved is why there is one outcome. The line between "what is observed" and "who observes" can be drawn after any link of the chain, and no laboratory has found anything to happen at the last one. The only experiment that could find it needs an observer that can be sealed and run backwards, and that observer is not a man.**

— And what is solid here, and what is yours?

— Solid: the clocks in the aeroplanes and on the satellites; the footballs that lose their stripes when they are heated, with nobody catching the light; that the line can be moved without changing a number; that the laureate took his own answer back. Argued about: why one outcome; whether the wave is in the world or in a notebook; whether the constants need explaining at all. Mine, that is the author's: there are no passive observers, only participants. Corrected today: what I pinned on Heisenberg in 2011. Today's: that the word came in through the sentence and not through the formula. And an axiom, marked as one: the world lacks no information about itself.

— And where is the gloom? I came for it.

— There is none today. We took the deputy for general questions for the director. That is funny.

— A pity, all the same. — Oleg scratched the dog behind her standing ear. — I liked it that the electron noticed me.

— It does notice you. Exactly as it notices the wall.

Oleg whistled to the dog, got up, and stopped.

— One thing. The boy has confessed. He never opened that article: it is for subscribers only. One of those retold it to him for nothing, and he sent me the retelling.

— So its author was not paid.

— And the author retold your Viennese, and they retold Heisenberg. Nobody pays anybody. Though I did read that the writers got a billion and a half dollars out of the maker of one of those. Three thousand a book. And who will pay Pushkin? He is in there whole.

— And who will pay the one who invented the wheel?

— What has the wheel to do with it?

— Tomorrow.

— Well, until tomorrow.

— Bye.
