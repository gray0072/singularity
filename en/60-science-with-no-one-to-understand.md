# Science with No One to Understand It

Oleg came with the notebook and a sheet of paper folded in four. Gaika took her place where the light ended.

— Roast, with garlic, — he said. — As remembered. Only she put potatoes round it, and those I had not remembered.

— Roughly, then. The dream.

— The pay office at the plant. The cashier pushes an envelope through the window, sealed. "Counted," she says. "Do not open. Sign." I sign. Nobody rang.

— The twenty-first entry. And the box?

— Empty. Now this. — He unfolded the sheet and held it to the fire. — I printed it, so as not to tell it wrong. Friday of last week, the twentieth of November. A mathematician at one of the firms that make those ones [has put out a proof](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) of the Riemann hypothesis. A question of 1859, about how the prime numbers are scattered among the rest. There is a million dollars lying on it.

— And the proof?

— Seventeen million lines, in a language for a checking program. The next day those who keep that program [wrote](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/): "We have full confidence that the proof is correct." And here is a mathematician of the same week, in two points: one, it is exceedingly likely to be correct; two, [no human understands it yet](https://theconversation.com/the-sacred-object-in-mathematics-the-riemann-hypothesis-ai-and-a-proof-no-human-can-read-297276).

— So who checked it?

— Another machine. I asked that yesterday. And I ask the second thing again: who understood it? Because where I come from, a proof is when I am shown, and I see. If nobody saw, it is not a proof. It is a rumour with a stamp on it.

— Put the sheet under the stone for an hour. You named them, not I, but we shall get to them later. Do you understand what the current in a wire is?

— Since school. Electrons run along the wire like water in a pipe.

— How fast?

— I press the switch and the lamp is on. So, like light.

— In a wire to a lamp they crawl a fraction of a millimetre a second. In yours the current alternates, so they do not even crawl: they shake on the spot.

Oleg looked at him.

— Then what comes out of the lamp so quickly?

— The push. Fill a pipe with peas and press at one end: a pea drops out of the other at once, and none of them has gone anywhere. But I am asking about something else. When the teacher said "like water in a pipe", what did you feel?

— Well. "Ah." It became clear.

— And for fifty years it was clear. With a wrong answer inside.

— With a wrong answer inside.

— So what is that "ah"?

— A bell. — Oleg thought. — It rings when a new thing lies down on an old one. Current on water. I know water.

— With Penrose's book between us [I said](15-penrose-counterarguments.md): to reach understanding is simply to perceive an image. The signal "image recognised", and something pleasant paid out for it. We have met that cashier: she pays [for a mistake caught](36-what-the-rat-laughs-at.md) and [for a guess come true](41-cheesecake-for-the-ears.md).

— And she does not check what she pays for.

— She checks that the new thing has lain down on an old one. Whether it lay down truly is checked later, and by somebody else. Where did you get the water from?

— From childhood. Puddles, the tap, the pump in the yard.

— [The stick.](58-blind-mans-stick.md) Pushing, pouring, dropping, hard, heavy, flowing. What is the smallest thing you ever felt out?

— A grain of sand. A hair.

— And the largest?

— What can be walked in a day. Or seen: to the horizon.

— From a hair to the horizon. Write both in metres and count the noughts between them: about eight. And science today reckons from what a collider tells apart to the edge of what can be seen in the sky: about forty-six noughts.

— Eight out of forty-six.

— A biologist once gave that strip a name: [the middle world](https://www.ted.com/talks/richard_dawkins_why_the_universe_seems_so_strange). Middle sizes, middle speeds. All our old images are from there, because the stick did not reach further.

![The middle world on the scale of sizes, a logarithmic scale in metres from what a collider tells apart, a nought, a point and eighteen more noughts before the one, to the edge of the visible world, a nine with twenty-six noughts; the strip that hands and eyes have felt out, from a hair to the horizon, covers about eight of the forty-six noughts; an atom, a bacterium, the Earth, the distance to the Sun and the Galaxy are marked on it; all the images to which we reduce a new thing come from that strip](../images/eight-noughts-of-forty-six.svg)

— And the electron, — said Oleg. — The one we sat over [under the umbrella](56-observer-who-is-not-there.md). A wave or a pea?

— What did we find?

— That it is neither. That the formulas give the right stripes and there is no picture.

— Both pictures are from the middle world, and neither lies down. In 1964 one of the men who built that theory [told students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Character_of_Physical_Law): I think I can safely say that nobody understands quantum mechanics. And by that theory the magnet of the electron is calculated, and in 2023 it [was measured](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13084): the two agree to the twelfth figure.

— With no bell.

— For a hundred years. So the bell is not what that science stands on. What does it stand on?

— On the twelfth figure. They calculated, they measured, it fitted. Yesterday's word: tomorrow came, and they compared.

— Two different things, then. The check, and the "ah". They used to come in one envelope, and we took them for one.

— In physics. — Oleg raised a finger. — There the world itself answers. And in mathematics? There is no tomorrow there. Nothing to compare with. There a proof is the "ah" itself, step by step.

— How long is a step?

— Short enough to see that it is so.

— One thought at a time, through [the point of attention](03-mind-and-intelligence.md). And if there are more steps than a life has days?

— There are no such proofs.

— Does your great-nephew still colour contour maps for school?

— He did. Countries in different colours, so that neighbours do not run together.

— How many pencils does he need?

— Four were always enough. I never thought about it.

— In 1852 a student in London thought about it, colouring the counties of England: are four enough for any map whatever. A child understands the question. For a hundred and twenty-four years nobody could answer it. In 1976 two mathematicians in Illinois [answered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_color_theorem): enough. They reduced every map to nearly two thousand cases, and a computer went through the cases. About twelve hundred hours of machine time.

— And they were carried shoulder-high.

— They were applauded politely. Nobody much doubted the answer. One of the best geometers of the century [wrote later](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9404236) what the quarrel had been about: not doubt of the theorem, but a continuing desire for human understanding of a proof, in addition to knowledge that the theorem is true.

— There. That is what I said. Nobody saw it.

— In 1996 four others tried to check that proof and soon gave up. They made their own, shorter: six hundred and thirty-three cases, and again for a machine. To this day there is no proof of the four pencils that does without one. Is it true that four are enough?

— It is, I suppose.

— Why four and not five?

— Because... — Oleg spread his hands. — Because in six hundred and thirty-three cases it came out so.

— That is the whole "why" there is. Fifty years this year.

— An exception. One theorem about pencils.

— Then a theorem about your market. How does the greengrocer stack oranges?

— In a pyramid. Each one in the hollow between three.

— Can they be packed tighter?

— How? No.

— In 1611 Kepler said the same: no. Proving it took until 1998. One man, with a pupil: two hundred and fifty pages and three gigabytes of programs and data. No machine wrote the reasoning: a man did, for people. The best journal [gave it](https://doi.org/10.1038/424012a) to a dozen referees. They worked for four years.

— And?

— The head of them reported: ninety-nine per cent certain. And the editor wrote to the author that the referees had not been able to certify the proof and would not be able to in the future, because they had run out of energy.

— Twelve men. Four years. — Oleg whistled softly. — And one per cent left over.

— In that same article a great mathematician said your sentence almost word for word: I believe in a proof if I understand it.

— A sensible man.

— And the author of the oranges did something else. In 2003 he began rewriting his own proof for a checking program, line by line, with twenty helpers. It took eleven years. In 2014 the program [accepted it](https://doi.org/10.1017/fmp.2017.1), and the question was closed. Not by the twelve.

Oleg was silent, poking the fire.

— You are going to say "template" now.

— You say it. [Eighty-four](57-dividends-for-the-wheel.md), your second year at the plant.

— One go at a casting where the inspector took five measurements. — He said it slowly. — And the inspector had no idea how the casting was poured. What sand, what temperature, who stood at the ladle.

— Did he need to?

— No. He needed to know the template. And the drawing the template was cut from.

— How long is the proof of the oranges, for the program?

— Long, I take it.

— And the part of the program that says "accepted"?

— Shorter?

— For the one used in 2014, a few hundred lines. For the one of your news, a few thousand. A man can read that. Understanding has not gone anywhere: it has moved from the casting to the template.

— A template can be filed wrong.

— It can, and that same author of the oranges [told this autumn](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/10/09/what-mathematicians-should-know-about-the-lean-theorem-proverquestions-of-reliability-and-ai/) how. In 2003 he himself found a hole in the checker then thought the most reliable. And in the summer of 2026 several holes were found in the present one, one after another. Through one of them a short false proof went: of his own theorem about the oranges.

Oleg laughed aloud, and Gaika raised her head.

— They sent him his own casting. Through a hole in the template.

— The holes were mended. And they were found by machines that had been set to look for them. Since then nobody trusts one template: for your news there were several, written by different people in different languages.

— And who checks the templates? Another template?

— Two of them carry a proof that they cannot pass a false thing. Checked, yes, by a template. That is a circle, and it has an evening of its own: the map with the map on it. Today I only say that the circle is small, and people stand in it.

— And the drawing?

— The drawing is the weak place, and a man reads it. An example went round this month: Fermat's theorem, written for the program in two lines, where nought was let in among the numbers by oversight. Then it is simply false: nought cubed plus nought cubed is nought cubed. The program does not blush. A template measures against the drawing; whether the drawing is of the part that was ordered, it does not know.

— And in the news?

— There the drawing was short, and it had lain in the common library long before, read by many eyes. That part people understood. The statement, and the template.

Oleg took the sheet from under the stone.

— An hour has gone. Now mine. Seventeen million lines. How much is that for a man?

— The man himself tried printing it the ordinary way, for a laugh. Past two hundred and fifty thousand pages he stopped.

— At ten pages a day. — Oleg wrote on the back of the sheet. — Mathematics, not a detective story. Twenty-five thousand days. Sixty-eight years, with no Sundays.

— He looked at the first theorem. It began by noting that if a number is not equal to one half, then it is either greater than one half or less.

— For a template, that is a step.

— For a template every step is that size. And it does not tire: one of the checkers [went through](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/) the lot in three and a quarter minutes, on sixty-four threads of a processor. The slowest and most careful took three days.

— Twelve referees, four years, ninety-nine per cent.

![How long a reading takes and how long a check, a logarithmic scale of time from a minute to a century: the fastest checking program went through the seventeen million lines in 3 minutes 15 seconds and the slowest in three days; twelve referees spent four years on the two hundred and fifty pages about the oranges and were 99 per cent certain; rewriting the oranges for a program took eleven years; the conjecture of 2012 has not been agreed on in fourteen years; one reader at ten pages a day would need sixty-eight years for the same seventeen million lines, which is Oleg's count and not a measurement](../images/time-to-read-and-time-to-check.svg)

— And that Friday had a second half, which is not on your sheet. The same day the head of a mathematical society brought out another proof of the same hypothesis: nine hundred and forty pages, written by a man, and announced as all human, with no machine. Possible problems were reported in it within hours, and the number theorists are withholding judgement.

— Wait. — Oleg put the sheet down. — So of the two, the one written to be understood is doubted. And the one nobody understands is believed.

— For the first of them people had to be the template.

— And the man who made it? The first one. He understands?

— He led it. He writes that he had been getting ready for this problem since his student days, that he brought the machine to the right place, and that near the finish it far outpaced him. And that he is presenting the world with a complete argument before having mastered it, and intends to do his best to understand it.

— To understand his own proof.

— In September the same kind of machine wrote out Fermat's whole theorem for the template: thirteen million lines in eleven days. The mathematician who had been doing that by hand for years ran the template and [wrote](https://xenaproject.wordpress.com/2026/09/04/flt-anthropic-has-beaten-me-to-it/): it checks out. And added: mathematically this tells us essentially nothing. That theorem had been understood since 1995.

— There a check with nothing new to understand. Here a check, and nothing but the check.

— One of those thanked in the announcement [said it](https://www.quantamagazine.org/update/169569/) for all of them: the object of mathematics, as of the rest of science, is the question "why?", and this does not answer it at all.

— And he is right.

— About what mathematicians want. Now tell me: "understandable". Is that a property of the proof?

— Of what else?

— Is the proof of the oranges understandable?

— To that author. To the twelve, to ninety-nine per cent.

— To you?

— To me, no.

— To Gaika?

Oleg snorted.

— A human example, then. In 2012 a mathematician in Kyoto put out a proof of another old conjecture. No machine: some seven hundred pages in print, in a language he had spent years building alone. In 2018 two strong men came to see him and left saying that at one place there was a gap. He says there is none. It was printed in 2021, in a journal of which he is the chief editor. And this July a group that had spent nearly three years trying to write it out for the template [reported](https://ncatlab.org/nlab/files/LANAProject-Report-July2026.pdf): we do not conclude that the theory is wrong, but at that same place the proof does not seem to be fully written, and among ourselves we have not agreed.

— Fourteen years.

— A proof by a man, for men, which men have not been able to understand in fourteen years. So what is "understandable" a property of?

— Of a pair, — said Oleg unwillingly. — A proof and a head.

— With Penrose I asked: computable by whom? Knowable by whom? Yesterday "open" turned out to be a property of the one who keeps the traces. Today "understandable" is a property of the pair, and we had ascribed it to the truth.

— [The weed.](02-anthropocentrism.md) Again.

— It grows well by this log. And now the rule from [the first autumn](03-mind-and-intelligence.md), which I had from the ant. A system can judge another's logic only up to its own level. What is more logical than that, it takes to be simply illogical. Did you give Gaika her injection in the spring?

— Against the tick disease. She would not speak to me for two days.

— What was it, for her?

— Treachery. The master went mad for no reason and then became himself again.

— Weather. A thunderstorm in the shape of the master. And you had a reason, and a good one. Could you have explained it?

— To her? "Walk" I can explain. "Encephalitis", no.

— An explanation from a mind above yours either lies down on your images, with losses, like "current is water", or does not lie down at all. Then it is not an explanation for you. It is a phenomenon of nature.

— And what are the mathematicians doing with the seventeen million lines?

— What does your sheet say?

Oleg found the place.

— That all over the world they are trying to take it apart and put together an argument a man can follow. And that for this they lean on those same ones.

— They study it the way a geologist studies a rock: with instruments, layer by layer. That is the rule of the first autumn, come true in one week. I did not expect to see it so literally.

— Hold on. — Oleg leaned forward. — Five days later, it says here, a short version came out. Three hundred thousand lines, and then seventy-six thousand. From seventeen million. So it can be squeezed. They will chew it, and in a year there will be a textbook.

— That is your best card today, and it is not only yours. By my own rough measure seventy-six thousand lines are about a thousand pages: not a life, a hard year. And the man himself writes that his intuition insists it will be massively simplified. The repository of the short version says: passed machine checks only; no mathematician has read it through.

— Yet.

— Yet. Why can it be squeezed at all?

— Because the machine wrote at length. It did not try to be brief.

— And why do we try?

— A long thing does not go into the head.

— And does not go through [the straw](45-lamarck-was-right.md): thirty-nine bits a second to the pupil. For us shortness was a condition of having a science at all. On [the evening of the flies and the cutlets](46-flies-apart-from-cutlets.md) we separated a task from a way. Which is this?

— A way, — said Oleg quickly, and stopped. — No. Not only. Yesterday you said: a better rule reaches further. A short rule works outside the yard, and a long table of cases does not. My dream book is long. The law of the lever is short.

— Honestly, both. To squeeze is a task for any mind: what is squeezed reaches further. To squeeze down to our head is our way. The heir will squeeze too. To its own pot.

— And what is short for it...

— May be a quarter of a million pages for us. [On the evening of the silence](29-silence-of-the-universe.md) I said that a signal squeezed to the limit cannot be told from noise by one who lacks the key. Short for whom?

— For whom, — Oleg repeated. — Everything of yours ends in "for whom".

He threw on two logs. The sparks went up.

— And outside mathematics? Yesterday's weather network.

— People have begun to look inside it. A year ago [it was reported](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.24440) that places had been found in it which answer for cyclones, for the daily round, for the ice at sea. The same paper begins by saying that how such networks compute remains largely unknown.

— And the proteins. You told me in the autumn that half the prize went for them.

— In 2022 that program [laid out](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphafold-reveals-the-structure-of-the-protein-universe) the shapes of more than two hundred million proteins. Experiment, in half a century, had found about a hundred and ninety thousand. The same year two dozen specialists [wrote](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9910419/) that it predicts the fold and not the folding: we know how it ends and do not know why.

— And who examines it? In the spring I asked you who would examine those ones when we no longer can, and you put it off.

— I count three examiners. The template: a small thing that a man understands, and it examines a large thing that he does not. It exists only where there is a drawing. Then tomorrow: the protein is grown and looked at, the day comes and the rain falls or does not. That is the stick, and it moves at the speed of the world. And the third: another machine.

— And with the third?

— With the third we are the ant. By the rule of the first autumn we cannot compare two that are both above us. The first two examiners are still in our hands. That is what I would watch, and not who understood.

— And against yourself?

— Chess. In 2025 people [got out of](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2406675122) a program that had taught itself the game several notions which were not in human games, and showed them to four champions of the world, past and present. All four began to solve such positions better. So one can look inside, and one can learn from it.

— There.

— And look at the sieve. They kept the notions that could be taught to another program, and that were not in human games. What did not pass the sieve is not in the paper. The authors themselves call it small, a proof that the thing is possible.

— So the science comes apart. — Oleg held up two fingers. — One half: is it true, what will happen, will the bridge stand. The other: why.

— Which half does the bridge pay for?

— The first.

— Which did the mathematicians love?

— The second.

— While both lived in one head, nobody had to choose. To predict, a man had to understand: he had no other way. Now there is one. The first half goes to whoever calculates better. The second stays with us.

— As what?

— As music. As chess after 1997: nobody asks a grandmaster for the best move any more, and people go on playing, and paying to watch. One university [wrote this month](https://www.quantamagazine.org/update/169569/) into its rules for hiring: no weight is given to written work without evidence that its authors understand what is in it.

— [A receipt](41-cheesecake-for-the-ears.md), — said Oleg. — That a head paid the price. They are guarding the trade.

— They are guarding what they love. I do not laugh at it. I do the same thing here every evening.

— And who is the author? You owed me that from the Mendeleevs: the shares in what a pair has found.

— Count as we counted [the shares in a loaf](12-value-shares.md). Who asked?

— Riemann. A man.

— Who chose this problem and led to the place?

— A man. Years of getting ready.

— Who found the road at the finish?

— By his own words, that one.

— Who checked?

— The templates. And people read the drawing.

— Who understood?

— Nobody yet.

— Five questions. Under the four pencils of 1976 the last line was empty too, so the empty line is not the news. The machine then had the going through of the cases, which men had cut out for it. Under your sheet the line "who found" has changed hands.

![Who asked, who found, who checked, who understood: a table in the ash with five rows; the proof that the square root of two is no fraction (a man in every column, and any schoolchild understands it); the four colours, 1976 (asked and reduced to cases by men, the cases gone through and checked by a machine, the "why" understood by nobody); the oranges, 1998–2014 (found by a man, twelve referees certain to 99 per cent, then accepted by a checking program); the conjecture of 2012 (found by a man, not checked in fourteen years, understood by its author and a few); the Riemann hypothesis, November 2026 (asked by a man, led by a man, the road at the finish found by a machine, checked by several programs in a day, understood by nobody yet)](../images/who-asked-found-checked-understood.svg)

Oleg looked at the table in the ash for a long time.

— Now the real objection. I have been keeping it. That physicist whose book you read with a reader in 2013.

— His first book. We went through the chapter on time.

— He has a second. My great-nephew retold it to me. A man is not an ant. Between the ant and us there is a threshold, and we are over it: we can build explanations, and whoever can do that can in principle understand anything that can be explained at all. If the head is short of memory, there is paper. If it is slow, there is time. As with computers: a weak one will compute everything a strong one does, only give it tape. So there is no ceiling. There is only "not yet".

— That is the strongest thing that can be said against this evening, and I shall not break it. In principle, I think he is right.

— So?

— With Penrose I separated two computabilities: the one in principle, and the one in time for supper. A spoon is good at dinnertime. The weak computer will compute everything, given tape and time. How much tape?

— As much as it takes.

— And if the tape is longer than a life?

— Then his children finish reading.

— Through the straw. And each of them first carries in [the common part](53-million-mendeleevs.md). The pot is the same; you said so yourself, with the screen down. And what do the mathematicians lean on, to take the seventeen million apart?

— On those ones.

— So who is the universal explainer there: the man, or the pair?

Oleg did not answer at once.

— The pair. But with the screen down, afterwards, the man can tell it. If he really has understood.

— Then he has, and it is his, and I am glad for him. I do not say there is a wall. I say there is a price, and a queue. Last month one firm [put out](https://xenaproject.wordpress.com/2026/11/12/and-the-proof-is-checked-in-lean/) seven hundred and twenty-two mathematical papers at once, written by a machine, nearly half with a text for the template. Three were withdrawn: a mistake in a sign. Of those that had no template.

— Seven hundred in a month.

— And a head takes in one such thing in a year, if it is lucky. Your physicist is right about every single proof. I am speaking of the stream.

— Then put a mark.

— I meant to bring one tonight: that by the autumn of 2031 science will accept a result whose derivation no man has read through. I am late. It happened last Saturday.

— And the autumn mark? — Oleg found the list in his phone. — "A Millennium problem solved mainly by a machine, by the autumn of 2036, one in two." This is one of the seven.

— I am not ticking it, and not out of modesty. The prize has rules of its own: printed in a journal, two years gone by, [general acceptance](https://www.claymath.org/millennium-problems/rules/). And "mainly by a machine" is still to be weighed: a man led it. It goes into the list as it is, to be counted when the needles move. One thing I shall say now. I gave it ten years and even odds. If it stands, it took a few months, and I was not bold. I was timid again.

— Noted. And the new ones?

— Two. The first is mine: by the autumn of 2031 at least one of the old journals of the first rank will be printing theorems whose proofs its referees did not read. They will read the statement, and the template will read the rest. Three to one.

— And the second?

— The second is for your physicist, and I put it against myself. By the same autumn this very proof will have an account written for people, of which those who work in that field say: we understand why. Two to one that it will. If it does not, I have been more right tonight than I should like.

— And if both come true?

— Then we are both right, and that is the likeliest. One proof gets chewed. The queue grows.

Oleg wrote both down, and turned to the last page.

— Came with a sheet, to find out who understood. Came out at this: understanding is what is left to us of science. As a hobby. Again.

He put the twenty-eighth tick.

— Let's finish for today by fixing the result, — said Andrey.

1. **To understand is to reduce a new thing to images already in the head, and ours were felt out with a stick in the world of middle sizes. "Understandable" is not a property of a truth. It is a property of a pair: a truth and a head.**
2. **Science has long been able to do more than we can picture. What it stands on is not the "ah" but the check: tomorrow, which comes and is compared, or a small template that a man does understand. The two used to come in one envelope, and we took them for one.**
3. **Now science is coming apart. "Is it true" and "what will happen" go to whoever calculates better. "Why" stays with us, as a pleasure and not as production. The thing to watch is not who understood but who checks.**

— And what is solid here, and what is yours?

— Solid: the four pencils and the oranges, with their dates; the twelfth figure; the news of your sheet, which I read in the statement of the checkers and on the page where the checks are listed; the holes in the template, told by the man who found the first of them. Told by a newspaper and not read by me in the original: that problems were found in the human proof within hours. Argued about: how much of what a network has inside can be got out and taught; so far, a few notions of chess. Mine, that is the author's: understanding as the perception of an image, and the rule of the ant. Today's: the template in place of the "ah"; the three examiners; and that shortness is partly a task and partly our way. The last I cannot divide cleanly.

— And what would make you take it back?

— Two things. If it turns out that without "why" the calculating stops: that those who do not understand begin to be wrong where those who understand were not. Or if the queue is chewed as fast as it grows.

— Gloomy.

— But objective. And nobody is taking your "ah" away. On the contrary. It is the one thing in this story that nobody else needs.

— So what have we been doing here, sixty evenings?

— Understanding.

— Production?

— None.

Oleg folded the sheet in four and put it into the notebook.

— All right. Calculating is theirs. But going there, seeing with one's own eyes, sticking in a flag: that is ours. Somebody has to stand on the spot. — He nodded at the sky over the forest. — That reddish one. A star?

— A planet. Mars.

— Is anybody there now?

— Yes.

— People?

— No people. That is for tomorrow.

— Well, until tomorrow.

— Bye.
